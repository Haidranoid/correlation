# Volume clock: buy before the news you can predict

**TL;DR:** This tests a published premium with a new calendar. Stocks tend to earn more around their scheduled earnings announcements (Frazzini & Lamont 2007; Barber et al. 2013; Savor & Wilson 2016). This project has no earnings calendar, so the volume clock predicts each stock's next news window from its volume spikes one and two years earlier. It buys six trading days before the predicted day and sells the day after. The clock fails its own first test. In 2006–2019 only 30.1% of its 7,478 predicted windows held a 4× volume spike within 3 trading days of the predicted day, against a bar of 40%. That is 3.8 times the 8.0% chance rate, so the clock does point at news weeks, but most of that news doesn't reach 4× volume. The premium isn't there either. The plan's measure, the mean market-relative log return per window, was −19 bps in 2006–2019 (t −1.75) and −7 bps in 2020–2025 (t −0.42), against a bar of +20 bps with t ≥ 3. In simple returns, a check added after seeing the results, it was +20 bps, but only +2.5 bps (t 0.24) more than the same stocks earned outside their windows. In the 16 unseen months it was +10 bps (t 0.33), and the account made +31.4% against +15.2% for the median random account (p = 0.11; p = 0.25 against volatility-matched ones). Over the 18 walk-forward years, the judge's long-only account beat turnover-matched random accounts by 0.8 points a year (se 2.1, ahead in 9 of 18 years), and volatility-matched ones by 1.2. Verdict: no edge. The clock fails test 1, and the published premium doesn't show up in this data.

## The idea

- **The premium:** the earnings announcement premium is one of the steadier calendar effects. It is explained as compensation for the risk of the announcement itself, and as attention-driven buying around it.
- **Without a calendar:** stocks with earnings dates have them because their volume shows them. Predicting the next window from last year's spikes, as Frazzini & Lamont predicted announcement months from twelve months earlier, needs only prices and volume.
- **Who carries the risk:** Savor & Wilson link the premium to announcers' co-movement with the market. Announcers carry information about everyone.

## Nearest prior work

From the earlier survey and well-known literature. A separate novelty check runs alongside, and its findings will be added here.

| Work | What it found |
|---|---|
| Beaver 1968 | Volume and volatility jump around earnings announcements |
| Chari, Jagannathan & Ofer 1988, *JFE* | Abnormal returns around announcements, especially for small firms |
| Frazzini & Lamont 2007, NBER w13090, "The earnings announcement premium and trading volume" | Stocks earn about 0.5–1% more in predicted announcement months, with the month predicted from 12 months earlier; the premium is larger for stocks with high past announcement volume |
| Barber, De George, Lehavy & Trueman 2013, *JFE* | The premium exists around the globe |
| Savor & Wilson 2016, *JF*, "Earnings announcements and systematic risk" | Announcers' higher returns compensate for the market risk they reveal |

**What's new:** the calendar comes from volume alone, and the clock's accuracy is checked before any return test.

**Novelty check (2026-10-21, a separate search; it changed no rules):** partly done. Nobody was found inferring announcement dates from volume spikes alone.
- **Frazzini & Lamont** predict the announcement *month* from actual dates a year earlier (80% accurate), or from the fiscal year-end alone (73%). Their premium was 7–18% a year over 1973–2004, largest for stocks with high past announcement volume.
- **Cohen, Dey, Lys & Sunder 2007** (*JAE*) find the premium smaller when measured against *expected* rather than actual dates, and smaller after 1988.
- **Heitz, Narayanamoorthy & Zekhnini, "The Disappearing Earnings Announcement Premium"** (SSRN 3296537; abstract only) report that the US premium vanished after the 2004 Form 8-K rule. That covers this whole sample.
- **Tsafack, Becker & Han 2023** (*Pacific-Basin Finance Journal*) find it still positive after the financial crisis.
- **Heston & Sadka 2008** (*JFE*) find 12-month return seasonality unrelated to earnings, which a 364-day projection could also pick up.

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Universe:**
- the judge-like liquid set (median price ≥ $5, median dollar volume ≥ $5M over the past 252 days, data on ≥ 90% of the past 756 days);
- not fund-like (`c2eval.twins(E)['fund_like']`, latest at or before the day).

**Spike:**
- volume at least 4× its median over the previous 60 trading days;
- only the first spike in any 5-day run counts.
- Volume comes from the store (`c2eval.store_panel(..., 'volume')`), or from the export's dollar volume ÷ close.

**The clock:**
- **Projection:** take each spike on calendar date D, project it to D + 364 calendar days (the same weekday a year later), and call the nearest trading day the predicted centre c.
- **Recurrence:** keep the prediction only if the stock also had a spike within ±5 trading days of D − 364. So the rhythm must recur over two prior years, which drops one-off spikes such as index additions and mergers.
- If several spikes predict centres within 10 trading days of each other, keep the earliest.

**The trade:** buy at the close of c − 6 (six trading days before the predicted centre) and sell at the close of c + 1. Every decision uses data from a year or more earlier.

**Secondary ranking** (Frazzini & Lamont's own conditioning; fixed now, so there are no ties): the stock's mean volume ratio (volume ÷ 60-day median) on its spikes over the past two years, higher first.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **The clock works.** The share of predicted centres with a real spike within ±3 trading days of c, over 2006–2019. **Pass:** ≥ 40%. If the clock misses, the premium can't be captured.
2. **The premium.** The market-relative log return (stock minus the export's `market`) from close c − 6 to close c + 1, averaged per event, 2006–2019, with standard errors clustered by entry date.
   - **Pass:** mean ≥ +20 bps with t ≥ 3, and positive net of 10 bps per round trip.
   - **Also reported:** the same stocks' average 7-day market-relative return outside their windows, and windows where the clock missed against windows where it hit.
3. **Later years.** 2020–2025-06 (old export): same sign, with t ≥ 1.5.
4. **The 16 unseen months** (new export, once, at the end): same sign.

**Money test (the judge)**
- Score = the secondary ranking value, written on every day c − 6 .. c of each predicted window (so the account holds from close c − 6 to close c + 1). NaN elsewhere.
- `rebalance=1`. Uniform and volatility-matched random accounts are both reported.
- On days with more than 20 predicted announcers, the account holds the 20 with the highest past announcement volume.

## The number for the file name

The judge's walk-forward change over random, with uniform random accounts, 0 bps (`c2eval.change_over_random`).

## Ways it can fool us

- **Survivor-only data:** announcers that later delisted are missing, which flatters the long-only account.
- **Spikes that aren't earnings:** index rebalancing (late June, the Russell reconstitution) and option expiry recur yearly too. Report the hit rate and the premium separately for the last week of June.
- **Moving dates:** companies that change their reporting dates are missed by the clock. That lowers the hit rate but doesn't bias the returns.

## Results (run 2026-10-21)

**What was built.** On the old export, 314,172 counted spikes gave 275,188 projections inside the data. 74,777 kept the recurrence and 70,093 survived the 10-day rule. Of these, 13,356 windows passed the universe and fund-like checks at entry: 72 in 2005 (before the test periods), 7,478 in 2006–2019 and 5,806 in 2020–2025-06. Of the last, 5,727 sell inside the data and 5,723 have a full hit check. Each window is one trade of 7 trading days.

**Plumbing check.** 5-day reversal through this folder's `save_scores` and `judge` path gave +11.8 points a year, 8 of 18 years, as expected.

### Caveats

- **Early years are thin.** The judge-like universe held about 300 stocks in 2005–07 and 900 by 2009, against 2,300 in 2025. Split-adjusted prices fail the $5 rule for companies that split often, as the judge's own universe does. So 2006–07 have only 70 and 126 windows.
- **Survivor-only data** flatters the account and the simple returns alike. The log-return premium is negative despite it.

## Decisions made while building it

All of these were fixed on 2026-10-21 **before any outcome data was seen** (before the event table existed), unless marked otherwise.

| Question the plan left open | Decision |
|---|---|
| Volume ratio | Store share volume ÷ median of the positive volumes over the previous 60 trading days (d−60..d−1), needing ≥ 40 of them; zero volume counts as missing (the bystanders' panel rule) |
| "First spike in any 5-day run" | A spike (ratio ≥ 4) counts if there was no spike in the 5 trading days before it (the bystanders' rule) |
| Which stocks spikes are found on | Every export stock; the universe applies only to the trade, at its entry day c − 6 |
| "Spike" in the recurrence check and the hit check | Counted spikes (first in run), as the spike rule defines them. The hit rate with raw spikes (any day with ratio ≥ 4) is also reported, but the verdict uses counted spikes |
| Nearest trading day to D + 364 and D − 364 | The export's trading day closest in calendar days; a tie goes to the earlier day. A projection past the export's last day is dropped |
| Order of the clock's filters | Projection, then recurrence, then the 10-day rule; the 10-day rule is greedy per stock: a centre is kept if it is more than 10 trading days after the last kept centre |
| Universe "data" and timing | Checked at the entry day e = c − 6: close finite on e; median export close ≥ $5 and median dollar volume ≥ $5M over e−251..e; finite closes on ≥ 90% of e−755..e. "Data" is a finite close, so the census variable `v` is never touched. Fund-like: the flag at the latest twins rebalance at or before e |
| Which period an event belongs to | Its entry day's calendar year: 2006–2019 (tests 1–2), 2020-01-01..2025-06-27 (test 3), 2025-06-30 on (test 4). An event needs c + 1 inside the export for the return and c + 3 for the hit check; otherwise it is dropped from that number |
| The return | ln(close[c+1] / close[c−6]) minus the sum of the export's `market` over days c−5..c+1 |
| Net of costs | Mean minus 10 bps (log), one round trip per event |
| Clustered standard error | Clusters are entry days: var = G/(G−1) × Σ_g (Σ_{i∈g} (x_i − mean))² / n² |
| The "outside" baseline | For each stock with events in the period: the mean 7-day market-relative log return over its start days d in the same period with d in the universe (checked on a 21-day grid, latest grid day at or before d), not fund-like, and [d, d+7] not overlapping any of its predicted windows [c−6, c+1]. Averaged over stocks weighted by their event counts |
| Chance hit rate (context, not a pass rule) | For the same stocks and weights: the share of their in-universe days in the period with a counted spike within ±3 trading days |
| "Last week of June" | Events whose centre c falls on June 22–30 (covers every Russell reconstitution day under the last-Friday rule) |
| "Past two years" for the ranking | Counted spikes in the 504 trading days ending at the entry day e (inclusive), computed once at e and held for the whole window |
| Money-test scores | The ranking value written on days c−6..c (7 columns) for every event that passes the universe and fund-like checks at e, NaN elsewhere; the judge applies its own yearly universe on top |
| Book size in the money test | **Before the money test ran:** the judge's own candidate-restricted scores (`--dump-scores`) give the names held each day, min(20, positive scores). Reported so the number isn't read as a 20-stock account |
| Clock diagnostics (hit ±10, ≥ 2× volume within ±3 with its chance rate, where the nearest spike falls) | **After seeing test 1.** Added to tell a wrong date from a too-high bar. Decides nothing |
| Simple returns and each window against its own stock's outside drift | **After seeing test 2** (log mean negative, median positive). Added because log means sit about half the variance below simple means, and the published premium is in simple returns. Decides nothing |
| New-export tables limited to the unseen months | **After the old-export results, before the new export was built.** Presentation only |
| Test 4's "same sign" | **After seeing the unseen months.** Coded like test 3: the sign of test 2's mean. The unseen mean is positive and test 2's negative, so test 4 fails as written; read as "positive, like the published premium", it passes in sign only (t 0.33). Neither reading changes the verdict, which stopped at test 1 |
