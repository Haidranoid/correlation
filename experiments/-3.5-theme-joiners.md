# Theme joiners: do stocks that start trading with the hot group keep going?

**TL;DR:** Style investing predicts that money chases winning groups of stocks, and that stocks added to a popular category start moving with it (Barberis & Shleifer 2003; Barberis, Shleifer & Wurgler 2005; Boyer 2011); those studies use official categories such as index membership, and taking the categories from the data, the project's co-movement groups, and turning "joining a theme" into a stock-level signal is new. Each month (232 refits of 30 groups among the 1,500 most-traded stocks, 2006-03 to 2025-06) the test took the 3 groups that had risen most and the 3 that had fallen most over 63 days, and scored each stock by how much its correlation with the hot groups had risen recently, minus the same for the cold groups. Over 2006-03..2019 (165 refits) the Fama-MacBeth coefficient on that score's percentile, with the plan's controls (own 63-day return, own 12-1 return, hot and cold membership), was −16.7 bps of 21-day market-relative return from the bottom to the top of the score (t = −0.65), against a bar of positive with t ≥ 3, so test 1 fails. Without the controls it was −45.6 bps (t = −1.21): the controls absorb about two thirds of a coefficient that already had the wrong sign. The residualized decile book lost 17 bps a month after costs (gross −2 bps, net t = −0.74), so test 2 fails too. The sign stayed negative later, at −0.4 bps in 2020–2025-06 (t = −0.01) and −59.8 bps over the 15 refits of the 16 unseen months (t = −0.46), never the predicted positive. The judge's account (top 20 by the residualized score, rebalanced every 21 days) made −1.5 points a year against uniform random accounts (se 3.9, ahead in 8 of 18 years) and −0.8 against volatility-matched ones; in the unseen months it made +15.0% against +19.7% for the median random account (p = 0.63) and +24.6% for SPY. The joiners it finds look sensible (truckers and regional banks starting to move with tech and defensives in April 2020, REITs and tankers starting to move with oil and banks in January 2021), but they don't keep rising. Verdict: no edge.

**What's new:** joining a data-driven theme, measured by a change in correlation, as a stock-level signal. New outright, as far as the earlier survey found.

**Novelty check (2026-10-21, a separate search; it changed no rules):** partly done, correlation-defined themes and comovement-sorted momentum are published at the basket level; a stock-level joining signal from a change in correlation was not found.
- **Wahal & Yavuz 2013** (JFE, "Style investing, comovement and return predictability"): Past style returns help predict a stock's return beyond size, book-to-market and its own past return, and momentum portfolios with high comovement with their style earn more than those with low comovement. A Brazilian replication (FGV thesis, 2000–2011) did not find the same pattern.
- **Chen, Singal & Whitelaw 2016** (JFE, "Comovement revisited"): With univariate regressions and matched controls, almost all excess comovement after index additions and splits disappears; winner stocks' betas rise, so much of the apparent new comovement is momentum. That is the plan's "joining is partly rallying" risk, found in published data.
- **Candès, Hastie, Hogan, Kahn, Luo & Spector 2025** (FAJ, "Thematic investing: a risk-based perspective"): Defines a theme as a basket whose residual returns show significant transient correlation; on an investment bank's baskets, the significant ones have more risk than predicted and trend significantly, and the insignificant ones don't trend on average. Basket level, not stock level.
- **Ben-David, Franzoni, Kim & Moussawi 2023** (RFS, "Competition for attention in the ETF space"): Specialized and thematic ETFs lose about 30% risk-adjusted over their first five years, because the stocks they hold are overvalued at launch; at the fund level, theme demand is followed by losses, not gains.
- **Not found:** a stock-level score from the rise in a stock's correlation with the hottest data-driven groups (minus the coldest), controlled for its own 3-month and 12-1 returns.

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Groups:**
- the groups map as breadth thrust built it (`research/breadth_thrust/`, copied from phantom portfolios' `pp_common.py`);
- 30 groups of the 1,500 most-traded stocks, from market-relative correlations over 252 days;
- refit every 21 trading days, minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)`;
- reuse breadth thrust's refits if they're cached.

**Hot and cold:**
- at each refit t, groups are ranked by their members' mean market-relative log return over t−62..t;
- H = the daily equal-weight market-relative return of the top 3 groups' members;
- C = the same for the bottom 3.

**Joiner score** for each of the 1,500 stocks, members included:
- J = [corr(s, H) over t−62..t − corr(s, H) over t−314..t−63] − [the same for C];
- on market-relative daily returns, needing at least 55 and 200 days.

**Controls** (fixed now):
- the stock's own market-relative return over t−62..t;
- its own 12-1 return;
- a dummy for membership of a hot group, and one for a cold group.

**Primary statistic:**
- At each refit, a cross-sectional OLS of the next 21 days' market-relative return (close t → t+21) on the percentiles of J, the own 63-day return and the own 12-1 return, plus the two dummies.
- The Fama-MacBeth mean of J's coefficient and its t, over refits.

**Book:**
- J residualized on the four controls (cross-sectional OLS);
- decile long-short, held 21 days, 5 bps per side.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **2006-03..2019:** J's Fama-MacBeth coefficient is positive with t ≥ 3.
2. **The residualized book:** net mean positive with t ≥ 2.
3. **2020–2025-06:** same sign of J's coefficient. **The 16 unseen months** (new export, once, at the end): same sign.

**Money test (the judge):**
- Score = the residualized J's percentile. Graded with `rebalance=21`.
- Uniform and volatility-matched random accounts are both reported.

## The number for the file name

The judge's walk-forward change over random, with uniform random accounts, 0 bps (`c2eval.change_over_random`).

## Ways it can fool us

- **Joining is partly rallying.** A stock's correlation with a hot group rises partly because the stock rallied with it. The own-return controls are the whole test.
- **Hot groups are momentum,** and plain and twin momentum earned nothing here.
- **Groups change between refits,** so H and C are rebuilt from members at each refit.

## Decisions made while building it

**Before any outcome data:**
- **Groups map:**
  - breadth thrust's cached refits (`~/.local/share/correlation2/experiments/breadth_thrust/old/refits.pkl`) were used as they are, read-only: each refit's universe, labels and twin-fund label;
  - a copy of breadth thrust's universe and grouping code (`tj_refits.py`, `tj_groups.py`) rebuilt four of them (2006-03-03, 2011-05-04, 2019-09-09, 2025-06-13) identically.
- **Two of breadth thrust's decisions are kept, for consistency:**
  - **no $5M dollar-volume floor:** the floor left about 1,070 stocks in 2008, so the universe is simply the 1,500 most-traded;
  - **the first refit is 2006-03-03,** the first with 1,500 qualifying stocks.
- **Calendar:** refits on the twins calendar (export day 252 + 21k). Refit t uses closes through t only, and its score holds for days t .. the next refit − 1.
- **Market-relative:**
  - means the daily log return minus the export's `market` (the equal-weight mean daily log return of the liquid stocks), with beta 1;
  - this matches breadth thrust's group returns and outcomes. It is not the groups map's beta-adjusted residual;
  - a beta-adjusted J (partial correlations given the market, beta fitted in each window) is reported alongside, reported only.
- **Hot and cold:** the group rank is the members' mean (log close t−63 → t minus the market over t−62..t). Top 3 are hot, bottom 3 cold.
- **H and C:**
  - the equal-weight mean over all members of the 3 groups pooled each day, not the mean of three group means;
  - a day needs returns from at least 50% of those members;
  - a hot member's own return is part of H. There is no leave-one-out, since the plan's dummy is the control for that and a stock is about 1/150 of H.
- **The joiner score:**
  - each correlation uses the days with both the stock's return and H (or C);
  - all four correlations need ≥ 55 of the 63 recent days and ≥ 200 of the 252 older days;
  - the stock needs to be in the 1,500 only at t.
- **Prices:** a close may be up to 5 trading days stale (the last finite close) for the own returns and the outcome. The 12-1 return is log close t−252 → t−21.
- **Percentiles:**
  - (rank − 0.5) / n, over the stocks with J and both own returns, computed without the outcome. So the scores and the regressions use the same values;
  - the regression runs on those of them with a 21-day outcome;
  - market-relativity of the own returns doesn't matter for percentiles, since the market is the same for every stock at a refit.
- **Outcome:** for the regressions, the log return close t → t+21 minus the market over t+1..t+21. The book uses simple returns, the money it would actually make.
- **Residualizing:** J's percentile on an intercept, the two own-return percentiles and the two dummies, the same regressors as the primary model.
- **The book:**
  - deciles of n/10 stocks (149) a side, equal weight;
  - cost is 5 bps × the sum of absolute weight changes over both legs against the previous refit's book, ignoring drift; the first book is charged from zero.
- **Test 2's period** isn't stated in the plan. It was taken as test 1's, 2006-03..2019, with later periods reported.
- **Test 3's "same sign"** was read as the same sign as test 1's coefficient, as breadth thrust read it. Whether that is the predicted positive sign is also stated.
- **Periods:** refits are assigned by refit date. Refits without a full 21-day outcome are left out of the tests (2025-06-13 on the old export, 2026-10-15 on the new).
- **The reporting dates** were fixed before any outcome: the first refit on or after 2007-01-02, 2009-03-02, 2014-01-02, 2020-04-01, 2021-01-04 and 2025-06-13, and in the unseen months 2026-01-02 and 2026-07-01.
- **The unseen months:**
  - refits rebuilt on the new export with `tj_refits.py`, from 2025-06-01: 17 refits, so 2025-06-13's covers the judge's first days;
  - identical to breadth thrust's own new-export refits for all 17;
  - built, scored and judged once, after the walk-forward results above were final.

**After building, before any outcome:**
- **No score on the first refit:** on 2006-03-03, H and C exist on only 256 of the 315 days, so no stock has 200 older days. The first score is on 2006-04-03.

**After results** (no rule changed):
- **The unseen months' by-year table** in `results_new.txt` first included the 2025-06-13 refit, which is before the unseen months. A corrected table was added below it from the saved per-refit results, without rerunning the judge, and the code was fixed.
- **The mean without 2009-10** under the judge's per-year table was added after seeing that year.

## Files

**Code** (`research/theme_joiners/`, numpy only, system `python3 -I -B`):
- `tj_common.py`: the fixed rules as constants, paths, masked correlations, percentiles and Fama-MacBeth.
- `tj_groups.py`: the groups map's clustering, copied verbatim from `research/breadth_thrust/bt_common.py`.
- `tj_refits.py`: the refits, as breadth thrust builds them:
  - `check` rebuilds four old-export refits and compares them with breadth thrust's cache;
  - `new 2025-06-01` builds the unseen months' refits.
- `tj_build.py`: per refit, the hot and cold groups, H and C, J (and the beta-adjusted J), the controls and the outcomes.
- `tj_tests.py`:
  - the Fama-MacBeth regressions, the book and the by-year tables;
  - the hot and cold groups and the top joiners at the fixed dates;
  - the scores file and the judge. `new` runs the unseen months.
- `sanity_reversal.py`: the judge sanity check. 5-day reversal at `rebalance=5` gives +11.8 points a year, 8 of 18 years at p < 0.1, as expected (`sanity_reversal.txt`).

**Outputs** (`research/theme_joiners/`):
- `results_old.txt`: every test on the old export, the dated tables and the walk-forward judge;
- `results_new.txt`: the unseen months, run once;
- `judge_old_uniform.txt`, `judge_old_volatility.txt`, `judge_new_uniform.txt`, `judge_new_volatility.txt`: the judge's reports.

**Large files** (`~/.local/share/correlation2/experiments/theme_joiners/`):
- `old/table.pkl`, `new/table.pkl`: per refit, the score, the controls and the outcomes; `old/results.pkl`, `new/results.pkl`: the per-refit coefficients and book;
- `new/refits.pkl`: the unseen months' groups map;
- `scores_old.npy`, `scores_new.npy`, each with the judge's JSON;
- `sanity_reversal5.npy`;
- the build and refit logs.
