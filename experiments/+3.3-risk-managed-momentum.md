# Risk-managed momentum: does scaling momentum by its own risk fix its crashes?

**TL;DR:** This replicates published work; nothing in the method is new. Barroso & Santa-Clara (2015) scaled the winners-minus-losers momentum portfolio (WML) each month by 12% ÷ its realized volatility over the previous 126 trading days, and report that over 1927–2011 this nearly doubled momentum's Sharpe ratio and virtually eliminated its crashes (the plan's 0.53 → 0.97; the paper's table wasn't reachable, so those two figures are unverified). Daniel & Moskowitz (2016, 1927–2013) showed that momentum crashes come in market rebounds after a fall, and that a dynamic version about doubles the Sharpe ratio. What's new is running the rule on this project's liquid, survivor-only stocks over 2006–2025-06 and the 16 unseen months, so every test month is after both papers' samples. The replication is faithful: our daily WML correlates 0.90 with French's UMD (0.5 needed), and the same rule on French's own UMD over 1927–2011 gives 0.49 → 0.94. On this data the scaling does what the papers say. Over 2006–2019, net of costs, it raised the Sharpe ratio from −0.26 to +0.21 (difference +0.47, block-bootstrap p 0.001), and it cut the March–May 2009 crash from −78% to −22%, a crash in which the losers rose 157% here against 163% in Daniel & Moskowitz. The Sharpe difference has the same sign in the 500 largest (+0.35), in 2020–2025-06 (+0.19, p 0.06), in the 16 unseen months (+0.13, p 0.13), and over 2010–2019 without the crash (+0.20). But it scales a strategy that earns nothing here: French's own UMD made 0.7% a year over 2006–2025 (Sharpe 0.05, worst month −34% in April 2009). The scaled book, RMOM, netted +0.20% a month over 2006–2019 (t 0.66, where 2 is needed; FF5 + UMD alpha +0.12%, t 0.58), +0.45% over 2020–2025-06 (t 1.37) and +0.25% in the unseen months (t 0.30). The file-name number, (Sharpe RMOM − Sharpe WML) × 15 before costs over 2006–2025-06, is +5.4. Verdict: fails test 1. The risk management works as published, but it turns a losing momentum book into a small, insignificant earner, not one that pays.

## Published claim

| Paper | Sample | What it reports |
|---|---|---|
| Barroso & Santa-Clara 2015, *JFE*, "Momentum has its moments" | US, 1927–2011 | Scaling WML to 12% target volatility raises its Sharpe from 0.53 to 0.97 and largely removes crash risk |
| Daniel & Moskowitz 2016, *JFE* | US, 1927–2013 | Momentum crashes follow market rebounds; dynamic scaling about doubles the Sharpe ratio |

**Our test period is after both samples.**

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Universe:** the judge-like liquid set, minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)`.

**WML (winners minus losers):**
- At each calendar month-end, rank stocks by their 12-1 return (t−251..t−21, export closes).
- Long the top tenth, short the bottom tenth, equal weight (there is no market cap; the paper is value-weighted, and that difference is noted).
- Held for the calendar month. Daily WML returns are recorded.

**Scaling:**
- At each month-end, σ̂² = 21 × the mean of WML's squared daily returns over the previous 126 trading days, annualized.
- The month's weight = 12% ÷ σ̂. The scaled book is RMOM.

**Costs:** 5 bps per side on turnover, including the turnover from changes in the scale.

**Fidelity check, done before any test is read:**
- Our daily WML's correlation with French's daily UMD over 2006–2025 must be at least 0.5.
- If it isn't, fix only bugs, and record them.

**Variant, reported:** the same with the top and bottom tenths of the 500 largest.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **2006–2019:**
   - RMOM's mean monthly net return, **pass at t ≥ 2**;
   - **and** Sharpe(RMOM) > Sharpe(WML), with a 21-day block-bootstrap p < 0.10.
2. **Crashes:** RMOM's loss over March–May 2009 is less than half of WML's. The 500 largest give the same sign of the Sharpe difference.
3. **2020–2025-06:** same sign of the Sharpe difference. **The 16 unseen months** (new export, once, at the end): same sign.

**Reported:** FF5 + UMD alphas of both, the average scale, and its range.

## The number for the file name

(Sharpe of RMOM − Sharpe of WML) × 15, before costs, 2006–2025-06. That is points of yearly return at 15% volatility. It isn't a comparison with random accounts, because the judge can't vary exposure.

## Ways it can fool us

- **Two near-zero strategies:** if WML earns nothing here, a Sharpe comparison is noise. That's why RMOM must also pay on its own.
- **Equal weighting:** small stocks dominate the deciles. The 500-largest variant is the check.

**Reading it.** The scaling replicates: on French's data it doubles UMD's Sharpe ratio over the papers' sample, and since publication it still lifts it from 0.05 to 0.42. On our stocks it lifts it by about 0.4 in 2006–2019 and 0.3 since 2020. What has changed is momentum itself. French's UMD barely broke even over 2006–2025, and our equal-weight liquid version lost money over 2006–2019. Doubling a Sharpe ratio near zero leaves a Sharpe ratio near zero. The scaled version, 0.42 on French's UMD and 0.26–0.30 on ours over 2006–2025-06, isn't significant at the plan's bar over 20 years.

## Decisions made while building it

All of these were made on 2026-10-21 **before any WML or RMOM return was computed or looked at**, unless a line says otherwise.

**Books**
- **Formation** is at the close of each calendar month's last trading day t, using the export's days. The old export's last day, 2025-06-27, counts as June's end.
  - The book is held, buy and hold, to the close of the next month-end t1.
  - Daily leg value V_d = the mean over members of P_d ÷ P_t, on forward-filled export closes. Daily leg return = V_d ÷ V_{d−1} − 1.
  - **Daily WML = winners' leg return − losers' leg return**, French's construction for the daily factor. **Monthly WML = V_W(t1) − V_L(t1)**, the month's return on $1 long and $1 short.
  - RMOM = scale × WML, daily and monthly.
- **The score** is ln(C[t−21] ÷ C[t−252]), `momentum.py`'s formula, read-only. It needs both closes and a close at t, all actual (not forward-filled) export closes.
- **Ranking universe:** the shared liquid set (`night_and_day/common.py`'s `universe()`, copied, which applies `c2eval.excluded`) at the latest twins-calendar day on or before t.
  - A month needs ≥ 100 ranked stocks; it always had at least 295.
  - Deciles are floor(10% × n) names each side, with stable tie-breaking.
- **The 500 largest:** the shared definition (top 500 by 63-day median dollar volume at that calendar day), ranked within themselves.
- **No risk-free rate is subtracted:** WML and RMOM are zero-cost spreads.

**Scaling**
- σ̂² = 252 × the mean of the squared daily WML returns over the 126 trading days t−125..t. That is the plan's "21 × mean, annualized" (× 12), not demeaned, as in the paper.
  - Those are the returns of the books actually held on those days. All 126 must exist, so RMOM starts in 2005-08.
- The weight is 0.12 ÷ σ̂, uncapped, set at t and held through the month.

**Costs**
- **Positions** are in units of capital: +scale ÷ n_W for each winner and −scale ÷ n_L for each loser.
  - Last month's positions drift with returns within each leg, still at last month's scale.
  - **Turnover = Σ |new − drifted old|** over all names, so changes in the scale are traded.
  - Cost = 5 bps × turnover. The first book pays full entry.
- **On daily series,** the month's cost is charged on its first holding day.

**Dividends**
- **The dividend-corrected panel is primary** for every number, the file name's included. Prices are multiplied by exp(cumulative `pubdata.div_fix`), so both the ranking and the returns use total returns, as CRSP's do. The correction is 0 before 2022-07, so 2006–2019 is identical either way.
- **Uncorrected ("raw") numbers are reported** beside every period touching 2022-07 on.

**Statistics**
- **Sharpe ratios** for the tests and the file-name number are from daily returns × √252. The plan's bootstrap uses 21-day blocks, which implies daily data, as in correlation portfolios. The paper's monthly × √12 is reported beside them.
- **Bootstrap:** paired circular blocks of 21 days, 10,000 draws, seed 0. The one-sided p is the centred share (bootstrap − estimate ≥ estimate), copied from `research/correlation_portfolios/report.py`.
- **All tests use net returns;** gross returns are reported.
  - Test 1's t is the plain t of the 168 monthly net means; Newey-West (3 lags) is reported.
  - The crash is the compounded net return over return months 2009-03..2009-05.
  - Test 2's 500-largest comparison is over 2006–2019, test 1's period.
  - "Same sign" means the sign of the net daily Sharpe difference matches test 1's.
- **Alphas:** monthly net returns on French's monthly Mkt-RF, SMB, HML, RMW, CMA and UMD, plain OLS.
- **The unseen months:** the new export, with every series rebuilt, so σ̂ uses the new export's own WML history.
  - Return months are 2025-07..2026-10, with October partial through 10-19.
  - The daily return of 2025-06-30 belongs to June's book and is dropped.
- **Added before results:** the rule applied to French's daily UMD (1927–2011 and our periods), as a check of the rule itself.

**Made after seeing results**
- **`extra.py`:** the Sharpe difference over 2010–2019, 2006–2019 without 2009, and 2006–2008. It asks whether the gain is only the crash, and it is reported, not a test.
- **Nothing in the rules or the code was changed after results were seen.** The fidelity check passed on the first run.

**Data notes**
- The two exports disagree on some past years: 2024's WML (net, summed monthly) was +16.3% on the old export and +9.2% on the new one. They hold different stock sets: survivors to 2025 against survivors to 2026. Only the unseen months come from the new export.
- 2006–2007 rests on about 300 stocks, about 30 a side. That is a feature of the export, which most stocks enter in 2005.
