# Pairs trading: does the textbook distance method still pay?

**TL;DR:** This is a replication of the best-known published strategy built on correlation between stocks. Gatev, Goetzmann & Rouwenhorst (2006, *RFS*, US 1962–2002) took each stock's closest match by price path over a year and traded the 20 closest pairs for six months. When a pair drifted two standard deviations apart, they shorted the one that ran ahead and bought the one that fell behind, then closed when the two met. With a one-day wait, the top 20 earned 0.52% a month on committed capital and 0.90% on employed capital (about 11% a year, t 9.3), while their random pairs earned about −0.09%. Do & Faff (2010, 2012) found the profits declining and largely gone after costs since 2002. Our test period, 2006 to 2025-06, is entirely after Gatev et al.'s sample and overlaps Do & Faff's only in 2006–2009; this project's twin reversal (`+1.1-twin-reversal.md`) tested stock-against-fund residuals, while this is the published stock-against-stock rule. The rule behaves as published: 19.5 of 20 pairs open, 1.6 round trips a pair, and utilities and share classes dominate the pairs. But it no longer earns anything. Over 2006–2019 the committed-capital book made +0.06% a month before costs and +0.002% after 5 bps a side (Newey-West t 0.04; FF5 + UMD alpha +0.2% a year, t 0.4). That was less than the median of 1,000 random-pair books (+1.9% a year gross; 89% of them did as well), so it fails test 1 and test 2. It turned positive later: +0.24% a month net over 2020–2025-06 (t 1.4, ahead of about 96% of random books, alpha t 1.9), and +0.25% a month in the 16 unseen months (+3.0% a year, t 3.0 with Newey-West but 1.9 plain, with 19% of random books doing as well). Most of the post-2020 gain came in turbulent stretches (2020, early 2025), which matches Do & Faff. The 500 largest stocks earned less: +0.08% a month net in 2020–2025-06 and −0.07% in the unseen months. The two price sources agree (Δt 0.006), but only because the store's raw closes are identical to the export's wherever both exist. The dividend fix trims 2022-07–2025-06 from +2.2% to +1.6% a year gross. The file-name number is +0.3: the book's +1.50% a year against the random median's +1.20 over 2006–2025-06, before costs. Verdict: fails test 1; the published profits are gone in 2006–2019, and the modest post-2020 revival isn't enough to pass.

## Published claim

| Paper | Sample | What it reports |
|---|---|---|
| Gatev, Goetzmann & Rouwenhorst 2006, *RFS* | US, 1962–2002 | Top-20 pairs: excess returns of up to about 11% a year; random pairs earn nothing; profits declining over time |
| Do & Faff 2010, 2012 | US, to 2009 | Declining profitability; largely unprofitable after realistic costs since 2002, though still strong in crises |

**Our test period is after Gatev et al.'s sample.**

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Universe:** the judge-like liquid set, minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)`, with no missing close in the formation year.

**Formation** (12 months, 252 days), started every 21 trading days on the fixed calendar:
- Each stock's total-return index over the year, normalized to 1 at its start.
- The sum of squared differences (SSD) between every pair's normalized paths.
- The 20 pairs with the smallest SSD. A stock may appear in more than one pair.

**Trading** (the next 126 days):
- σ = the standard deviation of the pair's normalized price difference over the formation year.
- **Open** at the close after the one where the difference exceeds 2σ (Gatev et al.'s one-day wait): $1 long the one that fell behind, $1 short the one that ran ahead.
- **Close** at the close after the paths cross. Anything still open closes at the end of the period.
- A pair may reopen.

**Returns:**
- On committed capital: P&L ÷ 20 pairs (primary).
- On employed capital: ÷ the pairs that traded (reported).
- Six overlapping portfolios, one started each period; the monthly return is their average.
- Costs: 5 bps per side per stock trade, so 4 sides per round trip.

**Random pairs:**
- 1,000 books of 20 pairs drawn at random from the same universe at each formation, with the same trading rules.
- This is Gatev et al.'s own comparison.

**Data checks, fixed now:**
- **Prices, run twice:** everything runs on the export's closes and again on the store's raw closes, because the export's bad-print repair looks one day ahead, and a daily reversion rule would exploit that.
- **Disagreement:** if the two disagree in sign, or in t by more than 1, the verdict is "data, unresolved", not a pass.
- **Dividends:** returns after 2022-07 get the shared dividend correction, because the store's closes are raw after late 2022 and an ex-dividend drop is a fake divergence.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **2006–2019:** the committed-capital book's mean monthly net excess return. **Pass:** t ≥ 2.
2. **Beats random pairs:** the book's 2006–2019 mean is above the 95th percentile of the random books'.
3. **2020–2025-06:** same sign. **The 16 unseen months** (new export and store, once, at the end): same sign.

**Reported:**
- the 500 largest;
- FF5 + UMD alphas;
- trades per pair;
- the share of pairs that never converge;
- a few example pairs at one date, as a sanity check.

## The number for the file name

Yearly return on committed capital minus the median of the 1,000 random-pair books, 2006–2025-06, at 0 bps. Gatev et al.'s own comparison: a long-short pairs book, not the judge's 20-stock account.

## Ways it can fool us

- **Bad prints:** a daily reversion rule can profit from a repaired price. Hence the two price sources.
- **Bid-ask bounce:** the one-day wait is Gatev et al.'s guard.
- **Dividend drops after 2022:** the correction.

## Results (run 2026-10-21)

A "month" is a 21-trading-day block of the fixed calendar (export days 252 + 21k), so the six portfolios overlap exactly (126 = 6 × 21). Each block is the mean of the six live portfolios. Primary configuration: the export's closes, dividend-corrected from 2022-07, all stocks in the universe. The universe has a median of 1,033 stocks for formations of 2006–2019 and 2,005 for 2020–2025-06 (295 to 2,269 over all formations).

**Plumbing.**
- 5-day reversal through this folder's `save_scores` + `judge` path gave +11.8 points a year, beating random at p < 0.1 in 8 of 18 years, as required (`out/sanity_reversal.txt`).
- The Gram-matrix SSD matches brute force exactly (same top 20, SSD within 1e-12) at five formations on 150-stock subsets.
- The vectorized simulation matches a plain scalar loop exactly on 12 formations × 20 pairs: the same P&L, costs and trade counts. Each trade's summed daily P&L equals its buy-and-hold leg returns from prices to within 4e-15 (`out/check_sim_*.txt`).

## Decisions made while building it

Before any results:
- **Normalization:** the trading-period paths continue the formation's normalization; they are not reset to 1 at the trading start. Gatev et al.'s Figure 1 shows both stocks at about 1.1 on trading day 0. σ and the traded gap are therefore on one scale.
- **Market-wide gap day:** 2011-02-17, when nearly every close is missing in both exports, is forward-filled before the "no missing close in the formation year" rule. Without the fill, every formation spanning it would be empty. It was the only such day found.
- **Universe:** a stock needs a close in both price sources on every formation day, so the two runs have identical universes and differ only in prices.
- **Months and returns:**
  - A month is 21 trading days on the twins calendar.
  - P&L is marked to market daily on buy-and-hold legs that start at $1 each. It is summed within a month, not compounded.
  - Committed capital = P&L ÷ 20. Employed capital = ÷ the pairs that opened at least once in that portfolio's six months (Gatev et al.'s definition), and 0 if none opened.
  - A month is the mean of the live portfolios, always six from 2005-06 on.
- **Costs:** 5 bps × $1 on each leg at the open, and 5 bps × each leg's value at the close.
- **Trade mechanics:**
  - Direction is set on the signal day.
  - Signals are checked only at closes where both stocks have a real close.
  - No position opens on the period's last close (it would close on the same close).
  - The forced close at the period end pays costs.
  - Missing closes inside the period are forward-filled.
  - If a stock's data ends, the pair closes at the earlier stock's last close, as Gatev et al. do with delistings.
- **Identical series:** pairs with SSD ≤ 1e-12 are skipped.
- **Dividend correction:** added to daily log returns from 2022-07-01, on days with a real close. It enters both the formation paths (they are total-return indices) and the trading returns.
- **Significance:** the t for the pass is Newey-West with 6 lags (Gatev et al.'s); the plain t is also reported. The data check compares this t on test 1's series.
- **Periods:** a month belongs to the period of its last day. 2006–2019 means months ending 2006-01-01..2019-12-31; 2020–2025-06 runs to the old export's last day, including its final 9-day month.
- **Yearly return:** 12 × the mean month.
- **The file-name number:** dividend-corrected export closes, all stocks, gross.
- **Random books:**
  - The full 1,000 per formation; they take about 80 seconds per configuration, so 200 was never needed.
  - Pairs of distinct stocks are drawn uniformly from the same universe, with the same seeded draws for both price sources and both dividend settings.
  - Test 2 compares net returns (test 1's measure); gross is also shown.
- **The 500 largest:** pairs and random books are formed only within them.
- **Alphas:** each month's FF5 + UMD factor is the sum of French's daily factors over its days.

After results:
- **The unseen window.** The first pass used the fixed calendar's months that start on or after 2025-06-30.
