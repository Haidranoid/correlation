# Phantom portfolios

**TL;DR:** This tested whether baskets of stocks that still move together after the market, the groups map and six price-based styles are taken out ("phantom portfolios") bounce back after an unwind day: the whole basket falls 3σ against its recent direction, on heavy volume, with no single member's news behind it. The baskets exist but are loose. Every 21 days about 23 of 50 residual clusters beat a shifted-dates null at p < 0.01. They are not industries (adjusted Rand index 0.02 against the groups map), but little of their membership survives from one month to the next (0.11). Because each basket's σ comes from the same 126-day correlation period that selected it, the fixed rule fires about 3 times a year, not the plan's 150. That gave 62 events in 2004–2025: 22 of them in 2020, and 39 (63%) on days the market moved more than 2%. The baskets don't bounce. Over 2005–2019, members kept moving the event's way: −0.51 of the basket's move over 5 days beyond their own reversal (t = −3.3). That is worse than same-size quiet cluster moves (t = −2.6 for the difference) and group moves (t = −3.7), so test 4 fails with the wrong sign. In 2020–2025 the sign flipped (+0.06, t = 2.2) without beating the quiet moves (t = 1.4). The plan's money test made +13.7 bps per 5-day hedged trade before costs and +3.7 bps after 5 bps per side. That beat random guessers who draw stocks one by one (p = 0.037) but not guessers who draw whole clusters (p = 0.12). It was positive after costs in only 5 of 19 years. The judge's account earned −0.1 points a year over random, ahead in 6 of the 14 years that had any event. Its long-short (24 bps/day, z = 0.53) mostly compares members of one event with each other. The 16 unseen months held one event, regional banks on 2025-10-16, which kept falling; the judge's account made +2.7% against +1.7% for random (p = 0.21). The known episodes don't light up reliably. One run found two unwinds on 2007-08-08 and the other none. Neither run found an event in the 2009 momentum crash, and the new export found none in the summer-2025 quant wobble. The 13F ownership check was blocked, because sec.gov refuses requests without a contact email. Verdict: no edge; the unwind bounce isn't there.

**Question:** can we find the baskets that crowds of funds hold together from prices alone, and buy them back when they get dumped?

## The idea

Funds that hold the same stocks make those stocks move together, for reasons that have nothing to do with the businesses. When a crowded hedge fund cuts risk, it sells its whole book at once. Every stock it holds drops together, whatever industry each one is in.

So after the market and the industry groups (the groups map) are taken out, the co-movement that is left over should partly be the fingerprint of shared owners. These are **phantom portfolios**:
- the hedge-fund favourites basket;
- a factor fund's longs;
- the most-shorted names;
- retail favourites;
- a theme basket.

**The trade:** watch each phantom portfolio every day. An **unwind day** has three features:
- the whole basket falls together on heavy volume;
- the fall runs against its recent direction;
- no single member's own news explains it.

That looks like forced selling. Forced selling tends to bounce back once the seller is done, and whoever buys from the seller is paid for providing liquidity. The mirror case is a **squeeze**: a short basket jumping together. It should fade.

## Why it might work where the census didn't

- **It trades only on events.** A cluster unwinds a few times a year, so turnover is low and each trade comes after a large basket move. The census and the cluster lead-lag paid costs every day for a few bps.
- **The crowd changes and the clusters follow it.** Fixed factors (momentum, value) are crowded and uncrowded at different times. Clusters rebuilt every month from the latest co-movement follow whichever basket is crowded now.
- **The reward is for providing liquidity, not for spotting a mispricing.** Buying from forced sellers is compensated for risk (Nagel 2012; Coval & Stafford 2007), so it is less likely to be arbitraged to zero than a pure pattern.

## What's new (nearest prior work)

The pieces exist separately. No published work combines these three:
- inferring crowd portfolios **from returns alone**;
- **checking them against 13F filings**, which would itself be a new result: holdings recovered from prices;
- trading **daily unwind events**.

| Work | What they did | How this differs |
|---|---|---|
| Antón & Polk 2014, *JF* | Shared active mutual-fund ownership (13F) predicts excess co-movement; a cross-stock reversal strategy on ownership links. | Ownership from quarterly 13F, which is 45 days late, long-only and misses shorts and leverage. | Lou & Polk 2022, *RFS*, "Comomentum" | Abnormal correlation among momentum stocks measures crowding; when it is high, momentum reverses. | A fixed strategy (momentum deciles), monthly to yearly. Here the baskets are found from data and the events are daily. |
| Jin, Cucuringu & Cartea 2023, ICAIF | Cluster the correlation matrix of market-residual returns and trade mean reversion within clusters. | Kinlaw, Kritzman & Turkington 2019, *JPM*; Baltas 2019, *FAJ* | Crowding measured from correlations (asset centrality; pairwise correlation within factor portfolios). | Crowding of known sectors and factors, used for timing. Here: unknown baskets, unwind events. |

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**1. Residual returns:**
- Start from market-relative daily returns.
- Remove the stock's group, using the group's return with the stock left out.
- Remove the style factors computable from prices: 12-1 momentum, 1-month reversal, 60-day volatility, market beta, dollar volume (a size stand-in) and price level. Each is a long-short tercile factor.
- What is left is the residual.

**2. Phantom clusters:**
- Every 21 days: residual correlations over the last 126 days for the 1,500 most-traded stocks, then spectral clustering into about 50 clusters.
- **Keep** a cluster only if:
  - it has at least 8 members;
  - its mean residual correlation beats a null that shifts each stock's dates in 21-day blocks, at p < 0.01.
- Report how stable clusters are from one refit to the next (adjusted Rand index, as for the groups map).

**3. Unwind day for a cluster:**
- The equal-weight residual return of the cluster is −3σ or worse, with σ from the previous 126 days.
- Its trailing 21-day residual return was positive (it moved against its recent direction).
- The median member traded at least 1.5× its normal volume.
- At least 70% of members fell.
- No single member with its own 4× volume spike accounts for more than half of the move.
- **Squeeze day:** the same rules with the signs flipped.

**4. Outcome:** the members' residual returns over 1, 5, 10 and 20 days after the event.
- From the event day's close.
- Also, from 2008, from the next open.

## Tests, in order (stop at the first failure)

1. **Is there structure?**
   - **Stop if** clusters don't beat the shifted-dates null, or if they change completely at every refit (adjusted Rand index near 0).
   - If they are just industries the groups map missed, they'll line up with the groups; report the overlap.
2. **Do they light up when they should?** Known unwinds as labelled checks, not as tuning:
   - the August 6–9 2007 quant meltdown;
   - the 2009 momentum crash;
   - late January 2021 (hedge funds cutting risk during the meme squeeze);
   - the summer-2025 "quant wobble" described by MSCI.
   - It's a bad sign if none of these shows an unwind day.
3. **Ownership check (optional, 2013 on):**
   - Do pairs in the same phantom cluster share 13F holders more than pairs in the same industry group but a different phantom cluster?
   - **If not,** call them hidden-factor clusters rather than ownership. The trade can still be tested, but the story changes.
4. **Bounce-back beyond the known effect.** The nearest known effect is each stock's own residual reversal (Da, Liu & Schaumburg 2014).
   - Regress members' future residual returns on the cluster's unwind-day move, controlling for each member's own residual move that day.
   - Compare with the same-size cluster moves on normal-volume days, and with same-size group (industry) moves.
   - **Stop unless** unwinds bounce back more than both, with t ≥ 3 over 2005–2019.
5. **The unseen years:** 2020–2026, with the same rules, the same sign and t ≥ 2.
6. **Money test** (the existing harness, as a new family):
   - **Trades:** at the close of an unwind day, buy the members equal-weight and hedge with their groups and the market. Hold 5 days. Do the mirror for squeezes.
   - **Random guessers:** the same number of stocks bought on the same days, drawn from clusters that did *not* unwind but moved at least 2σ.
   - **Pass:** beats random guessers at p < 0.05 after 5 bps per side in walk-forward, and positive after costs in at least two-thirds of years.

**The cost hurdle:**
- If about 50 clusters each unwind about 3 times a year, that is about 150 events and 2,000–3,000 stock trades a year.
- A 10 bps round trip then needs a mean 5-day bounce of about 15 bps or more per stock.
- The events are 3σ basket moves, so even a partial bounce clears that comfortably, if it exists.

## Ways it can fool us

- **A cluster can be a real economic factor**, such as interest-rate sensitivity or China exposure, so its "unwind" is news (a rate shock) and won't reverse. That is why the volume and breadth rules are there, and why same-day market and macro moves are reported. Drop FOMC and CPI days as a robustness check.
- **The style-factor list is incomplete.** Without fundamentals, value and quality can't be removed, so some clusters will be value or quality baskets. That is acceptable, since their unwinds are crowding events too, but label them.
- **The clusters change monthly, so look-ahead is easy.** Assign each day's clusters only from data before the refit date.

## Results (run 2026-10-21)

**Data:**
- **Old export:** the judge's export through 2025-06-27 (8,503 stocks), with raw volume and opens from `stooq-2025-06-27`. It has 257 refits, every 21 trading days from 2004-01-16; the first refit waits for 252 days of style factors and 252 days of loadings.
- **New export:** `EXPORT_NEW` through 2026-10-19 (9,508 stocks), recomputed from scratch once the rules were final, with 273 refits. Its refit grid sits 10 days later, because its first full factor day differs.
- **Survivors only:** both exports hold only stocks that survived. That flatters any rule that buys baskets that just fell. Residual outcomes and the matched controls reduce the bias but don't remove it.

- **Structure exists:** about half the clusters beat the null, so the test 1 kill rule (fewer than 5 kept clusters, or an adjusted Rand index below 0.05) is not met.
- **Not missed industries:** the clusters barely line up with the groups map.
- **But loose:**
  - kept clusters average a residual correlation of 0.16, against 0.08 for pure noise clustered the same way;
  - only about a quarter of their pair structure survives a month (0.26), against 0.57 for the groups map.
- **More of them in stress:** kept clusters per refit run 1–2 in 2004–05, 16–27 in 2006–2019, and 39 in 2020.
- **Selection inflates σ:** each cluster is chosen for its co-movement over the 126 days before the refit, and its σ comes from the same days. Out of sample, the kept clusters' daily z has a standard deviation of 0.72, not 1; the groups' is 1.03. So the rule's "−3σ" is about −4σ in practice, which is why events are rare (test 2).

**Labels.** Without fundamentals, value and quality can't be removed, so each kept cluster's residual is compared with label funds. Each fund is taken relative to the market, and the style spreads are used as they are. The comparison runs over the same 126 days.
- **Matches:** 27% of kept clusters track some fund at |r| ≥ 0.3. No single label covers more than 1.7% of them. The most common are homebuilders (ITB), gold miners (GDX), retail (XRT), emerging markets (EEM), solar (TAN), the dollar (negative UUP) and high yield (HYG).
- **Known styles are rare:** value (IWD−IWF, VLUE−SPY), small-cap value (IWN−IWO), momentum (MTUM−SPY), quality (QUAL−SPY) and min vol (USMV−SPY) each describe 0–2% of kept clusters.
- **Price-style tilts:** 16% of kept clusters lean strongly on a price-based style (mean |z| ≥ 0.75 across the universe), mostly low beta, low volatility, small or low-priced stocks. The tercile factors removed the average exposure, not each basket's.

### Test 3: ownership check (13F): blocked, skipped

### Test 4: bounce beyond each member's own residual reversal, 2005–2019. Fails, with the wrong sign

**The regression.** It is run over members of each event, with this setup:
- **Bounce:** the member's residual over the next h days, signed so that a reversal is positive.
- **C:** the basket's event-day move with the member left out, in the event's direction. This follows Wardlaw: the member's own event-day return is not in it.
- **O:** the member's own event-day residual, its own reversal (Da, Liu & Schaumburg).
- **Model:** bounce = b × C + g × O, fitted separately per event type, with no intercept. b is the fraction of the basket's move that comes back beyond the member's own reversal.
- **Standard errors:** clustered by calendar month. The 22 U events fall in only 13 months, so even these t values are rough.

**The event types:**
- **U:** unwinds plus squeezes, the squeezes mirrored.
- **N:** same-size (|z| ≥ 3) moves of kept clusters on normal volume (< 1.5×).
- **G:** same-size moves of the groups map's groups, measured before the group is removed.

- **Years without trades:** 2006–07, 2010–11, 2013–14, 2014–15 and 2017–18 had none.
- **Noise:** the standard error is 18.9 bps by trade and 38.7 bps by event, so the +13.7 bps mean is about t = 0.35 at the event level. The event-weighted mean is +20.7 bps.
- **Costs:** at 10 bps per side, −6.3 bps. Charging 5 bps per side on the hedge legs too (mean hedge notional 1.26) gives −8.9 bps.
- **By side:** unwinds bought made +63.6 bps per trade (782 trades); squeezes shorted lost 27.5 bps (948 trades).
- **Entry at the next open,** 2008 on: +30.6 bps, against +16.1 bps from the close for the same trades.
- **Post-hoc, the right null:** the picks hold whole clusters, so their noise is event-level. The plan's guessers draw single stocks from many clusters at once, which narrows their spread. Guessers who draw the same number of whole clusters on the same days have a median of −24.6 bps (5th–95th percentile −84.8 to +26.8) and beat the picks 11.5% of the time (p = 0.115).
- **Pass rule** (p < 0.05 after 5 bps per side and positive after costs in ≥ 2/3 of years): p = 0.037, but positive after costs in only 5 of 19 years (5 of the 14 years with trades), so it fails. The rule was written for about 150 events a year. With about 3, years without trades fail it by construction, but the 14 years with trades are also mostly negative.
- **The 16 unseen months:** one event, 38 trades, +15.0 bps gross and +5.0 after costs. The script printed "PASS", but it is degenerate. The event day had no 2σ mover of its own, so every random draw came from a single nearby day (−139.6 bps). One event can't pass or fail anything.

**The judge.** Scores: +|z| on t..t+4 after an unwind, −|z| after a squeeze. Daily rebalance, walk-forward:
- **Account:** −0.1 points a year over random (0 bps; −0.1 at 5 and 20 bps). Ahead in 6 of 14 years; beat random at p < 0.1 in 1 of 14 (2015–16, p = 0.001).
- **Years graded:** 2006–07 had too little history, and 2010–11, 2013–14, 2014–15 and 2017–18 had no scored stock at all. In 2008–09 and 2023–24 every event was a squeeze, so the long-only account held nothing (0.0% against 0.0%).
- **Long-short:** +24.3 bps/day, z = +0.53, positive in 9 of 14 years.
- **Rank IC in actual returns:** z = +1.19.
- **What the long-short measures:** most scored days hold one event (32 of 183 days had both an unwind and a squeeze side). On those days, "top 10% minus bottom 10%" splits one basket's members by index order, so it carries little meaning. No year had a degenerate t (every year had at least 5 long-short days).
- **The 16 unseen months** (start 2025-06-30, no walk-forward): +2.7% against +1.7% for the median random account (p = 0.214), so +1.0 point; SPY made +24.6%. The long-short (−115.6 bps/day) comes from 5 days of a single event.

## Decisions made while building it

**Before any outcome data:**
- **Returns and the market:**
  - simple close-to-close returns from the export's closes;
  - "the market" is the equal-weight mean return of each day's factor universe: close ≥ $5 the day before, median dollar volume over the prior 63 days ≥ $5M;
  - it correlates 0.998 with the export's own market series.
- **Universe at each refit:** stocks with returns on ≥ 95% of the 252 days before the refit, ranked by median dollar volume over those days. The top 1,500 are clustered.
- **The groups map:**
  - the numpy port of `together::groups` (20 groups, 252 days, the market removed with beta, spectral clustering with k-means) on the top 1,000 of those stocks with ≥ $5M, refit on the same 21-day schedule;
  - the other ~500 stocks join the group whose mean market-relative return they correlate with most, because the map only covers 1,000.
- **The residual:**
  - one regression per stock over the 252 days before the refit, of its return on the market, its group's return without it, and the six tercile style factors, with an intercept that is fitted but not subtracted, so a past winner's alpha doesn't build a reversal into its residual;
  - the group is removed by its fitted beta, not 1:1;
  - loadings are fixed for the refit's next 41 days, so every event-day and outcome residual is out of sample.
- **Style characteristics** as of the previous close:
  - 12-1 momentum;
  - 21-day return;
  - 60-day volatility;
  - 252-day market beta;
  - log 63-day median dollar volume;
  - log close.

  The close is split- and dividend-adjusted, which distorts the price level for old dates (as with the project's $5 rule). Factors are top minus bottom tercile among the factor universe.
- **The null:**
  - each stock's 126-day residual series is cut into six 21-day blocks, and each block is replaced by a random circular 21-day stretch of the same stock. A shift by whole blocks would have left 1 pair in 6 aligned;
  - the shifted panel is re-clustered 20 times per refit, with the same spectral clustering;
  - a cluster's p is the share of null clusters (≥ 8 members, pooled over sizes) whose mean correlation is at least its own;
  - re-clustering matters: noise clustered into 50 already has a mean correlation of 0.077.
- **Unwind details:**
  - "normal volume" is the median raw share volume (from the store) over the 63 days before;
  - "fell" means a negative residual that day;
  - a member "accounts for more than half the move" when its residual ÷ (members × basket move) > 0.5 and it traded ≥ 4× normal;
  - a basket needs data for ≥ 80% of its members that day;
  - σ needs ≥ 100 of the 126 days and uses the current members' residuals going back 126 days;
  - a qualifying day within 5 trading days after another of the same type, direction and cluster is the same event.
- **Test 4:**
  - the member-level regression is as described above, no intercept, with standard errors by calendar month;
  - h = 5 is primary, matching the hold;
  - the kill rule takes the strict reading of "bounce back more than both, with t ≥ 3": both differences positive with t ≥ 3. Test 5 uses the same rule with t ≥ 2.
- **Controls:**
  - N: kept-cluster moves of |z| ≥ 3 on normal volume, with the other rules dropped;
  - G: groups-map groups' |z| ≥ 3 moves, measured before the group is removed (the residual removes the group, so a group's bounce would vanish by construction);
  - strict versions, with every other unwind rule applied, are secondary.
- **Test 1 kill rule:** fewer than 5 kept clusters per refit on average, or a mean refit-to-refit adjusted Rand index below 0.05.
- **Money test:**
  - the hedge uses betas on the market and the member's group (without it) from the 252 days before the refit, summed daily over 5 days;
  - costs are 5 bps per side on the stock leg, the plan's 10 bps round trip; hedge-leg costs are reported separately;
  - if a day had no eligible random pool, the nearest day within 5 trading days is used;
  - statistics are per stock trade, over the judge's 19 June-to-June years.
- **Judge scores:** +|z| for unwinds and −|z| for squeezes on t..t+4, summed if a stock is in two events.
- **Episode windows** as in the table. The summer-2025 window comes from MSCI's text (June 1 – July 25, then a reprieve), read before the new export was opened.
- **13F:** one request without an email address. Blocked, so skipped.
- **FOMC and CPI days:**
  - FOMC: the Fed's monetary-policy press releases;
  - CPI: BLS's release archive, from the Wayback Machine because bls.gov refuses scripts.

**After seeing the z distribution and the event counts, before any outcome:**
- **σ comes from the selection window,** so out-of-sample cluster z has a standard deviation of 0.72. The rule was kept literally as the primary. A 2σ sensitivity was added: 2σ here is about 3σ against unselected days (2 ÷ 0.72 ≈ 2.8).
- **Power:** with 62 events, 22 of them in 2005–2019, a kill for lack of power still counts as a kill. Money-test years without trades count as not positive.
- **Judge degeneracy:** long-short day counts per year were checked for a degenerate t. None was found.

**After results** (post-hoc, reported as diagnostics only):
- **The market split** (|market| ≤ 2% or > 2% on the event day), after seeing most events on big market days.
- **Whole-cluster random guessers,** after seeing p = 0.037 from stock-by-stock draws, which spread across clusters and so understate the picks' noise.
- **A crash in the new-export run,** after the judge had finished: the single-holdout JSON keeps its numbers under `report.strategy`. The key was fixed and the run repeated with no rule changed; it is deterministic, so the outputs are identical.

## Files

**Code** (`research/phantom_portfolios/`, numpy only, system `python3`):
- `pp_common.py`: the fixed rules as constants, the groups map's spectral clustering and k-means ported from `together.rs` and `cluster.rs`, the adjusted Rand index, and OLS with month-clustered errors.
- `pp_panels.py`: returns, open-to-close returns, volume ratios, the market, the six style characteristics, the tercile factors and the label-fund returns.
- `pp_refits.py`: one refit every 21 days, run 8 processes at a time: the universe, the groups map, loadings and residuals, the phantom clusters with 20 null clusterings, and the daily basket and member tables.
- `pp_tests.py`: tests 1–6, the events list, the scores file and the judge.
- `pp_calendar.py`: the FOMC and CPI days (`fomc_cpi_days.tsv`).

**Outputs** (`research/phantom_portfolios/`):
- `results_old.txt`: every test on the old export;
- `results_new.txt`: the unseen months, recomputed on the new export, run once;
- `judge_old.txt`, `judge_new.txt`: the judge's reports;
- `events_old.tsv`, `events_new.tsv`: every unwind and squeeze, with z, volume, breadth, the same-day market, the label and the top members.

**Large files** (`~/.local/share/correlation2/experiments/phantom_portfolios/`):
- `old/` and `new/`: panels, plus `refits/R*.npz`, one per refit;
- `scores_old.npy` and `scores_new.npy`, each with the judge's JSON;
- `raw/`: the calendar pages;
- the run logs.
