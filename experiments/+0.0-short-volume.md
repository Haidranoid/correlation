# Short volume: does off-exchange short selling, measured against peers, predict next week?

**TL;DR:** Do stocks whose off-exchange short selling jumps, against their own past and similar stocks, do worse the next week? Slightly: in 2016–2019 the ranking held (t 2.9), but it trades so often that costs of 5 basis points a trade leave −0.5 basis points a week, and after costs the project's 20-stock test account only matches SPY (+0.0 points a year). Verdict: real but too small to pay.

## Nearest prior work

| Work | What it found |
|---|---|
| Boehmer, Jones & Zhang 2008, *JF* | Heavily shorted NYSE stocks underperform (proprietary order data) |
| Diether, Lee & Werner 2009, *RFS* | Daily short sales (Reg SHO, 2005) rise after gains and predict lower returns |
| Boehmer, Jones, Zhang & Zhang 2021, *JF* | Retail order imbalance predicts returns over the following weeks |

**What's new:** FINRA's public daily files, measured against each stock's history and its peer group, with the sign learned first. New data for the project. The files mix informed short selling with retail buying, because market makers sell short to fill retail buy orders.

**Novelty check (2026-10-21, a separate search; it changed no rules):** partly done, FINRA's daily files and an own-history abnormal ratio were tested on 2010–2015 and the abnormal ratio failed; the peer-group adjustment and learned sign were not found.
- **Wang, Yan & Zheng 2020** (JFE, "Shorting flows, public disclosure, and market efficiency"): On FINRA's daily short volume for all US common stocks, 2010–2015, the past five days' short volume ratio predicts lower returns over the next 20 days, 12.6% a year equal-weighted (t 6.00) and 6.0% value-weighted (t 4.03), 2.2 bps a day in large stocks. But the abnormal ratio (past week minus the prior month or quarter, close to this plan's own-history measure) does not predict: 0.2 bps a day equal-weighted (t 0.47), 0.4 bps value-weighted (t 1.17).
- **Goyal, Reed, Smajlbegovic & Soebhag 2025** (JFE, "Stealthy shorts: informed liquidity supply"): Splitting exchange short volume into liquidity-supplying and liquidity-demanding trades, they find the short volume ratio's known predictive power comes mostly from liquidity-supplying shorts, strongest on news days and in anomaly stocks. Their data are exchange trades (sample reported as 2014–2018, unverified), not FINRA's off-exchange files.
- **Ardia, Aymard & Cenesizoglu 2024** (arXiv 2403.17095, "Revisiting Boehmer et al. (2021)"): Over 2016–2021, retail order imbalance's predictive power weakens sharply; it no longer predicts weekly returns on large caps, and the long-short strategy is no longer profitable. That weakens the retail-buying reading of a high off-exchange ratio after 2015.
- **Barber, Huang, Jorion, Odean & Schwarz 2024** (JF, "A (sub)penny for your thoughts"): With 85,000 real retail trades (December 2021 to June 2022), the subpenny method flags only about 35% as retail and signs about 28% of those wrongly, so retail measures built from off-exchange prints are noisy.
- **Not found:** an abnormal ratio measured against stocks sharing the same tracking fund, a sign learned on 2010–2015 and held fixed, or any test of FINRA's daily files on data after 2019.

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Download:**
- every trading day from 2009-08-03 to 2026-10-19, into its own new, empty directory;
- where a day has both a CNMS file and per-facility files, check them against each other.

**Per symbol and day:**
- ShortVolume and TotalVolume, summed across facilities (or taken from CNMS);
- SVR = Short ÷ Total, missing when TotalVolume < 10,000 shares;
- FINRA symbols mapped to export tickers, including share classes.

**Weekly signal** at decision day t (every 5 trading days, fixed calendar from 2010-01-04):
- **Abnormal SVR:** the mean SVR over t−4..t, minus the stock's mean SVR over t−256..t−5 (at least 150 days).
- **Group-relative abnormal SVR (primary):** that, minus the mean abnormal SVR of the other universe stocks sharing its twin (at least 3).
- Own abnormal SVR is reported.

**Universe:** the judge-like liquid set with a twin, minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)`.

**Outcome:** the market-relative log return, close t → close t+5 (export).

**Sign:** the sign of the mean weekly rank IC over decision days 2010-01..2015-12, the training period. It is fixed from then on.

**Book:** weekly, long the tenth predicted best and short the tenth predicted worst, equal weight, 5 bps per side.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **2016–2019:** the weekly IC in the training sign with t ≥ 2.5, and the book's net mean positive.
2. **The 500 largest:** same sign.
3. **2020–2025-06:** same sign (IC). **The 16 unseen months** (new export, once, at the end): same sign.

**Money test (the judge):**
- Score = the oriented signal's percentile from 2016-01 on, NaN before. Graded with `rebalance=5`.
- Uniform and volatility-matched random accounts are both reported.

## The number for the file name

The judge's walk-forward change over random, with uniform random accounts, 0 bps, over the years it grades.

## Ways it can fool us

- **Market makers dominate the short volume.** That's why the sign is learned, not assumed.
- **Structural breaks:** the consolidated files from about 2018, the 2016–2018 tick-size pilot and shifts between venues. Measuring against peers is the guard.
- **Ticker changes:** FINRA uses the symbol of the day.

**What it means:**
- The training sign is the informed-shorting one: abnormal off-exchange shorting comes before weak weeks. It held, weakly, in every later period.
- But the signal is small, and it trades too much for its size. A 5-day window means a new book every week. The decile book needs costs under 4.8 bps a side in its best period and under 2.5 bps after 2020.
- Most of the IC in 2016–2019 comes from the newest day's file, which arrives after the close. A trader would get the weaker lagged version: +0.0024 (t 1.1) and 6.4 bps a week gross.
- The judge's +6.0 is the most consistent number here: ahead in 8 of 10 years, 2.9 standard errors from zero, and the same when trading a day late. But it comes from a 20-stock account that beat random at p < 0.1 in only one year, and the verdict rests on the plan's own tests.

## Decisions made while building it

**Before any return was computed:**
- **Which files exist.** Probing three days a year, 2009–2026 (`research/short_volume/out/prefix_probe.txt`), and FINRA's own page for the files:
  - FNSQ (Nasdaq TRF Carteret) and FNYX (NYSE TRF) exist on every trading day from 2009-08-03;
  - FNRA (the ADF; FINRA's page names it FNRA, not FADF) exists on most days, but not on 720 of them, mostly in 2013, 2015 and 2016;
  - FNQC (Nasdaq TRF Chicago) and CNMS (the consolidated file) exist from 2018-08-01;
  - FORF (the ORF) covers OTC securities.
  - A missing file returns 403. All 17,327 requested files were either fetched or returned 403, and no request failed (`manifest.tsv`).
- **The facility rule.** ShortVolume and TotalVolume are summed over FNSQ + FNQC + FNYX + FNRA, which is the set FINRA says CNMS combines (TRFs and ADF). A missing facility file counts as no volume there. The ORF is left out.
  - CNMS was only a fallback, for a day missing FNSQ or FNYX (or FNQC from 2018-09-10). No day needed it.
  - **Check:** on all 2,065 days with both (2018-08-01..2026-10-19), the facility sum equals CNMS for every symbol, to 0.01 shares.
  - Volumes became fractional on 2026-02-23 and are read as decimals.
- **The ORF.** In the monthly ORF samples, universe stocks appear 0–3 times a day (name collisions or OTC spells), except on 2009-10-01, when 174 listed stocks had ORF prints. That day falls only in early baselines, before the first decision day.
- **File quirks:**
  - two layouts (ShortExemptVolume added 2011-02-28), read by header;
  - one file without a header row (FNSQ 2010-11-22), read by field count;
  - a trailer count line, skipped;
  - the Date field matches the file name in every row.
- **Symbols.** FINRA writes class shares as `BRK.B` until 2012–13 and `BRK/B` after; the export writes `BRK-B`. `.`, `/` and space become `-`, then the symbol must match an export ticker exactly.
- **A guard against reused tickers.** FINRA uses the symbol of the day, so a ticker the export's company took over later matches the previous owner's history (e.g. `B`, `CB`, `JCI`, `WTW`, `ELV` before their current owners). Those show up as volumes that don't move together.
  - **Rule:** a stock-day's SVR is used only if the correlation of log FINRA TotalVolume with log export volume (dollar volume ÷ close) is at least 0.3 over the 63 days through d, with at least 40 common days.
  - The median correlation is 0.84–0.89 in every year. The guard removes 1.1–1.4 points of universe stock-days a year through 2021 and 0.3–0.7 after.
  - **Broken export volumes:** a day where FINRA's off-exchange volume exceeds 20× the export's consolidated volume is a broken export print and is left out of the correlation. The new store has volumes of a few dozen shares on 2026-03-03 for about 700 stocks. Without this, 17% of the universe would fail the guard in March–May 2026; with it, 0.25% of 2026 stock-days fail.
- **Coverage:** FINRA records exist for 89% of universe stock-days in 2009–2015, rising to 99% by 2024–25 (`out/mapcheck_old.txt`). The tables are under Results.
- **The signal:**
  - SVR = ShortVolume ÷ TotalVolume, missing when TotalVolume < 10,000 shares;
  - the recent mean over t−4..t needs at least 3 of the 5 days;
  - the baseline over t−256..t−5 needs at least 150;
  - the twin group comes from `c2eval.twins` at `rebal_index(t)`. The "others" are the other universe stocks with the same fund and a finite abnormal SVR, at least 3, and exclude the stock itself.
- **Universe:** night and day's judge-like liquid set (point in time on the twins calendar, minus `c2eval.excluded`) restricted to stocks with a twin at that rebalance. The 500 largest are the top 500 of it by median dollar volume over the trailing 63 days.
  - Ranks and deciles are re-formed inside the 500 largest. The signal itself still uses the full universe's peers.
- **Two calendars.** The tests use the plan's weekly decision days, export index of 2010-01-04 + 5k. The judge rebalances on its own calendar, so it gets a daily score panel. The first decision day with a signal is 2010-05-13: the volume guard needs 40 days from 2009-08-03, and then the baseline needs 150.
- **Outcome:** log(close t+5 ÷ close t) − Σ market(t+1..t+5), where market is the export's equal-weight mean daily log return of the liquid stocks.
  - **Check:** market(d) correlates with the median stock's return on day d, not d+1.
  - The book uses simple returns, close t+5 ÷ close t − 1.
- **Statistics:**
  - IC is Spearman with tied ranks averaged, on weeks with at least 50 names;
  - t is plain over the weekly series, since the weeks don't overlap.
- **The book:**
  - deciles are round(10%) of the universe names with a finite signal;
  - members without a close at t+5 are dropped from that week's means;
  - turnover is Σ|Δw| per side with weights 1/m, and cost is 5 bps × (long turnover + short turnover). "5 bps per side" is read as 5 bps per trade, charged on turnover, as the judge and the night-and-day plan use the term; it is not a flat 5 bps a week per side;
  - "net mean positive" is the mean weekly net over the period.
- **Periods,** by decision day:
  - training: 2010-01-04..2015-12-31;
  - Test 1: 2016-01-01..2019-12-31;
  - Test 2: the 500 largest over the same 2016–2019, where "same sign" means the mean IC is positive in the training sign;
  - Test 3: 2020-01-01..2025-06-27, IC positive;
  - the unseen months: the same calendar's decision days dated from 2025-06-30 in the new export, with close t+5 by 2026-10-19, IC positive.
- **Own abnormal SVR** (reported, not tested) gets its own training sign.
- **Posting time.** FINRA posts day t's file by 6 pm ET, after the close of t. So the plan's window t−4..t can't be traded at t's close.
  - The plan's signal stays the primary.
  - Two checks run alongside it: the same signals one day later (window t−5..t−1, baseline t−257..t−6, with the primary's sign), and the judge with `delay=1`.
- **Money test:**
  - Score = the oriented G's percentile, (rank + 1) ÷ n, among universe names with a finite G, every day from 2016-01-01, NaN before; `rebalance=5`.
  - The judge's 2015-06-28..2016-06-28 year therefore has picks only in its second half; strategy and random accounts both hold nothing before.
- **Plumbing check:** 5-day reversal through this folder's path with `rebalance=5` gives +11.8 points a year and beats random in 8 of 18 years, as it should.

**The sign, from 2010–2015 only** (`train.py`, `out/train_old.txt`; written here before any 2016+ statistic was computed):
- **G, the group-relative abnormal SVR (primary):** mean weekly IC −0.0093 (t −4.02, 284 weeks from 2010-05-13 to 2015-12-24, median 846 names). **Sign −1:** a stock whose off-exchange short ratio rose more than its twin group's tends to do worse the next week. That is the informed-short-seller reading, not the retail-buying one.
- **A, the own abnormal SVR:** −0.0106 (t −3.92). Sign −1.
- **The one-day-later checks:** G_lag −0.0066 (t −2.90) and A_lag −0.0077 (t −2.89). They take the signs of G and A.
- The last training decision day, 2015-12-24, has its outcome at the close of 2016-01-04, two trading days into 2016. The plan defines training by decision day.
- **The order:**
  1. the decisions above;
  2. the training ICs;
  3. this sign, written here;
  4. only then `tests.py` and `money.py` on 2016 onward;
  5. the unseen months last.

**After the results:**
- **The diagnostics in `posthoc.py`** were chosen after seeing the walk-forward results. They are not tests:
  - the overlap with 5-day reversal, prompted by Diether, Lee & Werner's finding that short sellers trade against recent gains;
  - the long and short sides of the book.
- **The unseen months** were run once, after every walk-forward number above was final: `tests.py new`, `money.py new` and `posthoc.py new`.
