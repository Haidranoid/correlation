# Co-movement market timing: hold more stock when stocks move together?

**TL;DR:** This is the project's first market-timing test; every earlier experiment picked stocks. It follows Pollet & Wilson (2010), who found that the market's future return is higher when stocks move together more. Each month the rule held SPY fully if the average correlation among the 500 most-traded stocks over the last 63 days was above its 5-year median, and half SPY, half cash otherwise. It doesn't work. Over 242 months from 2005-04 to 2025-06 it made 7.0% a year, against 7.3% for random timing with the same 73% average exposure (p = 0.62) and 9.8% for buy-and-hold. It fails the first test: in 2005–2019 it trailed random timing by 0.1 points a year (p = 0.54), and its Sharpe ratio of 0.44 was below buy-and-hold's 0.53. Volatility-managed SPY at the same exposure did far better (Sharpe 0.76; bootstrap p = 0.87 for the rule being better), and in 2020–2025 the rule trailed random timing again, by 0.6 points. SPY's next month averaged +0.89% after high-correlation months and after low ones alike. Because correlations jump when markets fall, the rule is fully invested in crashes and took all of 2008's drawdown (−57%). In the 15 unseen months the correlation stayed below its median, so the rule sat at 50% throughout and made 9.0% a year against SPY's 18.5%. Verdict: no edge. Co-movement doesn't time the market here, and plain volatility management is the better timing rule.

## Nearest prior work

From the earlier survey; a separate novelty check runs alongside.

| Work | What it found |
|---|---|
| Pollet & Wilson 2010, *JFE*, "Average correlation and stock market returns" | Average correlation among large stocks predicts market returns positively, in sample |
| Kritzman, Li, Page & Rigobon 2011, *JPM* | The absorption ratio (variance share of the top eigenvectors) rises before drawdowns |
| Moreira & Muir 2017, *JF*, "Volatility-managed portfolios" | Scaling exposure inversely to recent variance raises the Sharpe ratio; disputed out of sample (Cederburg et al. 2020) |
| Welch & Goyal 2008; Goyal, Welch & Zafirov 2024 | Most market predictors fail out of sample |

**Novelty check (2026-10-21, a separate search; it changed no rules):** partly done; the exact rule was untested.
- **Pollet & Wilson 2010:** average correlation predicts the next quarter's excess market return.
- **Follow-ups:** industry-level, 1963–2018; 27 countries (*Global Finance Journal* 2022), strongest in the most recent subperiod. Buss, Schönleber & Vilkov find correlation implied by option prices predicts out of sample.
- **The other direction:** Kritzman et al. 2011 (the absorption ratio reads high co-movement as a reason to cut risk); Moreira & Muir 2017.
- **Cederburg et al. 2020** (*JFE*): volatility management fails in real time across 103 strategies. On this data, though, volatility-managed SPY was the best of the rules.

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**State at the end of each month** (every 21 trading days, on a fixed calendar):
- the average pairwise correlation of daily log returns among the 500 most-traded stocks (median dollar volume over 63 days, minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)`), over the last 63 trading days;
- compared with its own median over the previous 1,260 days of month-end values.

**Rule:** if the average correlation is above that median, hold 100% SPY for the next 21 trading days; otherwise hold 50% SPY and 50% cash. Cash earns 0, so the comparisons are fair because every rule gets the same.

**Baselines:**
- **(a) buy and hold:** 100% SPY;
- **(b) random timing:** the same number of 100% and 50% months as the rule, in random order, 10,000 draws;
- **(c) volatility-managed SPY:** exposure = c ÷ SPY's realized variance over the last 21 days, capped at 1. The constant c is set so the average exposure matches the rule's.

**Reported but not pass/fail:** the absorption-ratio rule. Hold 100% when the share of variance in the top 5 eigenvectors of the same 500 stocks is below its 5-year median, otherwise 50%.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **2005–2019:** the rule's annual return beats random timing at p < 0.05 (one-sided), and its Sharpe ratio is at least buy-and-hold's.
2. **Beats volatility-managed SPY:** a higher Sharpe ratio, with a bootstrap p < 0.10 for the difference.
3. **2020–2025-06:** same direction against random timing. The 16 unseen months (new export and store, once, at the end): reported. That is only about 15 months, too few to judge.

## The number for the file name

- **What it is:** the rule's annual return minus the median random timing's (same average exposure), over 2005–2025, in points a year.
- **Why not the judge:** this isn't a stock-picking account. `experiments/README.md`'s key notes this.

## Ways it can fool us

- **Few independent observations:** 20 years is about 240 months, and a timing rule changes only a few dozen times. The bootstrap and the random-timing null are the honest yardsticks.
- **The survivor-only stock list:** this only shifts the average correlation slightly; SPY itself is real.

## Results (run 2026-10-21)

**The tests, in order:**
1. **2005-04..2019-12 against random timing and buy-and-hold: fails.** The rule made 6.2% a year against 6.3% for the median random timing (p = 0.54, needed < 0.05). Its Sharpe ratio of 0.44 was below buy-and-hold's 0.53.
2. **Against volatility-managed SPY: fails.** Its Sharpe ratio was 0.44 against 0.76 at the same 74% exposure, with a bootstrap p of 0.87 for the rule being better (needed < 0.10).
3. **2020-01..2025-06: fails.** 9.0% a year against 9.6% for random timing (p = 0.62).

The verdict stops at test 1. All the numbers are computed anyway.

- **No sign of Pollet & Wilson's effect:** SPY's next 21 days averaged +0.89% after the 111 high-correlation months and +0.89% after the 131 low ones (t = 0.00). The state's correlation with SPY's next 21-day return is +0.04 (t = 0.56).
- **The rule is fully invested in crashes.** Correlations jump when markets fall, so it held 100% from 2007-07 to 2010-11 except for one month (2008-08). Its drawdown (−57%) matched buy-and-hold's; random timing at the same exposure lost 46% at the median, and volatility-managed SPY 23%.
- **Costs don't matter:** 39 switches in 20 years. At 5 bps on the half of the account that moves, that is 0.05 points a year.
- **Sensitivity, with only 500-stock states in the median** (decisions from 2007-06, 216 months): −0.6 points against random timing (p = 0.73). Sharpe 0.43, against 0.55 for buy-and-hold and 0.64 for volatility-managed SPY.

## Decisions made while building it

All made before any return of the rule, a baseline or SPY over a holding month was computed, unless marked otherwise. The only data looked at first was SPY's price series, for bad prints and its first day.

- **Calendar:** the twins' fixed calendar, export days 252 + 21k (`tw['rebal']`), so `excluded(E, tw)[k]` lines up by construction. The state is read at the close of day t0; the position is held from that close to the close of t0 + 21.
- **SPY starts late in the store:** its first close is 2005-02-25, so the first month with 21 days of SPY returns for the volatility-managed baseline starts at the close of 2005-04-04. Every strategy starts there. The periods are "2005-04..2019-12" and "2020-01..2025-06", each month assigned by its decision day t0. Only complete 21-day months count: the last starts 2025-05-14 and ends 2025-06-13, and the 10 days after it are dropped. That leaves 242 months, 177 in the first period and 65 in the second.
- **The 5-year median can't exist in 2005:** 60 month-end values need 1,260 days, the export starts 2001-12-31, and `excluded()` exists only from day 252 (2003-01). So the median is over the previous month-end values, at most 60 and at least 24, not counting the current one. It covers the full 60 from about 2008-01 on. In 2005 it uses 27 values.
- **Before 2005-02-25 the export has only about 320 stocks** (found while computing the states, before any return): Stooq's long histories, mostly large companies (91% of them are in the top 500 by dollar volume in mid-2005). From that day it has about 2,030. So the states from 2003-01 to 2005-05 are on all 315–319 eligible stocks, which is the same rule applied to what exists, and the first two decisions (2005-04, 2005-05) use them too. The first 500-stock state is 2005-06-02.
  - **Check, signal only:** over 2005-06..2010-06, the average correlation of the subset of the 500 that existed before 2005 runs +0.009 above the 500's on average (−0.025..+0.049), about +0.03 in 2005's months. So the early median history is close; it is kept. For the absorption ratio the subset runs +0.027 higher (up to +0.07 in 2007), which pushes the absorption rule toward 100% in 2005–2007; it is only reported, so it is kept too and flagged. Files: `check_universe.py`, `check_universe.txt`.
  - **Sensitivity, reported only:** the same rules with only 500-stock states in the median, so decisions start once 24 of them exist (2007-06). The verdict rests on the main version.
- **The universe at t0:** drop `excluded(E, tw)[k]`; keep stocks with a median dollar volume above 0 over days t0−62..t0 and a daily log return on at least 90% of those 63 days; take the 500 with the highest median dollar volume. The data filter is applied before choosing the 500. No $5 price rule; the plan has none.
- **Average correlation:** each stock's 63 daily log returns are standardized over its own finite days, missing days count as 0 (as `c2eval.twins` does), and the average pairwise correlation is the mean of the off-diagonal entries, (‖ΣZ‖² − n) / (n(n − 1)), equal-weighted.
- **Above the median:** strictly greater holds 100%; anything else 50%.
- **Absorption ratio:** the covariance matrix of the same 500 stocks' demeaned daily log returns over the same 63 days, missing days 0 after demeaning; the share of the total variance in the top 5 eigenvalues (taken from the 63 × 63 Gram matrix, same nonzero spectrum). Same median rule, opposite direction: 100% when strictly below the median.
- **Holding:** buy and hold within the month: 100% or 50% of the account in SPY at t0, the rest in cash at 0, not rebalanced daily. SPY is the store's adjusted close, forward-filled on its one missing day, 2011-02-17, so the next day carries two days' move. The bad-print check found nothing: every SPY move beyond 7% is a real 2008, 2020 or 2025-04-09 day.
- **Measures, the same for every strategy:** annual return = compound annual growth over the period's months (252 days a year); Sharpe = mean ÷ sd of daily returns × √252, risk-free 0 because cash earns 0; maximum drawdown from the daily value path; exposure = the mean monthly weight in SPY.
- **Random timing:** 10,000 random orderings of the rule's own monthly weights within the period. p = (1 + draws with annual return ≥ the rule's) ÷ 10,001, one-sided. The file-name number is the rule's annual return minus the median draw's over the whole span 2005-04..2025-06 (one null over all months), rounded to 0.1.
- **Volatility-managed SPY:** weight = min(1, c ÷ variance of SPY's 21 daily log returns ending at t0, ddof 0), set monthly on the same calendar and bought and held like the rule. c is found by bisection so that the mean weight equals the rule's over the window being judged: 2005-04..2019-12 for test 2, the whole span for the main table, 2020-01..2025-06 for that period. Setting c on the window itself is the plan's own mild in-sample choice, made for the baseline only. The five-year blocks in the by-period table use the whole span's c.
- **Bootstrap p (test 2):** a paired circular block bootstrap of months (the same months for both strategies), blocks of 12 months, 10,000 draws, Sharpe recomputed from the daily returns of the drawn months; p = share of draws where the rule's Sharpe minus the volatility-managed one is ≤ 0. The same bootstrap is reported for the rule against buy-and-hold.
- **Costs:** the plan charges none. Reported only: the number of switches, and what 5 bps on the half of the account that moves at each switch would cost a year.
- **By period:** 2005-04..2009, 2010–2014, 2015–2019 and 2020–2025-06, random timing reordered within each.
- **The unseen months:** before using the new export, check that its first 5,911 days equal the old export's, so the calendar continues. The states are recomputed on the new export with `twins(E_new)` and the new store's SPY. The months are the calendar points from the old export's last day (2025-06-27) on whose 21 days fit before 2026-10-19: 15 months. The volatility-managed baseline reuses c from the whole old span rather than fitting 15 months.
- **After results, the unseen months' p:** the average correlation stayed below its median in all 15 unseen months, so the rule held 50% throughout and every random ordering equals it. The formula gives p = 1.0; it is reported as "no p" instead, since there is nothing to test.
