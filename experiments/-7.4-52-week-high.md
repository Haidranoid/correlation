# The 52-week high: do stocks near their yearly high keep rising?

**TL;DR:** This replicates a published anomaly; nothing in the method is new. George & Hwang (2004) ranked US stocks on PTH, the price divided by its 52-week high. Over July 1963–December 2001, a book long the top 30% and short the bottom 30%, with six overlapping six-month cohorts, earned 0.45% a month (t = 2.00), level with Jegadeesh-Titman momentum's 0.48% (t = 2.35). In their Fama-MacBeth regressions its pure profit was 0.65% a month (t = 4.08) against momentum's 0.38%, so the 52-week high "dominated" momentum. What's new is running their book on this project's liquid, survivor-only stocks, entirely after their sample: 2006–2025, plus 16 unseen months. The book uses 21-day periods and 5 bps a side. Over 2006–2019 it lost 0.38% a period (t = −0.97), so test 1 fails. The loss is the 2009 momentum crash (−59% over 2008–2009); over 2010–2019 it earned about zero (+0.06%). Its FF5 + UMD alpha was −0.38% (t = −2.66). Here the 52-week high is momentum. Regressed on 12-1 momentum, which itself lost 0.37% a period, the book's intercept is −0.02% (t = −0.08), with slope 0.96 and R² 0.75, so test 2 would fail as well. 2020–2025-06 gave −0.02% a period (t = −0.03; −0.00% with dividends restored), and the unseen months −9.3% in all (−8.7% with dividends). The one-week skip and the 500 largest changed nothing. Later replications say the same: Chen & Zimmermann's post-publication return for this signal is 0.18% a month (t = 0.56), and Lobão & Fernandes find −0.34% (t = −0.91) over 2001–2014. In the money test, the judge's top 20 by PTH (in practice, a random 20 of the stocks at their high) trailed uniform random accounts by 5.3 points a year (se 3.6, ahead in 7 of 18 years) and volatility-matched ones by 3.9 (se 2.8). In the unseen months they lost 5.8% while the median random account made 19.0%. Verdict: no edge. The 52-week high earned its money before about 2001; since then it has been momentum, and momentum has earned nothing here.

## Published claim

| Paper | Sample | What it reports |
|---|---|---|
| George & Hwang 2004, *JF* | US, 1963–2001 | Nearness to the 52-week high predicts returns, dominates Jegadeesh-Titman and industry momentum, and doesn't reverse in the long run |

**Our test period is after the paper's sample.**

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Universe:** the judge-like liquid set, minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)`.

**Signal:**
- PTH = close_t ÷ the highest close over t−251..t.
- Export closes are split-adjusted, so a split doesn't fake a fall from the high. The ratio is the paper's anchor; the adjustment keeps it free of splits.

**Book:**
- Every 21 trading days (the fixed calendar), long the top 30% by PTH and short the bottom 30%, equal weight.
- Each cohort is held 6 periods (126 days); six overlapping cohorts each carry 1/6 of the capital, as in the paper.
- Market-relative returns, 5 bps per side.

**Variant, reported:** a one-week skip between formation and holding.

**Baseline:** 12-1 momentum with the same 30/30 split, 6-month hold and calendar.

**Alphas:** FF5 + UMD (French).

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **2006–2019:** the book's mean monthly net return. **Pass:** t ≥ 2.
2. **Beats momentum, the paper's main claim:** regress the book's monthly returns on the momentum baseline's. The intercept is positive with t ≥ 2.
3. **2020–2025-06:** same sign. **The 16 unseen months** (new export, once, at the end): same sign.

**Reported:** the 500 largest, and the FF5 + UMD alpha.

**Money test (the judge):**
- Score = PTH's percentile. Graded with `rebalance=21`.
- Uniform and volatility-matched random accounts are both reported.

## The number for the file name

The judge's walk-forward change over random, with uniform random accounts, 0 bps (`c2eval.change_over_random`).

## Ways it can fool us

- **It is a momentum strategy,** and momentum has been weak here. The standalone test (1) comes before the comparison (2).
- **Low volatility:** stocks near their highs are often calm ones. The volatility-matched judge line is the check.

## Results (run 2026-10-21)

**What the data allowed.**
- **The universe exists from 2004-12-31.** It needs 756 days of history, so six cohorts are active from 2005-06 on, and in every test period.
- **Until 2007 the liquid set has only about 300 stocks** (the export's stocks with data before 2005), so each leg holds about 90 and the "500 largest" are the whole set. From 2008 it has about 1,000 stocks, rising to 2,250 by 2025. Legs hold a median of 385 stocks (89 to 684).
- **Test 1 covers the 167 periods ending 2006-02-01..2019-12-06; test 3 the 66 ending 2020-01-08..2025-06-13.** A period is 21 trading days. The old export's last 9 days, after the final grid day, are dropped.
- **Many stocks sit exactly at their high.** On a median day, 43 universe stocks close at a new 52-week high (PTH = 1), and on 67% of days more than 20 do; on 2021-01-07, 452 did. They never reach the book's 30% cut, but they decide the judge's top 20 (see Decisions).
- **Plumbing checks** (`out/check.txt`, `out/sanity_reversal.txt`):
  - PTH recomputed by hand on 2,000 random stock-days: 0 mismatches.
  - One cohort's long and short leg returns, recomputed by hand from the raw closes, match the code to six decimals.
  - The 12-1 baseline correlates 0.91 with French's UMD over 2006–2019, and 0.88 over 2020–2025-06.
  - 5-day reversal through this folder's scores and judge path gives +11.8 points a year, 8 of 18 years beating random at p < 0.1, as required.
- **Costs are small:** 5 bps a side at formation and exit costs 0.033% a period (0.4% a year), the same for the book and the baseline.

- **The book lost money,** t = −0.97 against the +2 required. **The verdict stops here.** Every later number was still computed.
- **Both legs are wrong-way, about equally.** The long leg trailed the equal-weight market leg by 0.18% a period (t = −1.06). The short leg beat it by 0.16% (t = −0.73).
- **The negative alpha is not a short bet that paid.** The book loads +0.74 on UMD, and UMD itself earned little: +0.12% a period over the same days (t = 0.30). With that loading, a pure momentum position would have earned about +0.09%, and the book lost 0.38%. The alpha says the 52-week high did worse than its own momentum exposure, by 0.38% a period.

**Source:** George & Hwang (2004), *Journal of Finance* 59(5), 2145–2176. I read the typeset JF version of the article, hosted on the first author's site at [bauer.uh.edu/tgeorge/papers/gh4-paper.pdf](https://www.bauer.uh.edu/tgeorge/papers/gh4-paper.pdf). Every number in the four GH rows was read from its Tables I, II and V. Its text says: "The returns to these strategies are very close, all around 0.45% per month", and "a self-financing 52-week high momentum strategy yields 0.65% ... per month, which is much greater than 0.38% for JT and 0.25% for MG."

**Reading it.**
- **The paper's portfolio-form result was itself marginal:** 0.45% a month with t = 2.00, no better than momentum's 0.48%.
- **Its "dominates momentum" claim comes from cross-sectional Fama-MacBeth regressions** with dummies for all three strategies, controls for size and last month's return, and a one-month skip. Test 2 here is a simpler time-series regression of one book on the other.
- Our 21-day periods average about a calendar month, so the units compare.
- **Here the 52-week high book matches momentum period by period** (R² 0.75) and earns what momentum earns: nothing since 2006, and a 59% loss in the 2009 crash.

### Differences from George & Hwang

| | George & Hwang 2004 | Here |
|---|---|---|
| Universe | All CRSP stocks, delisted ones and microcaps included | Liquid survivors: $5 and $5M medians, listed in 2025, funds removed |
| Sample | 1963-07..2001-12 | 2006–2025-06, plus 16 unseen months; entirely after theirs |

## Decisions made while building it

All of these were made on 2026-10-21 **before any book return, test statistic or judge result was looked at**, unless a line says otherwise.

**Calendar and periods**
- **"Months" are the fixed 21-day grid** (`c2eval.twins`' calendar, export index 252 + 21k). Cohort k is formed at the close of grid day t_k and held to the close of t_k + 126. A period is (t_j, t_{j+1}], and it belongs to a test window by its end day.
- **Windows:** test 1 is the periods ending 2006-01-01..2019-12-31 (167 periods, since the first ends 2006-02-01); test 3 those ending 2020-01-01..2025-06-27. The old export's 9-day stub after its last grid day (2025-06-13) is dropped.
- **The unseen months:** on the new export (everything rebuilt), the period that contains 2025-06-27 is split there, and the stub after the last grid day (2026-10-15) runs to 2026-10-19. The unseen periods then cover 2025-06-30..2026-10-19 exactly, with a short first and last period. Cohorts formed before 2025-06-30 that are still held after it belong to the book, as they would live.
- **The universe exists from 2004-12-31** (it needs 756 days of history), so six cohorts are active from mid-2005 and in every test period. Until 2007 the liquid set has only about 300 stocks (the export's stocks with data before 2005); from 2008 it has about 1,000, rising to 2,250. So in 2006–2007 each leg holds about 90 stocks and the "500 largest" are the whole set.

**Signal**
- **PTH** = the export's close at t ÷ the maximum of its finite closes over t−251..t, from the raw export closes (split-adjusted, and dividend-adjusted until late 2022, as the export is). NaN unless close_t is present and at least 227 of the 252 closes are (the universe's 90% rule).
- **12-1 momentum** (the baseline): log(Cf[t−21] ÷ Cf[t−252]), with Cf the forward-filled closes.
- **GH's own Jegadeesh-Titman signal, reported only:** the past 6-month return log(Cf[t] ÷ Cf[t−126]), no skip. It is their Table I comparison.
- **The one-week skip:** rank on PTH at t_k − 5 and form the book at the close of t_k, so the calendar is unchanged.

**Book**
- **Eligible at t_k (a common sample for the book and the baseline):** the judge-like universe at t_k with a finite PTH and a finite 12-1 momentum. The skip variant also needs a close at t_k and a finite PTH at t_k − 5. A formation needs at least 100 such stocks; it always had about 300 or more (legs of 89 to 684 stocks).
- **Legs:** the top and bottom round(0.3 n) by the signal, equal weight, $1 per leg. Ties (many stocks sit exactly at their high, PTH = 1) are broken by a seeded random key; they never reach the 30% cut.
- **Accounting:** each leg is bought and held on forward-filled closes, so positions drift. A stock whose closes stop is held flat at its last close; the data hold survivors only. Over a period the cohort earns r_L − r_S, each leg's own growth ratio over the period. This is the Jegadeesh-Titman / George-Hwang convention: winners' return minus losers' return. The book's period return is the mean over the cohorts active in it.
- **Costs:** 5 bps of each leg's notional at formation (10 bps in the cohort's first period), and 5 bps of each leg's drifted value at exit (in its last period). They are charged per cohort, with no netting of trades across cohorts, which is the conservative reading of "when a cohort forms and when it exits".
- **"Market-relative":** the book is $1 long and $1 short per cohort, so the market drops out of r_L − r_S. Each leg is also reported against a market leg: all eligible stocks, equal weight, held the same way.
- **The 500 largest:** the shared definition (top 500 of the universe by 63-day median dollar volume at t_k). The 30/30 split is redone within them, for the book, the baseline and the skip variant.

**Statistics**
- **The t of test 1** is the plain t of the mean period return, Jegadeesh-Titman's and George-Hwang's convention. Newey-West with 6 lags (the hold) is reported alongside, since five of a period's six cohorts are shared with the next one's.
- **Test 2:** OLS of the book's net period returns on the 12-1 baseline's net period returns over test 1's periods. The test is the intercept, with t ≥ 2 (plain OLS errors), and NW(6) is reported alongside. The slope and R² are reported.
- **Alphas:** French's daily Mkt-RF, SMB, HML, RMW, CMA and UMD, compounded over each period's days (`pubdata.on_days`; the daily dates match the export's). The market excess is Π(1 + Mkt) − Π(1 + RF), and the others are Π(1 + f) − 1. Monthly French files don't line up with 21-day periods, so they aren't used. The book is self-financing, so RF isn't subtracted from it. French's data end 2026-08-31, so unseen-month alphas use only the periods inside that.
- **"Same sign"** in test 3 and the unseen months means the mean net period return has test 1's sign. Alphas are reported.
- **Dividends:** the primary numbers use the export's closes as they are, as the judge does. The dividend-corrected book reruns the identical code on the total-return index C × exp(cumsum(`pubdata.div_fix(tag)`)), with the signals unchanged. The panel is 0 before 2022-07, so earlier periods are identical. Every period touching 2022-07 or later is reported both ways. Expected direction, stated in advance: the correction should help the long leg slightly, since calm dividend payers sit nearer their highs than distressed stocks do.
- **January:** a reported split of periods with at least 11 of their days in January against the rest, for comparison with GH's Table II. It is not a test.

**Money test**
- **Daily score:** PTH's percentile within the universe of the latest grid day on or before d, among stocks with a finite PTH at d: r ÷ (n − 1), with r the 0-based rank, so the top scores 1.
- **Ties:** the judge sorts scores stably, so tied stocks would be taken in row (ticker) order. On a typical grid day 20–450 universe stocks close exactly at their 52-week high (PTH = 1), far more than the 20 picks. Ties are therefore broken by a seeded pseudo-random key drawn per stock and day, which makes the judge's top 20 a random draw from the stocks at their high.
- **Grading:** `rebalance=21`, uniform and volatility-matched random accounts, walk-forward on the old export. The unseen months use the new export, once, at the end.

**Made after seeing results**
- **Nothing in the construction, the tests or the money test was changed after results were seen.** Every script ran once on the old export, then once on the new one.
- **Added to the write-up after results, as description only:**
  - the worst periods;
  - the 2010–2019 mean (the average of the 2010–2014 and 2015–2019 rows);
  - the UMD-loading arithmetic in test 1 (loading × UMD's mean);
  - the "Later replications" table, from a literature search run in parallel and finished after the results.
- **The 16 unseen months** were run once, at the end, after the walk-forward numbers above were final.
