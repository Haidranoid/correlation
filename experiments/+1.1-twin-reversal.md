# Twin reversal: does a stock that drifts from its twin come back?

**TL;DR:** When a stock drifts from the fund that tracks it best over a month, does it come back? No: among the 500 most-traded stocks, buying the laggards and shorting the leaders, hedged with their funds, lost 0.18% a month in 2006–2019 (t −0.69). The 20-stock test account beat SPY by +1.1 points a year after costs, mostly from one year, 2020–21, and not significantly. Verdict: no edge; the hedge cuts risk, not returns.

## The idea

- **What reverses:** a stock's month splits into what its twin did (the sector or theme) and the stock's own part. Published work finds the own part reverses more reliably than the raw move, because much of it is liquidity, not news.
- **Why use the twin:** the hedge finder's twin is the tradeable fund that tracks the stock best, chosen from co-movement, which is the part of correlation that persists and helps (`+5.1-co-movement-tools.md`). Examples: XOM→XLE, JPM→XLF.
- **This project so far:** plain 21-day reversal gave +3.7 points a year (not significant). The bystanders test found that stocks' own quiet moves reverse (t −2.0).

## Nearest prior work

From the earlier survey and well-known literature; no new search this round.

| Work | What it found |
|---|---|
| Blitz, Huij, Lansdorp & Verbeek 2013, *J. Financial Markets*, "Short-term residual reversal" | 1-month reversal on factor-model residuals earns about twice raw reversal, net of costs even in large caps |
| Da, Liu & Schaumburg 2014, *Management Science* | Within-industry, non-fundamental residuals reverse strongly; cross-industry moves carry momentum |
| Avellaneda & Lee 2010, *Quantitative Finance* | Mean reversion of residuals against sector ETFs or PCA factors, at horizons of days; faded after 2003 |
| Gatev, Goetzmann & Rouwenhorst 2006; Chen, Chen, Chen & Li 2019 | Pairs trading faded; correlation-peer signals are reversal plus industry momentum |

**What's new:** the residual is against the single best-tracking fund, chosen from co-movement and refreshed monthly, at a monthly horizon in large caps. It is a close cousin of Avellaneda & Lee's sector-ETF residuals, at Blitz et al.'s horizon.

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Universe:**
- the 500 most-traded stocks (median dollar volume over the past 63 days), price ≥ $5;
- not fund-like, with a twin, both from `c2eval.twins` at the latest twin rebalance;
- refreshed every 21 trading days on a fixed calendar.

**Signal:** s = −`c2eval.twin_residual(E, tw, 21, scaled=True)`. That is minus the stock's 21-day log return relative to β × its twin's, in units of a typical 21-day residual move. It is computed every day.

**The plan's portfolio**
- Every 21 trading days: long the 10% of the universe with the highest s (fell most against its twin), short the 10% with the lowest.
- Each stock is paired with −β × its twin, so the position is hedged.
- Equal weight per stock, held 21 trading days, with daily returns from closes.
- **Timing:** decided at close d and traded at close d, as in all the project's tests. A check trades one day later (close d+1).

**Baselines** (same universe, calendar and leg sizes):
- **(a) plain 21-day reversal:** s = −(21-day log return), as a long-short;
- **(b) market-residual reversal:** s = −(21-day return − β_market × the market's 21-day return), hedged with SPY at the stock's market beta.

**Costs:** 5 bps per side on stock trades and 1 bp per side on fund trades, on actual turnover.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **2005–2019:** the twin long-short, hedged. **Pass:** mean monthly return with t ≥ 3 gross, and positive net of costs.
2. **Beats the baselines:** the paired monthly difference against both (a) and (b). **Pass:** t ≥ 2 for both.
3. **2020–2025-06** (old export): same sign, with t ≥ 1.5. The one-day-late check must have the same sign.
4. **The 16 unseen months** (new export, once, at the end): the plan's portfolio and the judge.

**Money test (the judge)**
- Scores = s, every day; NaN outside the universe. Grade with `rebalance=21`.
- The long-only account holds the 20 stocks that fell most against their twins, unhedged in the judge.
- Uniform and volatility-matched random accounts are both reported.

## The number for the file name

The judge's walk-forward change over random, with uniform random accounts (`c2eval.change_over_random`, 0 bps), as for every other file.

## Ways it can fool us

- **Twin quality:** in early years fewer stocks have a liquid fund. Report how many qualify each year, and the median twin correlation.
- **News vs noise:** a stock that drifted on real news keeps drifting (bystanders found news spreads). Residual reversal pays only where the drift was noise. Report earnings-season months separately; a volume spike in the stock during the month is a proxy for news.
- **Survivorship:** the losers it buys are survivors, which flatters the long leg. The hedged long-short and the 500-largest universe limit this.

### Verdict

- **Fails Test 1:** the hedged twin long-short is negative over 2006–2019 (t −0.69).
- **Also fails:**
  - Test 2: it doesn't beat plain reversal or the market residual;
  - Test 3: 2020–2025 is t +0.33, and negative a day late;
  - Test 4: the unseen months are t −0.35.
- **What the twin does deliver is risk control.** It halves the long-short's volatility against plain reversal. That fits the project's lesson that same-day co-movement is useful for risk, not returns.
- **In this large-cap universe since 2006,** 1-month reversal, raw or residual, has nothing left to hedge.

## Decisions made while building it

All made **before seeing any results** unless marked otherwise.

- **Test 1 starts 2006-02-01, not 2005.** The old export holds only about 320 stocks and a single fund (QQQ) until 2005-02-25, and twins need 252 days of history. So the first rebalance with a full 500-stock universe, more than one distinct twin and SPY betas is 2006-02-01. "2005–2019" is in practice 2006-02..2019-12.
- **Calendar:** the portfolio rebalances on `c2eval.twins`' own calendar (export days 252, 273, …). A stock's twin, its β and the universe stay fixed through each 21-day hold. Test 3 runs from the first rebalance of 2020 to the last whose hold, one day late included, ends inside the old export (formed 2025-05-14).
- **Universe order:** price ≥ $5, not fund-like and has a twin are applied first; the 500 with the highest 63-day median dollar volume are then taken, so the universe is always 500. How many of the plain 500 most traded lack a twin or are fund-like is reported separately.
- **Ranked set:** each month all strategies rank the same stocks: universe members whose twin signal, plain signal and market-residual signal are all finite at the close. Each leg is round(10% × n), normally 50.
- **Returns:** buy-and-hold from close d to close d+21, equal weight at formation, from forward-filled closes. A long stock carries −β × its twin, a short one +β × its twin, also bought and held. A fund's daily log move beyond ±50% counts as zero. The monthly long-short is the mean hedged long leg minus the mean hedged short leg ($1 of stock long, $1 short).
- **Costs:** turnover at each rebalance against the previous month's positions as they drifted. Fund positions are netted across stocks (hundreds of stocks share IWM, SPY or XLF). Stocks pay 5 bps per side, funds 1 bp. The first month pays for building the book.
- **Baseline (b):** β to SPY is estimated the way `c2eval.twins` estimates the twin's β (trailing 252 days, same calendar). The 21-day market residual uses log returns, like the twin residual. As the plan writes them, both baselines are unscaled. Diagnostics, not used for pass/fail: a scaled market residual, an unscaled twin residual, and the twin signal hedged with SPY.
- **t-statistics:** plain t of the non-overlapping monthly returns. Test 2 uses the paired gross differences over Test 1's period; net differences are reported too.
- **One-day-late check:** signal at close d, positions entered at close d+1 and held to d+22, with the twin chosen at d. Test 3 requires it positive over 2020–2025-06; it is also reported for 2006–2019. "Same sign" in Test 3 means the plan's direction: positive, so reversal pays.
- **News proxy:** a stock "had news" if its largest daily share volume in the signal window (d−20..d) is at least 3× its median over the 63 days before the window. Each month's legs are split by this flag.
- **Earnings-season months:** at least half of the signal window's days fall in Jan 15–Feb 15, Apr 15–May 15, Jul 15–Aug 15 or Oct 15–Nov 15.
- **Money-test scores:** s on every day, for the universe of the latest rebalance at or before that day; NaN elsewhere and before 2006-02-01.
- **Unseen months:** the same calendar on the new export (it also starts 2001-12-31, so the day indices match). The holds counted are those formed on or after 2025-06-27 that end by 2026-10-19: 15 holds, formed 2025-07-16..2026-09-16. The calendar's first formation after 2025-06-27 is 2025-07-16, so the plan's portfolio covers 2025-07-16..2026-10-15, while the judge covers the whole 2025-06-30..2026-10-19. The book starts in 2025-01 so that the first unseen month pays real turnover. The twins for the new export are computed with `cache=False`, because c2eval's twin cache would write outside this experiment's folder.
- **A data gap (found while checking universe sizes, before any returns were computed):**
  - On 2011-02-17 the export has closes for 571 of about 2,530 stocks and 67 of about 690 funds.
  - Each 21-day window spanning it has two missing returns for almost every stock, so `c2eval.twin_residual` (at most one missing return) returned NaN for the whole 2011-03-04 month.
  - The fix: on days where fewer than half as many series have a close as the median of the previous 10 days, missing closes are forward-filled from the previous close, in both the stocks' and the funds' closes. This uses only past data. Only 2011-02-17 qualifies after 2005.
  - The filled closes are passed to `c2eval.twin_residual`; the twins themselves are c2eval's, unchanged.
- **After seeing Tests 1–3 (no effect on any verdict):** `tests.py` first took Test 3's "same sign" from Test 1's realized mean, which was negative. It was changed to the plan's direction (positive), as recorded above. Test 3 fails either way.
- **After seeing the unseen-months portfolio (no rule changed):**
  - `c2eval.change_over_random` raises `KeyError: 'costs'` on the judge's single-holdout JSON, which keeps its cost levels under `report.strategy`.
  - `unseen.py` reads them there instead, and was run a second time to write its files. The portfolio numbers are deterministic and came out identical.
  - `c2eval.py` was fixed for this by someone else at 09:16 the same morning, while the run was in progress. The fix reads the same fields, so the figures are the same.
