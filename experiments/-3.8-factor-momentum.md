# Statistical-factor momentum: do the market's hidden factors trend?

**TL;DR:** Factor momentum is published: factors that did well over the past year tend to keep doing well (Gupta & Kelly 2019; Ehsani & Linnainmaa 2022), and it is concentrated in the factors that explain the most variance. Those studies use named factors such as value, size and profitability. What's new here is that the factors come straight from the correlation matrix. They are principal components 2–21 of the daily returns of the 500 most-traded stocks, each traded as an eigenportfolio (Avellaneda & Lee 2010). Each month the book went long a component if it rose over the past year (skipping the last month) and short if it fell, scaled each to 10% volatility, and held the book 21 days. Over 2006–2019 (167 holds) it made +0.17% a month before costs (t +1.16) and +0.07% after 5 bps costs (t +0.49). The pass mark was t ≥ 2.5, so it fails Test 1. Against plain 12-1 momentum and the twin funds' momentum together, its net alpha was +0.16% a month (t +1.60, where Test 2 needed 2; +2.48 before costs), so it fails Test 2 too. It behaves largely like plain momentum at a fifth of the volatility: correlation 0.73, monthly sd 1.9% against 8.8%. Unlike the published result, the components that explain the most variance (2–5, mostly sectors) contributed nothing (+0.02% a month). What little return there was came from components 12–21. These are the least stable from month to month: their best match with the previous month's components has |cos| 0.56–0.69, against 0.88–0.95 for components 2–5. Only one of the 20 components was significant on its own, about what chance gives. The book has no market beta ex ante (+0.00), but half of its weight changes each month, costing 10 bps a month. Its realized beta to SPY was −0.16, mostly from the 2008–09 momentum crash (−0.05 without those years). The sign held later: +0.15% a month over 2020..2025-05 (t +0.45), and +1.09% a month (t +2.26) in the 15 unseen holds (2025-07..2026-10), a stretch matched by only one 15-hold window in 2006–2025. In the money test, the judge's long-only account of the 20 largest positive weights made 2.0 points a year less than uniform random accounts (standard error 3.0, ahead in 7 of 18 years) and 0.4 less than volatility-matched ones. Plain momentum on the same universe made +4.2 points. In the unseen months that account made +43.3%, against +18.8% for the median random account (p = 0.058). Verdict: no edge. It fails Test 1 and Test 2.

## Nearest prior work

| Work | What it found |
|---|---|
| Gupta & Kelly 2019; Ehsani & Linnainmaa 2022, *JF* | Factor momentum in named factors; concentrated in their top principal components; it may explain stock momentum |
| Avellaneda & Lee 2010, *QF* | Eigenportfolios of stock returns as factors, for residual mean reversion |
| Moskowitz & Grinblatt 1999, *JF* | Industry momentum |
| This project, `+2.1-momentum-12-1.md` and `-0.1-twin-momentum.md` | Plain momentum matches SPY after costs, and earns nothing among the 500 largest since 2006 |

**What's new:** factor momentum on statistical factors drawn from the correlation matrix itself. New for this data; close to published work.

**Novelty check (2026-10-21, a separate search; it changed no rules):** partly done, momentum in principal components of factor returns is published; momentum in eigenportfolios of individual stock returns was not found.
- **Ehsani & Linnainmaa 2022** (JF, "Factor momentum and the momentum factor"): Most factors are positively autocorrelated, 51 bps a month after a positive year against 6 bps after a negative one (published abstract); momentum in the high-eigenvalue principal components of factor returns subsumes most forms of stock momentum. The components are of named factors, not of stocks.
- **Arnott, Kalesnik & Linnainmaa 2023** (RFS, "Factor momentum"): Cross-sectional factor momentum subsumes industry momentum, not the reverse, and concentrates in the first few highest-eigenvalue factors (51 factors, 1963–2016 in the working version). Byline unverified: IDEAS lists Tarun Ramadorai as a fourth author; a CV lists three.
- **Falck, Rej & Thesmar 2020** (arXiv 2009.04824, "Is factor momentum more than stock momentum?"): On 72 factors, factor momentum replicates, but after controlling for stock momentum and factor exposure its Sharpe ratio is significant only in versions that include the last month of returns. The plan's primary signal (12-1) leaves that month out.
- **Fan, Li, Liao & Liu 2022** (Financial Review, "A reexamination of factor momentum: how strong is it?"): In 22-factor and 187-factor samples, only about 22–27% of factors show strong continuation, and factor momentum does not beat the matching long-only factor holdings. That suggests much of factor momentum is holding factors with positive premia, which components 2–21 of a correlation matrix don't have.
- **Not found:** momentum tested on principal-component eigenportfolios of individual stock returns, with the market component dropped, against plain and industry momentum.

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Universe:**
- the 500 most-traded stocks of the judge-like liquid set (median dollar volume over 63 days), minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)`;
- refreshed every 21 trading days on the fixed calendar.

**Components at formation t:**
- daily log returns over t−251..t (export closes); a stock needs at least 90% of days;
- each stock's returns standardized by its own mean and sd, with missing returns then set to 0;
- the correlation matrix, and its eigenvectors in order of eigenvalue;
- components 2–21 are the 20 factors. **Component 1, the market, is left out** (fixed now).

**Eigenportfolio k:** weights `v_ki / σ_i`, scaled to gross 1 (sum of |w| = 1).

**Past return:**
- eigenportfolio k's return with today's weights over t−251..t−21 (12-1);
- the 1-month version (t−20..t) is reported.

**Book:**
- For each factor k, the sign of its past return.
- Each factor is scaled to 10% annualized volatility, from its own daily returns over the window.
- Total weights = Σ_k sign_k × scale_k × w_k, then the whole book is scaled to $2 gross.
- Held 21 days with fixed weights, close t → close t+21.
- Costs: 5 bps per side on turnover. The flip of an eigenvector's sign changes nothing, because the position flips with it.

**Baselines** (same universe and calendar):
- **(a) plain 12-1 momentum:** decile long-short;
- **(b) twin-group momentum:** each stock gets its twin fund's 12-1 return; decile long-short.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **2006–2019:** the book's mean monthly net return. **Pass:** t ≥ 2.5.
2. **Beyond both baselines:** the book's monthly returns regressed on both baselines' returns. The intercept must be positive with t ≥ 2.
3. **2020–2025-06:** same sign. **The 16 unseen months** (new export, once, at the end): same sign.

**Reported:** the book's market beta, turnover, and how stable the components are from one formation to the next.

**Money test (the judge):**
- Score = the book's weight on each stock at formation; positive weights are the long side. The score holds every day until the next formation. Graded with `rebalance=21`.
- Uniform and volatility-matched random accounts are both reported.

## The number for the file name

The judge's walk-forward change over random, with uniform random accounts, 0 bps (`c2eval.change_over_random`).

## Ways it can fool us

- **Unstable components:** eigenvectors change from month to month, which shows up as turnover and as factors that don't persist. Both are reported.
- **Sector bets:** components 2–5 are mostly sectors, so the book can be industry momentum in disguise. That is baseline (b).
- **Market beta:** dropping component 1 doesn't guarantee a market-neutral book. Its beta is reported.

## Results (run 2026-10-21)

All returns are actual returns, from export closes. The book is $2 gross at formation (about $1 long and $1 short); each baseline is $1 long and $1 short. "% a month" is per 21-trading-day hold on the twins' fixed calendar, labeled by its formation date. Sharpe ratios are annualized (12 holds a year). t-statistics are plain t of the non-overlapping holds.

**The book in plain words.** Each month, the stocks' past year of daily moves is split into the market (component 1, left out) and 20 further independent patterns of co-movement, from the biggest (component 2) to the 21st. Each pattern is a long-short portfolio. The book goes long a pattern if it made money from a year ago to a month ago, and short it if it lost. Each pattern gets the same expected volatility, so the small patterns get as much risk as the big ones. The stock weights of all 20 positions are added up and scaled to $2.

### Verdict

**No edge.**
- **Test 1 fails:** the book made +0.07% a month after costs over 2006–2019 (t +0.49, against 2.5).
- **Test 2 fails:** what it earned beyond plain and twin-group momentum is +0.16% a month (t +1.60, against 2).
- **What the book is:** a low-volatility relative of plain momentum, with half its weight changing each month.
  - The factors that explain the most variance carry none of its return.
  - The small, unstable ones carry a little, not significantly.
- **Later periods:** the sign held in 2020–2025 and strongly in the unseen months, but those come after the failure.
- **Money test:** the judge's account trails random accounts by 2.0 points a year, where plain momentum on the same stocks gains 4.2.

## Decisions made while building it

All made **before seeing any results**, unless marked otherwise. "Results" means any hold return, book or baseline return, or judge output. They were written to `research/factor_momentum/out/decisions_draft.txt` after the coverage and code checks and before `tests.py` first ran. No rule was changed after results were seen.

- **The correlation matrix** is the cosine of each stock's return vector, which `c2eval` also uses:
  - returns standardized by the stock's own mean and sd (ddof 0) over its finite days, missing days set to 0, each row scaled to unit length;
  - so the diagonal is exactly 1 and the eigenvalues sum to the number of stocks.
  - Inputs are log returns of export closes, with no bad-print filter.
  - The plan's wording would also allow ZᵀZ/(n−1), whose diagonal is slightly below 1. The two differ only in scale and in how missing days count.
- **Eigenvectors** come from `numpy.linalg.eigh`, reordered by descending eigenvalue; components 2–21 are kept. σ_i in `v_ki / σ_i` is the same daily log-return sd used to standardize.
- **Factor returns:**
  - **In sample (t−251..t),** w_k · the stocks' simple daily returns, with a missing return counted as 0. This is a daily-rebalanced portfolio at fixed weights.
  - The 12-1 past return is the sum of those over t−251..t−21 (231 days). The 1-month return is the sum over t−20..t. The volatility is their sd over all 252 days × √252.
  - **Out of sample,** the hold is buy-and-hold from close t to close t+21: Σ_i W_i (P_i(t+21)/P_i(t) − 1).
  - The two definitions differ (daily-rebalanced against buy-and-hold). That is immaterial for a sign and a volatility scale.
- **A stock that leaves the export or has no close during the hold keeps its last close:** zero return from there on, using forward-filled closes. Every universe stock has a close at or before the formation.
- **One market-wide data gap,** 2011-02-17, is forward-filled from the previous close (past data only), as in twin momentum.
- **Costs:**
  - 5 bps × Σ |new weight − old weight as it drifted over the hold|, at each formation.
  - The first formation pays for building the book. Baselines are costed the same way.
  - The book and both baselines run from the first formation with a universe (2004-12-31), so 2006's first hold pays real turnover, not the build.
- **Universe and Test 1's period** (decided after counting the universe, before any returns):
  - The judge-like set needs 756 days of history, so it holds about 300 stocks until 2007-11 and 500 from 2007-12-03.
  - Test 1 keeps the plan's 2006–2019: formations dated 2006-01-01..2019-12-31, the first being 2006-02-01.
  - Before 2007-12 the book uses the whole set of about 300, which is under 500. The 500-stock part is reported beside it.
  - This is the loners experiment's choice.
- **Baseline (a):** ln(close[t−21] / close[t−252]), as `stockai/stockai/baselines/momentum.py` computes it.
  - The function was copied rather than imported; `checks.py` confirms it matches exactly.
  - Decile long-short, round(10% × n) stocks a leg, equal weight, over the universe stocks with a finite score.
- **Baseline (b):**
  - Each universe stock's twin at the formation (`c2eval.twins` fund_row at that rebalance).
  - The twin's 12-1 return is the sum of the fund's daily store-close log returns over t−251..t−21. A move beyond ±50% (c2eval's bad-print rule) or a missing day counts as 0.
  - Stocks without a twin are dropped. Every stock had one: twin coverage was 99–100%.
  - Many stocks share a twin, so groups of tied values straddle the decile edge. Such a group shares the remaining slots equally (fractional weights), so ties never depend on row order.
  - (b) counts at a formation only if ≥ 80% of the universe has a twin and there are ≥ 10 distinct twins. That holds from 2006-02-01; in 2005 every twin was QQQ. This rule was fixed from coverage alone.
- **Test 1** is the plain t of the net hold returns.
- **Test 2:**
  - Uses Test 1's 167 holds, with the book's **net** returns regressed on both baselines' net returns.
  - Pass/fail uses the plain OLS t. Newey-West (3 lags) and the gross version are reported as checks.
- **Test 3's "same sign"** means the plan's direction (positive: factor momentum pays), using the net mean, as in twin momentum.
  - The old part runs from the first 2020 formation to the last whose hold ends inside the old export (formed 2025-05-14; 65 holds).
  - The unseen part is the holds on the new export formed on or after 2025-06-27 that end by 2026-10-19: 15 holds, formed 2025-07-16..2026-09-16. The plan's "16 unseen months" are 15 complete 21-day holds here.
  - On the new export the book runs continuously from 2004-12-31, so the first unseen hold pays real turnover. The new export starts on the same day, and its day indices were checked to match the old export's.
- **Stability:**
  - Eigenvectors (unit length, in the correlation matrix's space) of consecutive formations are aligned on the union of their stocks, with 0 for absent stocks.
  - Each component is compared with the previous formation's top 50.
  - How often a component is held long is **not** reported: an eigenvector's sign is arbitrary, so only sign × return means anything.
- **Beta:**
  - Realized beta is the OLS slope of the book's monthly gross return on SPY's buy-and-hold return over the same hold (store closes, forward-filled).
  - Ex-ante beta is Σ_i W_i β_i, with β_i from simple daily returns over t−251..t (days where both the stock and SPY have a return).
- **Judge scores:**
  - W_j at formation t, on days t through the day before the next formation. NaN for stocks outside the book and before 2004-12-31.
  - The weights are raw, not ranks.
  - Plain 12-1 momentum, graded for reference, is the daily signal for the universe of the latest formation, as in twin momentum.
- **The judge wiring** was checked first: 5-day reversal through this experiment's path gives +11.8 points a year, 8 of 18 years beating random at p < 0.1, as it should.

## Files

**Code,** in `research/factor_momentum/`. Each script runs with `systemd-run --user --scope -q -p MemoryMax=8G -p MemorySwapMax=0 -- env OMP_NUM_THREADS=4 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=4 MKL_NUM_THREADS=4 RAYON_NUM_THREADS=4 python3 -I -B <script>`. Each takes under 2 minutes:
- `common.py`: the universe (copied from night and day), the gap fill, the eigen-decomposition and the book at one formation, the baselines' signals, decile weights with tie sharing, buy-and-hold holds with costs, and the statistics.
- `books.py`: runs the book, the 1-month book and both baselines over consecutive formations. It records per-component contributions, eigenvalue shares, stability and ex-ante beta, and saves each formation's weights.
- `checks.py old`: coverage and code checks (no returns) → `out/checks_old.txt`, `out/coverage_old.tsv`.
- `tests.py`: Tests 1–3 on the old export and everything reported with them. Writes:
  - `out/tests_old.txt`, `out/tests_old.json`;
  - `out/monthly_old.tsv`: every hold, with the book's gross, net, costs, turnover, exposures and ex-ante beta, SPY, the 1-month book and both baselines;
  - `out/components_old.tsv`: every formation × component, with the variance share, past returns, volatility, scale, contribution, signed return and stability;
  - `out/eigen_old.tsv`: every formation's top-21 eigenvalue shares.
- `money.py old` / `money.py new`: the scores and the judge for the book and plain momentum, uniform and volatility-matched. Writes `out/money_old.txt`, `out/money_old.json`, `out/money_new.txt`, `out/money_new.json`.
- `unseen.py`: the unseen months on the new export → `out/unseen.txt`, `out/unseen.json`.
- `sanity_reversal5.py`: the judge-wiring check → `out/sanity_reversal5.txt`.
- `out/decisions_draft.txt`: the decisions as written before the first results.

**Large files,** in `~/.local/share/correlation2/experiments/factor_momentum/`:
- the scores `book_old.npy`, `book_new.npy`, `plain_old.npy` and `plain_new.npy`, with the judge's JSON next to each;
- `book_old.npz` and `book_new.npz`: each formation's stock rows and weights;
- the cached universes `universe_old_*.npz` and `universe_new_*.npz`;
