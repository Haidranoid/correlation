# Announcement-day beta: are stocks that move with the market paid only on news days?

**TL;DR:** Do stocks that move most with the market earn their extra return only on scheduled news days (Fed decisions, inflation and jobs reports), as Savor & Wilson found? In 2006–2019, yes (t 3.09), mostly in the 2008–09 crisis. But trading it netted only +9.5 basis points a news day after costs (t 1.02), the effect has been gone since 2020, and a 20-stock account trailed SPY by 0.6 points a year after costs. Verdict: no edge.

## Nearest prior work

| Work | What it found |
|---|---|
| Savor & Wilson 2013, *JFQA* | The market's excess return is concentrated on inflation, jobs and FOMC announcement days |
| Savor & Wilson 2014, *JFE*, "Asset pricing: a tale of two days" | Beta is priced on announcement days and not on other days |
| Lucca & Moench 2015, *JF* | Large S&P 500 returns in the 24 hours before scheduled FOMC announcements, 1994–2011 |
| This project, `-7.7-volume-clock.md` and `-5.5-betting-against-correlation.md` | The earnings announcement premium is gone here; low-beta picks trail in rising markets |

**What's new:** for this project, macro-calendar timing of co-movement exposure. A published effect, new to this data.

**Novelty check (2026-10-21, a separate search; it changed no rules):** done, holding high beta on announcement days and low beta otherwise is published as a daily strategy; the effect is reproduced through 2023.
- **Cui, Zhao & Cui 2023** (SAGE Open, "Macroeconomic announcements: how announcements shape trading strategies"): A "betting on and against beta" strategy bets on beta on inflation, unemployment and rate-decision days and against beta on other days; over 1964–2021 in US stocks it has higher average daily excess returns than Frazzini-Pedersen BAB, significant against value, size and momentum (no return figures in the abstract). This is the plan's money-test score.
- **Chen & Jiang 2025** (Financial Review, "High-beta stock valuation around macroeconomic announcements"): High-beta returns are negative the day before a scheduled announcement, positive on the day, and negative the day after; combining betting against beta and betting on beta over the three-day window earns 25.28% a year (sample period unverified).
- **Ai, Bansal & Guo 2023** (NBER w31923, "Macroeconomic announcement premium"): Reproduces Savor & Wilson on beta-sorted deciles over 1961–2023, with FOMC, payrolls, GDP, ISM and CPI/PPI days: the announcement-day security market line slope is 6.81 bps. The announcement premium averaged 16.33 bps per announcement from January 2020 to August 2023, above the full-sample 10.68 bps.
- **Kurov, Wolfe & Gilbert 2021** (Finance Research Letters, "The disappearing pre-FOMC announcement drift"): Extending Lucca & Moench to December 2019, the pre-FOMC drift essentially disappeared after 2015, with or without press conferences; Ai, Bansal & Guo cite the FOMC premium as low from 2016 to 2019.
- **Ernst, Gilbert & Hrdlicka 2021** (working paper, "More than 100% of the equity premium"): Once all announcements of each type are counted and day-of-month effects are controlled, announcements account for about half the equity premium, not more than all of it, and Sharpe ratios are similar on announcement and other days.
- **Not found:** a separate post-2014 test of the beta slope on CPI and jobs days alone, apart from FOMC days.

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Announcement days A:**
- scheduled FOMC statement days, with unscheduled moves dropped;
- CPI release days;
- Employment Situation release days;
- 2006 to 2026-10, trading days only.

All other trading days are N.

**Beta:** each stock's beta to SPY over t−251..t−1, from daily log returns, with at least 227 days. It is known at the close before the day it is used.

**Universe:** the judge-like liquid set, minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)`.

**Slopes:** on each trading day d, a cross-sectional OLS of the stocks' close-to-close log returns on their beta as of d−1, giving slope_d.

**Strategy:**
- On each A day, long the top beta tenth and short the bottom tenth, bought at the close before and sold at the A day's close.
- Flat on N days.
- Costs: 5 bps per side.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **2006–2019:**
   - the mean slope on A days minus the mean on N days is above 0 with t ≥ 3 (Welch);
   - **and** the A-day strategy's net mean is above 0 with t ≥ 2.
2. **Each kind alone** (FOMC, CPI, jobs): the mean A-day slope is positive.
3. **2020–2025-06:** the A − N slope difference has the same sign. **The 16 unseen months** (new export, once, at the end): same sign.

**Money test (the judge):**
- Graded with `rebalance=1`.
- Score each day t = the beta percentile if t+1 is an A day, else 1 − the beta percentile. That is, high beta into announcements and low beta otherwise.
- Uniform and volatility-matched random accounts are both reported.

## The number for the file name

The judge's walk-forward change over random, with uniform random accounts, 0 bps (`c2eval.change_over_random`).

## Ways it can fool us

- **Few days:** about 40 a year.
- **The slope is mostly the market's own move:** it is the market's announcement-day premium times the beta spread. That's why the test is the A − N difference.
- **Crash days in 2008 and 2020:** the result is reported with and without them.

### What it means

- **The textbook pattern is in this data in 2006–2019, but it is the market's pattern.**
  - SPY averaged +12.9 bps on announcement days against +1.8 bps on other days, and high-beta stocks earned that premium times their beta.
  - The year-by-year slope difference rises and falls with SPY's own announcement-day difference (correlation 0.75).
  - 2008 and 2009 contribute most. With the crash days out, t drops to 2.95, just under the plan's bar.
- **It is too small to trade daily.**
  - The long-short makes +29.5 bps gross per announcement day, about 31 days a year.
  - A round trip on both legs costs 20 bps, which leaves +9.5 bps (t = 1.02).
- **Since 2020 the market no longer earns more on announcement days** (+3.2 bps against +5.6), and the beta slope difference has gone with it.
  - The unseen months reverse it: SPY fell on announcement days (−19.2 bps) while rising on the others.
  - CPI days turned negative for beta in 2020–2025 (−34.3 bps, t = −1.48), the years of the inflation scare.
- **FOMC days carry most of the effect** (post hoc, chosen after seeing the tables):
  - the slope was +38.5 bps in 2006–2019 and +59.3 in 2020–2025-06, and the long-short made +54.8 and +104.4 bps gross on those days;
  - but the 10 FOMC days in the unseen months gave −3.4 and −15.9;
  - it is one kind of three, picked after the fact, and not tested.
- **The judge's +7.7 is mostly one year.**
  - In 2008–09, announcement days were huge up days, and the account held the 20 highest-beta stocks on them.
  - Against volatility-matched random accounts the number is +0.6.

## Decisions made while building it

All made before any slope, strategy return or judge result was computed, unless marked otherwise.

- **Scheduled FOMC days** come from the Fed's meeting calendars, not from the tsv's FOMC list:
  - each scheduled meeting heading ("… Meeting - YYYY", not "Conference Call", "(unscheduled)" or "(cancelled)") on `fomchistorical2006..2020.htm`, and each meeting row with a statement on `fomccalendars.htm` (2021+);
  - the statement day is the meeting's last day, which matches its statement link in every case but June 2007;
  - "July 31-August 1" is read as August 1;
  - phantom portfolios' cached pages were reused read-only.
  - The tsv's other FOMC days are dropped, and the two scheduled statements it missed are added (listed above).
- **Jobs days:**
  - the links `empsit_MMDDYYYY` on BLS's archive page, with HTML comments stripped first, since the page carries commented-out placeholders for 2026-11-06 and 2026-12-04;
  - the shutdown-delayed releases keep their actual dates;
  - October 2025 had no separate jobs report and no CPI, so 2025 has 11 of each.
- **CPI days:** the tsv's, plus 2026-10-14, the one release between its archive snapshot (2026-10-02) and 2026-10-19.
- **Downloads:**
  - five pages, all through the Wayback Machine, into a new empty folder: the jobs archive, the current CPI and jobs releases, and the two release schedules;
  - fetched with curl and a plain browser-like User-Agent, no email address sent;
  - read with `python3 -I`.
- **Non-trading days:** a release on a non-trading day is dropped, not moved. These are nine Good Friday releases, seven jobs reports and two CPI. "Trading days only" was read as the stricter rule.
- **A day with two releases** is one A day and counts in each kind's row.
- **Beta:**
  - read literally: return days d−251..d−1 (251 returns) for the beta used on day d, with at least 227 days where both returns exist;
  - stock returns are log returns of export closes, SPY's of store closes, and |log return| > 0.5 counts as a bad print (missing);
  - SPY in the store starts 2005-02-25, so the first beta is on 2006-01-23, and 2006 starts three weeks late.
  - The rolling sums were checked against direct regressions on five random stock-days per export.
- **Universe:** copied from `research/night_and_day/common.py` (without the 500 largest; the plan has none). Day d uses the latest rebalance at or before d−1.
- **Slopes:**
  - OLS with an intercept on the universe stocks with a beta and a return that day (|log return| ≤ 0.5);
  - a day needs at least 50 stocks.
- **Strategy:**
  - tenths are the lowest and highest ⌊10%⌋ of the ranked stocks, equal weight, in simple returns from export closes;
  - a ranked stock with no close on the A day counts as 0: 148 stock-days over 599 A days;
  - every A day pays a full round trip on both legs (4 × 5 bps = 20 bps), with no netting when A days are consecutive.
- **Test 2's period** isn't stated in the plan. It was judged on 2006–2019, like test 1, with the other periods reported.
- **Test 3:**
  - "same sign" means the sign of the 2006–2019 difference, on point estimates;
  - the unseen months are graded on all their trading days with a slope (2025-06-30..2026-10-19).
- **Crash days:**
  - defined before any slope was computed: the trading days in 2008 and 2020 on which SPY's close-to-close log return was 4% or more either way;
  - the tests are judged on all days, and "without" is reported alongside, as the plan says.
- **Judge score:**
  - percentile = (average rank + 0.5) / n among the universe stocks with a beta, so every universe stock has a positive score on both kinds of day;
  - the score at t uses the beta for t+1, built from returns through t, and the universe as of t;
  - the last export day gets no score.
- **The unseen months:** run once on the new export with its own twins, universe and store SPY, after the walk-forward tables were final.
- **After the results:**
  - the always-low-beta control and the FOMC-only reading were added post hoc and are labelled so;
  - no rule, period or threshold was changed.
