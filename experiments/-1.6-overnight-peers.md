# Overnight peers: do the stocks that share your news predict your next month?

**TL;DR:** Peers with economic links (customers, shared analysts) predict each other's returns a month later (Cohen & Frazzini 2008; Ali & Hirshleifer 2020). Peers picked by same-day correlation don't: in this project they came out slightly reversed (`-5.2-peer-momentum.md`), probably because such peers mostly share price pressure that reverts (Chen et al. 2019). What's new here is picking each stock's peers from overnight co-movement alone, since company news mostly arrives outside trading hours. The overnight peers really are different stocks. They share about 6 of 20 names with whole-day peers (mean Jaccard 0.17), while intraday peers share about 13 (0.46). Apple's overnight peers are its suppliers (LITE, CRUS, SWKS), and its intraday peers are the other megacaps. But the overnight peers' past month doesn't predict the stock's next month. Over 2009–2019 the overnight-peer book (long the top tenth of the residual signal, short the bottom tenth, rebalanced monthly, 5 bps a side) lost 0.31% a month net (t −0.87); the plan needs t ≥ 2.5. It beat the whole-day-peer book by 0.18% a month (t 0.96; the plan needs 2) and lost again in 2020–2025-06 (−0.36% a month, t −0.45). All three peer types came out slightly reversed, even after the stock's own reversal was removed. The judge gives −1.4 points a year against uniform random accounts (se 4.4, ahead in 7 of 17 years) and −3.7 against volatility-matched ones. In the 16 unseen months the book made +0.82% a month (t 0.62, 15 months). The judge's picks made +37.5%, against +19.5% for the median random account (p 0.12), which is +18.0 points from one noisy period. Verdict: no edge. It fails Test 1, the first test, and Tests 2 and 3 too.

## Nearest prior work

| Work | What it found |
|---|---|
| Cohen & Frazzini 2008; Ali & Hirshleifer 2020 | Customer and shared-analyst peers predict a stock's next month |
| Chen, Chen, Chen & Li 2019, *Management Science* | A correlation-peer signal is mostly own-stock reversal plus one-month industry momentum |
| Lou, Polk & Skouras 2019, *JFE* | Overnight and intraday returns have different clienteles |
| Wang 2025/26, *JFQA* | Analyst-linked peers' overnight moves carry overnight, then reverse intraday |
| This project, `-5.2-peer-momentum.md` | Whole-day co-movement peers: slightly reversed, z −2.79 |

**What's new:** choosing peers by overnight co-movement. New outright, as far as the earlier survey found.

**Novelty check (2026-10-21, a separate search; it changed no rules):** partly done, overnight-versus-daytime networks across stocks exist; peers chosen by overnight correlation for a monthly signal were not found.
- **Wang 2025/26** (JFQA forthcoming, "Decoding momentum spillover effects"): Peers' overnight returns predict the focal stock's next overnight return, which then fully reverses intraday, while peers' intraday returns carry into intraday returns. The author attributes the overnight part to individual investors distorting opening prices, which cuts against the plan's premise that overnight co-movement means shared news.
- **Lu, Zhang, Reinert & Cucuringu 2025** (SSRN 5371952, "A tug of war across the market: overnight-vs-daytime lead-lag networks"): Builds directed lead-lag networks separately from overnight and daytime returns, clusters stocks into leaders and laggers, and reports significant alphas beyond firm-level tug-of-war reversal over 2000–2024. Taken from a search summary of the SSRN abstract only; costs and horizon unverified.
- **Kumar & Lee 2006** (JF, retail investor sentiment and return comovements): Retail trades are correlated and explain comovement in stocks with heavy retail ownership that are costly to arbitrage, not macro news or forecast revisions. Berkman et al. (2012) find retail demand concentrates at the next open, so overnight co-movement may carry shared flows as well as shared news.
- **Not found:** peer sets chosen by overnight return correlation, and a test of whether such peers' past month predicts the next month better than whole-day correlation peers.

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Universe:**
- the judge-like liquid set, minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)`, on the twins calendar;
- from 2009-01, because a year of opens is needed.

**Returns:**
- Each day, three kinds, all from the store's opens and closes, so they add up:
  - overnight `log(O_d / C_{d−1})`;
  - intraday `log(C_d / O_d)`;
  - whole day `log(C_d / C_{d−1})`.
- Each is market-adjusted by subtracting that day's cross-sectional mean over the universe.
- Bad prints follow `research/night_and_day/`'s rule.

**Peers at each calendar day t:**
- Over d = t−251..t, each stock's 20 most-correlated other universe stocks on market-adjusted overnight returns.
- A peer needs at least 200 common days and a correlation of at least 0.10.
- A stock needs at least 5 such peers.
- "Intraday peers" and "whole-day peers" are chosen the same way from the other two kinds of return.
- Whole-day peers are the baseline: the same definition as peer momentum's 20 strongest same-day co-movers.

**Signal:**
- the peers' mean market-adjusted whole-day log return over t−20..t;
- then, across stocks at each t, the residual from an OLS of that on the stock's own t−20..t return. This removes own reversal.

**Book:**
- every 21 trading days (fixed calendar), long the top tenth and short the bottom tenth of the residual signal, equal weight;
- held close t → close t+21, in market-relative returns (export closes minus the export's market);
- 5 bps per side.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **2009–2019:** the overnight-peer book's mean monthly net return. **Pass:** t ≥ 2.5.
2. **Beats whole-day peers:** the overnight-peer book minus the whole-day-peer book (same rules), t ≥ 2. Intraday peers are reported.
3. **2020–2025-06:** same sign. **The 16 unseen months** (new export and store, once, at the end): same sign.

**Reported:** how much the overnight and whole-day peer sets overlap.

**Money test (the judge):**
- Score = the overnight-peer residual signal's percentile among universe stocks with a signal, every day, from the latest calendar day. Graded with `rebalance=21`.
- Uniform and volatility-matched random accounts are both reported.

## The number for the file name

The judge's walk-forward change over random, with uniform random accounts, 0 bps (`c2eval.change_over_random`).

## Ways it can fool us

- **Noisy overnight returns:** opens are noisier than closes, and overnight correlations are low. That's why the 0.10 floor and the 5-peer minimum are there.
- **Same peers anyway:** if overnight and whole-day peers are mostly the same stocks, the test can't separate news from flows. The overlap is reported.

## Results (run 2026-10-21)

**The setup in numbers:**
- **Universe:** a mean of 1,223 stocks on the 132 calendar days of 2009–2019, 1,993 in 2020–2025-06 and 2,316 in the unseen months.
- **Signals:** a median of 1,393 stocks a calendar day have one on the old export and 2,297 on the new.
- **Books:** each side holds the top or bottom tenth, about 122 names a side in 2009–2019, 199 in 2020–2025-06 and 230 in the unseen months.
- **Months:** 132 holding periods in 2009–2019 (books formed 2009-01-02..2019-12-06), 65 in 2020–2025-06 (2020-01-08..2025-05-14) and 15 in the unseen months (2025-07-16..2026-09-16).
- **Units:** books are in % a month, long mean minus short mean, simple returns. t is from the monthly series.
- **Costs:** about 87% of each side is replaced every month (turnover 3.47 of a possible 4.0), so 5 bps a side costs about 0.17% a month.

**Returns check:**
- Overnight + intraday equals whole-day by construction.
- Whole-day matches `log(C_d / C_{d−1})` from the export's closes to 5.8e-8, on 18.6 million stock-days (old export) and 19.0 million (new).
- The bad-print rule drops 1.1% of the export's daily returns from 2008 on, through the stale-print and ±50% tests. Night_and_day's data check found stale prints on 1.4% of all stock-days, so most of the drops are probably stale prints.
- After market adjustment the adjusted legs still add up to the adjusted whole day (to 6e-8, and 1.2e-7 on the new export).

**What it means:**
- Overnight co-movement does find different peers, and some look like real news links (Apple's suppliers). So the idea wasn't defeated by its own definition.
- But no kind of co-movement peer predicts next month here, once the stock's own month is held fixed. Either the economic-link momentum in the literature needs links that prices don't reveal, or it has faded since 2009.
- Overnight peers are about 0.2% a month better than whole-day peers in 2009–2019 and no better since. That is the right direction for "news versus flows", but it is far too small and too noisy to trade.

## Decisions made while building it

**Before any outcome was computed:**
- **Returns:** read from `research/night_and_day`'s legs (`on_<tag>.npy`, `intra_<tag>.npy`, read-only), so its bad-print rule applies. A stock-day is dropped from all three kinds when a price is missing or ≤ 0, when open = close = high = low, or when either leg is beyond ±50% (log).
  - Whole day = overnight + intraday on those stock-days, which equals `log(C_d / C_{d−1})` (checked above).
  - The store's close equals the export's close on every stock-day (night_and_day's build log).
- **Market adjustment:** each day's mean over the universe of the latest calendar day at or before that day, since the universe exists only on calendar days. The same for all three kinds, so the adjusted legs still add up.
- **Universe:** night_and_day's `meta_<tag>.npz` (the shared liquid-set rule minus `c2eval.excluded`, point in time on the twins calendar), read-only. Calendar days run from 2009-01-02, whose window starts on 2008-01-04.
- **Correlations:**
  - pairwise-complete Pearson over the 252 days, each stock's returns first standardized over its own valid days;
  - computed from float32 matrix products (counts, sums, sums of squares, cross-products), combined in float64;
  - checked against a direct float64 computation to 4e-7 on 2,700 random pairs. The top-20 choice matched a brute-force pick for 60 of 60 stocks.
  - Only each stock's top 20 peers are stored.
- **Peers:** only among the universe of calendar day t. A stock with fewer than 5 qualifying peers gets none. One with 5–19 keeps those.
- **Signal:**
  - 21-day sums of market-adjusted whole-day log returns over d−20..d, missing at most 1 day (`c2eval.twin_residual`'s convention);
  - the peer mean is over peers with a valid sum, and needs at least 5;
  - the stock's own return is the same sum for the stock;
  - the OLS has an intercept, over universe stocks with both values, and needs at least 50 of them;
  - the same code for all three peer types.
- **Book:**
  - deciles are round(10% × the stocks with a signal);
  - a member's return is its export close at t+21, or its last close in t+1..t+21 (6 overnight-book members in 16 years), over its close at t, minus the export market's simple return over t+1..t+21 (exp of its summed daily log returns, minus 1);
  - long mean minus short mean;
  - costs are 5 bps × Σ|w_new − w_old| over both sides, against the previous calendar day's book, and the first book pays its entries.
- **Periods** are by the first held day t+1, and a period needs t+21 inside its export.
  - The 2025-06-13 book straddles the end of the old export, so it is in no period. On the new export it serves only as the turnover reference for the first unseen book.
- **Test 2** uses 2009–2019, Test 1's period, and the paired monthly difference of net returns. **Test 3's "same sign"** means the overnight book's mean net return is positive, the sign Test 1 needs. Statistics are the plain t of the monthly series.
- **Money test:**
  - "Every day, from the latest calendar day" is read as `-4.3-loners.md` and `-5.5-betting-against-correlation.md` read it. Peers and universe come from the latest calendar day at or before d; the 21-day windows and the OLS are at d.
  - The judge's rebalance days follow each holdout year's start, not the twins calendar, so carrying the calendar-day signal forward would hand it signals up to 20 days old.
  - Percentile = average rank ÷ (n − 1) among stocks with a signal that day.
  - Scores start on 2009-01-02.
- **Point in time:** checked on three dates. With every return after d removed, the peers and the residual at d are unchanged (to 1e-8).
- **Extra outputs** were in the first run's code, as diagnostics, not tests: the raw-peer-mean books, the long and short sides against the universe, and the monthly rank IC.
- **Examples:** the date (the latest calendar day on or before 2019-12-31, which is 2019-12-06) and the stocks (AAPL, MSFT, JPM, XOM, KO, NVDA) were fixed in the build script before it ran.
- **The unseen months:** peers and signals rebuilt from night_and_day's new-export legs and universe, with calendar days from 2025-06-13.

**After the results:**
- The judge's 2008–09 mean without its empty first half (−3.5) is descriptive only.
