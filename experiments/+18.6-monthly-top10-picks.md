# Picking the month's biggest gainers (stockai-30d)

**TL;DR:** Can a model pick the 10 stocks that will rise most next month? Over 2010–2023 its picks beat SPY by +18.6 points a year after costs, and comparable stocks by 2.05 points a month (t 3.6), but the data only includes companies still listed in 2025. A stress test says the edge survives pessimistic delisting losses, thinly: worth a rerun on data with delisted stocks. Verdict: the one open lead.

### Notes

- **The model:** a LightGBM trained on each day's ranking of next-month returns, tested over 162 non-overlapping months (2010–2023), each year by a model trained only on earlier data.
- **Comparable stocks** have the same volatility and past-year fall as the pick.
- **The price data** contains only companies still listed in 2025.
- **Concentration:** half the extra return came in 5 of the 162 months.
- **Among the project's leads,** this is the only one that made money after costs, and the least trustworthy.

**Collapsed stocks:**
- **Share of picks:** 32.7% of the pick weight had fallen more than 50% over the past year.
- **What they earned:** stocks down more than 80% gained 8.0% a month on average in this data, the clearest sign of survivorship bias.
- **Reverse splits** let some sub-$5 stocks into the universe, which is the same bias again. NBR rose 294% in a month, TXMD 355%.

## What would settle it

The survivorship-free rerun on Sharadar data. Sharadar "Prices", full history, costs $39 for a month. The code is ready: delisted stocks are counted at their last price if bought out and as a total loss if they failed, and the $5 test uses prices as traded. The pass rule is fixed in advance: under that rule the picks must beat comparable stocks at t ≥ 2, the null must not, and the account must beat SPY at 20 bps. See `stockai/docs/sharadar.md` and `scripts/sharadar_runs.sh`.

## Survivorship stress test, without new data

**Why.** The Sharadar rerun costs $39. This test asks first, using only the data in hand, whether survivorship can plausibly explain the edge. If it can, the rerun probably isn't worth buying; if the edge survives pessimistic assumptions, it is. It is not proof either way.

### Rules (fixed 2026-10-21, before anything was computed)

**Part 1: how often stocks like these vanish.**
- **Universe:** the monthly test's own eligible stocks (stockai's rules, as its candidates were chosen) on 2025-06-27, in the older store (`stooq-2025-06-27`). If those rules can't be applied, use the judge-like liquid set, and say so.
- **Disappeared:**
  - no close after 2025-06-27 under the same ticker in the newer store (`stooq-2026-10-19`);
  - also a stock whose data in the newer store ends before 2026-10-01; its last price there is an observed delisting return.
- **A ticker change is not a disappearance:** a newer-store ticker whose closes over 2025-04-01..2025-06-27 match the old ticker's within 0.5% (a constant ratio) is the same company.
- **Hazard model:** a logistic regression of disappearing within the window on characteristics at 2025-06-27:
  - the prior 12-month return, plus a flag for down more than 50%;
  - 63-day volatility;
  - log price;
  - log median dollar volume over 63 days.
- A predicted window probability p becomes a monthly hazard h = 1 − (1 − p)^(1/m), with m = the window's trading days ÷ 21.
- **Raw rates, also reported:**
  - by prior 12-month return: below −80%, −80% to −50%, −50% to 0, above 0;
  - by price below $5.
- **Locked year:** these prices include stockai's locked year. They are read only to describe stocks for the hazard model; no strategy is graded on them.

**Part 2: the haircut.**
- **Picks:** the 162 months' top-10 picks, exactly as the file's account made them (the tie-break run its dollar figures come from).
- **Each pick i in month t:**
  - h_i comes from the hazard model applied to its characteristics at formation;
  - adjusted return = (1 − k·h_i)·r_i + k·h_i·L.
- **The grid:**
  - L ∈ {−30%, −55%, −100%}: the average delisting returns for performance delistings in Shumway 1997 (NYSE/AMEX) and Shumway & Warther 1999 (Nasdaq), and a total loss;
  - k ∈ {1, 2, 3}: the model's picks delist k times as often as similar stocks.
- **Benchmarks get the same treatment with k = 1,** each stock using its own h: the average eligible stock, and the comparable stocks the file already matches on.
- **Crisis sensitivity:** hazards doubled in 2008–2009, because the 2025–26 window was calm.
- **Reported for each (L, k):**
  - the adjusted extra over comparable stocks (with t);
  - the adjusted extra over the average stock;
  - the adjusted $10,000 account at 5 and 20 bps against SPY;
  - and, for each L, the break-even k at which the extra over comparable stocks reaches t = 2.

**Verdict, using this file's own pass rule (picks beat comparable stocks at t ≥ 2, and the account beats SPY at 20 bps):**

| Verdict | Condition |
|---|---|
| **Worth the Sharadar rerun** | The rule still passes at L = −55%, k = 2 |
| **Survivorship can explain it** | The rule fails at L = −30%, k = 1 |
| **Unresolved** | Anything between |

### Results (run 2026-10-21)

**Verdict: worth the Sharadar rerun.** The plan's pass rule needs both conditions: the picks beat comparable stocks at t ≥ 2, and the account beats SPY at 20 bps. It still holds at L = −55%, k = 2: +1.52 points a month over comparable stocks (t 2.62), and $81,239 against SPY's $65,715. It also holds in every cell with k = 1 and at L = −30% for every k. It fails at L = −55%, k = 3 and at L = −100%, k ≥ 2. In every failing cell except L = −100%, k = 3 the comparable-stock test still passes; the account is what fails. The margin is thin. The rule holds only up to k = 2.4 at L = −55% and k = 1.4 at L = −100%. If every hazard is doubled (a whole 2010–2023 as harsh as a crisis), the result drops to Unresolved. The plan's crisis rule, doubling hazards in 2008–2009, could not be applied as written, because no test month falls in those years; a pre-registered substitute is reported below.

**What the data in hand can say about disappearance.** Of the 2,264 candidates alive on 2025-06-27, 123 disappeared within 15 months: 5.4%, or 0.37% a month.
- **How they disappeared:**
  - 111 left the newer store entirely. Their exit dates can't be seen, so some may have left after 2026-10-01; counting them over 316 days slightly overstates the monthly hazard, which errs on the pessimistic side;
  - 6 had their ticker taken over by a different price series (BRKL, GOGL, LAZR, PINC, RNA, WOLF);
  - 6 ended early.
- **Renames:** 144 stocks had no close after 2025-06-27 under their own ticker. 33 of those were renames, and so are not disappearances. Counting PARA → PSKY, whose old ticker now belongs to another series, there were 34 renames in all.
- **Collapsed stocks** vanished more often only at the extreme: 3 of the 8 down more than 80%. Those down 50–80% vanished at 8.0%, against 4.6% for stocks that had risen.
- **Hazard model:** the past fall, the collapse flag and volatility show no detectable effect. Only low price (z −3.6) and low dollar volume (z −2.2) predict a disappearance, and the model is weak (AUC 0.67).
- **Hazards it gives the picks:** 0.54% a month, almost exactly that of their comparable stocks (0.53%) and above the average eligible stock's 0.40%. So at k = 1 the comparable-stock excess barely moves (+1.87 → +1.85). Everything hinges on k, which these data cannot measure.

**Observed delisting returns.** Only 6 stocks in the newer store simply end early, and none of them is a failure:
- from 2025-06-27 to their last close they returned +52% on average, and 4 of the 6 rose;
- their last month was flat, which looks like takeovers trading at a deal price;
- none had collapsed.

Failures leave no price in Stooq: they vanish with the ticker. So the data in hand holds no observed L for a failed stock, and the grid's L values (Shumway's −30% and −55%, or −100%) are assumptions, not estimates.

**What the haircut cannot see.** It charges only the month a pick delists. Survivor-only data also leaves out the months before a failure, when a stock headed for delisting is still trading and still pickable. This is the bias behind the +8.0% a month earned by stocks down more than 80%. That part is not in this test, so passing it is necessary but not sufficient.

**Crisis rule as written.** It doubles hazards in 2008–2009, but no test month falls there: the test runs 2010-06 to 2024-06. Read literally, it changes nothing. ### Decisions made while building it

**Made before any number was computed** (`research/survivorship_stress/decisions.md`):
- **Universe:** stockai's `judge_universe` on data through 2025-06-27 (2,286 candidates).
- **Same-ticker check:** a same-ticker series must also pass the price-match test, so a reused ticker counts as a disappearance. The plan's literal rule would have counted these 6 as survivors.
- **Rename candidates:** newer-store tickers that are absent from the older store.
- **Window:** m = trading days after 2025-06-27 and before 2026-10-01 (316), divided by 21.
- **Regressors:** standardized with the 2025 universe's mean and sd, and applied unchanged in 2010–2023. Missing values are set to the mean (6 such values among all eligible stock-months, none among picks). The model is a numpy IRLS logistic, which converged without the ridge fallback.
- **Picks:** seed 2's picks as the grader made them (top 10 positive scores) on the 162 graded days. None lacked a forward return.
- **Neighbours:** those of `metrics.comparable_excess`, kept fixed; only returns are adjusted, and k·h is capped at 1.
- **Account:** the grader's daily account, with each rebalance block's growth factor shifted by (1/10) Σ k·h·(L − r) over its picks. Here r is the pick's own return over the block, and h is scaled to the block's length. At k = 0 this reproduces $230,343 exactly.
- **SPY bar:** total return from pubdata, with the Stooq SPY also shown.
- **Crisis substitute:** months with SPY at least 20% below its 252-day high, plus the all-months bound.
- **Extra sensitivity:** the hazard model without log price, because 2010–2023 closes are split-adjusted as of 2025.

**Made after the first Part 1 run, before any Part 2 number:**
- **Universe needs a close on 2025-06-27.** 22 candidates (ALTR, AZPN, DFS, X, ITCI, JWN, …) had already stopped trading. They were counted as disappeared until this fix.
- **The price-match test tolerates adjustment steps.** The newer store's adjusted closes step at each 2025 dividend ex-date, so the level test failed 16 obvious survivors under their own tickers (UNH, HON, FDX, BDX, …). The test is now: at least 40 common days in 2025-04-01..2025-06-27, and at most 4 days when the new/old ratio moves more than 0.5%.
- **A price match needs a price that moves.** ESGR and FYBR were pinned at takeover prices, so they "matched" unrelated flat series (IPEX, NTRL). The rule now requires at least 20 days with a daily move above 0.5%; the real renames have 37 or more. The other 34 renames were confirmed independently: the history starts on the same first date in both stores, and at most 4 steps occur over a full year. 3 flat series (BAC_L, NEA, UITB) could not be tested, so they were kept under their own ticker.
