# Return seasonality: do stocks repeat their good calendar months?

**TL;DR:** This is a replication of a published effect; nothing in the method is new. Heston & Sadka (2008) found, in US stocks over 1965–2002, that a stock's return in a calendar month tends to repeat in the same month of later years, at annual lags up to 20 years. By the plan's account, which couldn't be checked against their paper, a stock's returns in the other months point the other way. Keloharju, Linnainmaa & Nyberg (2016) found such seasonalities in anomalies, commodities and country indexes. Their 20-year same-month strategy in US stocks made 1.19% a month (t 6.27) over 1963–2011, but only 0.40% (t 1.35) in their last subperiod, 2003–2011. Every month tested here is after Heston & Sadka's sample, and everything from 2012 on is after Keloharju et al.'s. Each month-end, the test bought the tenth of liquid stocks with the best average return in the coming calendar month over the past ten years and sold the worst tenth. By the plan's rule the test starts in 2007-05, the first month with 300 stocks holding a signal. Over 2007-05..2019-12 (152 months) the book lost 0.06% a month before costs (t −0.28) and 0.23% after them (t −1.05). Its FF5 + UMD alpha was −0.23% a month (t −1.02), so it fails test 1. The plan's bar was t ≥ 2. The signal changes completely every month, so the book turns over 3.4 of a possible 4 each month, which costs 0.17% a month at 5 bps a side. The 500 largest lost 0.14% a month (t −0.52), and 2020–2025-06 made +0.28% (t 0.58, with the dividend correction; +0.26% without it). In the 16 unseen months (2025-07..2026-10) the book lost 1.81% a month (t −1.60), with an alpha of −2.6% a month (t −2.15). Heston & Sadka's contrast doesn't show either. Sorting on the other months of the same years gave −0.34% a month (t −0.60) by the plan's definition and +0.09% (t 0.27) by Keloharju et al.'s, against their −0.96% (t −4.12). The month-by-month regressions show no annual-lag pattern: the mean slope at annual lags was +0.11 × 10⁻², t 0.44. The file-name number comes from the judge's 20-stock account, which made 1.9 points a year more than uniform random accounts. It was ahead in 10 of 18 years, with a standard error of 4.5, so not significant, and 2.5 points more than volatility-matched accounts. In the unseen months it made +7.6% against +19.4% for the median random account. Verdict: no edge; the published seasonality doesn't show in this data.

## Published claim

| Paper | Sample | What it reports |
|---|---|---|
| Heston & Sadka 2008, *JFE* | US, 1965–2002 | Same-calendar-month returns at annual lags predict that month's return, for up to 20 years |
| Keloharju, Linnainmaa & Nyberg 2016, *JF* | Many asset classes | Seasonalities are pervasive; they reflect seasonal exposure to priced factors |
| Blitz, Hanauer, Honarvar, Huisman & van Vliet 2023, *FAJ* | MSCI World, 1985–2021 | Seasonality is among five short-term signals that, combined, pay after costs |

**Our test period is after Heston & Sadka's sample.**

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Universe:** the judge-like liquid set, minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)`.

**Monthly returns:** calendar-month log returns from the export's closes (the last close of each month).

**Signal for calendar month m of year y:**
- the mean of the stock's returns in month m of years y−1 to y−10;
- at least 5 of the 10 must be present.

**Contrast, reported:** the mean of the stock's returns in all the other months of the same years.

**Start:**
- The test period begins at the first calendar month with at least 300 universe stocks holding a signal. The export is thin before 2005, so expect around 2010.
- Report that month. Test 1 runs from it to 2019-12.

**Book:**
- At each month-end, long the top tenth by signal and short the bottom tenth, equal weight, held through the next calendar month.
- Market-relative returns, 5 bps per side.

**Alphas:** every period's monthly long-short returns are regressed on French's FF5 + UMD factors.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **Start month..2019-12:** the long-short's mean monthly net return. **Pass:** t ≥ 2, with a positive FF5 + UMD alpha.
2. **The 500 largest:** same sign.
3. **2020–2025-06:** same sign. **The 16 unseen months** (new export, once, at the end): same sign.

**Money test (the judge):**
- **Score:** the signal's percentile, held constant within each calendar month (it changes only at month-ends). Graded with `rebalance=1`, so the picks change only when the month does.
- Uniform and volatility-matched random accounts are both reported.

## The number for the file name

The judge's walk-forward change over random, with uniform random accounts, 0 bps (`c2eval.change_over_random`), over the years it grades.

## Ways it can fool us

- **Earnings months:** much of seasonality is the earnings-announcement premium recurring in the same months. The volume clock found that premium gone here, so seasonality may be weaker too.
- **Survivor-only data:** a stock needs years of history to have a signal, which favours long-lived stocks.

## Results (run 2026-10-21)

**What the data allowed.** The old export starts on 2001-12-31, so the first same-month signals (5 of 10 years present) appear for holding month 2007-01.
- **Start month: 2007-05,** formed at the close of 2007-04-30, with 300 universe stocks holding a signal out of 300. This was recorded in `start.txt` before any book return was computed.
  - The months before had 299.
  - After the universe widened in 2008-01 (976 stocks), the stocks with a signal stayed at 282–301, still the ~300 long-lived names with history back to 2002, until the newer stocks reached five years of history.
  - From 2010-03 there are 851 or more. That is why the plan expected about 2010, and 2010-03..2019-12 is reported beside test 1.
- **The book:**
  - test 1: on average 973 stocks with a signal, 97 a leg (29 a leg before 2010-03, 116 after);

- **Plumbing:**
  - 5-day reversal through this folder's scores and judge path gives +11.8 points a year, beating random at p < 0.1 in 8 of 18 years, as expected.

  - One signal checked by hand from dates (A, March 2006–2015) matches the panel.
  - The same decile arithmetic on 1-month reversal gives +0.33% a month (t 0.86) over 2007–2019.

## Decisions made while building it

Made before seeing any results unless marked.

- **Start month:**
  - The rule was taken literally: 2007-05 is the first month with ≥ 300 universe stocks holding a signal, and test 1 starts there although the count falls to 282–299 for 2008-03..2010-02. The rule says "begins at the first month", not "stays above".
  - 2010-03..2019-12, from where the count stays ≥ 851, is reported as a sensitivity. This was decided after seeing the monthly counts and before any book return was computed.
- **Monthly returns:**
  - The signal's return for a month needs a close inside that month and inside the month before: the stock's last close in each.
  - The book's holding return runs from the forward-filled close on the formation day to the forward-filled close on the month's last export day, so a stock without a trade in the month earns 0.
  - Returns aren't winsorized in the signal. The lag-profile diagnostic winsorizes at 1% and 99% because single regressions are sensitive to outliers.
- **Universe at formation:** the judge-like set of the latest `c2eval.twins` calendar day at or before the formation day, with `night_and_day`'s rule copied. A book is formed only when at least 100 stocks hold the signal.
- **Deciles:** ordinal ranks; short rank < n/10, long rank ≥ 9n/10.
- **Market-relative:**
  - The legs are reported minus the export's equal-weight `market`, compounded over the month.
  - The long-short ($1 a side) is long minus short, in which the market cancels.
- **Costs:** 5 bps × turnover. Turnover is the sum over stocks of |new weight − drifted old weight|; the first book counts as a full build.
- **The contrast:**
  - The plan's "other months of the same years" was read as the other 11 months of each of the ten lag-years, lags 1–120 except multiples of 12, with at least 55 of the 110 present.
  - Keloharju et al.'s definition, which skips lags 1–11, is reported beside it with at least 50 of 99 present. It is the one comparable with their −0.96%.
- **Dividend correction:**
  - The monthly correction is the sum of `pubdata.div_fix` over the holding window's days.
  - It is added both to the signal's monthly inputs and to the holding returns, so the version stays consistent. Raw closes would otherwise put each payer's ex-dividend months lower every year from 2022-07.
  - Test 3's pass is read on the corrected version, with raw reported beside it.
- **The judge's score:**
  - It is built from the raw-closes signal, because the plan applies the correction to test 3, not to the money test, and the judge grades raw closes. The corrected signal was graded as a sensitivity.
  - Day d carries the percentile for the calendar month of the next trading day, taken from the exchange calendar (the export's days; the next weekday after the last day). So the score switches at the close of each month's last trading day.
  - Scores start at the start month's formation, 2007-04-30.
- **Partial months:**
  - 2025-06 in the old export (through 2025-06-27) is included in test 3.
  - The unseen period includes partial 2026-10, making 16 holding periods; the 15 complete months are also shown.
- **Alphas:** French's daily FF5 and UMD, compounded over each holding window. Our months run from one last trading day to the next, so they match French's calendar months.
- **"Same sign" in tests 2 and 3** was read as the same sign as test 1's net mean. Since test 1 is negative and fails, the write-up also gives each against the published positive sign. This wording was decided after seeing results.
- **Literature:**
  - Heston & Sadka's own numbers couldn't be read. SSRN and a university repository refused, so the search stopped as the brief says.
  - Their lag-12 figure comes from Keloharju et al.'s footnote.
  - Keloharju et al.'s numbers are from the NBER working paper (w20815, December 2014), not the published *JF* version.
  - A 0.52% a month figure seen in a summary is Heston & Sadka's 2010 international paper (1985–2006), so it isn't used.
