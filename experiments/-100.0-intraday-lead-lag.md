# Intraday lead-lag in 2026: has the lead-lag the census couldn't find moved to minutes?

**TL;DR:** Every daily test in this project found lagged correlation between stocks at chance level, and the literature says lead-lag moved into minutes and seconds by the 2010s: Chinco, Clark-Joseph & Ye (2019) found 3-minute predictability across stocks in 2005–2012, lasting about 15 minutes. What's new here is the project's first intraday data: Yahoo's free bars for the 500 most-traded stocks and 20 funds, fetched on 2026-10-21, with the census idea run on them. On the graded 5-minute bars (59 days, 2026-07-29..10-20), the lead-lag isn't there. In the selection half, 331 of 267,806 ordered pairs clear the null's 99.9th-percentile |corr| of 0.105. The day-shifted null gives 267.9 a shift (171–431), so that's only 24% more than chance (5 of the 20 shifts reach 331), far short of the 2× the plan needs. Test 1 fails. The later tests fail too. Out of sample, the selected pairs' rank IC is +0.0056, with a day-clustered t of 2.8 against the 3 needed. It doesn't beat SPY's own lagged bar (paired t 1.2). The long-short earns +0.39 bp a bar gross and −2.9 bp net at 1 bp a side, with break-even at 0.12 bp. The 1-minute bars (21 days, reported, not graded) tell a different story. Lead-lag is plainly there: 909 pairs against 268 (+239%), 136 of them reappear in the test half with the same sign (6.5 expected by chance), and the out-of-sample IC is +0.029 (t 14), ahead of SPY's lag (paired t 2.3). Funds lead stocks at 27× the null rate, and the leader is the more-traded name in 75% of pairs. That is the nonsynchronous-trading signature Lo & MacKinlay warned about. It is a matter of bar size, not period: the 5-minute test half covers the same weeks and sits at chance (1.10× the null). It is worth 1.4 bp a minute gross, break-even 0.45 bp a side, so it loses money at the plan's optimistic 1 bp. The unseen months don't apply (all of this is new 2026 data), and the plan's own money test replaces the judge's. Verdict: no edge.

## Nearest prior work

| Work | What it found |
|---|---|
| Chinco, Clark-Joseph & Ye 2019, *JF* | A LASSO on all stocks' lagged 3-minute returns predicts, 2005–2012; the predictors last about 15 minutes |
| Huth & Abergel 2014; Curme et al. 2015 | High-frequency lead-lag networks; can't cover the spread |
| Lo & MacKinlay 1990, *JoE* | Nonsynchronous trading creates spurious lead-lag |
| This project, `+2.0-correlation-census.md` | Daily lagged pairs at chance |

**What's new:** the project's first intraday data, and a 2026 test of whether minute-scale lead-lag is still there. A published method on new data.

**Novelty check (2026-10-21, a separate search; it changed no rules):** partly done, intraday lead-lag in factor-adjusted S&P 500 returns was published in 2026; a test on 2026 bars with a day-shift null was not found.
- **Cartea, Cucuringu & Shi 2026** (SSRN 6811380, "Intraday lead-lag relationships in idiosyncratic stock returns"): Removes common factors from S&P 500 stocks' returns and builds Kendall-correlation lead-lag networks; the effects largely vanish in daily returns but stay strong and more persistent at higher sampling frequencies, and a network strategy on high-frequency residual signals has higher out-of-sample Sharpe ratios. Sample period, bar length and costs are unverified (abstract only).
- **Chinco, Clark-Joseph & Ye 2019** (JF, "Sparse signals in the cross-section of returns"): LASSO on all stocks' lagged returns improves 1-minute-ahead forecasts; the predictors are unexpected, short-lived, sparse, and tend to be stocks with fundamental news. This search found no replication on data after 2012.
- **Liu & Stentoft 2023** (working paper, "Intraday stock predictability everywhere"): Machine-learning forecasts of SPY, nine sector funds and S&P 500 stocks at 1–30 minutes from lagged returns of SPY and its constituents, February 2004 to October 2016, with net results counted only when forecasts exceed the spread. Their stock-level results were not visible; the related published market paper (Huddleston, Liu & Stentoft 2023, Journal of Financial Econometrics) reports a Sharpe of 0.98 after costs on 5-minute index returns.
- **Hasbrouck 2003** (JF, intraday price formation in US equity index markets; NYU working paper 2000): For the S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100, most price discovery happens in E-mini futures, and SPY contributes markedly to price discovery in the sector ETFs while the reverse is small. Ernst (working paper, 2020–2022) finds ETFs lead single stocks when the stock is a large weight and has firm-specific information asymmetry.
- **Not found:** any published minute-scale cross-stock lead-lag test on 2025–2026 data, any test on free retail-grade bars, or a null built by shifting one stock's days against another's.

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Universe:**
- the 500 most-traded stocks of the judge-like liquid set on the new export's last day, minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)`;
- plus the funds SPY, QQQ, IWM, DIA, XLB, XLC, XLE, XLF, XLI, XLK, XLP, XLRE, XLU, XLV, XLY, SMH, KRE, XBI, GDX and XOP.

**Data:**
- Yahoo's regular-session bars: 5-minute for the last 60 days, 1-minute for the last 30.
- Unadjusted, in America/New_York time.
- The twin gap fade check's rules apply: drop a symbol-day whose last bar close is more than 2% from Yahoo's daily close (splits); drop half-days.

**Returns:**
- the log change between consecutive bar closes within a day;
- the first bar's return (from the prior close) and anything overnight are excluded;
- **a bar with no trades is missing, and so are the returns into and out of it. No forward filling** (fixed now: forward filling manufactures lead-lag).

**Market adjustment:** each return minus β × SPY's same-bar return, with β per symbol from the selection half.

**Split:** the days in date order; the first half selects pairs, the second half tests them.

**Lead-lag:** for every ordered pair (A, B), corr(r_A(k), r_B(k+1)) over bars where both exist, on market-adjusted returns, with at least 1,000 bar pairs.

**Null:**
- the same statistic with A's days paired with B's days shifted: A's day i with B's day i+s, for 20 shifts s, wrapping within the half;
- the threshold is the 99.9th percentile of |corr| across all pairs under the null.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **Selection half:** the number of ordered pairs above the threshold, on market-adjusted returns, is at least 2× the null's count.
2. **Test half, out of sample:**
   - Each follower B's prediction for bar k+1 is the sum, over its selected leaders (pairs above the threshold in the selection half), of sign(corr) × r_A(k).
   - A rank IC across followers for each bar; the mean IC over bars needs t ≥ 3, with standard errors clustered by day.
   - It must also beat the baseline "SPY's last bar predicts the next bar" (each stock's selection-half lag-1 β on SPY): paired difference t ≥ 2.
3. **Money:**
   - Each bar, long the top tenth and short the bottom tenth of followers by prediction, held one bar.
   - Gross mean per bar, and net of 1 bp per side, which is optimistic for large caps. Net must be positive.
   - Break-even cost reported.

**1-minute bars:** the same three tests on the last 30 days, reported, not graded.

**The 16 unseen months:** the idea doesn't apply here; all of this is 2026 data the project has never used.

## The number for the file name

Census-style: the percent more pairs above the threshold than the null produces in the selection half, (count ÷ null count − 1) × 100, on market-adjusted 5-minute returns.

## Ways it can fool us

- **Nonsynchronous trading:** a liquid stock's price updates first and looks like a leader of a slower one. The market adjustment and the SPY baseline are the guard.
- **Bid-ask bounce:** it gives a stock's own returns negative autocorrelation, but it shouldn't create cross-stock lead-lag.
- **Sixty days is short:** that's why the null shifts days instead of assuming independence.

### Results by period

The halves are the periods. At 5 minutes the selection half (July–September) and the test half (September–October) each look like chance on their own:
- 1.24× and 1.10× the null;
- only 4 pairs above the threshold in both.

At 1 minute both halves are far above the null (3.4× and 7.1×), with 136 pairs above the threshold in both.

**The 5-minute test half (09-09..10-20) contains 21 of its 30 days in the 1-minute window,** and on its own it sits at chance (1.10× the null). So the gap between the bar sizes is bar size, not period: the 1-minute lead-lag has faded by 5 minutes.

### The 16 unseen months and the judge

- **The unseen months don't apply,** as the plan says. Every bar is 2026 data the project had never used, and the new export only picked the universe.

## Decisions made while building it

Logged with times in `research/intraday_lead_lag/out/decisions.txt`.

**Before any fetch** (13:57):
- **Universe:** `night_and_day/common.py`'s liquid-set rule, evaluated at the new export's last day (2026-10-19).
  - The exclusion mask is `excluded(E_new, tw)` at the latest twins rebalance (2026-10-15).
  - The top 500 by median 63-day dollar volume: 2,473 stocks were in the liquid set, and the 500th has $215M a day.
  - Plus the 20 funds, none of which is among the 500.
  - Tickers map to Yahoo symbols as the twin gap fade check does ('.' → '-').
- **Fetch:** the check's `fetch.py`, copied.
  - The daily window is moved to 2026-07-01..10-20 for the 2% rule.
  - The 1-minute bars come in 7-day pieces from 2026-09-22 through 10-20.

**After seeing the 5-minute bars' structure,** before any return was computed (14:02):
- **Missing bar:**
  - A bar is missing when it's absent from Yahoo's output or its close isn't finite. Yahoo leaves out no-trade bars and doesn't forward-fill them.
  - Volume-0 bars with a price range count as present. All 2,109 five-minute ones have a range, including SPY, QQQ and NVDA bars, so it's Yahoo's volume field failing, not "no trades".
  - A flat volume-0 bar at the previous close counts as missing.
- **Returns:** within the day only, and the 9:30 bar's return is excluded. A full day has 76 lag-1 slots at 5 minutes and 388 at 1 minute.
- **Full day:** SPY has every bar from 9:30, at UTC−4. This dropped today's partial day.
- **2% rule:** applied against the fresh daily closes. A symbol-day without a daily bar is dropped, which removed WBD.
- **Split:** selection = the first ⌊D/2⌋ days.
- **β:** an OLS slope with intercept on SPY's same-bar return, over the selection half.
  - SPY is out of the adjusted pair set, because its adjusted return is identically zero.
  - It stays in the raw same-bar context and in test 2's baseline.
- **Lead-lag:** the exact pairwise-complete Pearson correlation from six masked matrix products, rather than an approximation from standardized series.
- **Null:**
  - B's days are rotated by s = 1..20 within the half.
  - At 1 minute there are only 10 days a half, so the shifts are s = 1..9, all distinct and nonzero.
  - The threshold pools every pair and every shift. The null count is the mean count per shift.
- **Robustness variant,** pre-registered and reported, not graded: each symbol's adjusted returns divided by its own same-day sd.
  - The reason: volatility clusters by day across stocks. That can fatten the real alignment's tail with no lead-lag, and the day-shift null would miss it.
- **Test 2:**
  - Predictions use adjusted r_A(k), and a missing leader term is dropped, not zeroed.
  - The target is adjusted r_B(k+1).
  - A bar needs ≥ 10 followers.
  - The day-clustered SE is sqrt(Σ_days (Σ e)²) ÷ n_bars.
  - The baseline is β_lag × SPY's r(k), with β_lag the slope of raw r_B(k+1) on SPY's raw r(k) over the selection half. It is scored on the same followers at each bar.
  - A constant prediction across followers scores IC 0.
- **Test 3:**
  - P&L is in raw returns.
  - The cost is 1 bp × the turnover of the weights. Each leg is sized 1, and the book starts and ends each day flat, so a whole-book change costs 4 bp.
  - Break-even = gross ÷ mean turnover.
  - Ties at the decile cut are broken by symbol order.

**After the 5-minute results, before the 1-minute run** (14:25):
- **"Flat volume-0 bar at the previous close" was applied as "volume 0 and close = previous close".**
  - The panel keeps closes only, so this is as close as the rule can be checked.
  - It removed 36 five-minute bars and 14 one-minute ones.
  - The 5-minute results barely moved: 331 pairs before and after (+23.6), IC +0.00558 → +0.00556, and gross +0.388 → +0.386 bp.
- **Added to the output (reporting only):**
  - how many null shifts reach the actual count;
  - a cross-check of the bars against the twin gap fade check's earlier fetch.
- **Not done:** a 5-minute run on the 1-minute window's 21 days alone. With 10 days a half, it would have only 760 bar pairs per pair, under the plan's 1,000. It isn't needed: the 5-minute test half already covers those weeks and sits at chance (see "Results by period").
