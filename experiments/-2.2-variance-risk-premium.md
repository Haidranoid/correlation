# The variance risk premium: does fear priced above recent turbulence predict SPY's next quarter?

**TL;DR:** Does SPY earn more over the next quarter when VIX squared sits far above recent realized variance, as Bollerslev, Tauchen & Zhou found? Not reliably: over 2008–2019 the forecast had 5.3% less squared error than the historical mean, all of it from 2008–2009 (t 1.43, where 2 was needed), and it was 28% worse in 2020–2025. Used to tilt SPY's variance timing, it trailed SPY by 2.2 points a year after costs, against 3.4 for variance timing alone, a difference that isn't significant (p 0.24). Verdict: no edge.

## The claim

- **The premium.** VIX squared is the options market's price of the S&P 500's variance over the next 30 days, under the risk-neutral measure. Realized variance is what the market actually did. Their difference, implied minus realized, is the variance risk premium (VRP): what investors pay to insure against turbulence.
- **What it predicts.** Bollerslev, Tauchen & Zhou (BTZ) report that a high VRP predicts high S&P 500 excess returns, most strongly over the next quarter. As the page puts it: "When implied variance (VIX squared) sits far above recent realized variance, SPY earns more over the next one to three months."
- **Their numbers** (1990-01..2007-12, monthly observations, Hodrick (1992) t-statistics for the overlap; Table 2 of the July 2008 working paper, the version read):

| Horizon, months | 1 | 3 | 6 | 9 | 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Slope on IV − RV | 0.39 | **0.47** | 0.30 | 0.17 | 0.12 |
| t | 1.76 | **2.86** | 2.15 | 1.36 | 1.00 |
| Adjusted R² | 1.07% | **6.82%** | 5.42% | 2.30% | 1.23% |

- **Their realized variance uses 5-minute returns.** This project has only daily closes. BTZ ran that variant too (section 4.2.1), on the quarterly horizon: VIX with realized variance from daily returns gave **t 2.34 and R² 4.45%**, against 2.86 and 6.82% with 5-minute returns. (Black-Scholes implied variance with 5-minute realized variance gave t 1.85 and R² 3.30%; both "old" measures together gave t 1.72 and R² 2.16%.) So the version this project can build is BTZ's own weaker variant, and the expected effect is the smaller one.
- **The components alone don't predict** in their sample. At the quarterly horizon, implied variance alone has t 1.41 and realized variance alone t 0.00 (Table 4). With realized variance and the VRP in one regression, the VRP stays significant and realized variance doesn't (Table 6). That is BTZ's argument that the *difference* carries the information.
- **The VRP is not persistent:** its monthly first-order autocorrelation is 0.49, against 0.94–0.99 for valuation ratios. So the usual worry about spurious long-horizon regressions is smaller (BTZ, section 3.2).

## What the project already measured

- **Timing SPY by its own variance works for risk.** In `-3.2-market-timing.md`, exposure = min(1, c ÷ SPY's 21-day variance) had a Sharpe ratio of 0.70 over 2005–2025 against 0.58 for buy-and-hold. Its c was matched to another rule's exposure over the same period, a mild in-sample choice. The brief's bar for any market-timing signal is to add to that.
- **The same idea, run in real time, trailed SPY.** The J2a rule of an earlier volatility-timing test (not yet published here) scaled SPY by a variance forecast (HAR plus log VIX plus two cross-section terms), with c the trailing median of its own forecasts. Its baseline rule trailed SPY by 3.9 points a year after costs over 2008-04..2025-06, mostly by cutting exposure after the volatile months of 2019–2021 and missing the rebounds.
- **Nothing tested here has predicted SPY's direction.** `-3.2`'s average correlation: SPY's next 21 days averaged +0.89% after both high and low months (t 0.00).
- **The VRP would lean the other way from variance timing at the moments that matter.** It is high when implied variance stays elevated after realized variance has calmed, which is often just after a selloff, when variance timing is still holding less SPY. So a VRP tilt could add back exposure exactly where the earlier volatility-timing test's rules missed the rebounds. Test T measures that.
- **VIX, VXN and RVX were among the 1,753 outside series** of `+4.0-outside-data-leaders.md`. That tested their daily log changes as leaders of individual stocks' moves, a different question. No earlier experiment here built the VRP.
- **Risk, not returns,** is the project's standing lesson (`experiments/README.md`). The VRP is a claim about returns.

## Definitions (fixed)

- **Prices.** P_d is Yahoo's adjusted close of SPY (`pubdata.etf_adjclose`), a total-return index. r_d = ln(P_d ÷ P_{d−1}). Yahoo's SPY starts 1993-01-29; the store's starts 2005-02-25, which would leave the first fit about two and a half years of data. The store's SPY is used only as a check.
- **Day axis.** Yahoo's SPY trading days. From 2001-12-31 to 2026-10-19 they equal the exports' days exactly (checked), so `-3.2`'s calendar carries over unchanged.
- **Realized variance, trailing and known at the close of t:** RV_t = 10,000 × Σ r_d² over the 21 trading days t − 20 .. t, in monthly percent squared (BTZ's units). All 21 returns must exist. It uses close-to-close returns, so overnight moves are included, as in BTZ's measure.
- **Implied variance:** IV_t = VIX_t² ÷ 12. VIX is annualized percent, so VIX² is annual variance in percent squared, and VIX covers 30 calendar days. 21 trading days are 1/12 of a 252-day year, so IV_t and RV_t cover the same horizon in the same units. FRED's VIX before September 2003 is CBOE's own recalculation under the new method, back to 1990-01-02 (SEC release 34-48807), the same series BTZ used.
- **The premium:** VRP_t = IV_t − RV_t, in levels. It goes negative when realized variance overshoots, as in a crash, so no log is taken.
- **Excess return target:** y_t^h = Σ over days d = t + 2 .. t + 1 + h of [r_d − ln(1 + RF_d)]: SPY's log excess return from the close of t + 1 to the close of t + 1 + h.
- **The risk-free rate,** fixed now: French's daily RF (`pubdata.ff3_daily`, the 1-month T-bill) through its last day, 2026-08-31; after that `pubdata.rf_daily` (DGS3MO ÷ 252, the last value carried forward after 2026-10-15). The number of days using the fill is reported. BTZ used the 3-month T-bill; the difference is a few basis points a year.
- **Horizon:** h = 63 trading days, BTZ's quarterly horizon, where their effect is strongest. Other horizons are reported only.

## Rules (proposed; fixed before any result is seen)

### Timeline and leakage

- **Decisions:** everything at the close of day t uses data through that close. VIX's close for day t is set at 16:15, after the 16:00 stock close and before t + 1. The earlier volatility-timing test settled that this is allowed when trades happen at the close of t + 1, and the same rule applies to VXN and RVX. Any trade is at the close of t + 1 (the judge's `delay=1`), and the target starts there.
- **Missing values:** a SPY day without a VIX value takes the previous trading day's, at most 3 days back. The checks found two such days for VIX (1997-01-31, 1997-11-26), two for VXN (2003-08-22, 2004-09-10) and two for RVX (2005-10-10, 2005-11-11). FRED values on days SPY didn't trade (34 for VIX, among them 2004-06-11 and exchange holidays since 2022) are ignored.
- **Training eligibility:** a fit made at the close of day t may use origin s only if its whole target has been observed: s + 1 + h ≤ t. For h = 63 the newest 64 origins before each fit are left out. The same rule covers every estimated quantity: the regression slopes, the historical mean, the HAR model in the expected-VRP variant, and the mean used by trading rule T. The code asserts it on every fit.
- **Calendar:** fits and trading decisions happen every 21 trading days on `-3.2`'s fixed calendar (export day 252 + 21k: 2007-12-03, 2008-01-03, ...). Each fit's forecasts cover the days up to the next calendar date.
- **The old run reads nothing after 2025-06-27.** Yahoo's and FRED's files run to October 2026, so they are cut at that day, and the code asserts it.

### Periods

- **Training** starts with the first origin that has 21 returns, 1993-03-02. The first fit, on 2007-12-03, has 3,655 eligible origins (1993-03-02..2007-08-31), about 58 independent quarters, almost all inside BTZ's own sample. That is intended: the paper's period trains the model, and every scored year comes after it.
- **An origin belongs to a period only if the origin and its whole target lie inside it.** Otherwise the 2008–2019 test would score returns from the 2020 crash.
  - **Development, the pass test:** origins 2008-01-02..2019-09-30, 2,957 of them (targets inside 2008-01-02..2019-12-31).
  - **Check:** origins 2020-01-02..2025-03-26, 1,315 (targets inside 2020-01-02..2025-06-27). A consistency check, not an independent confirmation: `-3.2`, the earlier volatility-timing test and other experiments read SPY in these years, and `-3.2`'s finding shaped this plan's baseline rule.
  - **The 16 unseen months:** origins 2025-06-30..2026-07-20, 265 (targets inside 2025-06-30..2026-10-19). Run once at the very end, reported, never confirming.
  - Origins whose targets cross a boundary (2019-10..12, 2025-03-27..06-27) are scored only in the reported "2008–2025-06, all" row.
- **Trading months go by decision date,** as in `-3.2` and the earlier volatility-timing test, so that the months are contiguous: development decisions 2008-01-03..2019-12-06 (144 months); check 2020-01-08..2025-05-14 (65 months, the last holding ending 2025-06-16); unseen 2025-07-16..2026-09-16 (15 months).

**How little 63-day targets hold.** 2008–2019 has 3,021 trading days: 48 non-overlapping 63-day windows. The 2,957 scored origins overlap, so they carry roughly the information of 47 independent quarters, not 2,957. If the true effect is the size of BTZ's daily-return variant (R² 4.45%), the expected t over 47 independent quarters is about 1.5; at their 5-minute size (6.82%), about 1.8. So a pass needs an effect larger than the paper's own, and a fail is weak evidence against the paper. The effect sizes are reported with 95% intervals so that a fail can be read as either "still consistent with BTZ's size" or "rules it out". The check years hold about 21 quarters and the unseen months about 4.

### The bar

- **t ≥ 2,** the project's bar for published strategies (`experiments/README.md`, "How new experiments are graded": a published result is the prior, and these test years fall after the paper's sample). The brief's t ≥ 3 is for new ideas. Whether P1 also clears t ≥ 3 is reported.
- **One primary,** so Holm's correction has nothing to adjust. G is a gate and T a secondary; neither adds a hypothesis to the family. The replications, horizons and variants are reported, not candidates, so there is no grid and no Romano-Wolf step.
- **Tests in order:** the verdict stops at the first failure (P1, then G, then T). Every number is still computed.

### P1 (primary). Does the premium beat the historical mean?

- **Two models,** least squares on the same eligible origins (VRP and target finite):
  - the historical mean: an intercept only;
  - the VRP model: y_t^63 = a + b × VRP_t.
- **Fitting:** expanding window from 1993-03-02, refit on each calendar date, daily forecasts (the earlier volatility-timing test's scheme).
- **Statistics** over the development origins:
  - out-of-sample R² (Campbell & Thompson 2008) = 1 − Σ e_VRP² ÷ Σ e_mean²;
  - Clark-West, since the mean model is nested in the VRP model: f_t = e_mean² − e_VRP² + (ŷ_mean − ŷ_VRP)², and the t-statistic of its mean with Newey-West standard errors at 126 lags (2h, the earlier volatility-timing test's 2h convention);
  - a moving-block bootstrap of f minus its mean, blocks of 126 days, 10,000 draws; the one-sided p is the share of resampled means at or above the observed mean. The forecasts are fixed, not refitted.
  - **The blocks are 126 days, not the brief's default 21.** Targets that share up to 62 of 63 days make f dependent out to lag 62 by construction, so a 21-day block would understate the variance. 126 days (2h) covers the overlap with room for the VRP's own persistence, and leaves 24 blocks over 12 years. Blocks of 63, 189 and 252 days are reported.
- **Pass (2008–2019):** Clark-West t ≥ 2.0 (one-sided normal p ≤ 0.023), bootstrap p < 0.023, *and* out-of-sample R² > 0. Clark-West alone can be significant while the larger model's forecasts are worse.
- **Check (2020–2025-06):** effect sizes with 95% intervals and by year. It counts as consistent if the out-of-sample R² is positive and Clark-West's one-sided normal p is below 0.05.
- **The honest version without overlap (reported):** origins on `-3.2`'s calendar spaced 63 days apart, in each of the three phases (about 47 origins each in 2008–2019); for each, the out-of-sample R² and the plain t of the mean of f. And the monthly version: every calendar origin (about 141), BTZ's monthly sampling, with Newey-West at 6 lags.

### G (gate). Is it the premium, or the volatility levels it is made from?

**Why a gate and not a nested test.** The brief asks that the premium be judged against a baseline that already holds SPY's own trailing variance and log VIX. But the premium is built from exactly those two numbers:
- with IV and RV in a baseline in levels, baseline + VRP is perfectly collinear, and Clark-West is undefined;
- with log VIX and log RV in the baseline, the log of IV ÷ RV is already a linear combination of the baseline's terms, so adding VRP in levels only tests its functional form (a difference against a ratio), not whether the premium carries information.

So the comparison is made the way BTZ's Tables 4 and 6 make it, out of sample: the one-slope premium against its components, each fitted the same way.

- **Four component models,** each least squares with an intercept on the same eligible origins and the same schedule as P1:
  - (a) log VIX and log RV_t together, the brief's baseline;
  - (b) log VIX alone;
  - (c) log RV_t alone, SPY's own trailing variance;
  - (d) IV_t and RV_t in levels, as separate regressors. This is BTZ's unrestricted model: the VRP model is (d) with the two slopes forced equal and opposite.
- **Gate (2008–2019):** the VRP model's out-of-sample mean squared error is below each of the four. A gate is a comparison of point estimates, with no significance level; P1 carries the significance.
- **Reported with it:** each component model against the historical mean (Clark-West t, bootstrap p, out-of-sample R²), which shows whether volatility levels alone predict returns here; Clark-West for (d) against the VRP model, which tests BTZ's restriction; and the brief's nested form, (a) + VRP against (a), labelled as a test of functional form.

### T (secondary). Does the forecast add money to variance timing?

`-3.2`'s set-up with the earlier volatility-timing test's timing: decisions at the close of each calendar date t0, the trade at the close of t0 + 1, held to the close of t0 + 22. SPY or cash, **no leverage**: exposure between 0 and 1. Cash earns 0, as in every timing rule here, so the rules compare with `-3.2` and the earlier volatility-timing test. 5 bps on every SPY trade, including the first buy and each month's trim of drift back to the target.

- **Variance (both rules):** σ̂²_t0 = the variance (ddof 0) of SPY's 21 daily log returns ending at t0, `-3.2`'s measure.
- **The variance-timing rule (VT), the thing that already works:** w = min(1, c ÷ σ̂²), with c the median of σ̂² over the previous 1,260 trading days (at least 252). That is `-3.2`'s rule with the earlier volatility-timing test's real-time c instead of `-3.2`'s c chosen over the evaluation years. σ̂² needs no target, so c is known at t0 by construction.
- **The VRP rule:** w = min(1, max(0, (c ÷ σ̂²) × (μ̂ ÷ μ̄))), where μ̂ is P1's VRP-model forecast at t0 and μ̄ the historical-mean forecast at t0 from the same fit.
  - It is VT tilted by how far the forecast sits from the historical mean. When μ̂ = μ̄ it equals VT; a forecast at or below 0 holds cash. No constant uses the evaluation period.
  - It is a mean-variance rule (exposure ∝ expected excess return ÷ variance) whose risk aversion is set so that the historical-mean forecast reproduces VT.
  - If μ̄ ≤ 0 at a decision, the rule holds VT's weight that month and the month is logged. None is expected: μ̄ is SPY's mean excess return since 1993.
  - **The cap binds often.** When σ̂² is below its median, VT already holds 100%, and the tilt can only cut. The VRP rule can beat VT only by cutting when the forecast is low, or by adding back when VT is below 100% and the forecast is high.
- **Pass (2008-01..2019-12 decisions):** a higher Sharpe ratio than VT, with a paired 12-month circular block bootstrap p < 0.10 (`engine.block_bootstrap_sharpe`, read-only), after costs. This is `-3.2`'s test 2 and the earlier volatility-timing test's J2a. It counts only if P1 and G pass.
- **Check (2020-01..2025-05):** the same difference with its 95% interval, and whether it has the same sign.
- **Reported:** annual return, Sharpe ratio, maximum drawdown, average exposure, number of trades and yearly cost, for the VRP rule, VT, buy-and-hold and the tilt built from model (a)'s forecast (does a forecast from the volatility levels do the same?); each rule against 10,000 random reorderings of its own exposures; by period (2008–2009, 2010–2014, 2015–2019, 2020–2025-05); cash at the risk-free rate instead of 0; and, as an after-the-fact diagnostic only, VT with c matched to the VRP rule's average exposure over the whole span, as `-3.2` set it.

### Reported (not pass/fail)

- **Horizons** h = 21 (`-3.2`'s calendar), 42, 126 and 252, each against the historical mean: the shape BTZ report, peaking at the quarter.
- **Without 2008–2009, without 2020, and by year:** out-of-sample R² and Clark-West t.
- **The VRP model's slope at every fit:** its sign and size through time.
- **Variants,** each through P1's comparison:
  - volatility form √IV − √RV (BTZ section 4.2.2);
  - the log ratio ln(IV ÷ RV);
  - the expected premium IV − E_t[RV over t + 1 .. t + 21], with E_t from a HAR regression on the last 1, 5 and 21 days' realized variance, fitted under the eligibility rule (BTZ section 4.2.3);
  - realized variance over 22 days, the earlier volatility-timing test's HAR window;
  - Campbell & Thompson's restrictions: a fit with a negative slope uses the historical mean, and a negative forecast is set to 0;
  - the store's SPY for realized variance and the target, as a check of the price source.
- **Overlap with the volatility levels:** the correlation of VRP with log VIX and log RV in each period.

### The replications (reported, not primaries)

- **VXN with QQQ, and RVX with IWM:** the same definitions with the fund's own returns and realized variance and the matching volatility index (VXN on Nasdaq-100 options, RVX on Russell 2000 options), and the fund's own 63-day excess return as the target.
- **Prices:** IWM is Yahoo's adjusted close (`pubdata`). QQQ isn't among `pubdata`'s funds, so it is the store's close: the old store for the old span, the new store for the unseen months, with their overlap compared. The store back-adjusts dividends only until about October–November 2022, so QQQ's returns from then on miss its dividends, about half a percent a year. ### Code checks and fidelity (run before any out-of-sample result is read)

- **Data:** Yahoo against the store's SPY, daily log returns 2005-02-28..2025-06-27: their correlation, the largest gaps, and the ex-dividend days after 2022, when the store stops adjusting. The VIX gaps and the calendar match, as listed above.
- **Target window:** a direct check on a sample of origins that y_t^h sums exactly days t + 2 .. t + 1 + h.
- **Eligibility:** asserted on every fit; the number of fits is reported.
- **Noise calibration** (as in the earlier volatility-timing test): 200 predictors of AR(1) noise with the VRP's daily first-order autocorrelation over 1993–2007, each through P1's whole pipeline, to measure how often Clark-West t ≥ 2 and bootstrap p < 0.023 occur by chance. And a leaky oracle (the future target plus noise), which must win by far.
- **Fidelity to BTZ, in sample, on their period:** the VRP regression on month-end origins 1993-03..2007-12 at 21 and 63 trading days, slope in BTZ's units (annualized percent excess return per monthly percent squared) with Hodrick (1992) t-statistics. Compared with BTZ's daily-return variant (t 2.34, R² 4.45%) and Table 2. The means of IV, RV and VRP over 1993–2007 are compared with their Table 1 (33.23, 14.93 and 18.30 over 1990–2007). These describe the prior the out-of-sample test starts from, and aren't pass/fail. A unit mismatch larger than the different years explain is treated as a bug; nothing else changes.

### The two numbers for the README index

- **After costs** (the file name): the VRP rule's yearly return at 5 bps a side minus SPY's (Yahoo's adjusted-close total return) over the same days. Daily returns are compounded within each calendar year, by each day's date, over the decision months 2008-01-03..2025-05-14 (the holding from the close of 2008-01-04 to the close of 2025-06-16), and the 18 yearly differences are averaged. This is `research/after_costs/group_b/market_timing.py`'s method, shifted to the close of t0 + 1 as in the earlier volatility-timing test.
- **Before costs, vs random:** the VRP rule's compound annual return at 0 bps minus the median of 10,000 random reorderings of its own monthly exposures over the same 209 months, with the one-sided p, as in `-3.2` and the earlier volatility-timing test.
- The same two numbers are reported for VT, so the file can show how much the forecast changes them.

### Holdout and forward confirmation

- **The 16 unseen months** (2025-06-30..2026-10-19) have been read by most of the project's 73 experiments. Results there are reported but can't confirm anything. They hold about 4 independent quarters and 15 trading decisions.
  - `unseen.py` runs every test once, with the frozen code. It uses `EXPORT_NEW` / `STORE_NEW` for the calendar, QQQ and the SPY check, and first checks that the new export's first 5,911 days equal the old export's (`EXPORT_OLD` / `STORE_OLD`). Yahoo's and FRED's files are single downloads, read through 2026-10-19; the old run read them only through 2025-06-27. So the new run's SPY forecasts and exposures over the old span must equal the old run's exactly; any difference means the cut or the calendar is wrong, and stops the run. QQQ's come from a different store and are compared, not required to match.
- **Freezing:** before the unseen run, the sha256 hashes of every script are recorded here and in `research/variance_risk_premium/frozen_sha256.txt`, and checked again after it. No git commit or tag; Brandon hasn't asked for one.
- **Forward:** `forward.py` runs each later download (Yahoo SPY and IWM, FRED's VIX, VXN, RVX and DGS3MO, French's RF, a later store for QQQ and the calendar) through the frozen code.
  - Downloads are manual. A missed one is logged as a gap, never filled later from data that arrived afterward. Revisions to past values in later downloads are ignored; the first-seen value is kept.
  - Report every 12 months. Judge P1 and T at 60 months: even then, only about 20 independent quarters.

## What the results can and can't show

- **P1, G and T pass:** the premium predicted SPY's next quarter after the paper's sample, better than the volatility levels it is built from, and added money to variance timing. It would be a lead, judged on forward data.
- **P1 passes and G fails:** returns are predictable from implied and realized variance, but the premium form adds nothing over the levels, so the information isn't specifically the premium.
- **P1 and G pass, T fails:** the forecast is real but too small, or too concentrated in moments variance timing already handles, to add money without leverage.
- **P1 fails:** no out-of-sample predictability over 2008–2019 with daily realized variance. Because 47 quarters give little power, this rejects the paper's size only if the 95% interval of the out-of-sample R² excludes it.
- **Either way:** it tests the version this project can build, VIX with daily realized variance, not BTZ's 5-minute measure. Their own numbers say the daily version is weaker (t 2.34 against 2.86 in sample).
- **The replications** speak only to two other US indexes, with short training samples.

## Ways it can fool us

- **Overlap.** Daily origins with 63-day targets look like 2,957 observations and are about 47. Newey-West with long overlap over-rejects in samples this size (Ang & Bekaert 2007, the reason BTZ use Hodrick's errors). Hence the 126-day blocks, the non-overlapping phases and the noise calibration.
- **A few months decide.** October 2008 is the most influential observation for this regression in the literature (Camponovo, Scaillet & Trojani 2016). The VRP is deeply negative when realized variance overshoots, as in October 2008 and March 2020, and large positive afterwards. Results are reported without 2008–2009 and without 2020, and by year.
- **The premium is partly volatility.** VRP = IV − RV, so a high VRP often means realized variance has just fallen. Hence gate G, and T against the same variance timing with the same σ̂² and c.
- **The tilt redoes part of variance timing.** Both rules divide by the same σ̂², so they differ only through μ̂ ÷ μ̄. Any gain T reports comes from the forecast, not from a second dose of 1/σ̂².
- **No leverage.** With the cap at 1, the rule can't act on a high forecast in calm months. Leverage would change the result, and isn't how this project's timing rules work.
- **Training on the paper's own years.** The first fits estimate the slope on 1993–2007, inside BTZ's sample. That is the prior the test is meant to start from; every scored origin is after the paper.
- **Daily realized variance is noisier.** Measurement error in RV weakens the slope, and BTZ's daily variant was weaker. A fail is partly about the data.
- **A new price source for the signal.** Earlier timing files built their states from the store's SPY. This one uses Yahoo's from 1993 so that the first fit has 15 years. The store comparison checks the two agree.
- **The years have been read.** `-3.2`, the earlier volatility-timing test and others traded SPY over 2008–2025, and `-3.2`'s finding chose the baseline rule. Only the forward data is unread.
- **Choosing after looking.** h, the 21-day window, the units, the rule, the bar and every variant are fixed here.
- **Survivor-only stocks:** this test uses no stock prices. SPY, QQQ, IWM and the three volatility indexes are built on index constituents as they were, delisted companies included, so the store's survivor-only stock list can't bias it. The exports supply only the trading calendar.
- **The store's prices:** used only for QQQ and the SPY check. They are Stooq's adjusted closes, unrepaired, with dividends back-adjusted only to about October–November 2022 (the QQQ caveat above). Highs and lows aren't used.

## Results (run 2026-10-25)

**What this tests.** The version of BTZ's premium this project can build: VIX squared over 12 minus 21 days of SPY's squared daily returns, forecasting SPY's excess return from the close of t + 1 over the next 63 trading days. It is trained on 1993–2007 and scored on 2008–2019, entirely after the paper's sample. It is not BTZ's 5-minute measure.

**The tests, in order:**
1. **P1 (primary): fails.** Over 2008–2019 the VRP model's squared error was 5.3% below the historical mean's, but Clark-West t = 1.43 (needed ≥ 2) and bootstrap p = 0.10 (needed < 0.023). It doesn't clear t ≥ 3 either. Without 2008–2009 the improvement is −1.1%. The check years 2020–2025-06 are not consistent: −28.5%.
2. **G (gate): passes on 2008–2019, fails on 2020–2025-06.** The VRP model's error was below all four volatility-level models over 2008–2019. That doesn't count once P1 fails.
3. **T (secondary): fails.** The VRP rule's Sharpe ratio beat variance timing's by 0.063 over 2008–2019 (0.854 against 0.791), bootstrap p 0.24 (needed < 0.10).

The verdict stops at P1. Every number is computed anyway.

### Files

Code in `research/variance_risk_premium/` (numpy only, system `python3 -I -B`, each under `systemd-run --user --scope -p MemoryMax=5G` with 4 threads). Run in this order:
- `checks.py old` → `checks_old.txt`, `checks_old.json`: the data checks and the fidelity regression.
- `fidelity_diag.py` → `fidelity_diag.txt`: conventions A–D (added after the fidelity check).
- `forecast.py old --noise-calibration 200` → `check_noise_old.txt`, `.json`; then `forecast.py old` → `forecast_old.txt`, `.json`: P1, G, the reported comparisons and the replications' forecasts (a few seconds).
- `timing.py old` → `timing_old.txt`, `.json`, `months_old.tsv`: T, both index numbers, the replications' rules (about 20 s).
- `unseen.py` → `forecast_new.*`, `timing_new.*`, `months_new.tsv`, `unseen.txt`.
- `forward.py DOWNLOAD_DIR [STORE_DIR]`: each later download, through the frozen code.
- `vrp_common.py`: the fixed rules as constants, the loaders and the statistics.

Large files (`~/.local/share/correlation2/experiments/variance_risk_premium/`): `fc_<tag>.npz` (daily forecasts, fits and slopes), `timing_<tag>.npz` (exposures).
