# Silent-peer news drift: does news the peers ignore keep moving the price?

**TL;DR:** When a company has news but its usual co-movers stay silent, does its price keep drifting the news way next month more than when they echo it? No: over 2006–2019 silent news drifted −0.13% and loud news −0.19% (gap t +0.22, 2.5 needed); news here barely drifts at all. After costs of 5 basis points a trade its top 20 made −8.2 points a year against SPY. Verdict: no edge.

## The idea

- **What bystanders showed:** on a company's news day, its co-movers usually move only about a tenth of what their ordinary co-movement predicts. When they do move a lot, the news was bigger than the company.
- **The co-movers as a classifier:** that echo sorts news into *about this company* and *about the group* using nothing but prices.
- **The hypothesis:** the first kind is underreacted to and keeps drifting. The second is priced almost at once.

## Nearest prior work

From the earlier survey and well-known literature. A separate novelty check runs alongside, and its findings will be added here.

| Work | What it found |
|---|---|
| Bernard & Thomas 1989 | Post-earnings-announcement drift |
| Martineau 2022, *Critical Finance Review*, "Rest in peace post-earnings announcement drift" | The drift has disappeared in large caps in recent decades |
| Chan 2003, *JFE*; Savor 2012, *JFE* | Moves with news drift; moves without news reverse |
| Da, Liu & Schaumburg 2014, *Management Science* | Return components split into industry and firm-specific parts behave differently |
| Huang, Lee, Song & Xiang 2022, *JFE*, "A frog in every pan" | Investors underreact to information that arrives gradually, not to jumps |
| Hirshleifer, Lim & Teoh 2009, *JF* | On busy news days, underreaction is stronger |
| Gervais, Kaniel & Mingelgrin 2001, *JF* | High-volume days are followed by higher returns |

**What's new:** classifying a company's news by its co-movers' same-day echo, and testing whether that predicts the drift.

**Novelty check (2026-10-21, a separate search; it changed no rules):** partly done, and the main published evidence runs *against* the hypothesis.
- **Not found:** anything using co-movers' same-day realized move against their ordinary co-movement to classify the news.
- **Hui & Yeung 2013** (*JAR* 51(4)) split earnings news into industry-wide and firm-specific parts using analyst revisions. They find a large drift after *industry-wide* news and none after firm-specific news: the opposite of this idea.
- **Ayers & Freeman 1997** (*JAE*) found the drift in the firm-specific part, but **Elgers, Porter & Xu 2008** (*JAE*) attribute that to measurement bias.
- **Kovacs 2016** (*CAR*) and **Da, Liu & Schaumburg 2014** also point to underreaction to industry information.
- **Jiang & Zhu 2017** (*JFE*): price jumps continue for 1–3 months.
- **Martineau 2022:** post-earnings drift is gone for large stocks since 2006.

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Universe:** the judge-like liquid set, not fund-like (`c2eval.twins(E)['fund_like']`, latest at or before the day).

**News day for stock A:**
- volume ≥ 4× its median over the previous 60 trading days;
- a market-relative move (stock minus the export's `market`) of at least 3× its 60-day sd;
- the first such day in 5.
- **No "solo" filter:** unlike bystanders, sector-wide days stay in. They are the loud side.

**Co-movers:**
- A's 20 most correlated stocks, by same-day market-relative correlation over the previous 252 days, with correlation ≥ 0.3;
- B's ordinary response b_AB = the slope of B on A over that period.

**Echo:** E = Σ_B (B's market-relative move that day) ÷ Σ_B (b_AB × A's move). That is the share of the ordinarily expected sympathy that actually happened.

**Classes:** terciles of E among all news days in the previous 252 trading days (point in time). Silent = bottom third, loud = top third.

**Outcome:** A's market-relative log return from the close of t+1 to the close of t+21, signed by the direction of A's news move. Skipping the first day avoids bid-ask bounce. Also t+1..t+6 and t+1..t+63.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **Silent minus loud,** 20-day signed drift, 2006–2019, with standard errors clustered by event date. **Pass:** t ≥ 2.5.
2. **The silent drift itself:** positive with t ≥ 2. This makes sure the difference isn't just loud events reversing.
3. **2020–2025-06:** the same sign of the difference, with t ≥ 1.5.
4. **The 16 unseen months** (new export, once, at the end): same sign.

**Reported for context:**
- the drift after all news days;
- same-size big moves *without* a volume spike, which should reverse (Chan);
- a split by A's size.

**Money test (the judge)**
- For silent events, score = sign(A's move) × |A's move in sd units|, written on every day t+1 .. t+20. The account buys at the close of t+1 and holds through t+21.
- NaN elsewhere. Only good-news silent events get positive scores, so the long-only account holds them.
- `rebalance=1`. Uniform and volatility-matched random accounts are both reported.

**The plan's own long-short:** long silent good-news events, short silent bad-news events, held 20 days, net of 10 bps per round trip.

## The number for the file name

The judge's walk-forward change over random, with uniform random accounts, 0 bps (`c2eval.change_over_random`).

## Ways it can fool us

- **Survivor-only data:** bad-news stocks that later collapsed and were delisted are missing. That makes the short side look worse than it was, and the long side look a little better.
- **Echo and size:** small stocks may have weaker co-movers, so they'd look "silent" more often. Report silent minus loud within size halves.
- **Earnings season:** many companies report in the same weeks, so silent and loud events cluster in time. Clustering standard errors by date handles that.

- **Co-movers are scarce before 2015.** Market-relative correlation ≥ 0.3 leaves 18% of all news days with no co-mover and 20% with one or two. Before 2015 the median news day has 1–4 co-movers (2009 aside), so only 25–65% of those years' news days get a class. From 2020 the median is 7–20, and 72–85% get one.
- **The tercile cut-offs are stable:** silent means E below about 0.0 (the co-movers on average didn't move A's way at all), loud means E above about 0.3. That is the bystanders finding again: on a typical news day the co-movers move a small fraction of what ordinary co-movement predicts.
- **2006–2019** had 17,990 news days, 9,624 of them with enough co-movers to be sorted into thirds by the echo.

### Test 2: the silent drift itself — fails

Silent news drifts **−0.13% over 20 days (t −0.74)**. The pass needed a positive drift with t ≥ 2. By direction: silent good news −0.20% (t −0.80, 1,299 events), silent bad news −0.07% (t −0.32, 1,937 events). Neither side continues.

### What it means

- **The echo classifies news, but the class doesn't predict the drift.** Loud days really are sector-wide: twice as many co-movers trade heavily. After both kinds of news, though, the price goes on to do the same thing.
- **The premise is missing here.** The plan's mechanism needs underreaction to firm-specific news, but in the judge's liquid set news days don't drift at all over 2006–2019 (−0.05% in 20 days) and partly reverse since 2020. A classifier can't concentrate a drift that isn't there. This fits Martineau 2022, which finds post-earnings drift gone in large caps.
- **The one hint** is that the gap widens when E is measured on more co-movers (+0.68%, t 1.71, at a minimum of 10). That is post hoc and below the line; a new test of it would need its own fixed rules and fresh data.
- **Caveats:** the data holds only survivors. Classes before 2015 rest on few co-movers. Event-date clusters don't cover the overlap of 20-day windows, but month clusters give nearly the same t everywhere.

## Decisions made while building it

All of these were made **before any forward return was computed**, unless marked otherwise. Order of work: panels and the news-day table built, the counts by class and year looked at (they use no forward returns), these decisions written down, then Tests 1–3, the money test, and last the unseen months.

**Data and universe**
- **Market-relative move:** the stock's daily log return minus the export's `market` (the equal-weight mean log return of the liquid stocks), as the rules say. A is not left out of the market; with about 1,250 stocks in it, A's own weight is under 0.1%.
- **Universe (the judge-like liquid set):** the judge's rule, made point in time: at least 90% finite closes over the 252 days ending on the day, median close ≥ $5 and median dollar volume ≥ $5M over them, minus `c2eval.twins(E)['fund_like']`. It is recomputed on the twins calendar (every 21 trading days) and holds until the next recomputation, so it always uses the latest fund-like flag at or before the day. The code is the one in `research/free_timing`. Median size 1,252 stocks (282 to 2,398).
- **Volume** is share volume from the price store, as a ratio to its median over t−60..t−1 (≥ 40 positive days). **sd** is the sd of the market-relative log return over t−60..t−1 (≥ 40 days).

**News days and co-movers**
- **News day:** volume ≥ 4× and |market-relative move| ≥ 3 sd, A in the universe on t. **First in 5:** no such day for A on t−5..t−1, whatever A's universe status then (as in bystanders). No solo filter.
- **Co-movers:** among the universe stocks with a move on day t, the 20 with the highest correlation of market-relative log returns with A over t−252..t−1 (pairwise complete, ≥ 200 shared days), keeping those with correlation ≥ 0.3. b_AB = the OLS slope (with intercept) of B on A over the same days.
- **Echo E needs at least 3 co-movers.** Market-relative correlation ≥ 0.3 is a demanding bar here: 18% of news days have no co-mover, 12% one and 8% two, and before 2015 the median news day has 1–4. An E from one or two co-movers is mostly that stock's own noise. Three was chosen from those counts alone (no forward returns), and minimums of 1, 5 and 10 are reported beside it as a check; they don't decide.
- **Terciles:** the 1/3 and 2/3 quantiles of E over the news days with an E dated t−252..t−1 (same-day news days of other stocks are left out, so the cut-offs are fixed before day t). At least 100 such days are needed, so classes start in late 2004. Silent: E below the lower cut-off; loud: above the upper one.
- **Size half** (for the "Echo and size" check): A's median dollar volume over t−60..t−1 against the median of the same measure over the news days dated t−252..t−1.

**Outcomes and tests**
- **Outcome:** sign(A's news move) × [log(close t+1+h ÷ close t+1) − the sum of the export's `market` over t+2..t+1+h], h = 5, 20 and 62. The plan's "t+1..t+63" is 62 trading days, so the long horizon is labelled 62 days. NaN where either close is missing or the window runs past the export. The skipped day itself (close t → close t+1) is shown as "t+1" for context.
- **Periods** are by event date: the test period 2006-01-01..2019-12-31; Test 3 2020-01-01..2025-06-27 (the old export); Test 4 2025-06-30..2026-10-19 (the new export). Events in the last 21 trading days of an export have no 20-day outcome.
- **Statistics:** silent minus loud is the coefficient on a silent indicator in one regression over silent and loud events; standard errors are Liang–Zeger clustered by event date (CR1), the bystanders code. Event-date clusters don't cover the overlap of 20-day windows of events a few days apart, so calendar-month clusters are shown beside them; the pass rules use date clusters, as the plan says.
- **"Same sign"** in Tests 3 and 4 means the hypothesis's sign: silent drift above loud drift (a positive difference).
- **No-news contrast:** same-size moves (|move| ≥ 3 sd) on volume ≤ 1.5× the 60-day median (the bystanders' "normal volume"), A in the universe, no news day for A on t−5..t−1, and the first such quiet move in 5 days. Same outcome.

**Money**
- **Judge scores:** every silent event writes xA ÷ sd (sign × size in sd units) on t+1..t+20. If a later silent event of the same stock overlaps, its score replaces the earlier one from its own t+1 on. NaN elsewhere. Saved with `c2eval.save_scores`, graded with `c2eval.judge(path, rebalance=1)`, uniform and volatility-matched.
- **The plan's long-short,** two ways. Per trade: each silent event is one equal-dollar trade, P&L = sign × (A's simple return from the close of t+1 to the close of t+21 − the equal-weight universe's compounded simple return over the same days), net of 10 bps. Calendar time: $1 long the equal-weight good-news leg and $1 short the bad-news leg, each position held over t+2..t+21; a leg with no positions that day is replaced by the market; costs 5 bps × (entries + exits) ÷ positions per leg per day, so 10 bps per round trip. Judge years (June 28 to June 27), 2006-07..2024-25.

**Changed or added after seeing results**
- **No rule, threshold or parameter was changed after any forward return was computed.** The numbers above come from the rules as written before Test 1 was run.
- **A count line was added to the test report** (news days dated in each period, by class, including those without a 20-day outcome) after the first run of `analyze.py tests old`. The rerun reproduced every number exactly.
- **Per-trade and calendar-time long-short disagree in sign in the unseen months** (−44.2 bps per trade; +6.1% a year). This was checked for a bug afterwards with a throwaway script: position-weighted, the daily returns agree with the per-trade figure (long leg −4.4 bps per position-day against the market), while day-weighted, the legs of 10–30 names are dominated by a few volatile stocks on some days. Both are reported; neither is significant.
- **Test 4's "same sign"** is met (+0.37%, t +0.39), but the verdict had already stopped at Test 1.
- **The judge skipped 2006-07** ("only 0 stocks can be picked"), as it does for the 5-day reversal check, so the walk-forward has 18 years.
- **Sanity check of the grader:** the stored 5-day reversal grading (`_check_reversal5_old`) reads +11.8 points a year, 8 of 18 years, through `c2eval.change_over_random`, as expected.

## Files

- `build.py`: panels for one export (market-relative log returns against the export's `market`, 60-day sd, volume ratio from the store, 60-day median dollar volume, the judge-like liquid set on the twins calendar, an equal-weight simple-return market), then news days with their co-movers and echo E (no solo filter), and the quiet big moves for the no-news contrast.
