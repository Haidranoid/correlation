# The original pair finder (correlation v1)

**TL;DR:** Did the original app's pair finder, which saved any pair where A moves first and B follows with a correlation of 0.3 or more at any offset, find anything real? No. Rerun exactly on the 500 most-traded stocks, it kept as many pairs from pure noise as from real prices. Its picks trailed random accounts by 1.6 points a year and SPY by 3.6 after costs. Verdict: no edge; chance plus same-period co-movement.

## What it did

- **Data:** Stooq bulk zips imported into PostgreSQL. 26.5M daily prices for 11,431 US symbols, 1962 to 2025-06-27.
- **Analysis:** rake tasks compute the correlation between two chosen symbols' N-day % changes. They use windows of 30, 60, 90 or 180 days and offsets from 0 up to the full history. Results with |r| ≥ 0.3 and at least 10 points are saved.
- **Also:** company profiles from four providers (FMP, Alpha Vantage, Finnhub, IEX Cloud), a dashboard, stock pages and interactive explainer graphics.

## What it found, and why it doesn't hold

**What was stored:** 3 pairs, 6 window combinations, 1,246 rows.
- 7 rows have offset 0. They average r = 0.40 over about 9,880 days: real same-day co-movement, but inflated by overlapping windows.
- 54 rows have offsets of 1 to 30 days.
- **1,185 have offsets over 365 days**, with 236 points on average and 10 at the least.
- The top saved result: "MSFT predicts AAPL 14,201 days (39 years) later, r = −0.80 on 38 points".

| Problem | Effect |
|---|---|
| Overlapping windows: an N-day change computed every day | Neighbouring samples share N−1 days, so 1,000 points of 30-day changes hold about 33 independent observations. Strength and significance are overstated |
| Offset shorter than the window | A 40-day change ending at t and one ending at t+7 share 33 days, so the "predictive" correlation is mostly same-day co-movement |
| Best of thousands of tries | About 14,000 offsets per pair, keeping \|r\| ≥ 0.3. Noise passes |
| No chance baseline, no out-of-sample test | No way to tell a real relationship from a lucky one, or to know whether it persists |
| Raw price changes, no market adjustment | Most stocks correlate positively just by moving with the market |
| Speed | All pairs × 5 lags at 50 ms per correlation on 24 cores is about 16 days. The same work as matrix products takes 0.2 s in numpy and 7 ms per lag on the GPU |

- **Kept from v1:**
  - the Stooq import;
  - the multi-provider company-info design;
  - the vocabulary of offsets and leader/follower;
  - the explainer ideas.
- **The test below confirms what `ANALYSIS.md` suspected:** v1's relationships are chance plus same-period co-movement.

## The test (run 2026-10-21)

v1's method was taken from its code, re-implemented, and run on many stocks with a chance baseline, an out-of-sample replication check and the shared money judge. Its own settings were kept; nothing was fixed.

### v1's method, as found in its code

From `app/services/correlation_analysis_service.rb` (`find_best_correlation`, `test_offset_range`), `app/models/stock_price.rb` and `lib/tasks/correlation_analysis.rake`:

- **Changes:** the % change over `window` of the stock's own trading rows, `(close[i] − close[i−w]) / close[i−w] × 100`, computed at every row, so the samples overlap.
- **Windows:** any pair of windows can be passed in (`correlation:analyze[A,B,w1,w2]`). The README advertises 30, 60, 90 and 180 days. The saved rows show what was actually run: equal windows of 7, 14, 20, 21, 30 and 40 rows. Every saved row has window1 = window2.
- **Pairing:** A's change dated t goes with B's change dated exactly t + k calendar days. If t + k is not one of B's trading days, that point is lost.
- **Offsets:** every k from 0 to the largest multiple of 50 below (overlap span − window), in calendar days. That is 14,301 offsets for MSFT/AAPL. Offsets are never negative in this search.
- **History:** the whole overlapping history of the two stocks. A start/end date given to the rake task only caps the offset range; the correlations still use all data.
- **Correlation:** Pearson r on raw % changes, with no market adjustment.
- **Keep rule:** |r| ≥ 0.3 (and r² ≥ 0.09, the same thing) with n ≥ 10 aligned points. A = B is refused. All kept rows for that pair and window are saved, replacing earlier ones.
- **Check:** this re-implementation recomputes all 1,243 saved MSFT/AAPL rows from the 2025-06-27 Stooq store with the same n in every row and r within 1e−6 (v1 stored 6 decimals). The matrix form used below matches the per-pair form to 2e−7.

### Design (fixed before any outcome was computed)

**Universe:** for each training period, the 500 stocks with the highest median dollar volume over its last 252 days, among stocks with a median close ≥ $5 there. They must be listed from the period's first day, with at most 1% of closes missing; a missing close is carried forward. Only data before the period's end is used. (First drafted as "a close on every day"; changed before any grading, see Decisions.)

**Search:** v1's search over every ordered pair (249,500), at 8 equal windows: 7, 14, 21, 30 and 40 (the windows v1 was run with; 20 is folded into 21) and 60, 90 and 180 (the README's). Every calendar-day offset from 0 to v1's maximum is tried (about 2,500 for a 7-year period), matched exactly as v1 did. Nothing is capped.

**Training period:** a rolling 7 years. v1 used each pair's whole history; 7 years keeps the matrix form exact (every stock has a close every day, with the rare gap carried forward) and leaves unseen years to test on.

**Chance:** the same search with the targets (B) replaced by a sign-flip null. Each daily return is multiplied by a random ±1 that is the same for every stock on that day, as in the census, and the prices are rebuilt so the w-row changes overlap exactly as v1's do. Leaders (A) stay real. Three draws.

**Replication:** train 2015-06-29..2022-06-27. Each relationship (A → B, w, k) is recomputed on later data: B's changes whose whole window lies in 2022-06-28..2025-06-27, each paired with A's change k calendar days earlier. Reported by offset band (0, 1–30, 31–365, over 365 days):
- the share with the same sign as in training: kept relationships, against all relationships at the same offsets (a k-matched base rate) and against relationships the null picked;
- the mean of sign(r_train) × r_later.

**Money test:**
- **Walk-forward:** every test year 2006-07..2024-25 (the judge grades 18; it skips 2006-07, which has no candidates). Relationships are re-learned each year from the 7 years before it (fewer before 2009, since the export starts 2001-12-31), on that year's universe.
- **Score:** at the close of day d, stock B's score is the sum over its kept relationships (A → B, w, k) of r × A's w-row change ending on calendar date cal(d + w) − k. That is the relationship's own forecast of B's next w-row change, over days (d, d + w]. It is used only when that date is a trading day on or before d. So offsets shorter than the window's calendar span (about 42 days for w = 30) drop out, and misses contribute nothing, as in v1. Future trading dates come from the exchange calendar (weekdays after the export ends).
- **Headline:** w = 30, rebalance every 21 trading days, change over random at 0 bps averaged over the graded years.
- **Secondary:**
  - the other windows at rebalance ≈ 0.7 × w (7→5, 14→10, 21→15, 40→28, 60→42, 90→63, 180→126);
  - all 8 windows summed, at rebalance 21;
  - w = 30 held 30 days (rebalance 30);
  - the brief's literal recipe for w = 30 (A's change ending k days before d, which pairs with B's change ending at d);
  - relationships picked from one null draw (w = 30);
  - random scores on the same 500 stocks, which measures what the universe alone does against the judge's random accounts. Those draw from its whole liquid set: about 300 stocks in 2007–08 and about 2,000 later.
- **Unseen months:** once, at the end. w = 30, rebalance 21, relationships from 2018-06-28..2025-06-27 on the new export, graded from 2025-06-30.

**Optional check:** v1's 1,241 saved MSFT → AAPL rows, recomputed on AAPL changes dated after 2025-06-27 (the 2026-10-19 store), for the share that keep their sign.

## Results

### Chance: v1's rule keeps what noise keeps

v1's search on 2015-06-29..2022-06-27 tried 4.89 billion relationships: 249,500 ordered pairs × 8 windows × about 2,500 offsets. Its rule kept 820 million; the sign-flip null kept 824 million (mean of 3 draws).

| Offsets | Kept by v1's rule | Kept from the null | × chance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (same day) | 1.09 M | 0.20 M | 5.4 |
| 1–30 days | 15.2 M | 6.5 M | 2.3 |
| 31–365 days | 40.1 M | 38.6 M | 1.04 |
| over 365 days | 764 M | 779 M | 0.98 |
| long enough to trade on (at least the window's calendar span) | 782 M | 796 M | 0.98 |

By window, at offsets long enough to trade on:

| Window (trading rows) | Shortest usable offset | Share of relationships kept | × chance | Same sign later | Base rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | 10 days | 2.0% | 1.04 | 50.6% | 50.6% |
| 14 | 20 | 4.7% | 0.94 | 50.9% | 50.6% |
| 21 | 30 | 8.2% | 0.95 | 51.5% | 50.9% |
| 30 | 43 | 11.4% | 0.95 | 51.2% | 50.8% |
| 40 | 57 | 15.0% | 0.96 | 50.5% | 50.3% |
| 60 | 86 | 20.9% | 0.96 | 50.1% | 50.0% |
| 90 | 131 | 30.5% | 1.01 | 50.7% | 50.4% |
| 180 | 261 | 46.5% | 1.01 | 50.7% | 50.4% |

- **Overlap does the work.** Seven years of 180-row changes hold only about 10 independent observations, so nearly half of all relationships clear |r| ≥ 0.3 by chance, and the null clears the same share. At 30 rows, chance even produces more of the extreme ones (|r| ≥ 0.9: 74,000 real against 214,000 from the null).
- **The only excess is at short offsets.** At offsets 0–30 days, A's window and B's window cover the same days, so the "lead" is same-period co-movement. That is real (1.4× to 2,400× chance, depending on the window and band) but it can't be traded: by the time A's change is known, B's matching change has already happened.

### Replication: kept relationships don't keep their sign

Each relationship was recomputed on B's changes in 2022-06-28..2025-06-27. Base = every relationship at the same offsets, weighted like the kept ones. Null-picked = what the null kept, against the real later r.

| Offsets | Same sign later: kept | Base | Null-picked | Mean sign(r) × later r: kept | Base |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 90.2% | 83.6% | (no meaning) | +0.297 | +0.225 |
| 1–30 days | 80.9% | 72.0% | (no meaning) | +0.209 | +0.138 |
| 31–365 days | 51.7% | 51.1% | 47.8% | +0.014 | +0.009 |
| over 365 days | 50.7% | 50.4% | 49.4% | +0.004 | +0.002 |
| long enough to trade on | 50.7% | 50.4% | 49.4% | +0.004 | +0.0025 |

- **Short offsets persist** because co-movement persists.
- **At usable offsets, selection adds about 0.2 points of sign agreement** and +0.0015 of r over unselected relationships at the same offsets. Hundreds of millions of heavily overlapping relationships sit behind it, so even that is not distinguishable from zero. Relationships with |r| ≥ 0.5 do no better (50.0–52.4% by window).
- **Why the null-picked share is blank at short offsets:** with one shared flip per day, a single draw's null r has nearly the same sign for every pair there.

- **The headline:**
  - The picks averaged 8.9% a year, against 10.5% for the median random account and 11.0% for SPY.
  - At 5 and 20 bps per side the picks made 7.9% and 4.7%. The change over random stays −1.6, because the random accounts trade as much and pay the same costs.
  - The median year was −2.8. It was behind random in every year from 2020-21 to 2023-24, worst −22.3 in 2020-21.
- **The universe:** the judge's random accounts draw from its whole liquid set (about 300 stocks in 2007–08, 2,000 later). Random picks from v1's 500 beat them by 2.2 points a year, so every row should be read against +2.2, not 0.
- **Windows 60 and 90 (+3.2, +4.1)** are within one standard error. Against the universe they are +1.0 and +1.9, and their long-shorts are negative.
- **w = 180's rank IC z of +12.9 and long-short z of +3.2 are artifacts.** It rebalances twice a year, so each year's t rests on two observations; one year's IC t is 54. Across years the IC averages +0.008 (t = +0.41), positive in 8 of 18.
- **The real relationships trade like the null's** (−1.6 against −1.4).

## Decisions made while building it

- **Windows:** the README's 30/60/90/180 were never run; the database shows 7, 14, 20, 21, 30 and 40. Both sets were searched (8 windows, 20 folded into 21). The headline uses 30, the only window in both lists.
- **Rolling 7-year training, not v1's whole history.**
  - The matrix form needs every stock to have every day, which over 20+ years would leave only old listings.
  - Seven years also leaves unseen years to test on.
  - Inside the window everything is v1's: the offset range comes from v1's own rule (2,500 days for 7 years), and pairing is exact by calendar day.
- **Universe:**
  - The first rule (a close on every day of the training years) left only 150 stocks in 2006–09 and about 300 in 2010–17. Stooq has holes: on 2011-02-17, 1,973 of 2,518 stocks have no close, and half-days are thin.
  - Before anything was graded, the rule became: listed from the first day, at most 1% of closes missing, and a missing close carried forward (v1 would skip the row, shifting its window by a day).
  - That gives about 300 stocks (289–307) for test years through 2011-12, because the survivor-only export lists few stocks from 2002, and 500 from 2012-13 on.
  - Everything was rerun with this rule.
- **The null for overlapping windows:**
  - The census flips each non-overlapping observation. Flipping each of v1's overlapping changes independently would wipe out their overlap and make chance look far weaker than it is.
  - So each *daily* return of the targets is flipped (one sign per day for all stocks) and the price path rebuilt; the w-row changes then overlap exactly as v1's do.
  - At offsets shorter than the window, a single draw's null r has nearly the same sign for every pair (the shared flip meets same-period co-movement). Null counts there swing between draws, and the null-picked replication share means nothing there. It is quoted only for offsets the money test can use.
- **How a relationship becomes a forecast (the main choice):**
  - The relationship says A's change dated t goes with B's change dated t + k.
  - To forecast B's next w rows, (d, d + w], the score uses A's change dated cal(d + w) − k, so only offsets of at least the window's calendar span can be used on day d.
  - The brief's literal recipe (A's change ending k days before d) instead pairs with B's change ending at d, which is already known. It was run as a sensitivity row.
- **Holding period:** v1's windows count trading rows, so "30 days" is 30 trading days, about six weeks. The headline holds 21 days, as the brief suggests and comparable with the census's 21-day test; holding all 30 was run too.
- **Unequal windows (w1 ≠ w2) were not searched.** v1 accepts them, but every saved row has equal windows.
- **Calendar misses count as nothing:** if the needed date is a weekend or holiday, that relationship adds nothing that day, as in v1. Future trading dates come from the export's calendar, with plain weekdays past its last day.
- **No score:** a stock with no usable relationship that day gets NaN, not 0. A leader with a missing close adds nothing.
- **The universe control** was added because the judge's random accounts draw from its whole liquid set, not from these 500 stocks.
- **Optional check base rate:** the first base rate (every offset at the same window, 0.48) was too generous to v1. The saved rows sit in 30 tight clusters of offsets, so a local base (offsets within ±100 days) was added after seeing that.
- **Speed:** float32 matrix products (r agrees with float64 to 4e−7). Computing the eight-window search plus three null draws on 2015–2022 takes about 10 minutes. The first replication run briefly used 18 threads (numpy was imported before the thread limits were set); it was stopped and rerun at 8.
