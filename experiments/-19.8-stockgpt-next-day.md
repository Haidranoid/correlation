# stockGPT: a small GPT that predicts the next day's move

**TL;DR:** stockai trained a 0.85M-parameter GPT to predict the next day's move the way a language model predicts the next word. It read 256 days of each stock's market-relative daily moves, binned into 101 tokens. There were 42 models: one per test year 2010–2023, three seeds each, each trained only on earlier data. It learned a real but small signal: rank IC +0.0124 against next-day returns (z = 5.85, positive in 13 of 14 years), with nothing on a null model and no leakage tripwire. That signal is mostly one-day reversal plus a lean toward calm stocks. As a daily $10,000 account it averaged +9.3% a year against +6.2% for turnover-matched random accounts, about 3 points a year, and was better in only 4 of 14 years. After costs it loses money: −4.7% a year at 5 bps, −35.9% at 20 bps. Verdict: a real gross signal but no edge as a strategy; the 5-day reversal rule and LightGBM did better.

## What was built

- **Tokens:** correlation2's census variable (the daily move relative to the market, in units of the stock's typical move), binned in 0.1 steps from −5 to +5, plus a "missing" token.
- **Extra daily inputs:** the market's move, the stock's log volatility.
- **Model:**
  - nanoGPT decoder: 4 layers, 4 heads, width 128, 256-day context, 852,480 parameters;
  - trained by next-token cross-entropy;
  - early-stopped on validation rank IC.
- **Splits:** training from 2005. A 252-day validation year sits before a 5-day gap, then the test year. The locked year (2024-06-28 on) is never touched.
- **Score:** the expected next-day move under the predicted bin probabilities. The ensemble averages 3 seeds.
- **The judge:** correlation2's harness forked into `stockai/judge/`. It reproduces correlation2's own results bit for bit (G0: 22 of 22 lines, 792 of 792 JSON fields).

**Variants:**
- **Weekly:** +11.4% / +8.3% / −0.7% against random +6.5% / +3.5% / −4.8%; 4 / 4 / 4 years.
- **Trading a day late:** 2 / 2 / 1 years.

## What it learned

- **Its scores** correlate −0.31 with today's move (one-day reversal) and −0.47 with the stock's volatility (a lean toward calm stocks).
- **Its picks are calm:** an average volatility decile of 2.0, against 4.5 for random accounts.
- **A one-line rule, minus today's move,** matches its IC (z = 5.70) but does worse on money (2 / 2 / 2 years).
- **Why costs win:** the daily account replaces about 61% of its names each day, so costs take about 1.2 × c bps a day, about 15% a year at 5 bps.
- **The persistent part:** canaries (scores made 20 days stale, or shuffled within a year) keep part of the IC. About half of that is the low-volatility tilt; the other half is a slower trait not yet identified. It is not a leak.

## Found along the way

- **The census-unit artifact.** correlation2's variable subtracts each stock's trailing-year mean. So part of tomorrow's value, called k, is known today. A score that leans against last year's performance gets "census-unit IC" for free. Every comparison here uses actual returns. Details are in `-4.9-lightgbm-next-day.md`.
- **Two look-aheads in correlation2's data:** the $5 price rule uses split-adjusted prices, and the bad-print repair looks one day ahead.
