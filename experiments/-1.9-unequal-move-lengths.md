# Unequal move lengths: does A's week predict B's month?

**TL;DR:** This tested whether, when stock A moves over one period, stock B moves the same way over the period that follows. It covered 20 combinations of A's and B's move lengths fixed in advance, from a day to half a year, including your example of a week followed by a month. It used every day as an observation and a new chance comparison: B's daily market residuals (A's, when A's move is the longer one) are sign-flipped in blocks and the census variable is rebuilt from them. On 220 pure-noise series this comparison comes out at 0.99× chance for long moves, where the old one gives 1.87× (3.4× for a quarter followed by half a year). It never shows excess, but it can be a little cautious: down to 0.73× in combinations where the flipped side is noise that looks nothing like stock returns (sparse 0/1 events, counts). So it missed the calibration rule fixed beforehand for 10 combinations, all on the cautious side. Against it, real stock pairs come out at 0.80–1.09× chance. Only a week followed by 2 weeks reaches 1.09×, and no combination is more than 1.8 standard deviations above chance. The strongest pairs keep their sign in the next 5-year period 50.2–53.0% of the time, against about 50% for pairs picked from the null. That faint tilt clears two standard deviations for 5 combinations, but the next period's correlation is at most 0.007. In the money test ($10,000 in the top 20 picks, 18 walk-forward years), the 20 combinations averaged **+0.1 points a year** over turnover-matched random accounts, from −3.2 to +3.0. The best combined p after 5 bps was 0.08, against the 0.0025 required. Only a day → a week was ahead in two-thirds of the years (14 of 18), and not since 2020 (3 of 5). Your week → month came out at 0.94× chance, with 50.6% of top pairs keeping their sign against 50.4% for chance. It made +2.6 points a year over random (p = 0.13, ahead in 10 of 18 years and in 2 of the 5 since 2020). So no: after A's week, B does not reliably move the same way over the following month. In the 16 unseen months the combinations averaged −5.6 points against random, with 5 of 20 ahead (best p 0.14). Week → month made +16.3% against +19.5% for the median random account. Verdict: no combination of move lengths predicts. Unequal and long move lengths are at chance, like the rest of the census.

## The question in the project's terms

- **A's move length:** the number of days A's move is measured over, for example 5 = one week.
- **B's move length:** the same for B, for example 21 = one month.
- **Offset:** trading days from the end of A's move to the end of B's move. Offset = B's move length means B's month starts the day after A's week ends: "A moves first, B follows right after". Larger offsets leave a gap between them.

## Why long moves need care

- **Few independent observations.** The census uses non-overlapping observations, stepping by the longer of the two move lengths. A 5-year correlation period then has 60 observations for week → month, and 10 for anything involving half a year. That is too few to see anything.
- **The current null is too lenient at long moves.** Pure-noise series show 2.4× "excess" at 63-day moves under the sign-flip null, because long moves are persistent.

The fix for both:
- **Overlapping observations:** use every day.
- **A block sign-flip null:** flip signs in blocks at least as long as the longer move, so the null has the same persistence as the data.
- **Calibration first:** before anything else, rerun the 220 pure-noise series from the outside-data test (`+4.0-outside-data-leaders.md`). Every combination must come out at 1.0× chance for them.

## Tests, in order

1. **Calibration:** noise series at 1.0× chance for all 20 combinations with the block null. If not, fix the null before reading any result.
2. **Real vs chance per combination**, over the full history and over rolling 5-year correlation periods.
3. **Replication per combination:** do the strongest pairs keep their sign in the next period more often than chance-picked pairs (as `c2 census replicate` does)?
4. **Money test per combination:**
   - `c2 census holdout --rebalance B` restricted to pairs with A's move length = a;
   - each pick is held for B's move length;
   - turnover-matched random guessers;
   - walk-forward 2006–2025, plus the 16 unseen months (2025-06-30..2026-10-19).
5. **Pass rule (fixed now), with 20 combinations tried:**
   - beats random guessers at p < 0.0025 (0.05 ÷ 20) after 5 bps per trade in the walk-forward;
   - ahead in at least two-thirds of years, including 2020 on;
   - consistent on the unseen months.

## What to expect

On today's numbers the short combinations are already at chance (0.99–1.07×), and the money tests at weekly and monthly targets found nothing (`-11.4-census-picks.md`: +0.0 and −0.2 points a year against random). Those money tests used the first census, whose 15 combinations all had A's move at least as long as B's. So a short A followed by a longer B, your example, has been counted against chance but never traded. That part is open, along with the long combinations, which also have the least data. So the most likely result is a firm "no" for all 20. The fair null for long moves and the per-combination money test would be useful for every later census either way.

## Where to change things

- The census grid's move-length list is `Config::horizons` in `crates/c2-census/src/grid.rs`; `Config::full()` uses [1, 3, 5, 10, 21, 63]. Adding 42 and 126 computes all 49 combinations of [1, 5, 10, 21, 42, 63, 126]. The 20 above are the ones fixed in advance for pass/fail; the rest are reported.
- The holdout reads stored pairs by B's move length (`crates/c2-census/src/holdout.rs`); a filter on A's move length is the only addition needed there.

## Decisions made while building it

Each item says when it was decided. "Before results" means before any real stock-to-stock result (real vs chance, replication or money) had been computed. Calibration on noise was looked at, as the plan says, before anything real.

**The chance comparison.**
- **Flipping `v` itself can't be made fair (before results, from a simulation on pure noise).** The census variable is (residual − its trailing 252-day mean) ÷ its trailing 252-day sd. That trailing mean makes every stock's long moves slightly anti-persistent over about a year. Any sign flip applied to `v`, per day or in blocks of 63 to 630 days, gave 1.3× to 3× "excess" at |z| ≥ 4 on simulated noise for moves of 21 days and longer. This is the same mechanism behind the old null's 2.4× at 63-day moves.
- **So the null flips market residuals, then rebuilds `v` (before results).** The daily market residual (log return − beta × market, exactly as `crates/c2-census/src/variable.rs` computes it) is demeaned over the rows used, multiplied by block signs, and put through the census's own standardizing again (trailing 252-day mean and sd, at least 126 days, clipped at ±5). Moves are then built from that. Recomputing `v` from the export's closes and market reproduces it to 3e-6 with the same missing days, so the null and the real data are built the same way.
- **The side with the longer move is flipped (after a first one-period calibration look, before results).** The plan flips B. On the first calibration period, stocks A → noise B came out at 1.14–1.55× for the three combinations where B is shorter than A. The reason: the spread of r depends on the product of A's and B's own autocorrelations. If the flipped side is the shorter one, the other side's wide overlap pattern meets whatever persistence the blocks break. Flipping the longer side leaves only the shorter side's narrow pattern exposed. So B is flipped in combinations 1–17 and A in 18–20. Either is a valid chance comparison when nothing leads.
- **Blocks (before results):** L = max(63, 2 × the longer move) trading days (63 for moves up to a month, 84 for two months, 126 for a quarter, 252 for half a year). The same signs for every stock, so volatility alignment and same-day structure are kept. Half the blocks are +1 and half −1, shuffled, with a random starting phase, so a steady relationship cancels in the null.
- **One sign sequence per null copy over the whole history (after the second calibration run, before results).** At first each period drew new signs. But the rolling periods overlap by 4 of 5 years, so the real data is reused across them while the null copies weren't, which understates how much the real total can vary by up to √5. Now each combination and null copy has one sign sequence over all days, and each period takes its slice.
- **8 null copies per matrix (before results)** (the census used 3), so the null's own spread can be measured.
- **z with overlapping observations (before results):** z = atanh(r) × √(n ÷ c − 3), where n is the number of overlapping observations and c = Σ over lags of (1 − |lag|/a)(1 − |lag|/b), the overlap factor for move lengths a and b. For a day vs a week c = 1; for a week vs a month c = 4.6; for a quarter vs half a year c = 52.5. It only puts z on a familiar scale; real and chance use the same z, so the ratio doesn't depend on it.
- **Stock pairs with themselves (A = B) are left out (before results).**

**Correlation periods (before results).**
- **One 5-year period per test year:** the 1,260 trading days before each test year's first day (2006-06-28, 2007-06-28, …, 2024-06-28), plus one ending at the last day of the old export (2025-06-27). These 20 periods are the "rolling 5-year correlation periods". The money test uses exactly the period that ends before each test year.
- **The data start limits the first periods.** `v` exists from 2003-12-31, for only about 300 stocks until 2006 (the store has about 320 stocks before 2005). The 2006, 2007 and 2008 periods are therefore shorter (627, 878 and 1,131 days). The periods for test years 2006–2010 contain only those long-lived stocks (283–301), because the 90% rule needs data from the period's start. For the same reason the full history (2003-12-31..2025-06-27) has 308 stocks.
- **Observations:** every day where A's move and B's move both lie inside the period, so periods 5 years apart use disjoint data.
- **Universe per period:** at least 90% of the period's days with a value; median price ≥ $5 and median dollar volume ≥ $5M over its last 252 days; capped at the 1,500 with the highest median dollar volume (for compute). Missing days count as 0 in a move, as in the census.

**Calibration.**
- **220 noise series, 11 kinds × 20 (before results):** iid normal; fat-tailed; GARCH(1,1); AR(1) with φ = 0.3, 0.7 (plus a yearly cycle, like a temperature), 0.9 and 0.97; weekly values with zeros between; monthly values; sparse 0/1 events (3% of days); Poisson counts. Each goes through the census's standardizing as if it were a residual. iid daily values summed into long moves are the "random-walk increments" case.
- **Both directions (before results):** noise A → real stocks B (the outside-data test's headline direction), and real stocks A → noise B.
- **Pass rule (before results):** for every combination, real/chance for noise at |z| ≥ 4, summed over the 20 rolling periods and the full history, between 0.90 and 1.10, or within two standard errors of 1.0.
- **The standard error comes from the 220 independent noise series (after the first full calibration run, before results).** The first version used the spread of the 8 null copies, which ignores how much a single noise series can sway the count.
- **For comparison (before results), the same noise also goes through** the old method (non-overlapping observations stepping by the longer move, each observation's sign flipped) and overlapping observations with blocks of `v` flipped.
- **Outcome, and what was done about it (after the final calibration, before results).** The rule was missed for 10 combinations, all below 0.90 (cautious) and none above. The null was not changed again: the misses come from noise kinds unlike stock residuals; the direction that matches the real test (a real stock flipped) passes for 19 of 20; and stock-like noise gives 0.996 and 1.006 overall. Real ratios are judged against 1.0 as fixed, with the stock-like noise ratio printed beside them.

**Criteria for each combination.**
1. **Real vs chance (before results):** "excess" means real/chance ≥ 1.10 at |z| ≥ 4 over the rolling periods, and the real count above chance by at least 3 standard deviations of the null copies' counts. It is judged against 1.0. The noise ratio for the same combination (continuous noise kinds, noise A → stocks B) is printed beside it as the calibration reference (after calibration, before results).
2. **Replication (before results):** for each period from 2006 to 2020, the 2,000 strongest real pairs (by |z|, pooled over the three offsets) and the 2,000 strongest of each null copy. Their sign is compared with the real r of the same A, B, move lengths and offset in the period 5 years later, which uses disjoint data (both stocks need ≥ 90% of days there). It passes if the real pairs' same-sign rate beats the null copies' mean by at least 2 standard deviations of the 8 copies' rates.
3. **Money test (before results):**
   - **Pairs:** the 2,000 strongest real pairs by |z|, pooled over the three offsets, from the 5-year period that ends the day before the test year. The FDR rule was not used because in the census it almost never fires for lagged pairs.
   - **Scores:** B's score on day d is the sum of r × A's move over a days ending at d + b − offset (always ≤ d). Stocks with no pair get no score.
   - **Grading:** `c2eval.judge(path, rebalance=b)`: walk-forward on the old export, 1,000 turnover-matched random accounts, costs 0, 5 and 20 bps. The judge skips 2006-07 for every family, because no stock has its 756 days of history before then (the 5-day reversal check skips it too), so 18 years are graded: 2007-08..2024-25.
   - **"p < 0.0025 after 5 bps":** the 18 yearly p-values at 5 bps (the share of random accounts that did as well) combined with Stouffer's method (each clipped to 0.0005..0.9995), one-sided.
   - **"Ahead in at least two-thirds of years, including 2020 on":** at 5 bps, ahead of the median random account in at least 12 of the 18 years, and in at least 4 of the 5 years from 2020-21.
   - **"Consistent on the unseen months":** ahead of the median random account at 5 bps over 2025-06-30..2026-10-19.
   - A combination passes only if all three hold.
4. **Unseen months (before results):** all 20 combinations, run once at the end on the new export, with pairs from the 1,260 days before 2025-06-30.
5. **Sanity check of the grading path (before results):** 5-day reversal through `c2eval.judge` gave +11.8 points a year, 8 of 18 years, long-short 4.39 bps/day, z 2.93, exactly as expected.

**After the results.**
- **Median-null ratio column** in `real_vs_chance.txt` (see "Real vs chance"). Added for reading only; the verdicts use the rule fixed beforehand.
- **A parsing fix in `money.py`** for the unseen months: a single holdout's JSON keeps its costs under `report.strategy`, so the first attempt stopped after the judge's first run, before printing any number. The judge is deterministic and was rerun on identical inputs.

**Departures from the plan (all before results).**
- **Python/numpy instead of the Rust census,** as asked. "Where to change things" above was not used.
- **Overlapping observations** (every day) instead of the census's non-overlapping grid, as the plan says. The census itself was not rerun with 42- and 126-day moves.
- **Own 220 noise series.** The outside-data test's 220 are generated in memory by its Rust example. These follow the same idea (11 kinds × 20) and add AR(1) persistence.
- **The money test uses `c2eval.judge` with a scores file** rather than `c2 census holdout --rebalance B` with a filter on A's move length. It is the same harness, with turnover-matched random accounts.
- **The walk-forward is 2007–2025, not 2006–2025:** the judge skips 2006-07 for every family.
- **CPU only.** The GPU wasn't needed.
