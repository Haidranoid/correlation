# Bystanders

**TL;DR:** This tested whether a stock's usual co-movers that moved with it on its news day while trading normal volume themselves ("bystanders") give the move back over the next 1–10 days, beyond the plain reversal of the same stocks' own quiet, same-size moves. They don't. Bystanders are common (a median of 590 a month since 2010), and on a solo news day co-movers move only about a tenth of what their ordinary co-movement predicts. But over 2005–2019 a bystander's next 5 days are unrelated to its sympathy move (slope +0.007, t +0.2), while the same stocks' own quiet moves reverse (−0.034, t −2.0). The bystander effect is +0.041 (t +1.1): the wrong sign, against a pass mark of t ≤ −3. The twist, a news-day response learned for each pair, barely passes its persistence check (z = 2.07) and predicts no give-back either. Bystanders instead follow A's later path, continuing when A keeps going and reversing when it reverses. 2020–2026 gives the same wrong sign (+0.045, t +1.6), and so do the 16 unseen months alone (+0.052, t +0.8). In the money test the judge's account beat random by +3.3 points a year (se 2.7, ahead in 10 of 18 years), with a long-short of −0.9 bps/day (z +0.1). In the unseen months the long-only account beat random (p = 0.03), but the long-short had t +0.8. Verdict: no give-back beyond plain reversal; it fails Test 3 and the unseen years.

**Question:** when one company has news, do the stocks that usually move with it get dragged along that day and then give the move back?

## The idea

On a company's news day (earnings, guidance, a lawsuit, an FDA decision), its usual co-movers often move with it, though they had no news of their own. Sector funds, baskets and trading programs propagate A's move mechanically, because B normally moves with A.

The words used here:
- **A moves first:** A has its own news.
- **B follows at offset 0:** B moves the same day.
- **B gives it back at offsets 1–10.**
- **Bystander:** a co-mover with no news of its own.

We don't need a news feed to find these. News shows up as volume:
- **A's news day:** A trades several times its normal volume and makes a big move.
- **A bystander:** a usual co-mover of A that traded *normal* volume that day but still moved with A. Nobody was trading B for B's own reasons; it moved only because A did.

**The twist: two kinds of together.**
- Ordinary same-day co-movement says how B moves with A on a typical day. It doesn't say how B responds to *A's own news*. A rival may gain when A loses a contract; a supplier may not care about A's lawsuit.
- So for every pair we learn a second number from A's past news days: B's **news-day response**.
- A bystander whose news-day response is near zero or negative, but who moved with A anyway, is the clearest case of a move that doesn't belong, and should give back the most.

## Why it might work where the census didn't

- **The census averaged over every day.** If the effect lives on news days (2.65% of liquid stock-days on this data), an average over all days dilutes it about 40-fold.
- **It's rare, and each trade is big:**
  - Daily lead-lag needs 40% of the book traded every day for a few bps.
  - Here the selected sympathy moves are 2–4%, so a give-back of even 10–20% of the move (20–80 bps) clears a 10 bps round trip.
- **Your own data hints at it.** Peer momentum came out slightly *reversed* (z = −2.79): stocks whose co-movers rose did a little worse afterwards. That is what a give-back looks like when it's averaged over all days.
- **The volume rule is what nobody has used.** Papers on peers' reactions to news use earnings dates and industry codes. They don't separate co-movers that were trading on their own news from co-movers that were just pulled along.

## What's new (nearest prior work)

No published work combines the pieces below; this is from a search of SSRN, NBER, arXiv and journals in October 2026. The parts closest to existing work:

| Work | What they did | How this differs |
|---|---|---|
| Thomas & Zhang 2008, *JAR* | Industry (SIC) peers react to early earnings announcers. The reaction is an overreaction, corrected at the peer's own later announcement (about 1% a quarter, 1973–2005). | Earnings dates and SIC peers; outcome measured at the peer's next announcement. Here: news found from volume, peers from co-movement, bystanders from their own quiet volume, a 1–10 day give-back. |
| Chung, Hrazdil & Trottier 2014, 2015 | That overreaction disappears in recent years of heavy trading. | A warning for this window. The base-rate check below is step one. |
| Zhang, Jiang & Young 2026, *RAS* | Algorithmic trading through sector ETFs reduces peers' overreaction. | Evidence on the mechanism; here it's tested without holdings data. |
| Brochet, Kolev & Lerman 2018, *RAS* | Split peers into "true peers" and "rivals" by correlation of sales growth; partial reversal between the earnings release and the call. | Rivals from accounting data, measured within the day. Here: rivals learned from returns on A's past news days, predicting a multi-day give-back. |
| Frankel, Godigbe & Rabier 2025, *JAR* | Peer co-movement is lower on earnings dates than on other days. | In aggregate. Here it's pair by pair and used to predict. |
| Chan 2003; Savor 2012; Boudoukh, Feldman, Kogan & Richardson 2019 | A stock's own extreme moves reverse without news and drift with news. | Those are a stock's own moves. Here it's a co-mover's move on *someone else's* news. |
| Chen, Chen, Chen & Li 2019, *Management Science* | Each stock's 50 most-correlated stocks; divergence from them reverses the next month. | No news conditioning, monthly. |
| Llorente, Michaely, Saar & Wang 2002, *RFS* | High-volume moves reverse when trading is for risk sharing and continue when informed. | A stock's own volume and own return. |
| Medhat & Schmeling 2022, *RFS*, "Short-term momentum" | Last month's moves on low turnover reverse; moves on high turnover continue, strongest in large caps. | A stock's own move. Bystanders are low-volume movers by definition, so this is the baseline to beat: the test compares them with the stock's own same-size, same-volume moves on non-event days. |
| Honkanen & Schmidt 2022, *RAPS* | Fire-sale price pressure spills to economic peers at about a third of the size, and reverses. | Needs fund-flow data. |
| Hameed, Morck, Shen & Yeung 2015, *RFS*; Ramnath 2002, *JAR* | Thinly covered stocks follow bellwethers with a lag (underreaction). | The opposite direction. The "catch-up" arm below tests it. |
| Engelberg, Gao & Jagannathan 2009 | A pair that splits on one stock's news stays split; a split from liquidity converges. | Argues against the catch-up arm. |

The evidence on direction is mixed: overreaction then reversal in some studies, underreaction then drift in others. That is a reason to test it, not a reason to expect a result.

## Data

Everything needed is already in the price store: daily OHLCV for about 9,500 US stocks.
- **News-day frequency:** volume at least 4× the 60-day median occurs on 2.65% of liquid stock-days since 2005, about 6.7 times per stock per year. The quarterly reporting rhythm is visible: the gaps between spikes peak at 64 trading days.
- **Spikes cluster:** 34% of gaps are 1–2 days, so events must be de-duplicated.
- **Opens:** usable from 2008 on. Before then, 10–19% of liquid rows have an open exactly equal to the previous close, which looks filled in. Entry at the next open is therefore tested on 2008+.

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Universe:**
- Stocks only; the funds that Stooq files as stocks (SPYM, SPYU, ETHD …) are dropped.
- Close ≥ $5, and 60-day median dollar volume ≥ $5M, all exchanges.
- Returns are market-relative (equal-weight market, leaving out A on A's own news day).

**Co-movers of A:** the 20 stocks with the highest same-day correlation with A over the preceding 252-day correlation period, with correlation ≥ 0.3. B's ordinary response to A is the slope of B on A over that period.

**A's news day:**
- A's volume is at least 4× its median of the previous 60 days.
- A's market-relative move is at least 3× its 60-day standard deviation.
- It is the first such day in 5 days.
- **Solo:** fewer than 20% of A's co-movers have their own volume at least 2× normal that day. That rules out sector-wide news such as an oil shock.

**Bystander:** a co-mover with volume at most 1.5× its 60-day median that day. A co-mover with volume at least 2× normal is a **participant**: the comparison group.

**Sympathy move:** B's market-relative return on the day.

**News-day response:**
- The slope of B on A over A's earlier solo news days only: at least 8 of them, all before the day being scored.
- Shrunk toward the ordinary response, with a weight of n / (n + 8) on the news-day slope after n events.
- **Mismatch** = sympathy move − news-day response × A's move.

**Outcome:** B's market-relative return over 1, 5 and 10 days after the event.
- Close-to-close, from the event day's close.
- Also, from 2008, from the next open, so the trade doesn't use the close it trades at.

## Tests, in order (stop at the first failure)

1. **Base rate.**
   - How many qualifying bystanders per month, by year?
   - How big is the average sympathy move relative to the ordinary-response prediction? Frankel et al. suggest co-movement is weaker on news days.
   - **Stop if** fewer than about 20 a month after 2010.
2. **Is the news-day response a property of the pair?**
   - Run it the way `c2 census replicate` runs: does the gap between news-day response and ordinary response keep its sign from one 5-year period to the next more often than chance?
   - **If not,** drop the twist and keep the plain bystander test.
3. **Give-back beyond plain reversal.** This is the key test, because the nearest known effect is short-term reversal, which the harness already detects.
   - Regress each bystander's future return on its sympathy move. Do the same for B's own same-size moves on days with similar volume and no event: the **matched reversal**.
   - The bystander effect is the difference between the two slopes.
   - **Stop unless** it is more negative than matched reversal with t ≥ 3 over 2005–2019.
   - Also report:
     - participants (expected to give back less);
     - the twist (mismatch in place of the sympathy move);
     - a diagnostic split by whether A itself kept going over the next 5 days (real news at A) or reversed (noise everywhere).
4. **The unseen years.** The same rules on 2020–2026 must give the same sign with t ≥ 2.
5. **Money test** (the existing harness, as a new family):
   - **Trades:** each day, for that day's events, long bystanders that fell in sympathy and short those that rose. Positions are equal dollar, held 5 days, and market-neutral. Long-only isn't trusted, because the data has only survivors.
   - **Random guessers:** the same number of trades per day, drawn from same-day quiet co-movers of the same events, with random direction.
   - **Report:** walk-forward by year, gross bps per trade, trades per day, and net results at 5 and 10 bps per side.
   - **Pass:** beats random guessers at p < 0.05 after 5 bps per side, and positive after costs in at least two-thirds of years.

**The cost hurdle:**
- A round trip at 5 bps per side costs 10 bps.
- With 1.5σ+ sympathy moves (typically 2–4%), the mean give-back must be at least about 3–5% of the move.
- Turnover is set by the number of events, not the calendar. Expect tens of trades a day, each held 5 days.

## Ways it can fool us

- **Same-day news we can't see.** B's news may be in A's news (a merger between the two). Exclude pairs where both have volume ≥ 2×.
- **A mega-cap moves the market.** On A's day an equal-weight market without A keeps the benchmark clean.
- **Event-day closes include auction pressure** that reverses overnight; this is documented by Bogousslavsky & Muravyev. Check this with the next-open entry from 2008 on, and drop index-rebalance and quarterly expiry days.
- **Multiple testing:** three horizons, two arms and the twist. Report all of them, and only trust the pre-chosen 5-day bystander test for pass/fail.

### What it means

- **Bystander moves are not mechanical drag.** They follow A's later path in both directions and don't reverse on average. A co-mover moving with A on A's news, even on quiet volume, is mostly the market pricing what the news means for B.
- **The twist's idea, that news days differ from ordinary days, is real but already covers almost everything.** Co-movement nearly vanishes on A's news days, and that holds across all pairs. What is left that is specific to a pair is small (about 2 points of sign persistence) and doesn't predict returns.
- **Caveats:** the data holds only survivors. The judge's long-short is thin (about 3 names a side). Drawing the matched days at random moves the matched slope by up to about 0.01 between runs, far less than the gap to the pass line.

## Decisions made while building it

All of these were made **before any outcome (future-return) data was looked at**, unless marked otherwise. Order of work: panels and the event table built, Test 1 run (it uses no future returns), these decisions written down, then Tests 2–5.

**Data and universe**
- **Funds filed as stocks** are found by a fixed rule, not a hand list: an export "stock" is dropped if its daily returns correlate at |r| ≥ 0.95 (≥ 250 shared days) with 5 or more store ETFs, or with a store ETF whose price history starts earlier, or with another export stock whose history starts earlier at a slope outside 0.85–1.15 (a leveraged or inverse fund on that stock; share classes such as GOOG/GOOGL, slope ≈ 1, are kept). This drops 51 instruments in the old export (SPYU, JPEF, GPIQ, SOXQ, TSLL, NVDL, AAPU, ETHD …, plus a warrant, OXY-WS, and a preferred, ALB_A). Real stocks with single-stock ETFs on them (TSLA, NVDA, AAPL, COIN) are kept. Closed-end funds that track less tightly than 0.95 (ADX, RVT, GDV) stay in. List: `panels_<tag>/funds.json`. This was the first version of the rule; it was changed after the old-export results (see the end of this section).
- **Universe on day d:** close on d−1 ≥ $5, median dollar volume over d−60..d−1 ≥ $5M (≥ 40 days), a return on day d, not a fund. So the rule never uses the event day's own price or volume.
- **Returns** are simple close-to-close returns from the export's closes. **Market** is my own equal-weight mean over the universe, kept as a daily sum and count so A can be left out exactly: on A's news day, A's move, every co-mover's sympathy move and A's earlier news-day moves use (sum − r_A)/(count − 1). All other days use the plain equal-weight market.
- **Volume** is share volume from the price store (`c2eval.load_store` + `store_panel`), as a ratio to its median over d−60..d−1 (≥ 40 positive days). Dollar volume is used only for the liquidity rule.
- **The plan's data note is not reproduced.** With the fixed universe, volume ≥ 4× its 60-day median occurs on 1.23% of liquid stock-days since 2005 (3.1 times per stock per year), not 2.65%. Volume ≥ 3× gives 2.60%, so the note probably used a looser threshold or universe. The gap histogram does peak at 64 trading days, as stated. The rules section says 4×, so 4× is kept.
- **Thin history before 2005:** the store has only about 320 of these stocks before March 2005, so events in 2005 have few co-movers with 200 days of shared history. The 2005–2019 test period in practice starts in 2006.

**Definitions**
- **News day:** volume ≥ 4× and |market-relative move| ≥ 3 × the sd of market-relative returns over d−60..d−1 (a "spike"), A in the universe, and no spike for A on d−5..d−1.
- **Co-movers:** correlation of market-relative returns over d−252..d−1 (pairwise-complete, ≥ 200 shared days), among stocks in the universe on day d; top 20 with r ≥ 0.3. The census correlates a vol-scaled residual instead; market-relative returns were chosen so that the ordinary response, sympathy move and mismatch are all in the same units. **Ordinary response** = OLS slope (with intercept) of B on A over the same days.
- **Solo:** fewer than 20% of the co-movers have volume ≥ 2×. A news day with no co-movers counts as solo: it has no bystanders, but it still counts in A's history of news days.
- **Bystander:** a co-mover with volume ≤ 1.5×. The fixed rules put no size or sign filter on its move, so none is used; the regression on the move handles size. The subset that moved with A by ≥ 1.5 sd (the cost paragraph's "selected sympathy moves") is reported separately. **Participant:** volume ≥ 2×.
- **One row per stock-day:** if B is a co-mover of several As on one day, the row of the most correlated A is kept (the sympathy move is the same; the mismatch comes from that pair).
- **Quarterly expiry and index-rebalance days** (third Friday of March, June, September and December, moved to the day before if a holiday, and the Russell reconstitution day) are dropped as event days everywhere: bystanders, participants and matched days. They still count as news days for the first-in-5-days rule, the ±5-day exclusion and A's history of news days.
- **News-day response:** OLS slope (with intercept) of B's move on A's move over all of A's solo news days before the event day on which B was in the universe; at least 8; shrunk toward the ordinary response with weight n/(n+8). Events whose A has fewer than 8 earlier solo news days have no news-day response and no mismatch.
- **Outcome:** B's buy-and-hold return over d+1..d+h minus the equal-weight market's, h = 1, 5, 10. **Next-open entry (2008 on):** B's return from the open of d+1 to the close of d+h, minus the market's (the market's open-to-close on d+1 from the opens of universe stocks, then close-to-close).

**Test 2 (is the news-day response a property of the pair?)**
- Five-year periods 2005–09, 2010–14, 2015–19 and 2020–25. Pairs are the co-mover pairs seen on A's solo news days in a period. In each period: the news-day slope over A's solo news days in that period (≥ 8, B in the universe) and the ordinary slope over all days of the period (≥ 250 shared days).
- **The raw gap can persist for a trivial reason:** if news days attenuate co-movement for every pair (Frankel et al.; Test 1 shows they do, strongly), the gap is negative for nearly all pairs in every period. So the criterion uses the **residual gap**: within each period, the news-day slope minus its cross-pair OLS fit on the ordinary slope. The raw gap and a within-A shuffle (does it persist beyond A's own tendency?) are reported too.
- **Pass:** the residual gap keeps its sign more than 50% of the time with z ≥ 2 (standard error clustered by A), pooled over the two transitions inside 2005–2019. The 2015–19 → 2020–25 transition is reported but not used, to keep 2020+ unseen.
- **If it fails,** the money test uses −sympathy move; if it passes, −mismatch on events that have one.

**Test 3 (give-back beyond matched reversal)**
- **Matched days**, per the given recipe: B's own days in t−252..t−6 (the same year as the event, before it), B in the universe, volume 0.5–1.5× its 60-day median, no spike for B or for any of that day's As within ±5 days, not an expiry day, and a market-relative move of the same sign within ±25% of the sympathy move. Up to 5 drawn at random (seed 20261021), deduplicated on (stock, day). An event with no matched day is dropped from both sets, and the count is reported.
- **Slope difference and its t in one regression:** y = a + b·move + c·I + d·move·I over both sets, I = 1 for events; d is the bystander effect. Standard errors are Liang–Zeger, clustered by date (event date for events, matched date for matched days), with the CR1 small-sample factor. Date clusters don't cover the overlap of nearby 5- and 10-day windows, so calendar-month clusters are shown as a check.
- **Pass:** d < 0 with t ≤ −3 at h = 5, events dated 2005–2019. h = 1 and 10, participants, the twist, the A-kept-going split and next-open entry are reported but don't decide.
- **Turnover-conditioned reversal** (Medhat & Schmeling 2022) is the matched set itself: the same stock's own quiet-volume, same-size moves.

**Test 4 (unseen years)**
- First 2020-01-01..2025-06-27 on the old export. Then, once, at the very end, the new export (through 2026-10-19): the 16 months alone, and 2020–2026 pooled from the new export, which holds all of it in one data source. Pass: h = 5 slope difference of the same sign with t ≥ 2 (date clusters) on 2020–2026.

**Test 5 (money)**
- **Judge scores:** for each bystander event (B, t), score = −mismatch (twist passed) or −sympathy move (twist failed) on days t..t+4. If B has several events in that span, the scores add up (the unreverted sympathy pressure stacks). NaN everywhere else. `c2eval.judge(..., rebalance=1)`, walk-forward 2006-07 .. 2024-25; the unseen months on the new export with `walk_forward=False, start='2025-06-30'`.
- **The plan's own money test**, run as well: on each event day, trade every bystander event (one per stock-day) against its sympathy move (or its mismatch, if the twist passed), equal dollar, 5-day hold, P&L = direction × B's 5-day market-relative return, so the book is market-neutral. Costs are 2 × c per trade at c = 5 and 10 bps per side. Random guessers: 1,000 draws of the same number of trades per day from the same day's bystanders, random direction; p = share of draws with total P&L at least the strategy's. Years are the judge's (June to June). Pass: p < 0.05 and net-positive at 5 bps per side in at least two-thirds of the years.

**Changed or added after seeing results**
- **The fund rule (after the old-export results, before any new-export outcome was computed).** On the new export, rule (i) (tracks 5 or more ETFs) flagged NVDA, TSLA, AMD, AVGO, MSTR and PLTR as funds: by 2026 each has many single-stock ETFs on it. Rule (i) was dropped. Instead, the two "tracks an older instrument" rules are also checked over the export's last 504 days, which catches funds whose early Stooq prices track loosely.
  - **Old export:** 53 dropped. PSLV (a silver trust) and TSLQ (an inverse TSLA fund) are added, and PHYS stays.
  - **New export:** 76 dropped, including SPYM, the bitcoin and ether trusts and the leveraged single-stock funds. The six real stocks are kept.
  - **All old-export tests were rerun** on the corrected universe, and every number in this file comes from that rerun. The key numbers barely moved: Test 3's difference went from +0.044 (t +1.25) to +0.041 (t +1.14), Test 2's z from +2.36 to +2.07, and the file-name number from +3.32 to +3.31. The first run's outputs are kept in `research/bystanders/out_first_fund_rule/`.
- **Test 2 is close to its line** (z = 2.07). The twist was kept either way, and it made no difference: neither the mismatch nor the sympathy move gives back.
- **The 2015–19 → 2020–25 row of Test 2** was computed in the same run as the deciding rows, so it was seen before Test 3. It uses no future returns.
- **Test 4's "same sign"** is read as the hypothesis's sign (negative). The 2005–2019 estimate was already positive, and 2020–2026 is positive with t +1.59, so it fails on either reading.
- **The judge skipped 2006-07** ("only 0 stocks can be picked"), as it does for the 5-day reversal check, so the walk-forward has 18 years.
- **Single holdout:** `c2eval.change_over_random` reads the walk-forward layout, so for the unseen months `money.py` passes it `{'costs': report['strategy']}`. c2eval itself is unchanged.
- **The trade-terms diagnostic** (the plan's trade on the matched quiet days, `diag_trade.py`) was added after the results, to set the plan's money test beside plain reversal.
- **Base-rate months:** the 2026 row divides by 9.56 months (January 1 to October 19).
- **The unseen months were graded twice by the judge, with identical scores.** The first `money.py new` run graded both scores files, then crashed while reading the judge's single-holdout JSON (see above). It was rerun after fixing only the reading code. No rule, score or parameter changed between the two runs.

## Files

**Code** (`research/bystanders/`, system `python3` with numpy, `OMP_NUM_THREADS=8 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=8`, CPU only):
- `common.py`: panels for one export (returns, universe, equal-weight market with leave-one-out sums, 60-day sd, volume ratio from the store, spikes, opens) and the fund rule; also the expiry-day calendar.
