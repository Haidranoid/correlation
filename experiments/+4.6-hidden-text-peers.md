# Hidden peers: do companies that describe the same business, but don't trade together, predict each other?

**TL;DR:** Do companies that describe the same business in their annual reports, but whose stocks don't move together, predict each other a month later? No: buying stocks whose hidden peers rose and shorting those whose peers fell made −0.11% a month after costs (t −0.33; 2.5 needed). The 20 top-ranked stocks beat SPY by +4.6 points a year after costs, but not significantly, peers that do move together did as well, and 2019-20 carries much of it. Verdict: no edge.

## Nearest prior work

| Work | What it found |
|---|---|
| Hoberg & Phillips 2016, *JPE* | Text-based industries (TNIC) from 10-K business descriptions |
| Hoberg & Phillips 2018, *JFQA* | Text-based industry momentum, strongest from less visible peers; no replication found |
| Cohen & Frazzini 2008, *JF* | Investors are slow to price links between economically related firms |
| This project, `+0.1-imposters.md` and `-5.2-peer-momentum.md` | Co-movement drifts toward the text group without a price correction. Correlation peers reverse slightly |

**What's new:** text peers selected for *not* co-moving, the market's blind spot. A published effect's variant, new to this data.

**Novelty check (2026-10-21, a separate search; it changed no rules):** partly done, combining price-correlation and text networks to find less visible peers is published; text peers selected for low co-movement were not found.
- **Hoberg & Phillips 2018** (JFQA, "Text-based industry momentum"): Their "less visible" peers are 10-K text peers that don't share a SIC code; shocks to them produce large momentum profits, stronger than the firm's own momentum, while SIC peers give small, short-lived profits. This search also found no independent replication on data after their sample.
- **Ali & Hirshleifer 2020** (JFE, "Shared analyst coverage"): In their robustness table, all-TNIC-peer momentum (Hoberg's data, 1997-07 to 2015-06) is subsumed by shared-analyst momentum: its alpha is −0.08% a month after that control, while shared-analyst momentum keeps 0.58% (NBER working-paper version). It tests all text peers, not the less visible ones.
- **Ahn, Gorduza & Park 2024** (Financial Markets and Portfolio Management, "Hidden neighbours: extracting industry momentum from stock networks"): Combines a price-correlation network with a 10-K/10-Q text-embedding network to capture less visible industry momentum; reports 18.16% a year with Sharpe 0.85 over 2013–2022, mostly alpha. How the two networks are combined, and whether costs are charged, is unverified (abstract only).
- **Huang, Fan, Hu & Ye 2026** (arXiv 2604.19476, LLM-augmented semantic networks): On S&P 500 stocks 2011–2019, 10-K embedding peers are traded as pair-level mean reversion, not momentum; LLM filtering of spurious text links lifts the long-short Sharpe from 0.742 to 0.820. They note that embedding similarity links firms on topic rather than economics.
- **Not found:** text peers chosen because their prices don't move with the firm, and any post-2015 test of whether such peers' past month predicts the firm's next month.

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Text:**
- Item 1 of each 10-K, one per company-year: the one with the most words, as lazy prices did.
- Words are lower-cased runs of a–z.
- Each fiscal year, drop words appearing in more than 25% of that year's filings, and words in fewer than 3.
- Binary word vectors, L2-normalized, as Hoberg & Phillips do.
- Cosine similarity over all pairs of that year's filings with an Item 1 of at least 1,000 words.

**Text peers:**
- The firms whose similarity with firm i is in the top 2% of all pairs that year, capped at the 50 most similar.
- That is about the granularity of Hoberg & Phillips' TNIC-3.

**When it's known:**
- The imposters' filing-date rule: June 1 of the following year, or 120 days after a later fiscal-year end.
- Only the current year's peers count, as in lazy prices.

**Universe:** the judge-like liquid set, minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)`. Peers must be in it too, so stale-priced stocks can't pass as "hidden".

**Hidden versus visible:**
- For each firm at each calendar day t, the correlation of daily market-relative returns with each text peer over t−251..t.
- **Hidden:** the text peers below the median of that firm's text-peer correlations.
- **Visible:** the rest.
- A firm needs at least 3 hidden peers.

**Signal:** the hidden peers' mean market-relative log return over t−20..t, residualized across stocks on the firm's own t−20..t return (OLS at each t).

**Book:** every 21 trading days, long the top tenth and short the bottom tenth, equal weight, held 21 days, in market-relative returns, at 5 bps per side.

**Periods:** formations from 2007-01 to 2022-05. FY2020 peers stop counting on 2022-05-31.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **2007–2019:** the hidden-peer book's mean monthly net return. **Pass:** t ≥ 2.5.
2. **Hidden beats visible:** the hidden-peer book minus the visible-peer book, t ≥ 2. All text peers together, and peer momentum's whole-day correlation peers, are reported.
3. **2020-01..2022-05:** same sign. The 16 unseen months can't be tested: there is no 10-K text after FY2020, and SEC downloads need an email address.

**Money test (the judge):**
- Score = the signal's percentile, every day. Graded with `rebalance=21`; the judge grades only years with scores.
- Uniform and volatility-matched random accounts are both reported.

## The number for the file name

The judge's walk-forward change over random, with uniform random accounts, 0 bps, over the years with text.

## Ways it can fool us

- **Boilerplate:** common wording links unrelated firms. The 25% rule and the 1,000-word floor are the guard.
- **Conglomerates** describe several businesses and get peers in each.
- **Survivor-only data:** delisted peers and firms are missing.

## Results (run 2026-10-21)

**What the data allowed.**
- **Text:** EDGAR-CORPUS holds 6,700–9,300 10-Ks a year, already one per company. 5,251 (FY2019) to 7,528 (FY2008) have an Item 1 of at least 1,000 words, and 1,320 (FY2005) to 2,647 (FY2020) of those come from companies in the ticker map. The 25% and 3-filing rules leave 42,500–46,300 words a year. The top-2% cutoff is a cosine of 0.21–0.25. Counts by year: `research/hidden_text_peers/peers_counts.txt`.
- **The top 2% bunches in dense clusters.** Banks, REITs and blank-check companies describe themselves alike. So the mean filing has 105–151 pairs above the cutoff, but the median filing only 2–30. Between 13% and 38% of mapped filings have none at all; the 38% is FY2020, the year of the blank-check boom.
- **From text peers to usable peers.** On the formation days, 841 universe firms on average have a current-year filing. Each has 21.6 text peers (median 11); 9.6 of them are in the ticker map with both filings dated, and 4.6 are in the liquid universe. 35% of firms (290 a formation) have the 6 or more universe peers that 3 hidden ones need. That is 30 firms in 2007, about 170 in 2008–09, and 270–400 from 2010. The firms that drop out are mostly those whose peers are delisted, merged or private, which the survivor-only ticker map can't place.

### The money test (the judge)

Daily scores, the residualized hidden-peer signal's percentile among the day's firms with at least 3 hidden peers. Graded walk-forward with `rebalance=21`. Scores exist from 2007-01-03 to 2022-05-31, 321 stocks a day at the median (16 to 438). The judge grades 15 years, 2007-08..2021-22. It skips 2006-07 because no stock has a score at its start, and 2022-23 onward because no stock has a score.

|---|---|---|
| **Change over random, 0 bps** | **+4.7 points a year** (se 3.2) | +6.3 (se 3.0) |
| Years ahead of the median random account | 10 of 15 | 9 of 15 |
| Years beating random at p < 0.1 (chance about 1.5) | 5 of 15 | 5 of 15 |

- **Ranking quality:** rank IC in actual returns z = +1.03 (positive in 8 of 15 years), and rank IC in the judge's residual z = +0.19. The long-short of the top and bottom 10% of scores made +1.49 bps a day (z = +0.97), positive in 10 of 15 years. Turnover is 86% per rebalance.
- **The volatility-matched number is borderline** (+6.3, se 3.0, t 2.1 over 15 years), and one year carries much of it: 2019-20 adds 34 points; without it the mean is +4.3.
- **The picks bunch in one or two industries at a time,** because a whole cluster of text peers rises together. On 2008-07-01 they were utilities and oil (IMO, OGE, EIX, CVX, XEL, PCG, D, NEE, WMB). On 2016-07-01 they were utilities and REITs (NRG, CNP, NJR, CMS, AVA; SHO, DRH, PEB, RLJ, KRG, CUBE, BXP, FRT). A bunched account swings further than diversified random accounts, so its yearly p-values sit in both tails. Against uniform random accounts it beat random at p < 0.1 in 5 years and trailed at p > 0.9 in 3 (2009-10, 2016-17, 2018-19). The plan's own long-short, about 30 stocks a side, earns nothing.

### Differences from Hoberg & Phillips

- **Sample:** theirs is every CRSP/Compustat firm, delisted ones included. Here the text side is every 10-K in EDGAR-CORPUS, but only firms still listed in 2025 can be traded or count as peers. A firm loses every peer that was later delisted, merged or taken private, so only 35% of firms have enough peers to rank.
- **Words:** they keep nouns and proper nouns and drop very common words. The plan here keeps every a–z word that is in at most 25% of filings, so boilerplate shared by banks, REITs and blank-check companies decides much of the top 2%. That is where the GS → Netgear and Clorox matches come from.
- **Dates:** they use each filing's real date. Here a filing counts from June 1 of the next year at the earliest, because the corpus has no filing dates. That misses the first two to three months of any drift after a March filing.
- **Period:** this runs 2007–2022, much of it after their working paper circulated and the 2018 publication.

## Decisions made while building it

All of these were made on 2026-10-21 **before any return after a formation date was computed or looked at**, unless a line says otherwise.

**Text**
- **Source:** the lazy prices experiment's stream of EDGAR-CORPUS (`lazy_prices/edgar/`), field `section_1`. It also carries the fiscal-year end each filing mentions most, which the filing-date rule needs. The imposters experiment's own Item 1 files and word sets weren't used: its words are WordNet nouns of three or more letters, not the plan's a–z runs, and its sets cover mapped companies only.
- **Fiscal years 2005–2020.** FY2005 is the current year in January 2007, the first formation month.
- **"One per company-year, the one with the most words":** the most Item 1 words. In practice the corpus already holds one 10-K per CIK and year, so the rule never had to choose.
- **"Words":** every run of a–z in the lower-cased Item 1. The 1,000-word floor counts all such words, before any are dropped.
- **"That year's filings"** for the 25% and 3-filing rules and for the top-2% cutoff: every corpus filing of the fiscal year with an Item 1 of at least 1,000 words, mapped to a ticker or not. Hoberg & Phillips use every firm, and the cutoff should not depend on which firms survived to 2025.
- **The top 2%:** pairs at or above the 98th percentile of the cosine over all pairs i < j of the year's filings.
- **Where the 50-peer cap applies: among all that year's filings, before keeping only mapped and liquid peers.** This choice was made after looking at the peer counts (`peers_counts.txt`, input data only) and before any return was computed. The alternative was the 50 most similar *mapped* filings. That would give a bank 50 surviving bank peers where it really had 50 mostly delisted ones, so the ticker map, which is survivors-only, would decide who counts as a peer. The cost of this choice is coverage: a mapped filing has 4.7–10.1 mapped text peers on average, depending on the year, against 13.1–20.9 under the alternative (`peers_counts.txt`).
- **Peers are directed:** j can be among i's 50 without i being among j's.

**When it's known**
- **A pair counts from the later of its two filings' dates** (the imposters rule for each: June 1 of the next year, or 120 days after a later fiscal-year end mentioned in the text), until May 31 two years after the fiscal year. Only the current fiscal year's pairs count (the lazy prices rule), so FY2020 pairs stop on 2022-05-31.
- **The cutoff and the 50-cap use the whole year's filings,** including the few dated after June 1 (non-December fiscal years). So a pair can be pushed out of the 50 by a filing dated a few months later. Hoberg & Phillips do the same, and the effect on the peer lists is small.
- **Rows:** a CIK's peers go to every export row the map gives it, and the same for the peer. Preferred-share rows (NTRSO, BANFP and the like) are in the map but never in the universe.

**Correlation, split and signal**
- **"Market-relative":** the daily log return minus the export's `market`, the equal-weight mean daily log return of the liquid stocks. There is no beta. For the correlation only, |daily log return| > 0.5 is a bad print and counts as missing, as in the night-and-day experiment.
- **Correlation:** Pearson over t−251..t, on days where both returns exist, with at least 227 such days (90%). Otherwise the peer has no correlation that day and doesn't count.
- **"Below the median":** strictly below, among the firm's peers that are in the universe with a correlation that day. With an odd count, the middle peer is visible. So 3 hidden peers need at least 6 peers.
- **The 21-day return:** log(close at t ÷ close at t−21), from forward-filled closes, minus the market's sum over the same 21 days. That covers the daily returns of t−20..t.
- **The firm set and the residualization:** each day, the universe firms with at least 3 hidden peers and their own 21-day return. The hidden, visible and all-peer means are each regressed across those firms on the own return, with an intercept, and the residual is the signal. All books use the same firms, so hidden against visible is a like-for-like comparison.
- **Universe:** copied from `research/night_and_day/common.py`. On the twins' calendar (export days 252 + 21k): closes on at least 90% of the past 756 days; median close ≥ $5 and median dollar volume ≥ $5M over the past 252 days; minus `c2eval.excluded`. It holds until the next calendar day.

**Books and tests**
- **Formation days:** the twins' calendar days from 2007-01-01 to 2022-05-31 with 21 days of prices after them, 185 in all. A formation with fewer than 20 firms is skipped, which affects the first five of 2007.
- **Tenths:** rank by signal, lowest first; tenth = floor(10 × rank ÷ n). Long the top tenth, short the bottom, equal weight, bought at the close of t0 and held to the close of t0+21. Closes are forward-filled, so a missing day earns 0. Both legs are equal weight, so the long-short is the same in market-relative and raw returns.
- **Costs:** 5 bps × turnover, where turnover is the sum of |new weight − drifted old weight|. The first book pays full entry. This is the lazy prices code with tenths instead of fifths.
- **Test 2's period is test 1's, 2007–2019.** The other periods are reported.
- **Test 3's "same sign"** compares the net means, the quantity test 1 tests.
- **Peer momentum's correlation peers** come from the Rust census, which this Python code can't rerun. The stand-in: each firm's 20 most correlated universe stocks, by the same 252-day correlation of market-relative returns, with the same signal and residualization. It is reported only.
- **The 500 largest** (not in the plan, reported only): the universe's top 500 by median dollar volume over the past 63 days, as in night and day. The signal isn't re-residualized within them.

**Money test**
- **Score:** rank ÷ (n − 1) of the residualized hidden-peer signal among the day's panel firms. The lowest scores 0 and is never picked (the judge buys only positive scores); NaN elsewhere. Scores run from 2007-01-03 to 2022-05-31.
- **The unseen months were not run.** The new export holds no text after fiscal 2020, so the score would be NaN throughout.

**Made after seeing results**
- **Nothing in the rules, the code or the tests was changed** after any return was computed.
- **Added to the report only:** the by-year table, the 500-largest row, the counts of years at p > 0.9, the look at the money test's picks, and the check of why NVDA, HPE and others sit outside the universe in 2016 (split-adjusted prices; short histories). They describe the results and decide nothing.
- **Added after seeing the money test, report only:** the same judge test on the visible-peer and all-peer signals (`money_compare.py`). It was run to see whether the +4.7 had anything to do with hidden peers; it doesn't change the number for the file name or the verdict.
- **Not tried, on purpose:** noun-only vocabularies, a looser or tighter cutoff, or the cap among mapped filings. Each would be a search for a positive result after a null. A noun-only vocabulary, as Hoberg & Phillips use, is the obvious next step if this is ever revisited, and it would need its own fixed rules.
- **No bugs met in `c2eval`.**
