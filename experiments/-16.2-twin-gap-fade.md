# Twin gap fade: do a stock's own opening gaps fade by the close?

**TL;DR:** When a stock opens far from where its tracking fund's opening move implies, does the gap fade by the close? On paper, hugely: betting on the fade at the official opening price earned about 60 basis points a day before costs, 2008–2025 (t +52.9). An independent check with intraday prices found the profit exists only in that opening print. Entered at 9:35 instead, it loses 16.2% a year after costs. Verdict: an opening-print effect, not money.

## Nearest prior work

From the earlier survey; a separate novelty check runs alongside.

| Work | What it found |
|---|---|
| Berkman, Koch, Tuttle & Zhang 2012, *JFQA* | After attention-grabbing days, opens are inflated and reverse intraday |
| Akbas, Boehmer, Jiang & Koch 2022, *JFE* | Overnight gains reversed during the day predict the stock's later returns |
| Jiang & Zhu 2017, *JFE* | Price jumps on news continue for 1–3 months, so gaps with real news shouldn't fade |
| Lou, Polk & Skouras 2019 | Many anomalies split between overnight and intraday |

**Novelty check (2026-10-21, a separate search; it changed no rules):** partly done.
- **Della Corte, Kosowski & Wang 2015,** "Market Closure and Short-Term Reversal" (SSRN 2730304; read through a CXO summary): each day, long the stocks with the lowest close-to-open return and short the highest, held from open to close.
  - In the US, 1993–2014, it made 1.68% a day before costs and held up after 2006.
  - Most of the profit comes in the first minute after the open, which daily bars can't capture.
- **Berkman et al. 2012; Akbas et al. 2022:** opening premiums driven by attention reverse intraday.
- **Jiang & Zhu 2017:** large news jumps continue instead.
- **Not found:** a gap fade adjusted by an ETF or industry gap.

Branch & Ma (2006) found a −0.22 correlation between a stock's overnight return and the trading day that follows.

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Universe:** the judge-like liquid set with a twin, minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)`, from 2008. Also a 500-largest subset.

**Own gap on day t:**
- u = log(open_t / close_{t−1}) − β × the twin's same log gap, with β and the twin from `c2eval.twins`, latest at or before t−1;
- scaled by the trailing 252-day sd of u (data through t−1).

**Book:**
- At the open of t: long the 10% most negative scaled u (fell most at the open beyond the twin), short the 10% most positive.
- Each position is paired with −β × the twin from the twin's open to its close, so the book is hedged during the day.
- Exit at the close of t. Equal weight.

**Execution:**
- **Headline:** entry 10 bps worse than the open (longs pay 10 bps more, shorts receive 10 bps less), exit at the close, plus 2 bps per side on the twin legs.
- **Optimistic bound:** entry exactly at the open. Reported alongside.

**Baselines (same universe and execution):**
- **(a) raw gap fade:** rank by the stock's own gap, unadjusted;
- **(b) overnight-intraday reversal:** rank by yesterday's overnight return minus its intraday return (Akbas et al.). Uses data through yesterday only, so it is entered at the open with no look-ahead.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **2008–2019:** the headline book's mean daily return. **Pass:** t ≥ 3, net.
2. **Beats the baselines:** the paired daily difference against (a) and (b). **Pass:** t ≥ 2 for both.
3. **2020–2025-06:** same sign. The 16 unseen months (new export and store, once, at the end): same sign.

**Reported, post hoc only:** a split by the day's volume relative to its 60-day median (gaps with news vs without). Volume isn't known at the open, so this can't be part of the rule.

## The number for the file name

- **What it is:** the headline book's yearly return minus the median of 1,000 random books with the same structure (random 10% long and 10% short from the same universe, same hedges and costs), over 2008–2025, in points a year.
- **Why not the judge:** it can't grade an intraday book. `experiments/README.md`'s key notes this.

## Ways it can fool us

- **Looking at the open:** the signal uses the open itself. The 10 bps penalty stands in for trading just after it; report the break-even penalty.
- **Stale opens on thin stocks:** they make gaps fade mechanically. That's why the 500-largest subset is reported.

### Post hoc: is it the data?

An intraday long-short that clears 10 bps a side by 41 bps a day with t 29 is far outside anything else in this project. These checks were added after Tests 1–3; none changes a rule or a pass/fail.

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Stale opens: open = previous close (3.6% of the ranked set, 1.9% of the legs) or open = close (1.0%, 1.1%) | Dropping either or both leaves the book unchanged: 2008–2019 headline +41.0 (all) and +35.6 (500 largest), against +41.3 and +35.9 |
| Intraday bad prints (87 of 6.0M rows) | Kept with the return clipped at ±0.5: 2008–2019 headline +41.15 against +41.27 |
| Opening times: NYSE auctions often printed minutes after 9:30 before about 2016; Nasdaq's cross and the funds open at 9:30 | Nasdaq stocks fade as much as NYSE stocks: gross +55 (Nasdaq) and +36 (NYSE) in 2015–2019, +57 and +40 in 2020–2025 |
| The hedges' own opens: slope of intraday on overnight return, 2008–2019, for SPY, QQQ, IWM, XLF, XLE, XLK, XLU, GLD, TLT, EEM | −0.10 to +0.16, near zero, so the funds' opens are not noisy |
| The 20 most-traded stocks of 2019, the same slope | −0.18 to +0.15, so the mega-caps' opens don't reverse on their own |
| The 100 most traded (legs of 10; spreads of 1–3 bps) | Gross +50.8 (t +30.1) in 2008–2019, +36.0 (+11.9) in 2020–2025-06. Bid-ask bounce can't produce that |
| The profile across z (within-day 5% bins, 2008–2019) | Smooth and monotone in all 20 bins, from +36.8 bps a day in the lowest 5% to −30.5 in the highest, not just a tail effect. The 500 largest show the same profile |
| The open as the day's extreme | 9.8% of longs opened at the day's low and 10.0% of shorts at its high, against 4.9–5.1% for all ranked stocks |
| A second source of opens | None: the v1 app's database holds the same Stooq prices (identical opens and closes on the days compared) |

**What is left.** The pattern is in the data. It is broad, survives the obvious artifacts, and holds in the largest stocks and in the unseen months. Two things daily data can't settle:
- **Fills.** The book needs the official opening print, or a price within a few bps of it, on both sides of 100–200 names a day. An opening-auction order is placed before the gap is known. Trading just after the open costs whatever the price moves in the first minutes, and that is where an auction overshoot would unwind. The break-even penalty is the margin for this: 16 bps a side for all stocks since 2023, 13 for the 500 largest, 9 for the 100 most traded.
- **Capacity:** opening auctions hold a small share of a day's volume, so the size this could run at is small.

The literature fits: Branch & Ma report the overnight-intraday reversal weaker over 2011–2017 and absent among S&P 500 stocks in their regressions, and here the raw fade (a) is about zero or negative after the penalty in the 500 largest since 2015. What remains is the part measured against the twin.

**Survivorship:** the data holds only survivors. That flatters the long leg in 2008–2009, which bought collapsing opens of companies that lived; stocks that went on to fail are missing. 2010 onward is less exposed, and the short leg earns about as much as the long leg.

### Verdict

- **Passes every pre-registered test:**
  - Test 1: +41.3 bps a day, t +29.0;
  - Test 2: +24.6 over the raw fade (t +28.7) and +74.1 over Akbas (t +41.8);
  - Test 3: +21.6 in 2020–2025-06 and +32.6 in the unseen months.
- **Not yet money:** the profit rests on fills at the opening print, which daily data can't confirm.
- **The next step:** minute bars or official opening-auction prices (TAQ, or a vendor such as Polygon) for a sample of leg days, to measure the price available 1–5 minutes after the open.
- **The margin is thin where it matters:** in the 500 largest since 2023 the headline is +6 bps a day (t +1.9), with a 13 bps break-even.

## Decisions made while building it

All made **before seeing any results** unless marked otherwise.

- **Universe (the judge-like liquid set):** the judge's own rule (`stockai/judge/src/holdout.rs` `liquid_universe`, as in `research/analog_days`), made point in time on `c2eval.twins`' calendar: at rebalance day t0, `v` finite on ≥ 90% of the 756 days ending at t0, and median close ≥ $5 and median dollar volume ≥ $5M over the 252 days ending at t0 (export close and dollar volume). Then: a twin at t0 and not `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)` at t0. It is used for signal days t with `rebal_index(tw, t−1)` = that rebalance, so the twin, β and universe all come from closes through t−1 at the latest.
- **The 500 largest:** the 500 members of that universe with the highest median dollar volume over the 63 days ending at t0 (the export has no market caps; twin reversal used the same proxy). Its book is ranked within the 500, not filtered from the all-stock book.
- **Prices:** opens and closes of stocks and funds from the store (`STORE_OLD`), aligned to export days. Overnight = log(open_t / close_{t−1}) on consecutive export days; intraday = log(close_t / open_t). A stock or fund day with |overnight| or |intraday| log return > 0.5 is missing. Data check (`out/dataq.txt`): no missing or zero opens among liquid stock-days since 2008, opens equal the close on 1% and the previous close on 3.7% of them, and store closes equal the export's.
- **The intraday part of the bad-print rule uses the day's own close** (task-mandated). How many book positions it removes is reported, with a sensitivity that keeps those days with the intraday return clipped at ±0.5.
- **u and its scale:** u_t = overnight_stock − β × overnight_twin, with the twin and β of the latest rebalance at or before t−1. The trailing sd is over u on days t−252..t−1, recomputed with that same twin and β (so it scales today's u consistently), sample sd, needing ≥ 189 finite values. The window for early 2008 reaches into 2007, whose opens equal the previous close more often (about 9–10% in 2007-H1 against 3–4% later); this only adds noise to the scale and is accepted, so signals start 2008-01-02.
- **Common ranked set:** each day, all books rank the same stocks: universe members with a finite scaled u, raw gap, Akbas signal (which needs t−1's open and t−2's close), and stock and twin intraday returns. A stock whose twin has a bad print that day is dropped that day. Legs are round(10% × n).
- **Baseline (b)'s direction:** Akbas et al. 2022 find that more positive overnight returns followed by negative daytime reversals predict *higher* returns (abstract, JFE 145(3), 850–875). So (b) is long the 10% highest (overnight_{t−1} − intraday_{t−1}) and short the lowest. Like the other books it is twin-hedged, entered at the open with the same penalty.
- **Book returns:** $1 long and $1 short, equal weight. A long earns (close/open − 1) − β × (twin close/twin open − 1); a short the negative. Book return = mean long − mean short − costs.
- **Costs:** the entry penalty p is charged additively per $1 of each side (so the book pays 2p a day; p = 10 bps headline, 0 for the optimistic bound). Twin legs pay 2 bps per side (4 bps a day) on the fund positions **netted across stocks** each day, as twin reversal did (many stocks share SPY, IWM or XLF); un-netted is reported as a sensitivity. Stock exits at the close pay nothing, as the plan writes it. "Gross" means no costs at all.
- **Break-even penalty:** the p at which the book's mean is zero, i.e. (mean with p = 0, twin costs included) / 2, in bps per side of entry; reported for 2008–2019 and for 2008–2025-06.
- **t-statistics:** plain t of the daily returns. Test 2 is the paired daily difference (headline minus baseline, both with the same costs) over 2008–2019. "Same sign" in Test 3 means the plan's direction: positive, so the fade pays, net (headline).
- **The file-name number:** 252 × the mean daily return of the headline book over 2008-01-02..2025-06-27, minus the median of the same for 1,000 random books. Each random book draws, every day, a random 10% long and a disjoint 10% short from the same ranked set, with the same twin hedges, netted twin costs and penalty. Reported alongside: the per-calendar-year differences (mean, se, years ahead) and, as a check, the judge-style version (compounded return within each year, minus the random median, averaged over years).
- **Volume split (post hoc only):** the day's store volume over its median on the 60 days before; ≥ 2 counts as "volume spike (news)", below 2 as "no spike". Each day's legs are split by the flag, and the long-short is computed within each group.
- **The judge side check:** scores = the Akbas signal at the close, overnight_d − intraday_d, for the universe in force for day d+1; `rebalance=1`; uniform random accounts. It trades close to close, so it is not the intraday book; it is not the file-name number.
- **A data gap:** on 2011-02-17 the store has prices for 571 of about 2,540 stocks. That day and the next have thin books; nothing is filled.

## Independent check with intraday bars

**Why it gets one.** It is the only result in the project with t far above 3, and three of its signs are what noise in the opening print predicts:
- opens at the day's high or low twice as often as usual;
- gaps on ordinary volume fade while gaps on news volume continue;
- a break-even that shrank as spreads tightened.

None of the three is what a real overreaction uniquely predicts. Daily bars can't tell the two apart; intraday bars from a second source can.

### Rules (fixed 2026-10-21, before any intraday bar was fetched)

**Source:** Yahoo Finance, through `yfinance` in its own venv:
- 5-minute bars for the last 60 days, and 1-minute bars for the last 30;
- regular session only, unadjusted, compared with the store's raw opens and closes;
- a split or dividend in the window is flagged and the stock dropped on that day.

**Window:** trading days in the new store (through 2026-10-19) that Yahoo's bars cover. These are the last weeks of the unseen months.

**Positions:** the headline book exactly as tested: the all-stocks deciles, with the signal from the store's open, each position hedged with β × its twin. The 500-largest book is reported but not graded, because the window is too short for it.

**Entry times,** paired on the same positions and days:
- (a) the store's open, the book as tested;
- (b) the open of Yahoo's first bar;
- (c) 9:35, (d) 9:45, (e) 10:00;
- 9:31 too, where 1-minute bars exist.

At each time the twin hedge is entered at the same moment from the twin's own bars. The exit is the store's close, with Yahoo's last bar reported alongside.

**Variant:** the signal recomputed from Yahoo's first-bar open, the price a trader would actually have seen.

**Verdict, on the gross daily spread of the all-stocks book over the 5-minute window:**

| Verdict | Condition |
|---|---|
| **Real** | Entry at 9:35 keeps at least half of entry (a)'s spread on the same days, and is positive with t ≥ 2 |
| **Opening-print effect** (not collectable after the open) | Entry at 9:35 keeps less than a quarter |
| **Unresolved** | Anything else |

Net spreads at the plan's costs are reported for every entry time but don't decide the verdict.

**Reported on its own:** how often the store's open, Yahoo's daily open and Yahoo's first-bar open disagree, by more than 1 cent and by more than 0.5%, split by exchange and by dollar volume.

### Decisions made while building it

Logged with times in `research/twin_gap_fade_check/out/decisions.txt`.

**Before any intraday bar was fetched** (2026-10-21, 11:32–11:35):
- **Positions:** the book is rebuilt from `rows_new.npz` with `books.py`'s own logic: stable argsort of z, round(10% × n) per leg, intraday bad-print rows out.
  - The positions are recorded so they can be repriced.
  - On 2026-07-01..10-19 (77 days), the gross and twin costs equal `books.books()` exactly, and `daily_new.tsv` to its six decimals, for both books.
- **Price scale:** the store's docstring says Stooq's prices are dividend-adjusted, and Yahoo's intraday prices with `auto_adjust=False` are not.
  - So every Yahoo price is multiplied by k, the median of store close ÷ Yahoo daily close per symbol and per stretch between Yahoo's ex-dates. k uses closes only, never opens.
  - A stock-day whose close is off its k by more than 0.1% is dropped.
- **The rule's split-or-dividend drop:** a position is dropped on a day when its stock or its twin has a dividend or split ex-date.
- **Entry prices:**
  - (b) is the open of the first regular-session bar, whatever its time.
  - 9:31, 9:35, 9:45 and 10:00 are the open of the bar stamped exactly that time. There is no fallback: a missing bar means a missing price.
  - The twin uses the same definitions.
  - The exit is the store's close. The alternative exit is the close of the last bar before 16:00.
  - Bars are kept only between 9:30 and 16:00, America/New_York.
- **Windows:** the days on which SPY has a full session (78 five-minute or 390 one-minute bars, 9:30 to the last bar, UTC−4). This drops half-days and a partial first day.
- **One common set per window:** every entry time, (a) included, is priced on the same positions, which need every bar and a usable close. (a) is also reported on all positions and on all positions with bars.
- **Costs:** the plan's headline costs at every entry time. Net doesn't decide the verdict.
- **The verdict's terms:** share = mean daily 9:35 gross ÷ mean daily (a) gross on the same days, from the common set; t is the plain t of the daily 9:35 gross.
- **The variant:**
  - z is recomputed as (log(first-bar open × k / store's previous close) − β × the same for the twin) ÷ the trailing sd. That sd is recovered from the store as `build.py` computes it, and reproduces the rows' z to within 5e-5.
  - Rows without a first-bar open for stock and twin leave that day's ranked set.
  - The ranking always uses the 5-minute first bar. On the 1-minute window, those positions are priced with 1-minute bars.
  - Its share is measured against the main book's (a).
- **The fetch:**
  - 5-minute and daily bars for the window's whole ranked set and its twins, 2,858 symbols in all. The variant needs every ranked stock's first-bar open, and the positions already covered 2,421 of the 2,453 ranked stocks.
  - 1-minute bars only for the stocks and twins of the all-stocks, 500-largest and variant books on the 1-minute days.
- **Open agreement:** measured on position stock-days, split by the store's exchange and by the 100 most traded, 101–500 and the rest (`build.py`'s flags).

**After the 5-minute fetch began, before any intraday return was computed:**
- **Windows:** both follow what Yahoo serves: 60 trading days of 5-minute bars, from 2026-07-29; 1-minute bars from 2026-09-22.
  - The ranked stocks and twins of 2026-07-29..08-19 that the first list lacked (38 symbols) were fetched as a supplement.
- **Price scale, checked on daily closes:** the store matches Yahoo's raw closes in this window, so k = 1 (`out/scale_check.txt`).
- **Six symbols have no Yahoo daily bars.** One identical retry also failed, and nothing further was tried.

**After seeing the first full results:**
- **The intraday scale check, added for the 1-minute window:** the first run's 1-minute numbers were wrecked by splits.
  - **The cause:** Yahoo's 1-minute bars aren't adjusted for a later split, while its 5-minute and daily bars are. It affected CTVA (6.665:1 on 10-01), DXJ (3:1 on 10-09, a twin of many positions) and ETHA (1:3 on 10-06), on 20 symbol-days before their splits. Exiting (a) at Yahoo's last bar gave +100.6, against +67.8 at the store's close.
  - **The fix,** the rule's split drop extended beyond the ex-date: an intraday symbol-day counts only if its last-bar close is within 2% of Yahoo's daily close. It is applied to both windows and to the variant. It removes 7 of 165,086 five-minute symbol-days (none a split) and 25 of 50,892 one-minute ones.
  - **The verdict is identical before and after:** 9:35 kept 0.22 of (a) before (+16.18 against +73.96, t +3.18) and 0.22 after (+16.20 against +73.96, t +3.18).
- **Added post hoc, after the 5-minute results:** each entry time priced on its own positions, and the 5-minute book restricted to the 1-minute window's days. Neither changes a rule.
- **Process:** the first runs used `-I` without `-B`, so the venv's Python 3.13 wrote bytecode caches of `books.py` and `c2eval.py` into their `__pycache__` directories. Those two files were deleted. No source file outside `research/twin_gap_fade_check/` was changed.
