# Turn of the month: does the market earn its return in four days a month?

**TL;DR:** Does the market earn its return in the four days around each turn of the month, as McConnell & Xu found? Our code reproduces their 1926–2005 result exactly (t 8.2), but the effect has vanished since: over 2006–2019 those days earned no more than the rest (t −0.07). After costs of 5 basis points a trade, holding SPY only on those days made −9.6 points a year against always holding it. Verdict: no edge.

## Published claim

| Paper | Sample | What it reports |
|---|---|---|
| Lakonishok & Smidt 1988, *RFS* | Dow, 1897–1986 | Returns concentrate around the turn of the month |
| McConnell & Xu 2008, *FAJ* | US 1926–2005; 35 countries | All of the market's excess return comes on turn-of-month days; also out of sample abroad |

**Our test period is after both samples.**

McConnell & Xu also found the effect in 30 of the 34 other countries they tested.

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Turn-of-month (TOM) days:** the last trading day of each month and the first three trading days of the next, from the export's trading calendar. All others are other days.

**Returns:**
- SPY's daily total return from Yahoo's adjusted closes, close to close; excess over the daily T-bill rate.
- The export's equal-weight `market` is reported alongside.

**Rule:** in SPY over each TOM day (bought at the close before, sold at the TOM day's close); in cash otherwise. 1 bp per side.

**Random timings:**
- 10,000, each in SPY for 4 randomly chosen trading days per calendar month, as the market timing experiment did.
- Compared on compound annual return.

**Achievable precision, stated now:**
- About 670 TOM days in 2006–2019, with a daily SPY sd of about 1.2%.
- So the standard error of the TOM-minus-other difference is about 0.05%, and a gap of 0.10% a day gives t ≈ 2.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **2006–2019:**
   - the mean TOM-day SPY return minus the mean other-day return, **pass at t ≥ 2** (Welch);
   - **and** the TOM-only rule beats the median random timing, with p < 0.10.
2. **The equal-weight market:** the TOM-minus-other difference has the same sign.
3. **2020–2025-06:** same sign. **The 16 unseen months** (new store or Yahoo, once, at the end): same sign.

**Reported:**
- each TOM day's position (−1, +1, +2, +3) separately;
- with and without 2008.

## The number for the file name

Points of yearly return of the TOM-only rule above the median random timing (4 days a month), 2006–2025-06, at 0 bps. A timing rule against random timings, as in `-3.2-market-timing.md`.

## Ways it can fool us

- **Few big days:** a handful of large moves (2008, March 2020) can decide it. Results are reported with and without them.
- **Dividends:** SPY's ex-dates are around the third Friday of the quarter's last month, not at the turn of the month. Using Yahoo's adjusted closes takes them out anyway.

1. **2006–2019: fails.** SPY's TOM-minus-other excess return was −0.003% a day, with Welch t −0.07 (needed ≥ 2). ## Decisions made while building it

All made before any return was split by turn-of-month label, unless marked otherwise. The only outcome data looked at first was SPY's list of daily moves beyond 7% (a bad-print check: all are real 2008, 2020 and 2025-04-09 days).

- **Calendar:** the export's trading days. The new export's first 5,911 days equal the old one's, and Yahoo's SPY has exactly the export's days over 2005-12..2025-06 (4,923 days) and over the unseen months (330 days): none missing on either side.
- **Month ends:** the old export ends 2025-06-27, a Friday, which is not June 2025's last trading day (2025-06-30). Month ends are found on the new export's calendar; a calendar's first and last months get no label from their open side. The walk-forward's labels are then identical on either calendar.
- **The span:** 2006-01-03..2025-06-27, 4,902 days, 234 calendar months. Every month holds 4 TOM days (its own last day, −1, and its first three, +1..+3), except June 2025, whose −1 day (2025-06-30) is the first unseen day: 3. So 2006–2019 has 672 TOM days (168 at each position), 2020–2025-06 has 263.
- **Returns:** SPY's simple daily total return from Yahoo's adjusted closes, close to close on export days. Excess = minus `rf_daily` (DGS3MO / 252, forward-filled). The Welch test (test 1) is on excess simple returns. The export's `market` is an equal-weight mean daily *log* return, so its TOM-minus-other difference is in excess log terms (market − log(1 + rf)).
- **Cash earns `rf_daily`** for the rule and every random timing alike.
- **Random timings:** 10,000, seed 20261021 (`numpy.random.default_rng`). In each calendar month, as many days as the rule holds in that month (4, or 3 in June 2025), drawn uniformly without replacement from the month's trading days; each drawn day is held close to close in SPY, the rest in cash. Compared on compound annual return, exp(Σ log growth × 252 / days) − 1. One-sided p = (1 + draws ≥ the rule) / 10,001. One set of draws serves every period and exclusion (months are drawn independently, so a period's subset is a valid null for that period).
- **The market timing code doesn't fit:** `research/market_timing/engine.py` reorders whole-month weights. The random-day draws are written here instead.
- **Costs:** 1 bp per side, charged only when the position changes. The four TOM days are consecutive, so the rule pays one entry (the close before −1) and one exit (the close of +3) a month. Random timings pay on their own paths, so scattered days pay more.
- **Test 1b and the file-name number are at 0 bps.** The plan sets the number at 0 bps but doesn't say for test 1b; at 1 bp the random timings' extra trading would hand the rule up to about 0.7 points a year that has nothing to do with timing. 1 bp is reported alongside.
- **Test 2's period:** 2006–2019, the test-1 period; "same sign" means the same sign as SPY's 2006–2019 difference. Other periods reported.
- **Test 3's "same sign":** SPY's TOM-minus-other difference (the statistic tests 1 and 2 use) has the 2006–2019 sign, in 2020–2025-06 and in the unseen months. The rule against random timing is reported beside it.
- **Without 2008 / without March 2020:** drop every 2008 day, or every March 2020 day, from the Welch samples and from the compounding of the rule and every random timing alike. All four combinations are reported.
- **Dividends in the equal-weight market:** the export's closes are raw from about 2022-12-20, so its `market` misses dividends after that. Test 2 (2006–2019) is unaffected. The size of any TOM bias after 2022 is estimated from the shared `div_fix` panel (its cross-sectional mean on TOM days against other days) and reported only.
- **Published numbers:** taken from McConnell & Xu's article (FAJ 64(2), Table 1, read from a scanned copy). Lakonishok & Smidt's are as quoted by McConnell & Xu; their paper was not read. M&X's Table 1 reports raw mean daily returns, so ours are given raw and excess.
- **Reported only, not tested:** each position (−1, +1, +2, +3) against other days (Welch); the share of SPY's excess log return earned on TOM days; years in which TOM days beat other days; and, for comparison with Etula et al. (2020), the mean return at positions −5..−2 and +4, +5.
- **After the walk-forward results: the fidelity check.** McConnell & Xu's Table 1 was rerun on French's daily CRSP market (`fidelity.py`), to show the null result isn't a coding or data error. Reported only; it changed nothing.
- **After results: the signs in tests 2 and 3.** SPY's 2006–2019 difference came out at −0.003%, so "the same sign as 2006–2019" compares against a sign that is noise. Both readings are reported. As written, tests 2 and 3 fail; against the published positive sign they pass, at t 0.11 for each walk-forward check. The verdict stops at test 1 either way.
- **After results: a bug fix.** In the first run the 2020–2025-06 period shared its name with the last five-year block, and that dropped its "without March 2020" variant. It was fixed and rerun with the same seed; every other number was identical.
- **The unseen months** (set after the walk-forward, before `unseen.py` ran): 2025-06-30..2026-10-19 on the new export's calendar, with SPY's Yahoo dates checked to match. Random timings draw, in each calendar month, as many days as the rule holds there: 1 of 1 in June 2025, 4 in each full month, and 3 of October 2026's 13 days. Same seed, 20261021.
