# Formula mining with an honest holdout: can brute force find what the ideas missed?

**TL;DR:** This is a data-mining stress test, in the spirit of Yan & Zheng (2017) and the "101 formulaic alphas" (Kakushadze 2016). It is new for this project, whose tests have all been hypothesis-driven. 676 simple formulas, 1,352 counting both directions, were built from the data in hand. They are 26 base features (returns over several windows, volume ratios, ranges, close location, gaps, twin residuals, beta, skew and others) plus every pairwise product and difference of their ranks. Each was scored on how well it predicted next week's market-relative return over 2005–2014. The best reached t = 4.67, just past the 95th percentile of the best from 20 runs on shuffled data (4.45). One shuffled run did better, so the search found something beyond luck, but only just. What it found is short-term reversal. All 20 selected formulas bet against the last day or week (the stock's own return, close location or twin residual), and their composite has a rank correlation of 0.82 with plain 5-day reversal. On the untouched 2015–2025 holdout, the composite's weekly rank IC fell from t = 5.5 in training to t = 1.4, short of the 3 required. It was barely above reversal's (+0.0068 against +0.0054; difference t = 0.4). The 20 selections made from shuffled data held up better out of sample (median IC +0.016). They had picked slow momentum and size formulas instead. In the money test over 2015-16..2024-25, the picks made 6.0 points a year more than random before costs. That is not significant (standard error 5.9, ahead in 6 of 10 years; +5.8 against volatility-matched accounts), and it is less than 5-day reversal's +9.5 over the same years. The long-short's 3.9 bps a day gross would be eaten almost entirely by 86% weekly turnover at 5 bps a side. In the 16 unseen months, the composite's weekly IC kept its sign (+0.014, t = 1.0), but the picks lost 4.4% while the median random account made 17.3% (p = 0.96). Verdict: no edge. Brute force rediscovered short-term reversal, which had faded by the holdout.

## Nearest prior work

| Work | What it found |
|---|---|
| Yan & Zheng 2017, *RFS*, "Fundamental analysis and the cross-section of stock returns: a data-mining approach" | Of about 18,000 accounting signals, many survive bootstrap tests for luck |
| Kakushadze 2016, "101 Formulaic Alphas" | Short-horizon price-volume formulas used in practice, with low correlations to each other |
| Harvey, Liu & Zhu 2016; Chen & Zimmermann 2022 | Multiple testing needs higher bars; most published predictors replicate statistically |

**Novelty check (2026-10-21, a separate search; it changed no rules):** partly done.
- **Yan & Zheng 2017:** of about 18,000 accounting signals, those picked on 1963–87 still worked on 1988–2013.
- **Chordia, Goyal & Saretto 2020:** over 2 million strategies, a t of about 3.4–3.8 is needed.
- **Chen, Lopez-Lira & Zimmermann:** mined accounting ratios do as well as published predictors, and about half the predictability survives.
- **Mined trading rules fail:** Sullivan, Timmermann & White 1999; Bajgrowicz & Scaillet 2012.
- **Du, Walter & Ulrich 2026:** of 168 Alpha191 formulas on S&P 500 stocks, 17 are significant and 3 robust, with no holdout.
- **Not found:** a mine of daily price/volume formulas across US stocks with a fixed selection and test split and a shuffled-data null.

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Universe:** the judge-like liquid set, minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)`.

**Base features (about 25),** daily, data through the close, each ranked across the universe each day:
- **Returns:** over 1, 5, 21, 63, 126 and 252 days; 12-1 momentum.
- **Volatility:** 21 and 63 days.
- **Volume and size:** volume ratio 5/60 and 21/252 days; dollar volume (size).
- **Price shape:** average (H−L)/C over 5 and 21 days; average close location over 5 days; average overnight gap and average intraday return over 5 days (from 2008, opens).
- **Distribution:** maximum daily return over 21 days; skewness over 63 days.
- **Market:** beta and correlation with the market over 252 days.
- **Twin:** residual over 5, 21 and 63 days (`c2eval.twin_residual`, with `max_missing` ≈ 5% of the window); correlation with the twin.
- **Distance from the 252-day high.**
- **Bad prints:** use the close-location rules from `experiments/+2.5-close-location.md`.

**Candidates:**
- each base feature;
- the product of each pair of features' ranks;
- the difference of each pair;
- about 25 + 300 + 300 = 625 formulas, each in both directions: about 1,250.

**Selection, on 2005–2014 only:**
- each formula's weekly rank IC with the next 5-day market-relative return, as a t over weeks;
- keep the 20 with the highest |t|, each oriented by its training sign.
- **Composite** = the mean of the 20 oriented ranks.

**Luck calibration:**
- Repeat the whole selection 20 times with the training-period outcomes shuffled across weeks (whole weeks moved together).
- Record the best training t and the composite's later IC in each run.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **Training, 2005–2014:** the best real training t must beat the 95th percentile of the shuffled runs' best t. If not, nothing was found beyond luck.
2. **Holdout, 2015–2025-06:** the composite's weekly rank IC. **Pass:** t ≥ 3, and higher than 5-day reversal's IC over the same weeks.
3. **The unseen months** (new export and store, once, at the end): same sign.

**Money test (the judge):**
- Scores = the composite, NaN before 2015-06-28, so the judge grades only years after the selection period. Graded with `rebalance=5`.
- Uniform and volatility-matched random accounts are both reported.

## The number for the file name

The judge's walk-forward change over random, with uniform random accounts, 0 bps, over the test years 2015-16..2024-25 only.

## Ways it can fool us

- **Correlated features:** the 1,250 aren't independent, so the shuffled runs, not a formula, set the bar for luck.
- **Short-term reversal in disguise:** it is the strongest known effect, and the composite will likely rediscover it. That's why test 2 asks for more than reversal.

## Results (run 2026-10-21)

**The tests, in order:**
1. **Training, 2005–2014: passes, narrowly.** The best real |t| is 4.67 against a bar of 4.45; 1 of the 20 shuffled runs did better (5.09).
2. **Holdout, 2015–2025-06: fails.** The composite's weekly IC has t = 1.39 (3 needed). Its mean is above 5-day reversal's, +0.0068 against +0.0054, but the paired difference has t = 0.44. **The verdict stops here.**
3. **The unseen months:** the same sign (+0.014, t = 1.0), so this would pass, but the verdict had already stopped.

**Money test:** +6.0 points a year over uniform random (se 5.9) and +5.8 over volatility-matched random (se 5.7), neither significant. Both are below 5-day reversal's +9.5 and +8.3 over the same years. In the unseen months the picks trailed random by 21.6 points.

**What the search found is short-term reversal.** All 20 selected formulas bet against the last day's or week's move: the stock's own 1- or 5-day return, its 5-day close location, or its 5-day twin residual. Some are crossed with a volume ratio. Three are differences from a 12-month return (ret1 − mom12_1, ret5 − mom12_1, ret1 − ret252); they also hold the slow side, and they are the only three with a holdout t above 2. The composite's weekly rank correlation with plain 5-day reversal is 0.82. In training, the short-term formulas had the strongest t's (3.9–4.7) of all 676, so the selection is reversal 20 times over. Reversal itself weakened: plain 5-day reversal's weekly IC fell from t = 4.0 in training to 0.9 in the holdout (`+5.4-reversal-control.md` has it fading since 2021). The composite weakened with it: weekly IC +0.029 (t 5.5) in training, +0.007 (t 1.4) in the holdout.

**Seen only after the results (hindsight, not tested):**
- **The shuffled runs held up better.** Their selections did better on the holdout than the real one: median IC +0.016 against +0.007, with 17 of 20 above it. The shuffle keeps each stock's average outcome over 2005–2014 (see Decisions), so those runs picked slow formulas: 12-month momentum and long-window returns, often multiplied by dollar volume. "Big stocks that have been rising" kept rising in 2015–2025.
  - **Survivor-only data flatters exactly that.** The data holds only stocks that survived to 2025, and long-run winners are overrepresented among them.
  - **No rule picked them.** The plan selects on the real training ICs, where reversal won. Choosing the slow formulas now would be selecting on the holdout.
- **The few holdout winners were slow ones too.** Across all 676 formulas, the training t and the holdout t correlate at 0.44. Only 14 of the 676 reach |holdout t| ≥ 3, mostly slow ones such as 12-1 momentum × market correlation (3.66; training t 2.44) and dollar volume against the twin residual. None of them was in the top 20.

## Decisions made while building it

All made before any outcome data was seen, unless marked otherwise.

- **Universe on each day d:** the judge's liquid rule, point in time: a finite close on d, ≥ 90% finite closes over d−251..d, and median close ≥ $5 and median dollar volume ≥ $5M over those days; minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)[rebal_index(tw, d)]`. No twin is required; twin formulas just have no value for stocks without one.
- **Calendar:** decision days every 5 trading days from 2005-01-03, one fixed calendar. Outcome = log(C[d+5]/C[d]) minus the export's market over d+1..d+5, from the export's closes (actual returns).
  - **Training weeks:** decision days whose 5-day outcome window ends by 2014-12-31.
  - **Holdout weeks:** decision days from the first trading day of 2015, with the window ending by 2025-06-27, so training and holdout outcomes never overlap.
- **The 26 base features** (the plan's "about 25"): returns over 1, 5, 21, 63, 126 and 252 days; 12-1 momentum (log C[d−21]/C[d−252]); volatility over 21 and 63 days; volume ratios 5/60 and 21/252 (mean store share volume, the longer window including the shorter); dollar volume (21-day mean of the export's); average (H−L)/C over 5 and 21 days; average close location over 5 days; average overnight gap and average intraday return over 5 days; maximum daily return over 21 days; skewness over 63 days (population); beta and correlation with the export's market over 252 days; twin residuals over 5, 21 and 63 days; correlation with the twin; distance from the 252-day high of closes (log).
  - **Sources:** returns, volatility, skew, beta, highs and dollar volume use the export's repaired closes. Range, close location, gap, intraday and volume use the store's open, high, low, close and volume together, so a store close is never mixed with an export close.
  - **Bad prints:** a day's range is missing when (H−L)/C > 0.5, H < C, L > C or H = L (the close-location rules). Opens are used only from 2008-01-01, only on a valid day with L ≤ O ≤ H, and the gap also needs a valid previous day; gaps and intraday returns beyond ±0.5 (log) are treated as bad prints. Volume ≤ 0 is missing.
  - **Minimum data:** rolling windows of daily returns need ≥ 80% of their days (17 of 21, 51 of 63, 202 of 252); averages of range, close location, gap, intraday, volume and dollar volume need ≥ 60% (3 of 5, 13 of 21, 36 of 60, 152 of 252).
  - **Twins:** `twin_residual(E, tw, h, scaled=True, max_missing=max(1, round(0.05*h)))`; correlation with the twin is `tw['corr']` from the latest rebalance at or before d.
- **Ranks:** each base feature is ranked across the universe's stocks that have a value, as an average-tie percentile (r + 0.5)/n in (0, 1), as in the 101-alphas convention. Missing stays missing, and a pair formula is missing where either feature is.
- **Candidates:** 26 base ranks, 325 rank products and 325 rank differences make 676 distinct formulas. With two directions each, that is 1,352, the plan's "about 1,250". The 20 are the 676 formulas with the highest |t|, each oriented by its sign. Ranking all 1,352 would put both directions of the same formula in the top 20, and the pair would cancel.
- **IC:** the exact Spearman correlation over the stocks where both the formula and the outcome exist. A week counts if it has ≥ 100 such stocks. t = mean / (sd / √weeks) over the weeks that count. Gap and intraday formulas therefore have only the training weeks from 2008 on.
- **Composite (a reading of "mean of the 20 oriented ranks"):** each selected formula is re-ranked to a percentile across the universe, flipped if its training sign is negative, and centred at 0. A missing component counts as 0 (neutral), and a stock with fewer than 10 of the 20 has no score.
- **Shuffled runs:** the training weeks' outcome vectors are permuted across weeks with a seeded random permutation, matched by stock (a stock missing from the source week has no outcome). Fixed points are allowed. The whole selection is redone each run, and the run's best |t| and its composite's real holdout IC are recorded. The bar for test 1 is `np.percentile` of the runs' best |t| at 95 (linear interpolation).
  - **What the shuffle keeps:** moving whole weeks breaks the link between a week's features and its own outcomes, but keeps each stock's average outcome over the training years. Persistent cross-sectional effects (size, volatility or beta premia, survivor-only data) survive it. So the shuffled best |t| is not near zero, and a composite chosen on shuffled data can show real holdout IC. That makes test 1's bar conservative; it is the plan's design.
- **Test 2:** pass needs the composite's holdout t ≥ 3 and a mean weekly IC above 5-day reversal's (the base rank of −ret5) over the same weeks. The paired difference's t is reported too.
- **Test 3:** the composite's mean weekly IC over the unseen months' decision days (from 2025-06-30, outcome windows ending by 2026-10-19) must be above 0.
- **Money test:** composite scores on every trading day from the first trading day on or after 2015-06-28 (2015-06-29), ranked within that day's universe, NaN before. The judge skips the years 2006-07..2014-15 as having no scores, so its walk-forward JSON holds exactly the 10 test years and `change_over_random` over them is the file-name number. The judge's first decision of 2015-16 falls on the last close before 2015-06-28, which is NaN as instructed; 5-day reversal graded the same way gives +9.5 (uniform), against +11.8 over all 18 years.
- **Number of shuffled runs:** a timing test before any result ran one shuffled selection on a throwaway seed and printed only its time, 21 s. So all 20 runs the plan asks for were made, with seeds 20261021 + run.
- **Data facts seen while building (before any outcome):**
  - The universe has 300 stocks on 2005-01-03, because few stocks in the export have data before 2005. It has 891 by the end of 2006, about 1,000–1,350 through 2014 and up to 2,375 by 2025; the median over all decision weeks is 1,373.
  - 99.997% of universe stock-weeks have a 5-day outcome (survivor-only data). Inside the universe, every base feature covers ≥ 99.4% of stock-weeks, except the gap and intraday features, which start in 2008 (99.9% from then).
- **The unseen months:** the composite's IC uses the new export's own universe (2,253–2,647 stocks), and the judge uses its own 2,155 candidates. The judge's IC and long-short are on its candidates.
- **After the results, nothing was changed.** The shuffled runs' better holdout ICs and the slow formulas' holdout t's are reported as hindsight only.
- **Workarounds (no shared code edited):** `python3 -I` drops the script's folder from `sys.path`, so each script adds `research/formula_mining` itself. The system Python has no scipy, so average-tie ranks and Spearman are written in numpy (checked against brute force to 1e-15). No bug in `c2eval.py` was found.
