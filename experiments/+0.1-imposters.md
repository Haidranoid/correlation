# Imposters and undiscovered members

**TL;DR:** This compared what each company does, its 10-K business description (Item 1, from EDGAR-CORPUS), with what it moves with, a monthly groups map of the 1,500 most-traded stocks, from 2006 to 2022. "Imposters" move with a group whose business their filing doesn't describe; "undiscovered members" describe a group's business but don't move with it. The story half holds, but the price half doesn't. Over the next 12 months an undiscovered member's co-movement with the group rises and closes 81–87% of the gap (t ≈ 6–7; against matched controls, t ≈ 9–10). An imposter's closes about a quarter of it (t = 8.4) once the selection uses only odd days; the first, leakier version found no closing at all. Prices don't correct. An imposter's group-driven weekly moves are not given back: they continue slightly (+0.07 per unit over 4 weeks, t = +0.6, where the plan needed t ≤ −3). Undiscovered members don't follow their group's week (t = −0.3). When a hot theme cools, imposters fall *less* than matched true members (+1.0% over 63 days, t = +1.2). The weekly money test shorts imposters and buys true members after a group's strong weeks, and the reverse after weak ones. It lost 2.9% a year before costs, against −0.0% for the median same-group random guesser, and was ahead in only 5 of 16 years. So it fails, and every return test points the opposite way to the hypothesis: imposters carry the group's move forward. The judge's long-only account on the long side made +1.4 points a year over random (se 3.1, ahead in 8 of 15 years), and its long-short is noise (z = 0.67). The text stops at fiscal 2020, because SEC refuses requests without an email address, so classification ends in May 2022. The 2020–22 months look the same, with no return test significant, and the 16 unseen months (2025-06-30..2026-10-19) couldn't be tested. Verdict: no edge. The market slowly brings co-movement in line with the business description, but not in a way the price shows ahead of time.

**Question:** when what a company *does* (its 10-K) disagrees with what it *moves with* (the groups map), does the price correct?

## The idea

There are two ways to say what group a company belongs to:
- **The market's answer:** what it moves with. That is the groups map, and same-day co-movement is stable and real.
- **The company's own answer:** what it does, in the business description it files every year (10-K, Item 1).

Usually the two agree. Where they don't, one of them is wrong for now, and it is more likely to be the market, since a filing is a legal description of the business.

**Imposters** move with a group whose business they aren't in:
- the small company that mentioned AI and now trades like a chip stock;
- the ".com" name changes of 1999 (Cooper, Dimitrov & Rau 2001);
- a stock swept into a theme basket.

When the group moves, imposters move with it for reasons that don't apply to them. **Hypothesis:** an imposter's group-driven moves are given back, and the imposter falls harder than the real members when the theme cools.

**Undiscovered members** are the reverse. Their business is in the group's line, but the market hasn't put them in it yet, so they don't move with it. **Hypothesis:** they follow the group's moves late. This half is close to Hoberg & Phillips (2018) at a monthly horizon, so it serves as a calibration; the imposter half is the new part.

**The gap itself is a prediction too.** If the market learns, an imposter's co-movement with the group should fall toward what its business implies, and an undiscovered member's should rise. That can be checked before any trading test, and it tells whether the "temporary mistake" story is right at all.

## Why it might work where the census didn't

- **It brings in information the price-only tests never had.** Every family tested so far used prices (or outside time series) to predict prices. Text is a different kind of information about *structure*: who should move with whom.
- **It is slow and cheap to trade:** rebalanced weekly or monthly, group-neutral, not daily.
- **The behavioural story is well documented.** Investors trade in categories and labels, which creates co-movement that fundamentals don't support:
  - Barberis, Shleifer & Wurgler 2005 (index inclusion);
  - Boyer 2011 (style labels);
  - Green & Hwang 2009 (splits);
  - Kumar, Page & Spalt 2013.
  - What nobody has done is measure the mismatch continuously, for every stock, and trade it.

## Data

New data is needed; this is the one experiment that needs it. Everything listed is free.
- **10-K business descriptions, 1993–2020:** EDGAR-CORPUS (Loukas et al. 2021) on Hugging Face, already split by item, with CIK and year. Licence Apache-2.0.
- **10-Ks for 2021–2026:**
  - the EDGAR full index (`form.idx` lists form type, company, CIK, filing date and path);
  - then fetch each 10-K and cut out Item 1;
  - the SEC asks for a User-Agent header and at most 10 requests a second, so about 30,000 filings is an evening's download.
- **CIK → ticker:** SEC `company_tickers.json` is current-only. Stooq's data has only stocks listed today, so a current map covers most of the universe. The survivorship that hurts elsewhere helps here. Ticker changes need a name-match fallback using company names in `form.idx`.
- **Point in time:** a 10-K counts from its *filing date*, not its fiscal year end.
- **Hoberg-Phillips TNIC** is free (1989–2023) but keyed by Compustat gvkey, and there is no free gvkey→ticker map. So we build the similarity ourselves, from the same filings, keyed by CIK. Their replication code for one year is on their site.

**Text similarity, two options:**
- **Hoberg-Phillips style (start here):** a word list of each description's nouns, then cosine similarity. No model, so no look-ahead.
- **Embeddings:** a local embedding model on the RTX 5080. Don't let an LLM write or label descriptions: a model trained in 2025 knows which companies became "AI stocks" later, which is look-ahead. Only the filed text, embedded as it stands, is safe.

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Groups:** the groups map built every month: market-relative, 20–40 groups of the 1,500 most-traded stocks over a 252-day correlation period.

**Co-movement exposure** of stock i to group g: the slope of i's market-relative return on g's return, with g's return computed without i, over the last 252 days.

**Text similarity** of i to g: the cosine similarity of i's latest 10-K Item 1 to the average of g's members' (without i). Only filings made before the date count.

**Expected exposure:**
- Each month, fit across all stocks: exposure ≈ a + b × text similarity, separately for a stock's own group and for other groups.
- **Gap** = actual exposure − expected exposure.

**Classes:**
- **Imposter in g:** the top decile of the gap for the group it moves with most.
- **True member:** a gap near zero, with text similarity in the top quartile for that group.
- **Undiscovered member of g:** the bottom decile of the gap, for a group it is textually close to (top 5% of similarity) but isn't in.

## Tests, in order (stop at the first failure)

1. **Does the gap close?** This tests the story, not money.
   - Over the next 6 and 12 months, do imposters' exposures fall and undiscovered members' exposures rise, more than mean reversion alone predicts?
   - For the control, take stocks with the same starting exposure but a gap near zero.
   - **Stop if** the gap is a fixed trait that never closes. The market then isn't making a temporary mistake, it's seeing something the filing doesn't say.
2. **Imposters give back group-driven moves.** Do the group-driven parts of their weekly returns (exposure × group return) reverse over the next 1–4 weeks?
   - It has to go beyond the group's own continuation (industry momentum) and beyond the imposter's own short-term reversal.
   - **Stop unless** the effect is t ≥ 3 over 2005–2019.
3. **Undiscovered members catch up.** Does the group's return over the last week predict their next 1–4 weeks?
   - This is the Hoberg-Phillips calibration at a shorter horizon. Being right here makes the pipeline more credible even if imposters fail.
4. **When the theme cools:**
   - After a group's best 21-day stretch ends (its 21-day return turns from the top decile to negative), do imposters fall more than true members over the next 1–3 months?
   - Compare with beta-matched true members.
5. **The unseen years:** 2020–2026 with the same rules. That includes the AI theme, an obvious live case.
6. **Money test** (the existing harness, weekly rebalance):
   - Within each group, short imposters and buy true members after the group's strong weeks, and the reverse after weak weeks. Match the two sides on exposure, so the book is group-neutral and market-neutral. Hold 4 weeks.
   - **Random guessers:** the same group, the same number of stocks, picked at random from members.
   - **Pass:** beats random guessers at p < 0.05 after 5 bps per side in walk-forward, and positive in at least two-thirds of years.

**The cost hurdle:** weekly rebalancing with 4-week holds means roughly a quarter of the book is traded each week. At 5 bps per side the strategy pays about 2.5 bps a week, about 1.3% a year. Any effect worth having clears it.

## Ways it can fool us

- **10-K text is boilerplate-heavy and slow.** A company that moved into AI in March shows it only in next year's 10-K, so some "imposters" are really early movers whose filings lag. That works against the hypothesis, so a positive result is conservative; but a negative result could mean the text is stale. Report results by months since the last 10-K.
- **Conglomerates are similar to several groups.** Use the group with the largest exposure, and report multi-segment firms (similarity spread across groups) separately.
- **Survivorship cuts both ways.** Failed imposters are missing from Stooq, which flatters long imposter positions. The test shorts imposters, so missing failures bias it *against* finding the effect.

## Effort

About 3–5 days, mostly the data pipeline:
- download EDGAR-CORPUS;
- fetch and cut out Item 1 for 2021–2026;
- map CIKs;
- compute monthly similarities.

The co-movement side (groups, exposures) and the harness exist. Similarity for about 5,000 firms a year on the CPU takes minutes; embeddings on the GPU are an option.

- **The losses are concentrated in 2006–2010,** especially 2008-09. Since 2010 the book is close to random, ahead in 5 of 12 years.
- **The judge,** with scores = the book's net weight from the active cohorts (positive = long side), 15 years 2007-08..2021-22 (it skips 2006-07, which lacks three years of history before it):
  - weekly rebalance: change over random **+1.4 points a year** (se 3.1, ahead in 8 of 15 years), beat random at p < 0.1 in 3 of 15 years (chance 1.5); rank IC in actual returns z = +0.84; long-short −0.48 bps/day (2008-09 alone −32 bps/day), positive in 10 of 15 years, z = +0.67; 47% turnover per rebalance;
  - daily rebalance: +2.2 points a year (se 2.4, ahead in 8 of 15); long-short −0.78 bps/day, z = +0.75.
- **The judge's long-short agrees with the book on average:** −0.48 bps/day, about −1.2% a year, mostly from 2008-09. It is positive in 10 of 15 years, which is why its combined z is slightly positive. Both say there is nothing to trade.

**What it adds up to.** The "temporary mistake" story is half right. Co-movement does drift toward what the business description implies, strongly for undiscovered members and partly for imposters. But the return tests all point the other way, and none clears its bar. Imposters keep their group's moves rather than giving them back, fall less when a theme cools, and the book that bets on correction loses. Whatever moves co-movement toward the text happens without a price correction that a weekly trader could catch.

## Decisions made while building it

All of these were made on 2026-10-21 **before any outcome data was looked at** (no exposures after a classification date, no returns after it), unless a line says otherwise.

**Data and point in time**
- **10-K text:** EDGAR-CORPUS, fiscal years 2000–2020, only Item 1 kept (about 34 GB streamed, never stored raw). The corpus has no filing date, and its `year` is the fiscal year, not the filing year: in a sample of 2003 filings, 109 of 117 with a dated "year ended …" phrase name a fiscal year end in that same year, and in 2020, 59 of 60. So a filing counts from **June 1 of the next year**, or 120 days after a later fiscal-year end the text mentions most (retailers whose year ends in January), whichever is later. That covers the 90-day deadline and the 15-day extension. It is conservative: most filings were public 2–3 months earlier.
- **SEC refused requests** without an email in the User-Agent (403 on `company_tickers.json` and on the full index), so no EDGAR filings after the corpus were fetched. **The text stops at fiscal 2020.** The last month whose expected latest 10-K is in the corpus is May 2022, so classification months end at 2022-05. **The 16 unseen months (2025-06-30..2026-10-19) can't be tested.**
- **CIK → ticker:** a public mirror of SEC's `company_tickers_exchange.json` on Hugging Face (`DenyTranDFW/SEC_Company_Tickers_Exchange`, GPL, updated 2026-10-18), matched to the export's tickers exactly (after mapping `.` to `-`). Current tickers only, no name-match fallback.
- **Price data:** the old export (through 2025-06-27). It has closes for only about 320 stocks before 2005, so a 252-day correlation period with enough stocks first exists at the end of **2006-02** (825 stocks grouped; 311 a month earlier). Classification months are **2006-02..2019-12** for tests 1–4 (the plan said 2005–2019) and **2020-01..2022-05** for test 5. Grouped stocks rise from 825 to the full 1,500 by 2017.

**Groups and exposures**
- **Groups map:** re-implemented from `together.rs` (`groups`): non-ETF stocks with median dollar volume ≥ $5M and a return on ≥ 95% of the 252 days, the 1,500 most traded, market-relative returns (equal-weight mean of those stocks times each stock's beta), positive part of the correlation matrix, spectral clustering, k-means with 10 k-means++ restarts. **30 groups** (the middle of the plan's 20–40), refit at every month end.
- **Universe for classes:** the grouped stocks that have a valid 10-K (below). Stocks outside the 1,500 have no group and are not classified.
- **Exposure:** slope of the stock's market-relative daily return on the group's (equal-weight mean of the members' market-relative returns, without the stock), over the 252 days. Missing days count as zero.
- **Group's own return** (tests 2–4, money test): equal-weight mean of the members' market-relative returns, members fixed at the classification month. Market-relative returns after the classification date use the stock's beta from that month's correlation period and the same equal-weight market.

**Text**
- **Words:** Item 1 tokens of 3+ letters, lowercased, plural nouns folded to the singular with WordNet's noun list. A word is kept if it is noun-like (WordNet has a noun sense, or doesn't know the word), not a stop word, lowercase in at least 25% of the filings that use it (drops names, places, brands, acronyms), and used by at least 2 and at most 25% of that fiscal year's filings (all corpus filings). Each filing is a set of words, a unit vector; similarity is cosine. One filing per company and fiscal year (the one with the most words); at least 50 kept words.
- **Valid text:** the latest filing available at the date, if its fiscal year is no more than one year older than the expected latest one.
- **Text similarity of i to g:** cosine of i's vector with the mean of the vectors of g's members with text, without i. A group needs **at least 5 members with text**, or it is left out that month.
- **Relative similarity (changed before outcomes, after looking at the classes):** the raw cosine with a group's average vector rises with the length of the filing: a stock's mean similarity over all groups correlates 0.89 with the log of its word count, and long generic filings (Ford, CME, Berkshire, Qualcomm) came out as "undiscovered members" of three or four groups at once. So the similarity used everywhere is the cosine **divided by the stock's mean cosine over all valid groups**. This also fits co-movement better on the same day's data (correlation with own-group exposure 0.29 against 0.25, with other-group exposure 0.27 against 0.20, June 2008). No returns after the classification date were looked at.

**Expected exposure and classes** (each month, all pairs of a classified stock and a group)
- **Fit:** two cross-sectional OLS lines, exposure on text similarity: one over own-group pairs (the group the map put the stock in), one over all other pairs. Gap = exposure − fitted value.
- **The group it moves with most** = the group whose return its market-relative return correlates with most (leave-one-out for its own group). First set as the largest exposure, then changed before outcomes: the slope favours groups whose average barely moves (large, loose groups), so it disagreed with the map for 31% of stocks; correlation agrees with the map for about 80%. The gap is still in exposure (slope) units.
- **Imposter in g:** g is the group it moves with most, and its gap for g is in the top decile of all stocks' gaps for the group they move with most.
- **True member of g:** g is both its map group and the group it moves with most; gap near zero, meaning |gap| ≤ 0.25 × the standard deviation of those gaps that month; text similarity to g in the top quartile of all classified stocks' similarity to g.
- **Undiscovered member of g:** not in g on the map and g is not the group it moves with most; g is its textually closest group outside its map group (added before outcomes, so one generic filing can't be a candidate for many groups); similarity to g in the top 5% of the non-members' similarity to g; gap in the bottom decile of those candidate pairs (pooled over groups).
- **Multi-segment firms:** second-highest group similarity ≥ 90% of the highest. Reported separately.

**Test 1 (does the gap close)**
- Exposures 6 and 12 months later are to the **frozen** groups of the classification month (same members), over the 252 days ending 6 or 12 months later.
- **Selection on noise:** the top-decile gap partly selects lucky estimation noise, which would "close" by itself. So for test 1, classes are made from **odd-day** exposures (fit refit on them), and the starting exposure is the **even-day** exposure of the same 252 days, whose noise is independent of the selection.
- **Control:** for each imposter (undiscovered pair), the pair with the nearest starting (even-day) exposure among same-kind pairs whose odd-day gap is near zero (|gap| ≤ 0.25 sd), same month.
- **Statistic:** the monthly mean of (change in exposure − control's change), averaged over months, Newey-West t with 12 lags (6 for 6 months).
- **Fixed-trait check:** the share of the starting gap (even-day exposure minus the fitted value, relative to all pairs of the same kind that month) still there 12 months later. A fixed trait keeps all of it (1.0); measurement noise doesn't lower it, because the even-day gap and the later gap don't share noise with the selection.
- **Pass, per arm:** at 12 months the control difference has the hypothesised sign with |t| ≥ 3, **and** the gap's 12-month remaining share is below 1 with t ≥ 3. An arm that fails is stopped. Imposters failing stops tests 2, 4 and the money test; undiscovered members failing stops test 3. Later tests are still run and reported, marked as past the stopping point.

**Tests 2–4**
- **Weeks:** calendar weeks, from the last close of one week to the last close of the next. Classes from the latest month end on or before the week's last day.
- **Test 2:** weekly Fama-MacBeth regressions over imposters: future market-relative return over the next 1, 2 and 4 weeks on the group-driven part (exposure × the group's market-relative return this week), the imposter's own market-relative return this week, and the group's return this week. The effect is the coefficient on the group-driven part, negative if it is given back. **Primary: 4 weeks**, Newey-West t (lags = horizon − 1). Stop unless t ≤ −3 over 2006–2019.
- **Test 3:** weekly Fama-MacBeth over undiscovered pairs: future 1, 2, 4-week market-relative return on the group's return this week and the stock's own return this week. **Primary: 4 weeks**, pass if t ≥ 2 (the plan gives no bar; 2 because it is a calibration).
- **Test 4:** an event is the first day the group's 21-day market-relative return turns negative after being in the top decile of its own past 252 days of 21-day returns within the last 21 days (at most one per group and classification month). Each imposter of g is paired with the true member of g with the nearest exposure to g. Outcome: imposter minus true member, market-relative return over the next 21, 42 and 63 days. **Primary: 63 days**, pass if t ≤ −2, standard errors clustered by month.
- **Test 5:** tests 1–4 again on classification months 2020-01..2022-05. Passes if the primary statistics keep their sign (t reported; 29 months is short).

**Money test**
- **Book:** every week, in each group in the top third of the 30 groups by this week's market-relative return ("strong week"), short its imposters and buy the same number of true members matched on exposure to the group (nearest, without replacement where possible); in the bottom third ("weak week") the reverse. Middle third: no trade. Each week's set of trades is a cohort, equal weight, $1 long and $1 short; held 4 weeks; the book is the average of the last 4 cohorts. Returns are actual returns from `close`; costs 5 bps per side on the book's turnover. A market-relative version is also reported.
- **Random guessers:** for each classification month and group, the same numbers of stocks drawn at random from the group's members, held fixed through the month (so turnover is like the strategy's), with the strategy's sides each week. 1,000 of them.
- **Walk-forward:** the judge's years (June 28 to June 27); years with text: 2006-07..2021-22. Pass as in the plan: beats random guessers at p < 0.05 after 5 bps over all years, and ahead of the median guesser in at least two thirds of years.
- **Judge:** scores = the book's net weight from the active cohorts (positive = long, negative = short), times the group's absolute weekly return so that ties break toward stronger signals, written on every day of each 4-week hold. Graded with `rebalance=5` (weekly); the file-name number is its change over random at 0 bps. `rebalance=1` is also reported.

**Made after seeing results**
- **Test 1 on an odd-day groups map.** The rule needed a starting exposure whose noise is independent of the selection. The first build made the classes from odd days, but built the groups map from all 252 days. Group membership, which decides a stock's own group and who is a non-member, so used the even days too. After seeing that version's results, the map was rebuilt from odd-day correlations only and test 1 rerun. Both versions are reported. Neither changes the verdict: the imposter arm stops at test 2 and the undiscovered arm at test 3 either way.
- **6-month exposures over the 126 days after classification.** The plan's 252-day window 6 months on shares half its days with the classification period, including the odd days used to select. So it can't show closing. Both are reported, but only the non-overlapping version means anything.
- **"Share of the gap closed"** is reported as 1 minus the share remaining, with its t signed so that positive means closing, for both arms.
- **The judge's scores end on 2022-06-24,** the last decision day of the 2021-22 test year. Without that, the last cohorts' holds spilled three weeks into 2022-23, and the judge graded those weeks as a year of their own (with absurd z-scores). The judge grades 15 years, 2007-08..2021-22. It skips 2006-07, because too few stocks have the 90% of 756 days of history it needs before a test year. The book's own test covers 16 years, 2006-07..2021-22.
- **Tests 2–6 were run past the stopping points,** as asked, and are marked as such.
