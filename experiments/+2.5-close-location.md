# Close location: does where a stock closes in its day's range carry into next week?

**TL;DR:** Close location is a common technical indicator; testing it across the market, against each stock's twin and with the same-day move controlled for, appears to be new. It predicts nothing that the week's return doesn't already predict. P is where a stock closed in its day's range minus where its twin fund closed, averaged over 5 days. Over 2008–2019, a weekly regression held the same-day and 5-day returns fixed. With them held fixed, the stocks with the highest P did 0.07% a week worse than those with the lowest (t −1.40), against a pass mark of |t| ≥ 3, so it fails Test 1. That fixed the direction as reversal: buy low P, short high P. On its own P does predict (−0.16% a week, t −4.04), but it moves with the week's return (rank correlation +0.39), and the 5-day return absorbs most of that. The weekly book that buys the tenth with the lowest P and shorts the tenth with the highest earned +0.18% a week before costs (t +4.0). After 5 bps per side it earned +0.01% (t +0.2), because it replaces about 87% of its names every week. The controlled coefficient changed sign afterwards: +0.04% a week in 2020–2025-06 (t +0.39) and +0.27% in the 16 unseen months (t +1.67). In the unseen months the book lost 0.33% a week after costs (t −2.2). The judge's long-only account on −P beat turnover-matched random accounts by +8.2 points a year (se 3.7, ahead in 13 of 18 years; volatility-matched +8.3). That is plain reversal: 5-day reversal on exactly the same stocks and days gives +11.8, and P's own part, with the two returns regressed out each day, gives −1.5. In the unseen months the account made −6.4% against +19.3% for the median random account (p = 0.99). Verdict: no edge. Where a stock closes in its range, measured against its twin, adds nothing to plain short-term reversal, and it fails Tests 1, 2 and 3.

## The idea

- **The signal:** a stock that closes at the top of its day's range had buyers pushing it into the close; one that closes at the bottom had sellers.
- **Two readings:**
  - Late-day pressure often comes from institutions working large orders over several days, so it might continue.
  - Or it might be temporary closing-auction pressure that reverses.
- **The measure:** each stock's close location against its twin fund's (to take out sector days), averaged over a week. The test asks whether it predicts the next week.
- **The bar:** closing near the high is partly just an up day, so the test must beat the same-day and 5-day returns, not just random. The direction is fixed by 2008–2019 and must hold afterwards.

## Nearest prior work

From the earlier survey; a separate novelty check runs alongside.

| Work | What it found |
|---|---|
| Technical analysis (Williams %R, accumulation/distribution) | Close location as a buying-pressure gauge; rarely tested systematically |
| Bogousslavsky & Muravyev 2023, *JFM* | Closing-auction price deviations reverse quickly and almost completely |
| Heston, Korajczyk & Sadka 2010, *JF* | Intraday return patterns repeat at the same half-hour on later days (institutional order splitting) |
| Gao, Han, Li & Zhou 2018, *JFE* | Market intraday momentum: the first half-hour predicts the last |

**Novelty check (2026-10-21, a separate search; it changed no rules):** partly done, on thin evidence.
- **Johnston, "A High-Low Price Anomaly"** (Purdue PhD dissertation; year unverified): a close away from the high-low midpoint predicts abnormal returns across size groups and subperiods, which it attributes to attention. No formula or sign in the abstract.
- **No peer-reviewed test** of close location value, Williams %R or accumulation/distribution was found; only practitioner backtests on single stocks.
- **Bogousslavsky & Muravyev 2023:** closing prices sit away from the midpoint; half of that reverts soon after the close and all of it overnight. Closing auctions grew from 3.1% of daily volume in 2010 to 7.5% in 2018.

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Universe:** the judge-like liquid set with a twin, minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)`, from 2008.

**Bad prints:** high and low come only from the unrepaired store. Treat a day as missing when (H − L)/C > 0.5, H < C, L > C, or H = L.

**Close location:** CLV = ((C − L) − (H − C)) / (H − L), in [−1, +1].

**Signal:** P = the mean over the last 5 days of (CLV of the stock − CLV of its twin). The twin comes from `c2eval.twins`, latest at or before each day.

**Book:**
- Weekly (every 5 trading days, fixed calendar): long the top 10% of P, short the bottom 10%, or the reverse if test 1 finds the negative direction.
- Equal weight, held 5 days. Costs 5 bps per side.

**The direction rule:** the sign of P's effect is fixed by test 1 (2008–2019) and then held unchanged for every later test.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **2008–2019:** each week, a cross-sectional regression of the next 5-day market-relative return on P, the same-day return, and the 5-day return (all ranked). Average P's coefficient over weeks. **Pass:** |t| ≥ 3. That fixes the direction.
2. **The book in that direction, 2008–2019:** net of 5 bps per side, mean weekly return with t ≥ 2.
3. **2020–2025-06:** same sign as test 1. The 16 unseen months (new export and store, once, at the end): same sign.

**Money test (the judge):**
- Scores = P (or −P, per test 1) every day, graded with `rebalance=5`.
- Uniform and volatility-matched random accounts are both reported.

## The number for the file name

The judge's walk-forward change over random, with uniform random accounts, 0 bps (`c2eval.change_over_random`).

## Ways it can fool us

- **Same-day reversal:** a stock closing at its high had an up day, and up days reverse. The regression controls for it; report P's raw effect too.
- **Thin stocks:** their highs and lows are noisy. Report the 500 largest.

## Results (run 2026-10-21)

**How to read these tables:**
- **The universe:** the judge-like liquid set with a twin, which ranks a median of 1,103 stocks a week over 2008–2019 (300 in 2008, about 2,000 by 2024).
- **Returns:** actual returns from export closes.
- **"% a week":** per 5-trading-day hold.
- **Coefficients:** next-week return of the top of the ranked variable minus the bottom; the regressors are ranks scaled 0..1.
- **The book:** $1 long and $1 short. In Test 1's direction it is long the 10% with the lowest P and short the 10% with the highest.

### Verdict

- **Fails Test 1:** with the same-day and 5-day returns held fixed, P's coefficient is −0.073% a week, t −1.40, well short of \|t\| ≥ 3.
- **Also fails:**
  - **Test 2:** the book nets +0.009% a week, t +0.19.
  - **Test 3:** the sign is positive in 2020–2025-06 (t +0.39) and in the unseen months (t +1.67).
- **What P measures is reversal.** Alone it predicts (t −4.0), and the judge's account on it beats random by +8.2 points a year. But both are the 5-day return's reversal. Graded the same way, P's own part earns −1.5, and plain reversal on the same cells earns +11.8.
- **Neither reading of close location survives.** No sign of institutions' late buying carrying into the next week, and no closing-auction pressure reversing beyond what the price change itself reverses.

## Decisions made while building it

All made **before seeing any results** unless marked otherwise.

- **Universe (the judge-like liquid set):** the judge's own rule (`stockai/judge/src/holdout.rs` `liquid_universe`, as twin gap fade and analog days apply it), made point in time on `c2eval.twins`' calendar. At twin rebalance day t0: `v` finite on ≥ 90% of the 756 days ending at t0, and a median close ≥ $5 and median dollar volume ≥ $5M over the 252 days ending at t0. Then: a twin at t0, and not `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)` at t0. It applies to formation days d with `rebal_index(tw, d)` = that rebalance.
  - **2008 is thin.** `v` exists only from about 2006, so in 2008 the rule passes about 355 stocks; from 2009 on it passes 880–2,180. This is the judge's own restriction, so it is kept.
- **The 500 largest:** the 500 universe members with the highest median dollar volume over the 63 days ending at t0 (the export has no market caps; twin reversal used the same proxy). They are ranked within the 500, not filtered from the all-stock book.
- **Prices:**
  - CLV uses high, low and close all from `STORE_OLD` (`STORE_NEW` for the unseen months), stocks by `E['ids']` and twin funds by `tw['fund']`. One source keeps the bad-print comparisons consistent. On every universe stock-day from 2008, store and export closes agree to 0.1% (`out/build_old.txt`).
  - Every return (same-day, 5-day, next week, the book) comes from the export's closes, which are repaired, in actual returns.
- **Bad prints:** the plan's four rules, plus any of H, L or C missing or ≤ 0, for stocks and funds alike. They remove 0.002–0.07% of universe stock-days a year and up to 0.1% of twin fund-days. The exceptions for both are 2011 (stocks 0.26%, funds 0.34%: the 2011-02-17 gap in the store) and 2025 (stocks 0.34%, funds 0.25%: missing store rows). Counts by rule and year are in `out/build_old.txt`.
- **Start:** CLV is computed from 2008-01-02 only. P is first defined on 2008-01-08, the first day whose 5-day window lies entirely in 2008.
- **Twin by day:** each day's difference CLV(stock) − CLV(twin) uses that day's twin (`rebal_index` of that day). When the twin changes inside a 5-day window, the earlier days keep the earlier twin. P needs at least 4 of the 5 differences, as `c2eval.twin_residual` allows one missing day.
- **Weekly calendar:** formation days are export indices 1515 + 5m (2008-01-08 first), with holds from close d to close d+5. The new export also starts on 2001-12-31, so it has the same indices.
  - **Test 1** uses weeks formed 2008-01-08..2019-12-31.
  - **Test 3** uses weeks formed from 2020-01-01 whose hold ends by 2025-06-27.
  - **The unseen months** use weeks formed on or after 2025-06-27 in the new export whose hold ends by 2026-10-19.
- **Ranked set:** each week, universe members with:
  - a finite P;
  - an export close on day d;
  - a same-day log return (d−1 → d), a 5-day log return (d−5 → d) and a next-5-day return, all from forward-filled export closes.

  A week with fewer than 100 names would be skipped; none was.
- **The regression:**
  - **Dependent variable:** the next 5-day simple return minus the export market's 5-day simple return (`market` is the equal-weight mean daily log return of the liquid stocks). With an intercept, the market term only shifts the intercept.
  - **Regressors:** P, the same-day return and the 5-day return, each as a cross-sectional rank scaled to [0, 1], ties averaged. P's coefficient is the next-week return of the top of P minus the bottom, holding the two returns fixed, in % a week.
  - **The t:** OLS each week, then a plain t of the mean coefficient over the non-overlapping weeks (Fama–MacBeth).
  - **Actual returns:** the dependent variable is not ranked. A ranked-y version is a diagnostic only.
- **P's raw effect:** the same weekly regression with P alone. The decile means are also reported, along with the book, which has no controls.
- **Direction:** the sign of Test 1's mean coefficient, kept for everything after even if |t| < 3.
- **The book:**
  - **Legs:** the same ranked set, with legs of round(10% × n) each. Ties go by stock row.
  - **Holding:** equal weight, bought and held from close d to close d+5 on forward-filled export closes. The book's return is the mean long return minus the mean short return ($1 long, $1 short), unhedged.
  - **Costs:** 5 bps × traded notional, against the previous week's book as it drifted. Kept names pay only their resizing, and the first week pays the build (2.0).
  - **Test 2's t:** a plain t of the net weekly returns.
- **Test 3's "same sign":** P's mean regression coefficient over the period has the same sign as Test 1's, for 2020–2025-06 and for the unseen months alike. The book is reported alongside, but it is not the criterion.
- **Money-test scores:** the sign × P on every day from 2008-01-08, for the universe members in force that day that have an export close; NaN elsewhere. Graded with `rebalance=5`.
  - **2006–07 is skipped.** The judge skips years with no finite score (`holdout.rs`), so 2006–07 drops out.
  - **2007–08 is graded only from 2008-01-08.** Before that, the picks and the matched random accounts both hold cash.
- **Twins for the unseen months:** `c2eval.twins(E_new)`, read from its existing cache.
- **Diagnostics, not pass/fail:**
  - the stock's own 5-day mean CLV without the twin, with and without the controls;
  - the ranked-y regression;
  - the rank correlations of P with the two returns;
  - deciles and calendar years.
- **Code checks** (`out/checks.txt`):
  - P recomputed by hand from raw store rows for 300 random rows matches to 6e-8.
  - The legs are exact deciles.
  - An oracle signal gives t +52.
  - A planted 1.00% effect is recovered as 1.01%.
  - An unchanged book trades nothing after the build.
- **Before the unseen months, after the old-export results (no rule changed):**
  - The unseen book runs from 2025-01 so that its first unseen week pays real turnover, as twin reversal did. Only weeks formed on or after 2025-06-27 are graded.
  - The universe, twins and P for the new export are recomputed from the new export and `STORE_NEW` over the whole history; only the unseen weeks are used.
  - The judge's holdout covers 2025-06-30..2026-10-19.
- **After seeing the money test (post hoc, not pass/fail):** `posthoc.py` grades two diagnostic scores in the judge, on exactly the stocks and days the money test scored:
  - plain 5-day reversal;
  - P's own part: each day, rank(P) regressed on the ranks of the same-day and 5-day returns, times Test 1's sign.

  It answers whether the money test's +8.2 is close location or reversal. Neither result changes a verdict.
- **After seeing results (no effect):** `money.py` first stored the judge's per-year p-values under the wrong key (as None). It was fixed and run again; the judge is deterministic, and the change over random came out identical (+8.23).

## Files

**Code,** in `research/close_location/`; each script runs under the memory-capped scope, for example `systemd-run --user --scope -q -p MemoryMax=8G -p MemorySwapMax=0 -- env OMP_NUM_THREADS=4 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=4 MKL_NUM_THREADS=4 RAYON_NUM_THREADS=4 python3 -I build.py old`:
- `common.py`: constants, CLV with the bad-print rules, the judge-like liquid rule, ranks and t.
- `build.py old|new`: does the following, and writes the data-quality and coverage report to `out/build_<tag>.txt` and `out/build_<tag>_blocks.json`:
  - reads the store's highs, lows and closes and computes CLV;
  - builds the twin difference, P, the universe and the 500 largest;
  - writes the weekly ranked sets and the unsigned money-test scores.
- `checks.py`: the code checks → `out/checks.txt`.
- `tests.py`: Tests 1–3, the raw effect, the 500 largest, periods, calendar years and deciles. Writes `out/tests_old.txt`, `out/tests_old.json` and `out/weekly_old.tsv` (every week's coefficients, book and turnover).
- `money.py sanity|old|new`: the wiring check and the judge (uniform and volatility-matched). Writes `out/sanity_reversal5.txt`, `out/money_old.*`, `out/money_new.*` and `out/judge_<tag>_<random>.txt`.
- `posthoc.py`: the same-cells reversal and P's own part in the judge → `out/posthoc_old.txt`, `out/posthoc_old.json`.
- `unseen.py`: the plan's regression and book on the unseen months → `out/unseen.txt`, `out/unseen.json`.

**Large files,** in `~/.local/share/correlation2/experiments/close_location/`:
- `rows_old.npz`, `rows_new.npz`: the weekly ranked sets;
- `P_old.npy`, `P_new.npy`: unsigned P on the universe;
- `score_old.npy`, `score_new.npy`: −P as graded, with the judge's JSON next to each;
- `posthoc_reversal5_same_cells.npy`, `posthoc_P_own_part.npy` and `sanity_reversal5.npy`, each with its JSON.
