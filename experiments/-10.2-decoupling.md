# Decoupling: when a stock stops moving with its fund, does its own drift keep going?

**TL;DR:** This tested whether a stock that tracked its twin fund for a year and then stopped tracking it for months keeps drifting away from the fund in the direction of its gradual drift. The ingredients are prior art: gradual information is absorbed more slowly than sudden jumps (Da, Gurun & Warachka 2014), and moves without news reverse (Da, Liu & Schaumburg 2014). What's new is using a sustained break in correlation with the stock's own tracking fund as the event that marks a repricing. The drift doesn't continue. The rules give few events: 407 on the old export, 160 of them in 2006–2019. They bunch into a handful of months (late 2012, 2020–21, mid-2023) when a market-wide correlation spike was rolling out of the year-long "before" window. Over 2006–2019 the hedged drift in the event's direction over the next 63 trading days averaged −82 bps (t −0.80, clustered by month). The pass mark was +100 bps with t ≥ 3. The events also trailed their matched non-breaks by 115 bps (t −1.11; Test 2 needed a positive difference with t ≥ 2). From 2020 to June 2025 the drift was −75 bps (t −0.72), the wrong sign for Test 3. Neither direction works on its own (2006–2019: −40 bps when the drift was up, −122 bps when it was down). Putting the 3 big days back into the direction changes nothing (−81 bps). In the 16 unseen months, 62 events (53 with an outcome) gave +208 bps (t +0.60), the hypothesis's sign. That came only from the events as a group falling 573 bps against their twins (t −3.89) while most of them pointed down. In the money test, the judge's account buys the up-pointing events for 63 days. It beat uniform random accounts by +0.3 points a year (se 0.4, ahead in 8 of 17 years) and volatility-matched ones by +0.2, but it held nothing on half the days. In the unseen months it lost 1.2% against +4.2% for the median random account (−5.3 points). Verdict: no edge. It fails Test 1, the first test, and Test 2 and the 2020–2025 part of Test 3 as well. The unseen months have the hypothesis's sign, but only because most events pointed down.

## Nearest prior work

| Work | What it found |
|---|---|
| Da, Gurun & Warachka 2014, *RFS* | Momentum built from many small moves ("frog in the pan") persists more than momentum from a few jumps |
| Da, Liu & Schaumburg 2014, *MS* | Residual reversal works once news-driven moves are removed |
| This project, `+1.1-twin-reversal.md` and `-8.2-silent-peer-drift.md` | No reversal of drift from the twin; no drift after a news day |

**What's new:** a sustained break in correlation with the stock's own tracking fund, as the event that marks a repricing. New outright, as far as the earlier survey found.

**Novelty check (2026-10-21, a separate search; it changed no rules):** not found, a sustained break in correlation with the stock's own tracking fund was not found as an event; nearby results point the plan's way.
- **Engelberg, Gao & Jagannathan 2009** (SSRN 1330689, "An anatomy of pairs trading"): Pairs-trading profits are lower when the initial divergence comes with value-relevant news about one stock, because such divergences tend to persist; positions that don't converge within 10 days are better closed. Divergence on firm-specific news is drift, not noise.
- **Hou, Peng & Xiong 2013** (Princeton working paper, "Is R-squared a measure of market inefficiency?"): In US and international data, stocks with lower return R² show stronger medium-term momentum and stronger long-term reversal. That is the level of co-movement, not a drop in it.
- **Goyal, Jegadeesh & Subrahmanyam 2025** (Review of Finance, "Empirical determinants of momentum"): In out-of-sample international data, the frog-in-the-pan explanation (underreaction to news arriving in small pieces) gets "remarkably robust support" among the momentum explanations tested, with little long-run reversal; independent post-publication support for Da, Gurun & Warachka (2014).
- **Not found:** an event defined by a sustained drop in a stock's correlation with its sector or tracking fund, with the largest days removed, followed by a test of drift in the hedged residual against matched non-breaks.

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Universe:** the judge-like liquid set with a twin, minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)`.

**Residual:**
- e(d) = r_i(d) − β·r_twin(d), in daily log returns, as `c2eval.twin_residual` builds them;
- the twin and β come from the latest rebalance;
- the twin must be the same at t−63 and t, so a change of twin can't fake a break.

**Break measure at day t:**
- ρ_now = corr(r_i, r_twin) over t−62..t, leaving out the 3 days with the largest |e| in that window;
- ρ_before = the correlation over t−314..t−63, all days;
- Drop = ρ_before − ρ_now.

**Event:** all three hold:
- ρ_before ≥ 0.5;
- Drop ≥ 0.30, both at t and at t−21, so the break is sustained;
- no event for that stock in the previous 126 days.

**Direction:** D = the sign of Σ e over t−62..t, leaving out the same 3 days. That is the gradual part of the drift.

**Outcome:** D × Σ e over t+1..t+63, the hedged drift over the next three months.

**Baseline: matched non-breaks.** Stock-days without a break (Drop < 0.10 at t), in the same calendar month, with the same D, and in the same decile of |Σ e without the 3 days|. The matching is the control for the stock's own return.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **2006–2019:** the mean event outcome is at least +100 bps, with t ≥ 3 and standard errors clustered by calendar month.
2. **Beats the matched non-breaks:** the difference has t ≥ 2.
3. **2020–2025-06:** same sign. **The 16 unseen months** (new export, once, at the end): same sign.

**Reported:** the number of events per year, and the outcome with the 3 big days put back into D.

**Money test (the judge):**
- Score = D × |Σ e without the 3 days| for each event, on days t..t+62. Only positive scores are bought. NaN elsewhere.
- Graded with `rebalance=5`. Uniform and volatility-matched random accounts are both reported.

## The number for the file name

The judge's walk-forward change over random, with uniform random accounts, 0 bps (`c2eval.change_over_random`).

## Ways it can fool us

- **Takeover targets:** their prices are pinned to the deal, so the correlation drops and the drift goes to zero. That works against the test.
- **Market turmoil:** events cluster in it, which is why the standard errors are clustered by month.
- **Survivor-only data:** collapsed stocks are missing, which flattens the negative-D side.

### Test 3: 2020 to June 2025, and the unseen months — fails

**2020-01 .. 2025-06-27: −75 bps (t −0.72).** The hypothesis's sign is positive, so this fails. The matched difference is −23 bps (t −0.20).

**The unseen months: +208 bps (t +0.60)**, the hypothesis's sign. This part is met, but it isn't evidence of drift:
- The events fell against their twins as a group, by 573 bps unsigned (t −3.89).
- More of them pointed down (61%), so the signed mean came out positive.
- The up-pointing events lost 440 bps (t −1.75).
- Putting the 3 big days back into D gives +454 bps (t +1.77).
- Events after 2026-07-21 have no 63-day outcome yet; that leaves 53 of 62.

The long-short made −14.3 bps/day (t −0.47), and the rank IC in actual returns had t −0.09. SPY made +24.6% over the same months.

### What it means

- **A sustained correlation break with the twin doesn't mark a drift that continues.** After the break, the gradual part of the stock's move against its twin goes on neither more nor less than in comparable stocks without a break.
- **The rules mostly catch market-wide correlation regimes.** A year-long ρ_before inflated by a crash, followed by a calmer quarter, makes many stocks "decouple" at once. The busiest months (2012-10/11, 2020-08..2021-03, 2023-07..09) are exactly those, and a stock-specific repricing is the exception among them. A Drop measured against the cross-section (each stock's Drop minus the month's median) would target the idea better. That is a new rule for a new test, not a fix to this one.
- **Few, clustered events limit what the test can show.** 160 events in 58 months over 2006–2019 give a standard error near 100 bps, so +100 bps with t ≥ 3 would have needed about +300 bps. The sign is wrong anyway.
- **Caveats:** the data holds only survivors; delisted stocks lose their outcome (more than 3 missing days), which the plan expected to flatten the D < 0 side. Twin choice is `c2eval.twins`' and is sometimes odd (preferred-stock or bond funds as twins). The twin rule (the same twin at t−63 and t) is the main reason 29–46% of universe stock-days a year have no break measure.

## Decisions made while building it

All of these were made **before any forward return was computed**, unless marked otherwise. Order of work: the panels and the event table were built, and the event counts, coverage and example events looked at (no forward returns). These decisions were written down, and the panels checked independently. Then came Tests 1–3 on the old export, the 5-day reversal check, the money test, and last the unseen months.

**Data and the residual**
- **Returns** as `c2eval.twin_residual` builds them: stock daily log returns from the export's closes, with no cap and no filling of the market-wide gap days. Fund daily log returns come from the store's closes, with |log move| > 0.5 treated as missing. The twins are `c2eval.twins(E)` (cached) for each export.
- **One fund and one β per stock-day.** For day t, k = `rebal_index(tw, t)`. ρ_before, ρ_now, both sums and the outcome all use the fund and β of rebalance k. Drop is meaningless if the two correlations are against different funds.
- **"The twin is the same at t−63 and t"** is `fund_row[k−3] == fund_row[k]`. The twins calendar steps by 21 days, so t−63 always falls in rebalance k−3. Where it fails, the stock-day has no break measure.
- **Drop at t−21** is the break measure as it stood at t−21 (the panel value, with rebalance k−1's twin and β). Events also need `fund_row[k−1] == fund_row[k]`, so all four rebalances involved name the same fund. This removed 76 of 5,929 candidate stock-days on the old export.

**The break measure**
- **ρ_now:** the 3 days with the largest |e| are taken among the days of t−62..t where e exists (both returns present). The Pearson correlation of r_i and r_twin is then taken over the remaining days, which need at least 50.
- **ρ_before:** the Pearson correlation over t−314..t−63 on days with both returns, at least 200.
- **Σ e without the 3 days** (S3) sums e over those remaining days; **with them** (Sall) sums e over every day with both returns. D = sign(S3), and the "3 big days back" direction is sign(Sall).
- **Outcome:** Σ r_i − β Σ r_twin over t+1..t+63 with rebalance k's fund and β, missing days counted as 0. It is NaN if the stock or the fund has more than 3 missing daily returns in the window (`twin_residual`'s 5% default at h = 63), or if the window runs past the export. In log-return units; "+100 bps" is 0.0100.

**Universe and events**
- **Universe:** the judge-like liquid set, copied from `research/night_and_day/common.py`. Its rule, recomputed on the twins calendar and held until the next rebalance: closes on ≥ 90% of the trailing 756 days, median close ≥ $5 and median dollar volume ≥ $5M over the trailing 252 days, minus `c2eval.excluded`. Added to it: a twin at k. An event needs the stock in the universe at k(t) only. The 500 largest are the top 500 of the universe by median dollar volume over the trailing 63 days. Median size 1,284 stocks (max 2,284).
- **The 126-day rule** counts trading days and is applied in date order per stock: a candidate day is an event if the stock's previous event is more than 126 trading days earlier. Candidates suppressed this way don't start a new quiet period.
- **Periods** are by event date: Test 1 and 2 on 2006-01-01..2019-12-31, Test 3 on 2020-01-01..2025-06-27 (old export), the unseen months on 2025-06-30..2026-10-19. The unseen events come from a run over the whole new export, kept from 2025-06-30 on, so the 126-day rule sees earlier events.

**Matching and statistics**
- **Candidate stock-days** for the deciles are all universe stock-days of the calendar month with a valid break measure (Drop and S3 defined), events included. The deciles are the 10%..90% quantiles of |S3| over them.
- **Controls** are candidates with Drop < 0.10, the same sign of S3 as the event's D, the same decile, a 63-day outcome, and a different stock. A control's outcome is D × its own forward sum.
- **Sampling:** up to 20 controls per event, without replacement. One generator, seeded 20261021, handles the events in order of (t, stock). An event with no control would drop out of Test 2; none did.
- **The plan's literal rule for a non-break** (Drop < 0.10 only) is the test. A variant whose controls also need ρ_before ≥ 0.5, so they are stocks that tracked their twin and still do, is reported beside it. It doesn't decide.
- **Test 2's statistic:** the mean over events of (event outcome − mean outcome of its controls), with its month-clustered t. "Beats" means a positive difference with t ≥ 2.
- **Clustering:** Liang–Zeger CR1 for a mean, clusters = calendar month of t.
- **"Same sign" in Test 3** means the hypothesis's sign, a positive mean outcome (the Test 1 quantity). The matched difference is reported beside it.

**Money**
- **Scores:** each event writes S3 (= D × |S3|) on days t..t+62 of its stock, NaN elsewhere. All events on the export are included, so events before a holdout year can carry into it. The 126-day rule means one stock's score windows never overlap. Saved with `c2eval.save_scores('decoupling/scores_old' | 'scores_new')`. Graded with `c2eval.judge(path, rebalance=5)`, uniform and volatility-matched, and on the new export with `walk_forward=False, start='2025-06-30'`.
- **Sanity check of the grader:** 5-day reversal through this folder's own path (`decoupling/sanity_reversal5`) gave +11.8 points a year, 8 of 18 years at p < 0.1, as expected.

**Changed or added after seeing results**
- **No rule, threshold or parameter was changed after any forward return was computed.** The numbers above come from the rules as written before Test 1 was run.
- **The regime diagnostic** (`context.py`: monthly median Drop against events per month) was added after the results to explain the clustering. It uses no forward returns and decides nothing.
- **The judge's IC summary** was found degenerate after the money run (above). The file's number doesn't use it.

## Files

**Code** (`research/decoupling/`, system `python3 -I -B` with numpy, CPU only, each job under an 8 GB memory cap):
- `common.py`: settings, the universe (copied from `research/night_and_day/common.py`, plus "has a twin"), the break-measure panels per rebalance block (ρ_before, ρ_now, S3, Sall, the forward sum, all with one fund and β), and the month-clustered mean.
- `build.py`: panels, universe and the event table for one export, with the counts, coverage and example events (no forward returns).
- `checks.py`: recomputes the panel values one stock at a time with plain loops, for every event and 300 random universe stock-days. It also checks the event rules and counts what the twin-at-t−21 requirement removes.
- `tests.py`: the matched non-breaks and Tests 1–3, with the splits by D, the 3 big days back, the 500 largest, and years.
- `money.py`: the judge's scores and grading, uniform and volatility-matched.
- `sanity_reversal5.py`: the 5-day reversal check.
- `context.py`: the regime diagnostic (added after results).

**Outputs** (`research/decoupling/out/`): `events_{old,new}.txt` (counts, coverage, examples), `checks_{old,new}.txt`, `tests_{old,new}.txt`/`.json`, `events_{old,new}.tsv` (every event with its outcome and control mean), `money_{old,new}.txt`/`.json`, `sanity_reversal5.txt`, `context_old.txt`.

**Large files** (`~/.local/share/correlation2/experiments/decoupling/`): the panels `rho_b_`, `rho_n_`, `S3_`, `Sall_`, `fwd_<tag>.npy` ([N, T] float32), `univ_<tag>.npz` (universe and the 500 largest per rebalance), `events_<tag>.npz`, and the scores `scores_{old,new}.npy` and `sanity_reversal5.npy` with the judge's JSON next to them.
