# Same-day co-movement tools (risk, not prediction)

**TL;DR:** No prediction family made money after costs, but same-day co-movement is real and lasting, so the project has four risk tools, in the CLI and in the GUI's Together tab. This test ran the tools' own code at 18 yearly dates from 2008-10-17 to 2025-10-17, using data up to each date, and asked whether their advice beat a random choice over the following year. The last of those years, 2025-10..2026-10, is one the tools never saw. The advice held up for all four tools. Adding the best-diversifiers list's top fund or top stock at 10% of 300 test portfolios a year left next-year volatility 5.1% lower than adding a uniformly random eligible candidate (standard error 0.5, better in 17 of 18 years). It was 4.6% lower than a random candidate of the same volatility and 3.9% lower than SPY, and it gave 0.29 more independent bets than a random pick. The pick landed among the best 2% of candidates the next year. In the unseen year the edge was smaller, +2.6%. The hedge finder's top tracker, at its past hedge ratio, left 68% of a stock's risk the next year, against 91% for a random fund and 83% for SPY at its beta, and it beat SPY for 87% of stocks. Its own forecast of 64% was a little optimistic. The portfolio check's predicted independent bets correlate +0.72 with the next year's across portfolios. But the level for the whole market swings by up to 0.8 bets from year to year, so its error is only a little smaller than giving every portfolio the year's average. The groups map's groups kept 81% of their extra within-group correlation the next year: +0.16, against +0.005 for random groups of the same sizes. Verdict: the tools' advice beats random choices out of sample. The gains are lower risk, not returns, and the data holds survivors only.

## The tools

- **Portfolio check:**
  - portfolio volatility and beta to SPY;
  - independent bets = (weighted average volatility ÷ portfolio volatility)², which is 1 when holdings move as one and N when they move independently;
  - each holding's share of the risk;
  - near-duplicates;
  - correlations now and a year earlier.
- **Best diversifiers:**
  - The ranking is by correlation with the portfolio, lowest first. Correlation doesn't change with leverage.
  - A first version ranked by "bets added", and leveraged inverse funds topped it.
  - Look-alikes (≥ 0.95) are collapsed under the least volatile.
  - Inverse funds are listed separately as hedges.
  - Cash-like funds (under 3% a year) and funds over 75% a year are set aside.
- **Hedge finder:** what tracks a stock and what moves opposite, with hedge ratios and the risk left after hedging.
- **Groups map:**
  - spectral clustering of the 1,000 most-traded stocks after removing the market;
  - each group named by the fund that follows it most closely, skipping leveraged ones;
  - stability across the 5 earlier years against chance;
  - a heatmap in the GUI.

## What the last year looked like (2025-10..2026-10)

- **Large caps moved apart:** AAPL, MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, META and NVDA correlated only +0.07 to +0.54 with each other, against about +0.5 the year before. AAPL's correlation with SPY was +0.32, against +0.75 a year earlier.
- **Oil moved against stocks:** XOM was −0.32 with SPY, against +0.45 a year earlier.
- **Checked to be real data**, not a date shift: the same-day offset is the best match for every stock, and the largest moves are real events.
- **The lesson:** a diversifier picked from one year can stop working the next. The "a year earlier" column is there to show that.

## The test (run 2026-10-21)

The design below was fixed before any outcome was computed.

- **The question:** does each tool's advice, made with data up to a date, beat a random choice over the *following* year?
- **Dates:** October 17 of each year from 2008 to 2025, or the last trading day before it: 18 dates.
  - At each date the tools see only the two years up to it, exactly as the CLI builds them (`Universe::build(store, as_of, 2)`).
  - The following year runs from the next trading day to the next date.
  - For 2025-10-17 it runs to 2026-10-19, the last day of data. That is the year the tools never saw.
- **How:** a Rust example, `crates/c2-census/examples/co_movement_backtest.rs`, calls the tools' own functions (`portfolio`, `diversifiers`, `hedges`, `groups`) at each date. The advice is therefore exactly what `c2` would have printed that day.
- **Test portfolios, 300 at each date:**
  - **200 random:** 5 stocks each, equal weight, from the 500 most-traded liquid stocks (by median dollar volume over the year).
  - **100 concentrated:** a group drawn at random from that date's groups map, among the groups with at least 5 members, and then 5 of its members at random.
  - The seeds are fixed per date.

**1. Best diversifiers (the headline)**
- **The tool's advice:** `diversifiers(u, p, 0.10, 0.75, 15)`, the CLI's defaults. The pick is the first row of "Funds" and the first row of "Single stocks". Each is tested on its own (two arms).
- **The test:** the pick is added at 10% of the money, and the five holdings scale down to 90%. Then the next-year volatility of that 6-holding portfolio is measured as the tool computes it: daily returns over the days where all six have one, sd × √252.
- **Compared with:**
  - **Uniform random:** a candidate of the same kind (fund or stock) drawn uniformly from all those the tool's own filters keep. These are liquid, not held, with a return on 90% of the portfolio's days, 3–75% a year volatility, and not listed as hedges, before look-alikes are collapsed. The expected value over all of them is used, not a draw.
  - **Volatility-matched random:** the average over the 20 eligible candidates of the same kind nearest the pick in past volatility, the pick excluded.
  - **SPY** at 10%.
- **The number for the file name:**
  - For each portfolio and arm: 100 × (1 − next-year volatility with the tool's pick ÷ expected next-year volatility with a uniformly random pick).
  - A year's value is the mean over its portfolios and both arms. The number is the mean of the 18 yearly values, and its standard error is their sd ÷ √18.
  - **+ means lower volatility than random,** which is better here.
- **Also reported:**
  - next-year independent bets with the pick, minus with a random pick;
  - how often the pick beats the random expectation;
  - where the pick ranks among all eligible candidates next year;
  - the tool's own predicted volatility with the pick, against the realized volatility.

**2. Hedge finder**
- **The stocks:** at each date, each of the 500 most-traded liquid stocks. `hedges(u, ticker, all)` returns every candidate it scanned, with its own past hedge ratio and in the CLI's order.
- **The tool's advice:** the top tracker, which is the first row of "Tracks most closely", of any kind, as shown, at its past hedge ratio. The top-tracking *fund* (the first fund in that ranking) is tested too.
- **Risk left next year:** sd(stock − ratio × hedge) ÷ sd(stock), over the days where both have a return.
- **Compared with:**
  - a uniformly random fund among those the hedge finder scanned, each at its own past ratio (the average over all of them);
  - SPY at its past ratio (its beta).

**3. Portfolio check**
- **The portfolios:** the same 300 per date.
- **The test:** the predicted independent bets (last year) against the realized independent bets next year, using the same formula over next year's days.
- **Measures:**
  - the correlation across portfolios;
  - the bias (realized − predicted);
  - the mean absolute error, against predicting every portfolio that date's average.

**4. Groups map**
- **The tool's advice:** `groups(u, 20, 1000, ...)` at each date.
- **Next year:** the same 1,000 stocks' market-relative correlations, using the tool's own definition (each stock minus its beta times the equal-weight average of the 1,000), and the raw correlations.
- **The measure:** the average over all within-group pairs, against random groups of the same sizes. Their expectation is the average over all pairs, and 200 relabelings give the spread.

**Survivorship:** the store holds only stocks still listed in 2026, so every test portfolio and candidate is a survivor. This matters less for risk than for returns, because volatility and correlation don't depend on whether a stock later went bust. It does make the stocks a little calmer than a full universe would be in their final year, for the tool's picks and the random ones alike.

The top tracker's risk left goes from 70.9% to 69.5%. The correlation between portfolios' past and next-year volatility goes from +0.62 to +0.87. "Seen" years are 2008-10..2025-10, and the unseen year is 2025-10..2026-10.

## Decisions made while building it

- **Option (a), a Rust example that calls the tools' own functions,** rather than re-implementing them in Python. The advice tested is what `c2` shows. The example re-implements only what the random baselines need: the eligible-candidate filter of `diversifiers`, which is not exposed. At every portfolio it checks that the tool's pick is in that set (see "The checks held" below).
- **Uniform random is the headline baseline,** because "a random choice" from the same list is the plain reading. The volatility-matched baseline answers a different question: whether the tool's pick beats candidates of the same volatility. The tool's ranking is by correlation, but low-correlation candidates (bond, gold, currency funds) are also calm, so the uniform comparison credits the tool partly for picking calm things.
- **The expectation over every eligible candidate is used** instead of random draws, so the baseline has no sampling noise.
- **Both arms and both portfolio sets are pooled** in the headline, and each is reported separately.
- **Years are the units for the standard error.** Portfolios in the same year share one market and aren't independent.
- **Missing data next year:** a portfolio needs 126 next-year days with a return for all five holdings, and a candidate needs a return on 90% of those days. Otherwise it is dropped, and the counts are reported.
- **`hedges` is called with `show` = all,** so every scanned candidate comes back with its own ratio. The order and ratios are the CLI's. No look-alikes are collapsed in `hedges`, so a share-class twin (GOOG for GOOGL) can be the top tracker. It is kept as the tool shows it, which is why the top-tracking fund is reported too.
- **`groups` is called with no earlier years** (`years_back` = 0), because its in-sample stability check is what this test replaces. The universe has two years rather than the CLI's six, but the last year's returns, screen and dollar volumes don't depend on that. The grouping is the CLI's: at 2025-10-17 all 20 groups match `c2 groups --as-of 2025-10-17` in size, fund and first 8 members.
- **No look-ahead at the as-of date:** each tool's universe ends at the as-of day, so even the bad-print repair, which looks one day ahead, can't see past it.
- **A robustness run, decided after the smoke run and before the full run:**
  - The smoke run (two dates, ten portfolios) showed MSTU's +957% day, an unadjusted reverse split.
  - So `--clean` reruns everything with every next-year daily move of more than 2x up or down treated as missing. That is the 2x threshold of the tool's own bad-print repair, which only catches moves that undo themselves.
  - A median-random baseline was added at the same time. It gives +4.8, against +5.1 for the mean.
  - The headline stays the run on the data as the tools read it. The 18-year summaries of the two runs agree within 0.1.
- **The checks held:**
  - The example's `--check` reproduces `c2 diversify AAPL MSFT NVDA --as-of 2022-06-30` row for row (UUP, ERY, DUST...; PHYS, NEM, CEF...; 2,710 scanned) and `c2 hedge XOM`.
  - All 10,800 picks were in the re-implemented eligible set. 9,914 were its lowest-correlation member and 404 that member's less volatile look-alike. In the other 482, a look-alike that took the first row's place had a higher correlation, so after re-sorting a different row came first, as the CLI would show it.
  - No portfolio, pick or hedged stock was dropped for missing next-year data. With survivors only, almost everything trades through the next year.
- **Years, not portfolios, are the unit:** within a year many portfolios share a pick, with a median of 12 different fund picks and 24 stock picks a year. Treating the 10,800 cases as independent would overstate the precision.
- **Not tested:**
  - the portfolio check's risk shares and near-duplicates;
  - the hedge finder's "moves most opposite" list;
  - the fund each group is named after.

## Independent check (2026-10-21)

A separate agent rebuilt the example and re-ran it, and recomputed the headline numbers from the raw files with its own scripts. It changed nothing.

**Reproduced exactly:** all 12 output files and both run logs are byte-identical.

**Recomputed numbers, all confirmed:**

| Claim | Recomputed |
|---|---|
| Diversifiers against random | +5.06 (se 0.51), better in 17 of 18 years; only 2013–14 lost (−0.48); unseen year +2.62 |
| Against volatility-matched / SPY | +4.65 / +3.93 |
| Hedges, risk left | top tracker 68.4%, random fund 90.6%, SPY 82.9%; the top tracker beats SPY for 87% of stocks and in all 18 years |
| Groups | +0.161 against +0.0047 |

**No look-ahead or unfair baseline found:**
- The universe ends on the as-of day.
- The random pick comes from exactly the tool's eligible set.
- The pick, the random candidates and SPY go through the same 10% calculation.

**Caveats it raised:**
- **The $5 price screen uses split-adjusted prices.** AAPL, NVDA, AMZN and 113 other actively traded stocks are screened out in 2008. This affects the tool and the random baseline alike, so the comparison stays fair, but early-year advice isn't exactly what `c2` would have shown on that date.
- **The volatility match is loose for the calmest bond funds** (MBB, TIP, AGG): the matched set averaged 1.3–2× the pick's volatility. Where the match is good, the tool beats it by +5.0 (+6.7 for funds). So the +4.6 is, if anything, understated: the gain isn't just "picked a calm fund".
- **The stock pick is the weak half.** Gold and silver trusts filed as stocks (PHYS, CEF) are 28% of the stock picks. Without them the stock pick is +1.9 (se 0.8) against random, and −0.1 against SPY, better in only 11 of 18 years. The fund pick carries the result. Removing those stock picks moves the pooled headline only from +5.1 to +4.9.
- **A random fund leaving 91% of a stock's risk is a low bar.** SPY at its beta is the baseline that matters, and the top tracker beats it by 14.6 points.
