# Does lead-lag structure repeat from one period to the next?

**TL;DR:** When stock A moves first and B follows in one period, does the same pattern hold in the next? Comparing correlations among 1,806 stocks in 2016–2022 and 2022–2025, same-day co-movement repeats almost perfectly (similarity +0.65, z = 83). Lead-lag doesn't, at any offset from a day to a year: the match is at chance level, and the strongest pairs keep their sign about half the time. Verdict: no repeating lead-lag.

## Method

- **Why:** the pair holdout (`-22.0-pair-lead-lag-holdout.md`) tried only offsets of 1–5 days, so this check covers every offset from a day to a year.
- **Data:** 1,806 liquid US stocks and market-relative returns. Early period 2016-06-28..2022-06-27 (1,510 days); later period 2022-06-28..2025-06-27 (753 days).
- **For each offset k:** the all-pairs matrix C_k[i, j] = corr(stock i at t, stock j at t+k), computed in both periods.
- **Similarity:** the correlation, across all ordered pairs, between the early and later matrices. Real, stable lead-lag at offset k makes it positive even if no single pair can be proven on its own.
- **Chance:** the same similarity after shifting the later period's targets in time, giving z.
- **Top pairs:** the share of the 1,000 strongest early pairs with the same sign later.

## Results

| Scale | Offset 0 (same day) | Offsets tested | Above z = 3 | Expected by chance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Daily | similarity +0.649, z = 83.1, top pairs 100% same sign | 1–250 days | 1 of 250 (offset 227, z = 3.7) | about 0.3 |
| Weekly | +0.444, z = 62.3, 100% | 1–52 weeks | 0 of 52 | about 0.1 |
| Monthly | +0.200, z = 27.8, 99.8% | 1–23 months | 0 of 23 | about 0 |

**Learning from one year and testing on the next** (daily, 9 year-to-year pairs):
- **Same day:** similarity +0.54 (z = 36), top pairs 100% same sign.
- **Offset 1:** +0.005 (z = 2.6), top pairs 51.5% same sign.
- **Every other offset:** 48.7–51.6%.

## What it means

- **The method can see structure:** same-day co-movement shows up strongly.
- **Lagged correlations are noise:** the strongest ones in one period are a coin flip in the next.
- **The faint offset-1 excess** reappears in the census (`+2.0-correlation-census.md`) as 1.2× chance, and turns out to be mostly stale prices and time zones.
