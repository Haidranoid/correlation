# Lottery stocks (MAX): do stocks with a recent huge day underperform?

**TL;DR:** This replicates a published anomaly; nothing in the method is new. Bali, Cakici & Whitelaw (2011, *JFE*) sorted US stocks over 1962–2005 on MAX, the largest daily return in the past month. They found that the lowest-MAX tenth beat the highest by 1.03% a month value-weighted (t = 2.83) and 0.65% equal-weighted (t = 1.83). In Fama-MacBeth regressions MAX stayed negative with controls (t = −6.62), and it absorbed the idiosyncratic-volatility puzzle. What's new is running it on this project's liquid, survivor-only stocks over 2006–2025, which is entirely after the paper's sample. Here the low-minus-high MAX book (equal weight, every 21 days, 5 bps a side) lost 0.49% a month over 2006–2019 (Newey-West t = −0.92; FF5 + UMD alpha −0.23%, t = −0.74). MAX's Fama-MacBeth coefficient with all the plan's controls had the wrong sign: +0.20 points between the highest- and lowest-ranked stock (t = +0.92). So test 1 fails on both counts. The 500 largest gave the same wrong sign (−0.53%, t = −1.04). The plan expected the effect to weaken there, but there was nothing to weaken. Over 2020–2025-06, with dividends restored, the book made +0.11% a month (t = 0.13), but MAX's coefficient was still positive. In the 16 unseen months the book lost 15.4% in all (18.2% without the dividend fix). MAX's coefficient did turn negative there (−0.79, t = −0.86), but that is not significant. The published series agrees that the anomaly has faded. Chen & Zimmermann's open-source replication, value-weighted on CRSP with small and delisted stocks included, earned +0.23% a month over 2006–2019 (t = 0.44) and +0.20% since publication (t = 0.43). The judge's account of the 20 lowest-MAX stocks trailed uniform random accounts by 1.7 points a year (ahead in 6 of 18 years, standard error 2.5). It matched volatility-matched accounts (−0.0 points, ahead in 8 of 18). In the unseen months it made +12.9%, against +19.3% for the median uniform account and +4.5% for the median volatility-matched one. Verdict: no edge. The MAX effect isn't in this data after 2005, with or without controls.

## Published claim

| Paper | Sample | What it reports |
|---|---|---|
| Bali, Cakici & Whitelaw 2011, *JFE*, "Maxing out" | US, 1962–2005 | Lowest-MAX tenth minus highest-MAX tenth: over 1% a month, robust to controls including idiosyncratic volatility |

**Our test period is after the paper's sample.** The effect is strongest in small, illiquid stocks, which this project's liquid universe mostly excludes, so it may be weaker here by design.

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Universe:** the judge-like liquid set, minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)`.

**Signal:** MAX = the largest daily simple return over t−20..t (export closes).

**Book:**
- Every 21 trading days (the fixed calendar), long the lowest-MAX tenth and short the highest, equal weight, held 21 days.
- Market-relative returns, 5 bps per side.

**Controls** (Fama-MacBeth, each formation):
- next 21 days' return on MAX;
- idiosyncratic volatility (the sd of daily FF3 residuals over t−20..t, French's factors);
- the t−20..t return;
- beta (252 days, against SPY);
- size proxy: the log median dollar volume over 63 days (there is no market cap);
- the 12-1 return.

**Alphas:** FF5 + UMD.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **2006–2019:**
   - the long-short's mean monthly net return, **pass at t ≥ 2**;
   - **and** MAX's Fama-MacBeth coefficient with all controls negative, with t ≤ −2.
2. **The 500 largest:** same sign. The plan expects this to weaken, because the effect lives in small stocks.
3. **2020–2025-06:** same sign. **The 16 unseen months** (new export, once, at the end): same sign.

**Money test (the judge):**
- Score = 1 − MAX's percentile (low MAX is bought). Graded with `rebalance=21`.
- The volatility-matched random comparison is the relevant line, because low-MAX stocks are calm.

## The number for the file name

The judge's walk-forward change over random, with uniform random accounts, 0 bps (`c2eval.change_over_random`).

## Ways it can fool us

- **Volatility:** MAX and idiosyncratic volatility are highly correlated. The controls are the test.
- **Short-term reversal:** a big up-day last month reverses partly. The t−20..t control is the guard.

### Test 2: the 500 largest — fails (same sign: no)

The 500 universe stocks with the highest 63-day median dollar volume, with tenths and regressions rebuilt within them, 2006–2019:
- the long-short made **−0.53% a period** (t = −1.04; plain −0.96), Sharpe −0.26, FF5 + UMD alpha −0.27% (t = −0.96);
- MAX's all-controls coefficient was **+0.05** (t = +0.23).

**The plan expected the effect to weaken among the 500 largest, because it lives in small stocks.** It didn't weaken, because there was no effect to weaken: the 500 largest have the same wrong sign as the whole universe, and are just as insignificant. Their highest-MAX tenth (MAX 9.8% on average) earned +1.17% over the next 21 days, against +0.79% for the lowest.

### Test 3: 2020–2025-06 and the unseen months — fails

- **2020-01..2025-06** (old export, with dividends restored, primary):
  - the long-short made **+0.11% a period** (t = 0.13), alpha +0.03% (t = 0.05). That is test 1's expected sign, but nowhere near significant.
  - MAX's all-controls coefficient was **+0.20** (t = +0.41), the wrong sign.
  - Without the correction: +0.02% (t = 0.02) and +0.21 (t = 0.44).
  - **Same sign: no,** since the coefficient is positive.
- **The 16 unseen months** (new export, everything rebuilt, run once at the end). 15 periods start between 2025-07-16 and 2026-09-16; the coefficient covers 14 formations, since French's factors end 2026-08-31.
  - The long-short lost **0.95% a period** (t = −0.92), **−15.4% in all**. Without the correction: −1.17% a period, −18.2% in all.
  - MAX's all-controls coefficient was **−0.79** (t = −0.86): the expected sign, as in 2010–2014, but not significant.
  - **Same sign: no.** The long-short lost money.
  - Over the 13 periods French covers, the FF5 + UMD alpha was +1.22% (Newey-West t 1.80, plain t 0.91). With 13 observations and 4 lags the Newey-West t isn't reliable; the plain t is the safer reading.
  - In the unseen months the highest-MAX deciles earned the most: deciles 7–10 made +1.6% to +2.1% a period, against +0.8% for the lowest.
- **Check:** the rebuilt book correlates 0.998 with the old export's over their 245 shared formations (gross −0.25% against −0.23% a period).

### Bali et al.'s volatility control as a sort (reported, not a test)

Stocks were sorted into IVOL fifths, then the lowest and highest MAX tenth taken within each fifth and pooled. This keeps the two legs' volatility about equal.

| Period | Long-short net, % (t NW; plain) | FF5 + UMD alpha (t NW) |
|---|---|---|
| 2006–2019 | **−0.54 (−2.03; −1.61)** | −0.39 (−1.81) |
| 2020–2025-06, dividends restored | −0.07 (−0.16) | +0.02 (0.04) |
| Unseen months, dividends restored | +0.24 (0.34), 14 periods | +2.82 (3.79), 13 periods |

Holding volatility fixed, high-MAX stocks *beat* low-MAX ones over 2006–2019, with a Newey-West t of −2.03 (plain −1.61). Bali et al. found the opposite: +0.35% a month value-weighted (t = 2.42) and over 0.90% equal-weighted (t ≈ 7.9). In this universe, beyond its volatility, a big up-day predicts slightly *higher* returns. That fits the reversal and momentum literature better than lottery demand.

**Reading it.** Bali et al.'s headline is value-weighted. Their equal-weighted spread, which is this plan's book, was only t = 1.83 even in-sample. On CRSP's full universe the anomaly has earned about a quarter of its in-sample return since 2005, and is insignificant. Among liquid survivors it has the wrong sign. Two biases here run *against* the anomaly:
- **Survivorship:** high-MAX stocks that later delisted are missing, which flatters the short leg;
- **Dividends:** the store's post-2022 closes drop them, which hurts the dividend-heavy low-MAX leg. The correction fixes this one, and it moves the numbers by under 0.2% a period.

But CRSP's survivor-free series is flat after 2005 too, so survivorship can't be the whole story.

## Decisions made while building it

All of these were made on 2026-10-21 **before any MAX return, spread or coefficient was computed or looked at**, unless a line says otherwise.

**Calendar and periods**
- **Formations** are the fixed calendar's days (`c2eval.twins`: export day 252 + 21k), so the universe is the one built on that day. A book is formed at the close of formation day t and held over t+1..t+21. Export day 1008 (2005-12-30) is a formation, so test 1 starts with the first return day 2006-01-03.
- **A formation belongs to a period by its first return day** (t+1). Test 1: 2006-01-01..2019-12-31. Test 3: 2020-01-01..2025-06-27. The old export's last formation (2025-06-13) has only 14 return days and is dropped: only complete 21-day periods are used, in both exports.
- **The unseen months** are the formations whose first return day is on or after 2025-06-30, matching the judge's `start='2025-06-30'`: 2025-07-16 .. 2026-09-16 on the new export (15 periods). The days 2025-06-30..2025-07-16 fall in a period that started in seen data and are not covered. Everything is rebuilt on the new export, not spliced.
- French's factors end 2026-08-31, so in the unseen months idiosyncratic volatility (and so Fama-MacBeth) exists only for formations up to then, and the alphas only for periods ending by then.

**Signal and controls**
- **MAX:** the largest of the daily simple returns on days t−20..t (21 returns, each from two present export closes). |log return| > 0.5 is a bad print: missing. At least 15 of the 21 returns must be present, Bali et al.'s rule. MAX uses the export's closes as they are (the judge sees those); the dividend correction is applied only to the forward returns.
- **Idiosyncratic volatility:** the regression of the stock's daily excess simple return (minus French's RF) on a constant, Mkt-RF, SMB and HML over t−20..t, solved per formation in one batched normal-equation step with each stock's own missing days masked; at least 15 days; IVOL = the sd (ddof 1) of the residuals. Checked against `np.linalg.lstsq` on stock-days with and without missing returns (`check_ivol_old.txt`, `check_ivol_new.txt`).
- **The t−20..t return:** P_t ÷ P_{t−21} − 1 on forward-filled export closes. **12-1:** P_{t−21} ÷ P_{t−252} − 1.
- **Beta:** the OLS slope of the stock's daily log returns on SPY's over t−251..t, at least 200 days where both exist. **SPY is Yahoo's adjusted close** from the shared setup: the plan says only "against SPY", and the store's SPY starts on 2005-02-25, which would leave no beta for the first two formations of 2006.
- **Size proxy:** log of the 63-day median dollar volume, the measure that picks the 500 largest.

**Book**
- **Deciles:** among universe stocks with a MAX and a forward return, the round(0.1 × n) lowest and highest MAX. Equal weight, buy and hold for 21 days: a leg's return is the mean of P_{t+21} ÷ P_t − 1 (forward-filled closes).
- **Market-relative:** the market is the equal-weight mean 21-day return of the same candidates. For the $1-long, $1-short spread it cancels; it is reported for each leg.
- **Costs:** 5 bps × turnover, turnover = Σ|new weight − last period's weight drifted by its returns| over both legs (2 for the first book).
- **Alphas:** the intercept of the 21-day net long-short returns on French's FF5 and UMD daily factors, each compounded over the same 21 days. The long-short is self-financing, so RF isn't subtracted.
- **IVOL-controlled sort (reported, not a test):** Bali et al.'s check, here as IVOL fifths, then the lowest and highest MAX tenth within each fifth, pooled.

**Fama-MacBeth**
- **At each formation:** OLS across stocks of the next-21-day return minus the candidates' equal-weight mean, on a constant and the regressors. All specifications use the same stocks: universe members with all six regressors and a forward return.
- **Regressors are percentile ranks in [0, 1]** within that formation's sample (chosen before looking). A coefficient is then the 21-day return gap between the highest and the lowest stock on that variable, the others held fixed. Winsorized raw values (1st/99th percentile each formation) are reported as a secondary check, not a test.
- **Specifications reported:** MAX alone; IVOL alone; MAX + IVOL; all six (the test); all but MAX (to see what IVOL does without MAX, Bali et al.'s second claim).
- **The t is Newey-West with 4 lags** on the series of formation coefficients (Bali et al. report Newey-West t's; 4 ≈ 4(n/100)^(2/9) for about 170 formations). The plain t is shown alongside. The same t is used for the long-short's mean.

**Tests**
- **Test 1** needs both: the long-short's mean net 21-day return with Newey-West t ≥ 2, and the MAX coefficient in the all-controls regression with Newey-West t ≤ −2.
- **"Same sign" in tests 2, 3 and the unseen months** means both of test 1's statistics keep their expected signs: the long-short's mean net return > 0 and the all-controls MAX coefficient < 0.
- **Dividends:** for any period touching 2022-07 or later, the primary numbers add `pubdata.div_fix` to the forward returns (as the prompt says to); the uncorrected numbers are shown next to them. Before 2022-07 the correction is 0, so the two agree.
- **The 500 largest:** the shared definition at the formation day; deciles and Fama-MacBeth rebuilt within them.
- **Money test:** daily scores = 1 − the percentile of the day's MAX within the universe of the latest calendar day, so the lowest-MAX stocks score highest; graded with `rebalance=21`, uniform and volatility-matched.

**Made after seeing results**
- **Presentation only:**
  - two table headers in `tests.py` were relabeled (turnover "both legs, max 4"; the raw-value regression's units);
  - the decile table now averages IVOL with `nanmean`, because the last unseen formation has no IVOL (French ends 2026-08-31).

  Neither changes any number in a test.
- **Post-hoc descriptions, not tests:** the calendar-year breakdown, the worst periods (2009-03 and 2009-04) and test 1 without 2009.
- **The literature lookup came after the results:** Chen & Zimmermann's series, Yuan et al., and the Bali et al. working paper's tables. It feeds only the published-claim table.
- **No rule was changed after results were seen.**
