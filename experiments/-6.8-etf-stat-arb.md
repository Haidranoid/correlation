# Statistical arbitrage against funds: does Avellaneda & Lee's s-score rule still pay?

**TL;DR:** Avellaneda & Lee (2010) regressed each stock's daily returns on its sector fund over 60 days, modelled the cumulative leftover as a mean-reverting process, and traded its s-score: buy when the stock sat 1.25 equilibrium standard deviations below its fund, sell at 1.25 above, and close near equilibrium. That is published prior art. What's new is running their exact daily rule on this project's data, with each stock's twin fund in place of the sector fund. Their sample was 1997–2007 and our test period is 2006–2025-06, so two years overlap; the 16 unseen months come after both. In the paper the fund version earned a net Sharpe ratio of 1.1 over 1997–2007, and with actual sector funds 0.6 a year on average over 2003–2007; the 1.44 falling to 0.9 that the plan quoted is the paper's PCA version. Here the rule still finds reversion before costs. Over 2006–2019 the book made +1.44 bps per position a day gross (Newey–West t 3.9, Sharpe 1.24, about the paper's after-cost figure). But it trades about a fifth of every position each day, and 5 bps a side on stocks plus 1 bp on funds cost 1.16 bps a day. That leaves +0.28 bps (t 0.77, Sharpe 0.24), so test 1 fails. **Executed at the next close instead of the close the s-score is computed on, the book has the opposite sign: −0.24 bps a day (t −0.69).** About a third of the gross profit comes from filling at the very close that produced the signal. The other checks agree in sign with the primary book: the 500 largest (+0.17), 2020–2025-06 (+1.05 with the dividend correction, t 0.96, almost all of it from 2020 and early 2025), and the 16 unseen months (+0.23, t 0.18). The export's and the store's closes give the same numbers, within 0.01 bps a day. FF5 + UMD alphas are no larger: +0.20 bps a day (t 0.58) in 2006–2019 and +0.78 (t 0.80) in 2020–2025-06. The book breaks even at 6.4 bps a side on stocks, so the assumed 5 bps leaves little; at the next close break-even is 3.8 bps, below the cost. The judge's account, buying the 20 most oversold by s each day, made +7.8 points a year over uniform random accounts (se 2.9, ahead in 12 of 18 years) and +8.8 over volatility-matched ones, still below plain 5-day reversal's +11.8. In the unseen months it trailed the median random account by 22.0 points. Verdict: no edge after costs. The s-score still picks out short-term reversion against the fund, but too little of it survives 5 bps a side, and with a one-day delay the book is negative in 2006–2019 and in the unseen months.

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Universe:** the judge-like liquid set with a twin, minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)`.

**The fund:** each stock's twin from `c2eval.twins`, its best-tracking fund. Avellaneda & Lee used industry funds; the twin is the project's closer match, so that is a stated departure.

**Daily, at the close of t:**
- OLS of the stock's daily returns on its twin's over the last 60 days, with an intercept.
- X_k = the cumulative residual, k = 1..60.
- AR(1): X_{k+1} = a + b·X_k + ζ. Skip the stock if b ≤ 0 or b ≥ 1.
- κ = −ln(b) × 252; require κ > 252 ÷ 30 (mean reversion within about 30 days).
- m = a ÷ (1 − b); σ_eq = √(Var ζ ÷ (1 − b²)).
- s = −(m − m̄) ÷ σ_eq, where m̄ is that day's cross-sectional mean of m (the paper's adjustment).

**Bands** (the paper's):
- open long if s < −1.25, open short if s > +1.25;
- close a long if s > −0.50, close a short if s < +0.75.

**Positions:**
- $1 per open position, each hedged with −β × its twin; twin legs are netted across stocks.
- Daily return = total P&L ÷ the number of open positions.
- Costs: 5 bps per side on stock trades, 1 bp on the netted twin trades.

**Data checks, fixed now** (as in `+0.8-pairs-trading.md`):
- **Prices, run twice:** on the export's closes and on the store's raw closes, because the bad-print repair looks one day ahead.
- **Disagreement:** if the two disagree in sign, or in t by more than 1, the verdict is "data, unresolved".
- **Dividends:** the shared dividend correction for returns after 2022-07.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

2. **The 500 largest:** same sign.
3. **2020–2025-06:** same sign. **The 16 unseen months** (new export and store, once, at the end): same sign.

**Reported:**

**Money test (the judge):**
- Score = −s for stocks passing the κ filter (the most oversold are bought). Graded with `rebalance=1`.

The judge's walk-forward change over random, with uniform random accounts, 0 bps (`c2eval.change_over_random`).

## Ways it can fool us

- **Bad prints and dividend drops:** see the data checks.
- **The twin isn't the paper's fund.** That's the stated departure.
- **Costs:** daily turnover. The break-even cost is reported.

## Decisions made while building it

All of these were made **before any book return, test number or judge result was seen**, unless marked otherwise. Data-quality looks (no strategy returns) came first and are marked.

- **Returns in the fit:** daily simple returns, the paper's R_n (close-to-close change over the previous close), from the price source's closes. The twin's returns come from the store's closes, as `c2eval.twins` and `twin_residual` get them.
- **Which days count in the fit:** a day counts only if both the stock's and the twin's returns exist and each |log return| ≤ 0.5. That is c2eval's point-in-time bad-print rule for funds, applied to both legs. The fit needs ≥ 57 of the 60 days, the same as `twin_residual`'s `max_missing = max(1, round(0.05 × 60)) = 3`. A day that doesn't count adds 0 to X_k.
- **Var(ζ) = SSR ÷ 59**, the AR(1) residuals' mean square.
- **m̄** is the mean of m over that day's trading-set stocks that pass the b/κ filter. The paper averages over "all stocks", but m = a/(1−b) is unstable for fits that fail the filter.
- **Positions close when s is unavailable**: κ fails, b falls outside (0, 1), there are too few days, or the stock leaves the universe. This is the paper's own option "(ii) close open trades". A same-day flip is allowed (close a long at s > −0.50, then open a short if s > 1.25).
- **A trade in a stock on day d needs its close on d** in that price source; otherwise the state carries over.
- **Accounting:**
  - The stock leg is bought or sold at $1 at entry and then held, drifting. The paper resizes only new positions.
  - The twin hedge is reset at each close to −(that day's window β) × the stock leg's current value, netted across stocks per fund.
  - P&L comes from forward-filled closes; a fund's daily |log| > 0.5 counts as 0.
  - Costs: 5 bps on stock dollars traded (open $1, close at the current value) and 1 bp on netted fund dollars traded, charged to the next day's return.
  - Daily return = (P&L − costs) ÷ the positions held over the day. A day with none counts as 0.
- **The delayed version** follows the same decision path, but each decision executes at the next close, with the decision day's β and twin.
- **Periods** are by return date: 2006-01-03..2019-12-31, 2020-01-02..2025-06-27, and the unseen 2025-06-30..2026-10-19.
- **The pass t is the Newey–West t (5 lags)** of the mean daily net return. The plain t is reported beside it.
- **Sharpe** = net daily mean ÷ sd × √252, with no risk-free rate subtracted, since this is a self-financing hedged book per $1 position.
- **Universe:** the export's, in both price runs; only the signal and P&L prices change. The 500 largest run the same rules with the set restricted to them (m̄ within that set).
- **The price-source check is binding on two numbers:** 2006–2019 primary (sign, or t differing by > 1), and the sign of 2020–2025-06.
- **Break-even cost:** the stock cost per side at which the mean net return is 0, with the fund cost held at 1 bp.
- **Money test score:** −s from the export's closes, without the dividend correction (the judge's own data is uncorrected). It is NaN outside the trading set or where the filter fails.
- **Dividends:**
  - `pubdata.div_fix` is added to the stocks' daily log returns (signal and P&L). It is 0 before 2022-07.
  - The twins' own dividends aren't corrected; pubdata's Yahoo funds don't include them.
  - The primary 2020–2025-06 and unseen numbers are with the correction; without it is reported.
- **Data look, before results (`out/dataqc_old.txt`):**
  - The export's store ids map to the same tickers in `STORE_OLD` for all 8,503 rows.
  - On universe stock-days since 2005, the store's raw closes equal the export's to within 0.1% everywhere. The repair blanked 8 universe stock-days and changed none. So the two price runs can differ only on those days.
  - The universe has 300 stocks in 2005–2007, all twinned to QQQ until 2006: few funds pass the twins' liquidity rule that early. It has about 1,000 from 2008 and 2,250 by 2025.
- **Fidelity check, before results (`out/check_fit.txt`):** the vectorized rolling-sum fits match a direct `lstsq` fit of each window. The cases cover 21 stock-days: random ones, block starts, windows with missing days, and a twin day dropped by the 0.5 rule. The worst relative difference over β, α, a, b, Var ζ, m and σ_eq is 3e-13, and X_60 = 0 to 1e-16.
- **Reading the paper, before results:** the plan's "Sharpe about 1.4, falling to about 0.9" is the paper's PCA version. The fund version's figures are used in "Published claim vs this data". The plan's table is left as written.
- **After the first results** (reporting only, no rule changed):
  - The post-2022 split (2022-07-01..2025-06-27) was added to show the dividend correction where it acts.
  - The unseen alphas are reported over 2025-06-30..2026-08-31, because French's factors end there.
  - The monthly figures for 2008, 2020 and 2025 were looked up to describe the good years.
- **The judge's walk-forward** has 18 graded years (2007-06-28 onward) for this score, as for the 5-day reversal check.
