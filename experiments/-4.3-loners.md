# Loners: do stocks that move with nothing earn more?

**TL;DR:** Do loners, stocks whose best same-day match with any other stock or fund is weak, earn more than well-connected stocks of the same size and volatility? Not reliably. Buying loners and shorting connected stocks made 0.26% a month after costs in 2006–2019 (t = 1.47; 2.5 needed), then reversed, losing 23% in the 16 unseen months. As a stock-picking account it trailed SPY by 4.3 points a year after costs. Verdict: no edge.

## Nearest prior work

From the earlier survey; a separate novelty check runs alongside.

| Work | What it found |
|---|---|
| Merton 1987, *JF*, "A simple model of capital market equilibrium with incomplete information" | Stocks known to fewer investors need higher expected returns |
| Arbel & Strebel 1983 | Neglected firms (little analyst coverage) earned more |
| Morck, Yeung & Yu 2000; Durnev et al. 2003 | Low synchronicity (R²) means more firm-specific information in prices |
| Hou & Moskowitz 2005 | Delayed, less-connected stocks earn a premium |
| `-5.5-betting-against-correlation.md` | This project: low correlation with the *market* earns nothing significant here |

**What's new:** connectedness here is the best match with *any* stock or fund, not with the market. A stock can be highly correlated with the market and still have no close twin, or the reverse.

**Novelty check (2026-10-21, a separate search; it changed no rules):** partly done.
- **Asness et al. 2020:** betting against correlation with the *market*. Tested here in `-5.5-betting-against-correlation.md`, where it showed nothing significant.
- **Stowe & Xing 2010** (SSRN): low-R² stocks beat high-R² ones over 3 years in 22 of 32 cohorts.
- **Pozzi, Di Matteo & Aste 2013** (*Scientific Reports*): stocks on the edge of correlation networks had better risk-adjusted returns.
- **Lehavy & Sloan 2008:** low investor recognition is followed by higher returns.
- **The other way:** Ahern 2013 (central industries earn more); Chinese evidence of low synchronicity followed by lower returns.
- **Not found:** the maximum pairwise correlation with any stock or fund as the measure.

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Universe:** the judge-like liquid set, minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)`, refreshed every 21 trading days on a fixed calendar.

**Connectedness c:**
- the stock's highest correlation of daily market-relative log returns over the past 252 days with any other universe stock or eligible fund;
- funds as `c2eval.twins` defines them (same liquidity and volatility screens, not single-name), with stocks and funds alike excluded if `c2eval.excluded`.

**Book:**
- Each month, split the universe into dollar-volume terciles × volatility terciles (252-day), 9 cells.
- Within each cell, long the third with the lowest c and short the third with the highest.
- Equal weight; the book is the average of the 9 cells' long-shorts. Held 21 days, costs 5 bps per side.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **2006–2019:** the long-short's mean monthly return net of costs. **Pass:** t ≥ 2.5, with positive alpha against the market.
2. **The 500 largest:** the same book within them. **Pass:** same sign.
3. **2020–2025-06:** same sign. The 16 unseen months (new export, once, at the end): same sign.

**Reported:**
- loadings on low-volatility and on betting-against-correlation (copy its definition from `experiments/-5.5-betting-against-correlation.md`);
- what kinds of companies the loners are.

**Money test (the judge):**
- Score = 1 − (c's percentile within its dollar-volume tercile), every day. Graded with `rebalance=21`.
- Uniform and volatility-matched random accounts are both reported.

## The number for the file name

The judge's walk-forward change over random, with uniform random accounts, 0 bps (`c2eval.change_over_random`).

## Ways it can fool us

- **Survivor-only data:** loners skew small and idiosyncratic, the kind whose failures are missing. That flatters the long side. The 500-largest test is the guard.
- **Fund-like stocks:** a closed-end fund filed as a stock can look like a loner. The shared exclusion rule removes most of them; check the top loners by hand.

## Decisions made while building it

All of these were made on 2026-10-21 **before any returns after a formation date were computed or looked at**, unless a line says otherwise. `look.py` and `diag_excluded.py` (no returns) ran before the book.

**Calendar and universe**
- **Calendar:** `c2eval.twins`' rebalance days, export indices 252 + 21k, so `excluded[k]` and the twins line up with each formation. A book formed at the close of t0 is held to the close of the next calendar day (21 trading days).
- **The judge-like liquid set** is the judge's `liquid_universe` rule made point in time at t0, as in close location and the signal ensemble:
  - `v` finite on ≥ 90% of t0−755..t0;
  - upper median close ≥ $5 and upper median dollar volume ≥ $5M over t0−251..t0;
  - a close at t0;
  - then minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)[k]`.
  - No twin is required; the plan doesn't ask for one.
- **Test 1 keeps the plan's 2006–2019 (decided after counting the universe, before any returns).** In this data that means formations from 2006-10-02, the first day the universe exists, with about 300 stocks until 2008-03. The verdict rests on that period. 2008-03..2019, where the universe has ≥ 500 stocks (the betting-against-correlation experiment's start), is reported beside it.

**Connectedness**
- **Market-relative returns:** daily log return − β × the export's `market`, with β the OLS slope over the same 252 days. This is the groups map's `standardized(relative=true)`, not the census's trailing beta.
- **Correlation:** as `c2eval.twins` and the groups map compute it, the cosine of the two residual series standardized over their own finite days, with missing days 0.
  - Both series need returns on ≥ 90% of the 252 days.
  - Fund log returns beyond ±0.5 count as missing, as in `c2eval.twins`.
  - c is the signed maximum.
- **Co-movers:** other universe stocks (so stocks the rule excludes never count) and eligible funds.
- **Eligible funds** are store ETFs that pass `c2eval.twins`' fund-level screens, recomputed on each calendar day over the same window:
  - returns on ≥ 90% of the days;
  - median dollar volume ≥ $5M;
  - volatility ≤ 75% a year;
  - not single-name by twins' rule, using `tw['fund_like'][k]` as its stock mask, exactly as twins does.
- **"Excluded applies to stocks and funds alike."** `c2eval.not_common` is also applied to the fund tickers (funds have no fund-like flag). That drops 5–10 eligible funds on a date: USO, UNG, DBC, DBA, UUP, BNO, two bitcoin funds and two closed-end funds.
- Twins' pair-level limits (correlation > 0 and < 0.95) are not applied, because c is a maximum, not a twin choice.
- **Daily c for the money test:** the universe and eligible funds of the latest calendar day, with the window ending on day d.

**The book**
- **Volatility:** sd of daily log returns over t0−251..t0 with ≥ 120 returns (betting against correlation's). **Dollar volume:** the 252-day upper median of the liquid rule.
- **Terciles:** independent sorts of the stocks with c, volatility and dollar volume at t0 (floor(3 × rank ÷ n)). A cell is used only with ≥ 9 stocks; all 9 always qualified.
- **Thirds within a cell:** long rank < n/3 by c, short rank ≥ 2n/3.
- **Weights:** $1 long and $1 short per cell, equal weight within a leg, and the book is the mean of the cells. Buy and hold on forward-filled closes, so a missing day earns 0.
- **Costs:** turnover = Σ |new weight − drifted dollar weight| over every stock, which includes resizing the legs back to $1. The cost is 5 bps × turnover, charged in the formation month. The first month counts its full entry.
- **The 500 largest:** the 500 book stocks with the highest dollar volume, with terciles and thirds rebuilt within them and c unchanged.

**Statistics**
- **Months:** one return per formation, assigned to a period by its first holding day. The old export's last, incomplete month is dropped.
- **t** = mean ÷ (sd ÷ √n) of monthly net returns.
- **Alpha:** OLS intercept of the monthly net return on the equal-weight market minus the risk-free rate, both compounded over the hold, annualized × 12.
  - The market is the export's `market` (a mean of daily log returns, turned into a simple return).
  - The risk-free rate is FRED's 3-month yield, as in betting against correlation.
- **Test 1 passes** with t ≥ 2.5 net and alpha > 0 (its sign, as the plan says). **Tests 2, 3 and the unseen months** pass with a mean net return > 0.
- **Loadings:** monthly OLS of the net book on the market, low volatility and BAC, matched by formation day.
  - The factors use betting against correlation's code and definitions, gross, on this universe where its inputs exist (from the 2007-02 formation, ≥ 100 stocks).
  - BAC: 5-year correlations of 3-day returns with ≥ 750 of them, volatility quintiles, beta-scaled thirds, in excess of the risk-free rate.

**Money test**
- **Scores every day d:** among the stocks of the latest calendar day's universe with c at d, dollar-volume terciles by the calendar day's median dollar volume. Score = 1 − r ÷ (n − 1), with r the 0-based rank of c in the tercile.

**Unseen months**
- **Rebuilt on the new export:** everything, with `c2eval.twins(E_new)` from its cache.
- **The period** is the daily P&L of the continuing book from 2025-06-30 to 2026-10-19, with t from daily returns and the alpha from daily returns with Newey-West errors (5 lags).
- The period formed on 2025-06-13 (data through then) counts from 2025-06-30; its cost falls on 2025-06-16, before the window.

**Made after seeing results**
- **`diag_unseen.py`**, written after the unseen months' result, changes nothing. It holds the old-against-new plumbing check, the split into long and short legs, and the biggest contributors.
- **`diag_funds.py`**, the wide scan of SEC names and known closed-end funds and BDCs, was run after the results. It found ECC; OXLC had already turned up in `look.py`'s lists before any returns. Neither was removed, as the instructions require.
- **`look.py`'s count of formations** was corrected after the results, from 167 (every calendar day in the date range) to 159 (the formations with a book), and `look.py` was rerun for both exports. The lists and shares are unchanged.
- Nothing else was changed after results were seen.
