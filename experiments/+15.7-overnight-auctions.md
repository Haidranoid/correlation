# Overnight momentum at the auctions: can a daily trader collect a stock's own overnight drift?

**TL;DR:** Persistent overnight returns are published: stocks that gained overnight recently tend to gain overnight again and give some of it back during the day (Aboody, Even-Tov, Lehavy & Trueman 2018; Lou, Polk & Skouras 2019). Two things were new here: the overnight move was measured against the stock's twin fund, and the trade was built for a daily trader, with the signal known before the 15:50 closing-auction cutoff (opens through today, closes through yesterday, checked in code) and each stock bought in the closing auction and sold in the next opening auction. The drift is real before costs. Each day the book bought the tenth of liquid stocks with the highest 21-night mean twin-relative overnight move and shorted the lowest tenth, twin-hedged. Over 2008–2019 it earned +11.6 bps a day gross (t 22.8), and the same positions gave back 10.0 bps the next session. But opening and closing the whole book every day at 2 bps a side leaves +1.06 bps a day (t 2.08; Newey-West 1.43), short of the t ≥ 3 the plan requires. It breaks even at 2.3 bps a side. Among the 500 largest it loses 2.2 bps a day net (t −3.4), over 2020–2025-06 it loses 1.6 (t −1.4), and in the 16 unseen months it loses 6.6 (t −3.8). The plan's own money test is a 20-stock overnight-only account against 1,000 random overnight-only accounts. It gives +54.1 points a year (se 22.4, ahead in 18 of 18 years), and +49.0 at 2 bps a side. That number can't be compared with the judge's close-to-close file names. Three years carry it (2009 +399, 2008 +148, 2020 +120); the median year is +19.5. The data holds only survivors and the picks sit at the 84th volatility percentile: against volatility-matched random accounts it is +39.9, and inside the 500 largest +20.9 (+5.7 without the three big years). In the unseen months the account beat uniform random accounts by 14.4 points (p 0.058) but trailed volatility-matched ones by 7.6. The store's dividend adjustment stops in late 2022 for most payers, so later ex-date overnights are price-only. Dropping the Yahoo file's ex-dates in July–October 2026 moved the results by only 1–2 bps a day. Yahoo's 5-minute bars put the profit in the opening print. On 58 days of 2026 the account's positions were 20.6 bps a day lower at 9:35 than at the open (t −3.5), so exiting at 9:35 instead of the open turns +10.3 bps a day into −10.3 and keeps none of the overnight profit. Verdict: no money for a daily trader. It fails Test 1, and every test after it.

## Nearest prior work

| Work | What it found |
|---|---|
| Aboody, Even-Tov, Lehavy & Trueman 2018, *JFQA* | Past overnight returns, a proxy for firm-specific sentiment, predict the next overnight return, and lower returns further out |
| Lou, Polk & Skouras 2019, *JFE* | Overnight and intraday returns each persist, with different clienteles: a "tug of war" |
| This project, `-6.0-night-and-day.md` and `-16.2-twin-gap-fade.md` | The market catch-up has moved overnight since 2020. The official open is a real auction price, but one that reverts within minutes |

**What's new:** measuring the overnight drift against the twin fund, and trading it only through the two auctions. A published effect's variant, new to this data.

**Novelty check (2026-10-21, a separate search; it changed no rules):** partly done, auction-only overnight holding of persistent-overnight stocks is published; the twin hedge is not.
- **Lachance 2023** (Review of Financial Economics, "Night trading: lower risk but higher returns?"): Sorts stocks monthly on a past-year overnight bias and holds the positive-bias fifth only overnight, assuming market-on-close and market-on-open fills. The 2015 working version (1995–2014) reports 25.9% a year gross against 10.0% for the market, a gross long-short of 43.1%, and round-trip costs of 1.5–3.1 bps a day; the published version's sample end is unverified.
- **Akbas, Boehmer, Jiang & Koch 2022** (JFE, overnight returns and daytime reversals): Stocks whose overnight gains are most often reversed during the day earn more over the next month, 0.92% a month gross for the top decile over the bottom (1993–2017). A G7 replication (Hajiyev, Keiber & Luczak 2024) finds it in the US and Canada but not in France, Germany, Italy, the UK or Japan.
- **Bogousslavsky 2021** (JFE, cross-section of intraday and overnight returns): Arbitrageurs close positions before the close because overnight margins and lending fees are higher, so mispricing factors earn during the day and lose at the end of it; the pattern is stronger in the second half of the sample.
- **Haghani, Ragulin & Dewey 2022** (Elm Wealth note "Night moves", later in JOIM): The overnight drift is strongest in retail-favoured and meme stocks, and they judge that costs including market impact would erase most or all of the gain from trading it.
- **NightShares ETFs 2023** (WealthManagement.com news, night-effect ETFs closing): NSPY and NIWM held the S&P 500 and Russell 2000 overnight only from June 2022; by July 2023 they were down 6.9% and over 10% against index gains of 22% and 16%, and both closed in August 2023. NSPL followed in the same month. Index level, not cross-sectional.
- **Not found:** overnight drift measured against each stock's tracking fund, a 21-day signal that includes the same day's open, or an auction-to-auction book hedged with the fund.

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Universe:**
- the judge-like liquid set with a twin, minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)`, on the twins calendar;
- from 2008-01, when opens start.

**Overnight move:**
- `o_i(d) = log(open_d / close_{d−1})`, from the store's opens and closes;
- reuse `research/night_and_day/`'s panel and its bad-print rule (|log move| > 0.5 is missing);
- use the open-quality rules from the twin gap fade's "Decisions" (stale opens).

**Twin-relative move:** `x_i(d) = o_i(d) − β_i · o_twin(d)`, with the twin and β from the latest `c2eval.twins` rebalance.

**Signal at day t:**
- `S_i(t)` = the mean of `x_i` over the 21 overnights d = t−20..t, with at least 17 present.
- Day t's own overnight move is known at 9:30, so S is known before the 15:50 closing-auction cutoff. No close of day t is used.

**Book:**
- Each day t, long the top tenth by S and short the bottom tenth, equal weight.
- Each position is paired with −β × its twin.
- Bought in day t's closing auction (the store's close), sold in day t+1's opening auction (the store's open). Flat during the day.

**Costs:**
- 2 bps per side on stocks for auction fills, 1 bp per side on the netted twin legs.
- Turnover is the whole book every day, so both sides are paid daily. Break-even cost reported.

**Reported alongside:**
- the raw version: `o` instead of `x`, hedged with SPY at the stock's beta;
- the same positions' next intraday leg (open t+1 → close t+1), the tug of war;
- the 500 largest.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **2008–2019:** the book's mean net daily return. **Pass:** t ≥ 3 (plain; Newey-West reported).
2. **The 500 largest:** net mean positive with t ≥ 2.
3. **2020–2025-06:** same sign, net. **The 16 unseen months** (new export and store, once, at the end): same sign, net.

**Money test** (its own, because the judge trades close to close):
- **The account:** the 20 highest-S stocks each day, bought in the closing auction and sold at the next open; long only, unhedged.
- **Random accounts:** 1,000 that each day hold 20 stocks drawn uniformly from the same day's universe, over the same overnights.
- **Yearly returns:** compounded from the daily overnight returns, by calendar year.

**Reported extra:**
- The twin gap fade check's Yahoo 5-minute bars (2026-07-29..10-19, in `~/.local/share/correlation2/experiments/twin_gap_fade_check/`) cover the account's positions on those days.
- From them, report the share of the overnight leg's profit left if the exit is at 9:35 instead of the open.

## The number for the file name

Points of yearly return of the 20-stock overnight account above the median random overnight account, averaged over calendar years 2008–2025-06, at 0 bps. It is a 20-stock account against random accounts, like the judge's, but it holds only overnight.

## Ways it can fool us

- **Dividends and splits:** an ex-dividend day drops the open below an unadjusted previous close. Check how the store adjusts opens and closes (the night and day code knows), and drop ex-date overnights if they're unadjusted.
- **Bad opens:** the exit is the official open, which is what an opening-auction order gets, so an extreme open is a real fill here. A stale open (no trade) is not; the twin gap fade's stale-open rule handles it.
- **Capacity:** auction orders move auction prices. The 500 largest is the guard.
- **Survivor-only data:** delisted stocks are missing from both sides of the book.

## Results (run 2026-10-21)

**The setup in numbers:**
- **Universe:** the liquid set with a twin had a median of 1,094 ranked stocks a day over 2008–2019 and 1,996 over 2020–2025-06. Each decile leg holds about 104 names (189 since 2020), and 48 among the 500 largest.
- **Days:** 3,004 exit days in 2008–2019 (the first trade exits 2008-01-28, once 17 overnights of 2008 exist) and 1,379 in 2020–2025-06.
- **Units:** the books are $1 long and $1 short, in bps a day of the long-short, simple returns, t from the daily series (one value per day). Net = gross − 8 bps (2 bps a side on stocks, both sides every day) − the netted hedge cost (1 bp a side).
- **Timing check:** in 12 sampled days, garbling every close, high and low from day t on and every open from t+1 on leaves S(t) bit-identical; garbling day t's own open changes it for 1,250 of 1,273 stocks. S matches a direct computation from the store's prices to 1e-9 (`out/checks_old.txt`).
- **Plumbing check:** 5-day reversal through the judge, rebalanced weekly, gives +11.8 points a year and beats random in 8 of 18 years, as it should (`out/sanity_reversal5.txt`).

### Dividends: what the store holds, and what it changes

The store holds Stooq's back-adjusted prices, stored to 6 significant digits, so adjusted prices fall off the cent grid and raw ones sit on it (`divcheck.py`).
- **The boundary.** Each symbol has one raw boundary: before it, every open, high, low and close is scaled by the same factor; from it on, the prices are raw. Most dividend payers' boundaries fall in October–November 2022, in both stores (KO 2022-11-30, XOM 2022-11-14, T 2022-10-06, JPM 2022-10-05). SPY, AAPL and MSFT run later.
- **What it means for an overnight:** through about 2022 an ex-date overnight is a total return, because the previous close is scaled down by the dividend. From late 2022 on, including all the unseen months, the open drops by the dividend against an unadjusted close. A holder would receive the dividend, so the store understates that night.
- **How much of the data:** among universe overnights, 12–15% are in a raw region in 2008–2013 (nearly all of them stocks the store never adjusted for anything, so with no dividends to miss), 29% in 2019, 48% in 2022, and 94–99% from 2023. There is no dividend calendar on disk for history, so the plan's drop of unadjusted ex-dates can't be applied there.
- **Sensitivity (fixed before results):** everything is also reported on 2008–2022, where the store is adjusted.

| The account's legs against the ranked set's mean | Overnight +3.8 (t 0.87); intraday −8.3 (t −1.16); close to close −4.8 (t −0.57) |

- The book loses money net, so the unseen months fail Test 3's second half as well.
- The account's picks sat at the 88th volatility percentile. Its lead over uniform random accounts came in the second half of 2025, and it trailed volatility-matched ones.

**Dividends in the unseen months** (`divunseen.py`):
- **The data:** the twin gap fade check's Yahoo daily file lists dividend and split ex-dates for 98.2% of the ranked stock-days on exit days 2026-07-01..10-19. It lists 3,070 events.
- **The drop:** dropping those ex-date overnights (2,143 for stocks, 919 for twin funds) from the signal and the exits gives the following.

| Version | Twin book gross (t) | Twin book net (t) | Account minus median random | Daily excess over mean random (t) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Store as is | +1.65 (+0.49) | −9.82 (−2.89) | −2.8 points (p 0.79) | −3.26 (−0.42) |
| Ex-dates dropped | +3.28 (+0.95) | −8.06 (−2.34) | −3.9 points (p 0.87) | −4.61 (−0.61) |

- Unadjusted ex-dates cost the hedged book about 1.6 bps a day of gross in this stretch. That could explain part of 2023–2025's lower gross, but not a net loss of 6–8 bps a day.
- They don't flatter the money account; here they slightly help the random accounts.

### The reported extra: exiting at 9:35 instead of the opening auction (`extra935.py`)

- **The data:** the twin gap fade check's Yahoo 5-minute bars, 58 exit days 2026-07-29..10-19, read-only. Entry is the store's close; exit is the store's open (the opening auction) or the open of the 09:35 bar.
- **Coverage:** the account held 1,160 positions on those days. 1,037 have a 09:35 bar, and 1,009 (87%) are in the common set. The rest were dropped for no bar (64), an invalid exit (59) or an ex-date (4); none for a close that disagrees with Yahoo's.

| Exit | Account, bps a day (t) | Ranked set, bps a day | Share of the opening-auction exit kept | Minus the opening-auction exit (t) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opening auction (the store's open) | +10.3 (+0.82) | +9.5 | 1.00 | |
| **09:35** | **−10.3 (−0.77)** | +5.5 | **−1.01** | **−20.6 (−3.51)** |
| 09:45 | −3.9 (−0.28) | +2.1 | −0.38 | −14.1 (−1.80) |
| 10:00 | −10.8 (−0.72) | −1.5 | −1.05 | −21.0 (−2.33) |

- **The opening print is high for these stocks.** The account's stocks traded 20.6 bps lower at 9:35 than at the open, against 4 bps lower for the average ranked stock. Exiting at 9:35 keeps none of the overnight profit.
- **The size matches.** The gap is about the size of the account's whole historical overnight lead (14.7 bps a day in 2008–2019). It is the twin gap fade's opening-print pressure, seen from the selling side.
- **Only an opening-auction sell could collect it.** A market-on-open sell would receive that print, at a size too small to move the auction. Nothing later in the morning does.
- **This window's lead was nil:** the account's overnight return was about the ranked set's (+0.7 bps a day), so the share kept in excess of the ranked set is not meaningful here.

### The tests, in order

- **Test 1 fails:** +1.06 bps a day net of 2 bps a side, t 2.08 (Newey-West 1.43), against t ≥ 3.
- **Test 2 fails:** the 500 largest lose 2.22 bps a day net (t −3.44).
- **Test 3 fails:** 2020–2025-06 loses 1.58 bps a day net (t −1.39), and the unseen months lose 6.62 (t −3.81).
- **The money test** (computed, not graded by the verdict's order): +54.1 points a year over uniform random overnight accounts, +49.0 at 2 bps a side. The unseen months gave +14.4.

### What it means

- **The drift is real before costs.** A stock's recent overnight drift, measured against its twin, predicts its next overnight move strongly (gross t 22.8 in 2008–2019), and most of it reverses during the next session. This replicates Lou, Polk & Skouras's tug of war in this data.
- **The hedged book can't pay for itself.** It must open and close every position every day, so 2 bps a side plus the hedges takes 10.6 of its 11.6 bps a day. Its gross has also faded: after 2009 its net is above zero only in 2010 (+0.7 bps a day) and 2020 (+9.5).
- **The long-only overnight account looks far better,** because the comparison with random overnight accounts doesn't charge for the hedge or the short side. But:
  - three years carry its mean;
  - volatility and survivorship explain part of it;
  - its lead in the unseen months doesn't survive volatility matching;
  - what remains sits in the official opening print. A trader would have to collect it with market-on-open sells at the top-20 names' opening auctions every day, and the size those auctions can absorb is untested.

## Decisions made while building it

Logged with times in `research/overnight_auctions/out/decisions.txt`.

**Before any signal or outcome was computed** (only the dividend data check had run):
- **Dividends and splits.** The plan says to drop ex-date overnights if the store doesn't adjust them. The check (`divcheck.py`) found the store adjusted through about 2022 and raw after, and there is no dividend calendar on disk for 2008–2025, so the drop can't be applied to history. Instead, decided before any result:
  - the headline uses the store as is;
  - every test and the money test are also reported over 2008–2022, and Test 3 is split at 2023;
  - in the unseen months, the Yahoo daily file's ex-dates are dropped and the change reported.
  The "payer" flag (an adjusted region exists) was not used as a drop rule, because split adjustment also takes prices off the cent grid.
- **Timing.** The universe, twin, twin β and SPY β for day t come from the rebalance at or before t−1, asserted in the code. `checks.py` verifies it by garbling every close from day t on and every open from t+1 on: S(t) must stay bit-identical.
- **The overnight.** log(open_d / close_{d−1}) on consecutive export days. It is missing if:
  - a price is missing or ≤ 0;
  - |log| > 0.5 (night and day's bad-print rule);
  - d is before 2008-01-02;
  - the open equals the previous close.
  The last is the twin gap fade's stale-open definition. That experiment only tested it post hoc; here the plan says a stale open isn't a real fill, so it is dropped. Its other definition, open = close, needs day t's close and can't be used for the signal. The rule uses only open_d and close_{d−1}, and the twin's overnight gets the same rule.
- **x and S.** The twin and β in force for day t are applied to all 21 overnights. S is the mean of the present x, with at least 17 of 21 present. The raw version is the same mean of the stock's own overnights.
- **Exits.** A position-day counts if the stock's overnight on the exit day is valid and night and day's leg rule holds that day (|intraday| ≤ 0.5, not open = close = high = low); the same goes for the hedge. Invalid position-days are dropped from that day's means. Sensitivity: stale exits kept at a 0 return (+51.9 instead of +54.1).
- **Books.** Deciles are round(10% × n); the 500 largest are re-ranked inside the 500. Periods go by exit day.
- **Costs.** 8 bps a day on the long-short (2 bps a side, both sides daily), plus 2 × 1 bp × the netted hedge weights. Break-even per side = (gross − hedge cost) / 4.
- **Raw version's β:** the stock's OLS beta to SPY over the 252 days ending at the rebalance (export closes for the stock, store closes for SPY), with at least 227 pairs.
- **Test readings:**
  - Test 2 uses 2008–2019, like Test 1;
  - "same sign" in Test 3 means positive net;
  - pass/fail uses the plain t, with Newey-West (5 lags) reported.
- **Money test.** The 20 highest S of the all-stocks ranked set, by stable sort. The random accounts draw 20 distinct stocks uniformly from the same set (seed 20261021) and handle invalid exits the same way. Returns compound by the exit day's calendar year; 2008 starts at the first signal (exit 2008-01-28) and 2025 ends 06-27. The unseen months are one compounded period. Volatility-matched accounts weren't in the plan, so none were planned.
- **The 9:35 extra.**
  - **Positions:** the money account's positions on the 5-minute window days, where SPY has all 78 bars.
  - **Prices:** entry at the store's close, exit at the store's open or at the open of Yahoo's 09:35 bar, scaled by k = the store's close ÷ Yahoo's daily close on the exit day.
  - **Usable:** Yahoo's daily closes on both days within 0.1% of the store's, and no ex-date on the exit day.
  - **The measure:** share kept = mean daily 9:35 return ÷ mean daily open return, on the same positions.
- **Judge check:** night and day's 5-day reversal, copied, rebalanced weekly. It gave +11.8 and 8 of 18, as expected.

**Built later, before any outcome:** the timing check's control. Garbling day t's own open changes S for 1,250 of 1,273 stocks, so the check has power.

**After the money test (post hoc, `diag.py`; no rule, pass/fail or number changed):** the picks sat at the 84th volatility percentile on survivor-only data, and three years carried the mean. So these were added:
- volatility-matched random accounts (judge-style deciles of trailing 63-day volatility, pick by pick);
- the money test inside the 500 largest;
- the account restricted to stocks with close_{t−1} ≥ $5;
- the account's intraday and close-to-close legs;
- the median and the mean without 2008, 2009 and 2020. These years were chosen after seeing the by-year table.

**The unseen months** were run once, after the old-export results were drafted. The same diagnostics, the 9:35 extra and the dividend drop were applied, with scripts written before running them.

**Tools:** system Python has no pandas, so `extra935.py` and `divunseen.py` read the Yahoo files with the csv module. No bars were fetched.
