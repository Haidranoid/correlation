# Lazy prices: do companies that rewrite their 10-K underperform?

**TL;DR:** This is a published effect, tested on this data for the first time: Cohen, Malloy & Nguyen (2020, "Lazy Prices", *Journal of Finance*) found that companies whose 10-K changes a lot from last year's underperform over the following months, because investors are slow to read the changes. Here each company's Items 1, 1A and 7 (EDGAR-CORPUS, fiscal 2003–2020, 32,015 year-over-year pairs) were compared with last year's by the cosine similarity of their word counts. Every 21 trading days a long-short bought the fifth of liquid stocks whose filings changed least and sold the fifth that changed most. Over 2007–2021 it made +0.05% a month before costs and +0.03% after 5 bps (t = 0.26, where the test needed 2). Its alpha was +0.05% a month against the equal-weight market (t = 0.49) and +0.07% against SPY (t = 0.73). So it fails the first test. The 500 largest stocks give the same sign and nothing more (+0.05% a month net, t = 0.49). January to May 2022, the last months the text covers, went the wrong way (−1.34% a month, t = −1.5, five months). No section works on its own (Item 1A comes closest, t = 0.72), and dating filings from April 1 instead of June 1 changes nothing (t = 0.47). In the judge's money test, the 20 least-changed stocks made 2.2 points a year less than uniform random accounts (se 1.7, ahead in 6 of the 15 years 2007-08..2021-22) and 2.3 less than volatility-matched ones (ahead in 4 of 15). No year beat random at p < 0.1. The text ends with fiscal 2020, so the scores stop in May 2022 and the 16 unseen months (2025-06-30..2026-10-19) can't be tested. Verdict: no edge. The lazy-prices effect doesn't show up in this data.

## Nearest prior work

| Work | What it found |
|---|---|
| Cohen, Malloy & Nguyen 2020, *JF*, "Lazy Prices" | Firms that change their 10-K and 10-Q language substantially underperform the next year, a large long-short alpha (non-changers minus changers). Strongest in the risk-factor and MD&A sections. Prices react slowly; news about the changes arrives later |
| Brown & Tucker 2011, *JAR* | Large changes in MD&A text carry information, but price responses to them were muted |
| Loughran & McDonald 2011, 2016 | 10-K language and returns; text-analysis methods in finance |

**Novelty check (2026-10-21, a separate search; it changed no rules):** done, as expected.
- **Cohen, Malloy & Nguyen 2020** (*JF* 75(3)), 10-K and 10-Q filings from 1995–2014: non-changers minus changers earned 34–58 bps a month value-weighted, and up to 188 bps for Risk Factor changes. There was no reaction at the filing date and no reversal.
- **No peer-reviewed test after 2014 was found.** Quantpedia's Padysak (2020) reports that on 2007–2020 large caps, the sign reverses for positive language.

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Text:**
- Items 1, 1A (risk factors, from 2005) and 7 (MD&A) of each 10-K in EDGAR-CORPUS.
- Stream the corpus as `research/imposters/fetch_edgar.py` does, keeping only cik, year, filename and those three sections. Store them under `~/.local/share/correlation2/experiments/lazy_prices/`.

**Similarity:**
- For each company, the cosine similarity between this year's and last year's term-frequency vectors (lower-cased words, no stop-word list, no stemming), over the three sections concatenated.
- Also per section, reported.
- Pairs whose two filings are more than 15 months apart are dropped.

**When it's known:**
- A 10-K counts from the earlier imposters experiment's conservative date: April 1 of the year after its fiscal year. Check that agent's `research/imposters/` code for the exact rule and reuse it.
- Tickers come from the earlier experiment's map.

**Universe:** the judge-like liquid set, minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)`, with a similarity known as of the month.

**Book:**
- Monthly (every 21 trading days, fixed calendar): rank by similarity.
- Long the top fifth (non-changers), short the bottom fifth (changers), equal weight, held 21 days.
- Costs 5 bps per side.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **2007–2021:** the long-short's mean monthly return. **Pass:** t ≥ 2, net of costs, with positive alpha against the market.
2. **The 500 largest:** same sign.
3. **The last available months, 2022:** same sign. There is no 10-K text after fiscal 2020, so the 16 unseen months can't be tested; the file says so.

**Money test (the judge):**
- Score = the similarity percentile (non-changers highest), every day, NaN without a known similarity. Graded with `rebalance=21`.
- The judge grades only years with scores.
- Uniform and volatility-matched random accounts are both reported.

## The number for the file name

The judge's walk-forward change over random, with uniform random accounts, 0 bps, over the years with text.

## Ways it can fool us

- **Filing dates:** EDGAR-CORPUS doesn't give them, so the April 1 rule is conservative. Late filers' changes still show up after it. Report how many filings come from companies with non-December fiscal years.
- **Survivorship:** companies whose rewritten 10-K preceded a collapse and delisting are missing. That flatters the short side's victims, so it works *against* finding the effect.

## Results (run 2026-10-21)

**What the data allowed.**
- **Text:** EDGAR-CORPUS has 6,700–9,300 10-Ks a year. 1,350 (FY2003) to 2,987 (FY2020) are from companies in the ticker map, one filing per company and year. That gives 32,015 year-over-year pairs: 1,245 for FY2004 against FY2003, rising to 2,635 for FY2020.
  - About 40 pairs a year have no section with 100 words in both filings, and 40–85 a year fail the 9-to-15-month rule. Both are dropped.
  - Item 1A counts in both filings from FY2006 against FY2005 on (972 pairs, then 1,360–2,465 a year).
  - Counts by year: `research/lazy_prices/pairs_counts.txt`.
- **Similarities bunch near 1,** because common words dominate the counts: median 0.997, 5th percentile 0.980. The long fifth averaged 0.9989 and the short fifth 0.981.
- **Universe:** about 300 stocks in 2005–07 (the export has few stocks with three years of history before then), about 1,000 in 2008–13, and 1,900 by 2021. 70–75% of them have a similarity. So the book held 265 stocks in 2007 and 1,390 in 2021, with legs of 178 stocks on average.
- **Turnover:** 0.42 of a book worth 2.0 a month, so 5 bps per side costs about 0.02% a month.
- **Non-December fiscal years** (from "Ways it can fool us"): 24% of all pairs with a known fiscal-year end (7,383 of 30,601). Of the 15,316 company-years in test 1's books, 14,490 have a known end and 3,357 (23%) are non-December. Their filings are dated by the June 1 rule or 120 days after the year end, whichever is later. So a September year end is dated about six months after its filing.

### Differences from Cohen, Malloy & Nguyen

- **Filings:** they compare 10-Qs as well as 10-Ks, each with the same quarter a year earlier, so their signal refreshes every quarter. Here it is 10-Ks only, refreshed once a year.
- **Text and measure:** they use whole filings and several similarity measures (cosine, Jaccard, edit distance and others). Here it is three sections and one measure, raw word-count cosine with no stop-word list, as the plan fixed. Common words dominate it, so it may measure change less sharply than theirs.
- **Dates:** they use each filing's real date. Here the date is a conservative rule, June 1 or later, because the corpus has no filing dates. The April sensitivity suggests this isn't why nothing shows up.
- **Sample:** theirs is all CRSP stocks, delisted ones included, through 2014. Here it is liquid survivors ($5 and $5M a day medians, listed in 2025), 2007–2021, so most of it comes after the effect was found.

## Decisions made while building it

All of these were made on 2026-10-21 **before any return after a formation date was computed or looked at**, unless a line says otherwise.

**Text**
- **Years streamed:** EDGAR-CORPUS fiscal years 2003–2020, all three splits. The first test books (2007) compare FY2005 with FY2004; 2003 only feeds earlier, ungraded months.
- **Kept per filing:** cik, year, filename, Items 1, 1A and 7, and the fiscal-year end the filing mentions most. That last field is the imposters experiment's `fye`, computed while streaming exactly as its `fetch_edgar.py` does; its filing-date rule needs it.
- **Words:** the text lower-cased, and every run of letters a–z is a word. Numbers and punctuation are not words, because dates and amounts change every year whatever the company does. No stop-word list and no stemming, as the plan says.
- **One filing per company and year:** the one with the most words over the three sections, as the imposters experiment did (amendments are usually shorter).
- **A section counts when it has at least 100 words in both filings, and the similarity concatenates only those sections.** Item 1A first appears in FY2005 10-Ks, smaller companies may skip it, Item 7 is sometimes only a pointer to the annual-report exhibit, and the corpus's section split sometimes fails in one year. Counting any of these as a "change" would rank companies on the 2005 rule change and on extraction noise. **Sensitivity, reported and not pass/fail:** every non-empty section of each filing concatenated as is.
- **Per-section similarities:** each section where it counts in both filings.
- **Pairs:** consecutive corpus years of the same CIK. When both filings' fiscal-year ends are known, pairs more than 15 months apart are dropped (the plan's rule), and so are pairs fewer than 9 months apart, which would cover the same or an overlapping fiscal year (a misdated duplicate or a transition period). When either end is unknown, consecutive corpus years count as 12 months apart.
- **Checked on the inputs, before any returns:** with no stop-word list, common words dominate the vectors, so similarities bunch near 1 (FY2020 against FY2019: median 0.997, 5th percentile 0.985). Only the ranking matters, so this was left as the plan fixed it. A few fiscal-year ends read from the text are wrong (a future year, or a typo like 3019). So the gap rule drops about 3% of pairs, some of them wrongly, and a wrong later year end only delays a filing's date. Also left unchanged.

**When it's known**
- **The plan says April 1 and also says to reuse the imposters code's rule, and the two differ.** The code's rule is June 1 of the year after the fiscal year, or 120 days after a later fiscal-year end the text mentions, whichever is later. That rule is used. It misses about two months of any drift right after a typical March filing. **Sensitivity, reported and not pass/fail:** April 1 of the next year, or 90 days after a later fiscal-year end.
- **Only the current pair counts:** the pair of the expected latest fiscal year (the imposters experiment's rule: last year's from June 1, else the year before), so a similarity counts from its date until May 31 two years after its fiscal year. The imposters experiment also accepted text a year older. A change that old is stale, so it doesn't count here.
- **Rows:** a CIK's similarity goes to every export row the map gives it. No row has two CIKs.

**Universe and book**
- **Universe** at each of the twins' calendar days (export index 252 + 21k): a close on ≥ 90% of the past 756 days, median close ≥ $5 and median dollar volume ≥ $5M over the past 252 days, minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)` at that rebalance. It holds until the next calendar day. A stock also needs a similarity known at that day's close.
- **Fifths:** rank by similarity at the close of t0, lowest first; fifth = floor(5 × rank ÷ n). Long the top fifth, short the bottom fifth, equal weight, bought at the close of t0 and held to the close of t0+21. Closes are forward-filled, so a missing day earns 0.
- **Costs:** 5 bps × turnover, where turnover is the sum of |new weight − drifted old weight| over every stock. The first book pays full entry.
- **Periods are formation days.** Test 1: 2007-01-01..2021-12-31. Test 3: formations in 2022, which can only be January to May: FY2020 pairs, the last in the corpus, stop counting on 2022-05-31.
- **The 500 largest:** among universe stocks with a known similarity, the 500 with the highest median dollar volume over the past 252 days; fifths within them.

**Pass rules**
- **Test 1:** t ≥ 2 for the mean net monthly long-short, **and** a positive CAPM alpha against both the export's equal-weight `market` and SPY. The alpha comes from monthly net long-short returns on the market's excess return over the same 21 days, using FRED's 3-month Treasury yield, with plain OLS t (the months don't overlap).
- **Tests 2 and 3:** the mean net monthly long-short has test 1's sign.

**Money test**
- **Score:** rank ÷ (n − 1) among universe stocks with a known similarity that day (universe from the latest calendar day). The most-changed stock scores 0 and is never picked (the judge buys only positive scores); the least-changed scores 1. NaN elsewhere.
- Scores stop at 2022-05-31, so the judge skips 2022-23 onward. The file-name number covers the years it grades.

**Made after seeing results**
- **Nothing in the rules, the code or the tests was changed** after any return was computed.
- **Before the real pairs existed,** the long-short code was run once on random similarities, to check that it worked. That says nothing about the effect.
- **Added to the report only:** the by-year table, the annualized Sharpe ratio, and the look at the money test's picks. They describe the results and decide nothing.
- **Not tried, on purpose:** stop-word lists, tf-idf weights and Cohen, Malloy & Nguyen's other similarity measures. The plan fixed raw word counts, and trying variants after a null would be a search for a positive result. They are the obvious next step if this is ever revisited, and they would need their own fixed rules and their own holdout.
- **No bugs met in `c2eval`.** `change_over_random` and `summary_line` worked on the walk-forward JSON as they are.

- `fetch_edgar.py`: streams EDGAR-CORPUS fiscal 2003–2020 and keeps cik, year, filename, the fiscal-year end and Items 1, 1A and 7 → `edgar/` in the work folder (`fetch_edgar.log`).
- `vectors.py`: word counts per filing and section, words hashed to 64-bit ids → `vec/{year}.npz` (`vectors.log`).
- `pairs.py`: year-over-year similarities, per section and concatenated, with their dates → `pairs.npz`; counts by year in `pairs_counts.txt` (`pairs.log`).
- `panel.py`: the universe on the twins' calendar (cached as `universe_<fingerprint>.npz`) and the daily similarity panels.
- `tests.py`: tests 1–3, sections and sensitivities, coverage → `tests_out.txt`, `tests_out.json` (`tests.log`).
- `money.py`: the daily scores and the judge, uniform and volatility-matched → `money_out.txt`, `money_out.json` (`money.log`).
- `report.py`: the tables in this file → `report.txt`.
- `sanity_reversal.py` → `sanity_reversal.txt`: 5-day reversal through this folder's path, +11.8 points a year, 8 of 18 years.

In `~/.local/share/correlation2/experiments/lazy_prices/`: `edgar/` (the three sections, 4.3 GB; the raw JSONL was never stored), `vec/` (1.5 GB), `pairs.npz`, `universe_8b5ac076267c83ab.npz`, and in `scores/` the scores files `lazy_prices_old.npy` and `sanity_reversal5.npy` with the judge's JSON next to each.
