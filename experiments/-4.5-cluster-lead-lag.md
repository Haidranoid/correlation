# Groups that move first (cluster lead-lag, Bennett et al. 2022)

**TL;DR:** Single pairs don't hold up, so this tested whether groups of stocks move first and other groups follow, using the published method of Bennett, Cucuringu & Reinert (2022). It replicates before costs in its own years: Sharpe 0.86 in 2003–2012, and shuffled groups did as well only 0.5% of the time. Then it fades: gone after 2012, and since 2020 with the paper's settings. The edge is the market's slow followers rather than stock-to-stock lead-lag, because on market-relative returns it vanishes. The widest version (all exchanges) has the strongest gross signal in the project: a long-short of 4.7 bps/day (z = 3.63), about 6 points a year over random accounts. But it trades 42% of the book a day, so it breaks even only below about 2.8 bps per trade. In the 16 unseen months it made −4.4% against +11.0% for random with the paper's settings, and +49.5% against +30.5% on all exchanges (p = 0.185). Verdict: real before costs, but no money after them.

## Method (the paper's sections 3 and 6)

- **Universe:** every 42 trading days, the top 500 stocks by dollar volume over the prior 252 days. NYSE only by default.
- **Lead-lag measure:** how much each stock moves first, by distance correlation at offsets 1–5 days ("ccf-auc").
- **Groups:** 10, by Hermitian spectral clustering. The strongest 10% of group-to-group flows become links.
- **Forecast:** for each link, regress the follower group's next day on an exponentially weighted sum of the leader group's recent days.
- **Book:** long the groups forecast to rise, short those forecast to fall, scaled to 10% volatility.
- **Nulls:** shuffled group labels (200 copies, the paper's test) and the harness's random guessers.
- **Checks:**
  - the fast distance correlation matches the textbook formula;
  - planted groups are recovered;
  - later data never changes an earlier forecast.

## Results

**The paper's test** (Sharpe, annualized; "net" is after 5 bps per side):

| Variant | 2003–2012 | 2013–2019 | 2020–2026-10 | Net, all years |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper (NYSE, distance correlation, raw returns) | 0.86 (shuffled p 0.005) | −0.28 | −0.32 | −0.67 |
| Pearson instead of distance correlation | | 0.32 (2003–2019) | 0.12 | −0.55 |
| All US exchanges | 0.49 | 0.52 | 0.29 (shuffled p 0.025) | −0.34 |
| Market-relative returns, all exchanges, from 2007 | | −0.03 (to 2019) | −0.13 | −1.01 |

- **The market explains it.** Over the same days, raw returns give a Sharpe of +0.52 and market-relative returns −0.07. This is Lo & MacKinlay's (1990) slow followers, also known as Hou & Moskowitz price delay.
- **The groups are unstable:** adjusted Rand index 0.18 from one fit to the next.

**Random-guesser walk-forward:**
- **Paper settings:** beat random in 6 of 19 years (chance gives about 1.9): 4 of them in 2006–2013, none since mid-2020. Long-short 2.8 bps/day, z = 1.62.
- **All exchanges:** long-short 4.7 bps/day, positive in 14 of 19 years, z = 3.63. Costs take 1.69 × c bps/day at c bps per side, so it breaks even below about 2.8 bps.
- **All exchanges, weekly:** costs fall to 0.51 × c, but only 2.2 bps/day is left (z = 1.41), still below costs at 5 bps.

Several variants were tried, so a single p of 0.025 among them is weak evidence. A cheap follow-up is listed in `README.md`: split this strategy's profit into its overnight and intraday parts, to see whether it could be traded through the opening auction.
