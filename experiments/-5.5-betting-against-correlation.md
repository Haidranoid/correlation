# Betting against correlation

**TL;DR:** This is a published effect, tested on this data for the first time: Asness, Frazzini, Gormsen & Pedersen (2020) found that, within groups of equally volatile stocks, the stocks least correlated with the market earn more per unit of risk, and a beta-neutral long-short on it ("betting against correlation", BAC) earns a strong alpha. On this data it doesn't show up. Over 2008-03..2019 (the data has too few stocks before 2008) BAC made 2.4% a year after 5 bps costs, with a Sharpe ratio of 0.31. Its alpha was +3.0% a year against the equal-weight market (t = 1.3) and +3.2% against SPY (t = 1.4), short of the t ≥ 2 the test needed. The 500 largest stocks gave less (+2.2%, t = 0.9), and 2020–2025 lost 3.6% a year (t = −0.6). Correlation adds nothing beyond beta and volatility: BAC moves almost exactly like 1.3 × betting-against-beta minus 1.0 × low volatility (R² 0.84), with 0.7% a year left over (t = 0.7). In the 16 unseen months (2025-06-30..2026-10-19) BAC made +6.3%, but its alphas (t = 0.6 and 1.2) aren't significant either. The file-name number comes from the judge's long-only account of the 20 stocks with the lowest correlation for their volatility. It made 4.4 points a year less than random accounts (ahead in 7 of 18 years, standard error 4.8) and 3.7 less than volatility-matched ones. In the unseen months it lost 4.3% while the median random account made 19.6%. That account compares raw returns, and its stocks are low-beta: they beat random in the years the average stock fell or stood still and trailed in rising ones, so the number would be negative even if the alpha were real. Here the alpha isn't significant either. Verdict: no edge.

## The idea

- **The prediction:** leverage-constrained investors pay up for high-beta stocks. Within the same volatility, high correlation with the market means high beta.
- **What AFGP found:** they sort on correlation *within volatility groups*, which separates that leverage story from other low-risk explanations, and find the low-correlation side earns more per unit of risk.
- **For this project:** it uses same-day correlation, the kind the census found persistent, to predict returns at a monthly horizon, where trading costs are small.

## Nearest prior work

From the earlier survey and well-known literature; no new search this round.

| Work | What it found |
|---|---|
| Asness, Frazzini, Gormsen & Pedersen 2020, *JFE*, "Betting against correlation: testing theories of the low-risk effect" | BAC, correlation sorted within volatility quintiles and beta-neutral, earns strong risk-adjusted returns in the US and internationally |
| Frazzini & Pedersen 2014, *JFE*, "Betting against beta" | Low-beta stocks earn more per unit of risk; BAB factor |
| Novy-Marx & Velikov 2022, *JFE*, "Betting against betting against beta" | BAB's profits depend on non-standard construction (rank weighting, the leverage of the low-beta leg, microcaps). A caution for BAC too |
| Baker, Bradley & Wurgler 2011; Ang, Hodrick, Xing & Zhang 2006 | Low-volatility and low-idiosyncratic-volatility anomalies |

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Universe**
- The judge-like liquid set: median price ≥ $5 and median dollar volume ≥ $5M over the past 252 days, and data on ≥ 90% of the past 756 days.
- Drop fund-like stocks, using `c2eval.twins(E)['fund_like']` at the latest twin rebalance.
- Recompute every 21 trading days on a fixed calendar.

**Inputs**
- **Volatility:** the sd of daily log returns over the past 252 days.
- **Correlation:** with the market (the export's equal-weight `market`), using overlapping 3-day log returns over the past 1,260 days (5 years), with ≥ 750 days required. This follows AFGP: 5-year correlations on 3-day returns.
- **Ex-ante beta:** correlation × σ_i ÷ σ_market (σ over 252 days), shrunk to 0.6 β + 0.4.

**BAC portfolio**
- Split the universe into volatility quintiles. Within each, rank by correlation.
- Long the lowest-correlation third, short the highest third, equal weight within each leg.
- Scale each leg to ex-ante beta 1, as AFGP and FP do, so the long-short is beta-neutral.
- BAC is the average of the five quintile long-shorts. Rebalance every 21 trading days, with daily returns.

**Controls, built the same way**
- **BAB:** sort on ex-ante beta across the whole universe, without quintiles.
- **Low volatility:** long the lowest-volatility third, short the highest, beta-neutral.

**Costs:** 5 bps per side on actual turnover; gross and net both reported.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **BAC, 2005–2019:** alpha against the market (CAPM, with both the equal-weight market and SPY), plus the annualized Sharpe ratio. **Pass:** alpha t ≥ 2 and Sharpe ≥ 0.3, net of 5 bps.
2. **The 500 largest only:** BAC rebuilt from the 500 most-traded stocks. **Pass:** same sign, alpha t ≥ 1.5. Survivor-only data flatters small idiosyncratic names, as it flattered the monthly picks.
3. **2020–2025-06** (old export): same sign of alpha. Also the 16 unseen months (new export, once, at the end).
4. **Reported, not pass/fail:** alpha controlling for BAB and low volatility, which says whether correlation adds anything beyond them.

**Money test (the judge)**
- Score = 1 − (the stock's correlation percentile within its volatility quintile), so the lowest-correlation stocks score highest. NaN outside the universe.
- It is computed **every day**, so the judge's own 21-day calendar picks it up. Grade with `rebalance=21`.
- The account holds the 20 lowest-correlation stocks.
- Uniform and volatility-matched random accounts are both reported.

## The number for the file name

- **What it is:** the judge's walk-forward change over random with uniform random accounts (`c2eval.change_over_random`, 0 bps), as for every other file.
- **How to read it:** it compares raw returns, and the 20 lowest-correlation stocks are low-beta, so they can trail random in bull markets even when BAC's alpha is strongly positive. The verdict rests on tests 1–3.

## Ways it can fool us

- **Survivorship:** the store has only stocks listed in 2025. Low-correlation names are often small, idiosyncratic stocks, and the ones that collapsed are missing. Test 2 is the guard.
- **Fund-like stocks:** bond and gold funds filed as stocks would look like perfect low-correlation stocks. They are dropped through the fund-like flag; check the picks by hand.
- **Construction choices:** Novy-Marx & Velikov's critique of BAB applies here. Report equal weighting within legs, as fixed above, and say how sensitive the result is to beta scaling.

### Sensitivities

- **Beta scaling** (Novy-Marx & Velikov's concern). Without it ($1 long, $1 short in each quintile), BAC made 0.28% a year with a Sharpe ratio of 0.03 over 2008-03..2019, and its beta was −0.31. Its alpha was +2.29% (t = 1.02) against the equal-weight market and +3.70% (t = 1.57) against SPY. In 2020–2025 it made −6.14% a year.
  - So scaling each leg to beta 1 adds about 2 points a year of raw return. It levers the long leg (1/0.88) and shrinks the short leg (1/1.14), which takes out the negative market beta.
  - The alpha barely depends on the scaling, and no version reaches t = 2.
- **The plan's literal universe** (fund-like flag only, without the extra 50 funds, ETFs, preferreds and warrants): 2.32% a year, Sharpe 0.29, alpha +2.87% (t = 1.26). The money test gives −4.7 points (uniform) and −4.2 (volatility-matched). The extra exclusions change little.

### Differences from AFGP

- **Weights:** AFGP rank-weight every stock in a quintile, split at the median correlation. Here the legs are equal-weight thirds, as the plan fixed.
- **Universe:** AFGP use all CRSP common stocks, delisted ones included. Here only liquid survivors ($5 and $5M medians, listed in 2025), with funds removed.
- **Market:** AFGP use the value-weighted CRSP market for correlations and betas. Here the export's equal-weight `market` (a mean of daily log returns) is used for both, and SPY is a second benchmark for alpha.
- **Sample:** theirs runs from 1926 in the US, plus international data. Here only 2008-03..2025-06 and the 16 unseen months.
- **Alphas:** AFGP test against several factor models. Here only CAPM, plus BAB and low volatility in test 4.

## Decisions made while building it

All of these were made on 2026-10-21 **before any returns after a formation date were computed or looked at**, unless a line says otherwise.

**Calendar and universe**
- **Calendar:** the twins' fixed calendar, export day indices 252 + 21k (index 756 is the close of 2004-12-31). A portfolio formed at the close of t0 is held over t0+1 .. t0+21.
- **Universe at each calendar day t0:** a close on ≥ 90% of days t0−755..t0; median close ≥ $5 and median dollar volume ≥ $5M over the finite values of t0−251..t0; not fund-like at the twins rebalance on t0 (the same calendar, so "the latest" is t0 itself); and a valid volatility and correlation at t0. It is held until the next calendar day.
- **The test period starts in 2008-03, not 2005 (decided after counting the universe, before any returns).** The export's `market` is NaN for its first 252 days, and only about 320 stocks have closes before mid-2005. So the first valid correlations (750 three-day returns) exist at the close of 2005-12-30, for 299 stocks, and the broad universe only at the close of 2008-03-05 (985 stocks, 900–2,250 after that). BAC is run only when the universe has **≥ 500 stocks**, so tests 1, 2 and 4 cover daily returns **2008-03-06..2019-12-31** (the 500-largest version then exists on the same days). The 299-stock period 2006-01..2008-03 is reported separately, not graded. (The code builds the book from 2006 on and grades from 2008-03-06, so the first graded rebalance pays turnover against the 299-stock book rather than a full entry: under 0.1% once.)
- **Funds filed as stocks that the fund-like flag misses (decided after looking at which stocks are in the universe and the low-correlation legs, before any returns).** The flag catches only stocks moving ≥ 0.97 with a more-traded stock or fund. It misses Sprott's physical gold and silver trusts (CEF, PHYS, PSLV), closed-end bond and equity funds (PIMCO, Nuveen, BlackRock and others), an inverse ETF (SARK, correlation −0.81), other ETFs, preferred shares and warrants. So the universe also drops: tickers containing `_` (preferreds) or ending `-WS`, `-U`, `-R`, `-RT`; stocks whose current SEC name (the imposters experiment's map of SEC's `company_tickers_exchange.json`, read only) contains the word FUND, or starts with BLACKROCK and contains TRUST, or contains SPROTT PHYSICAL; and by hand ASA and TYG (closed-end funds without FUND in the name) and SARK, JEPQ, QTUM, ARTY, QQQE, WGMI (ETFs without an SEC name). Royalty trusts, REITs, BDCs other than those named FUND, gold miners and ADRs stay: they are operating companies. **The flag-only universe the plan fixed is reported as a sensitivity.** The new export is scanned the same way before its returns are computed.

**Inputs**
- **Volatility:** sd of daily log returns (both closes present) over d−251..d, with ≥ 120 returns (Frazzini & Pedersen's minimum; the file gives none). σ_market: sd of the export's `market` over the same days.
- **Correlation:** Pearson correlation of overlapping 3-day log returns, log(close[d]/close[d−3]) with both closes present, against the sum of the export's `market` over the same 3 days. The window is the 3-day returns ending d−1259..d, and needs ≥ 750 of them.
- **Beta:** ρ × σ_i ÷ σ_market, then 0.6 β + 0.4. A leg's beta is the equal-weight mean of its members' shrunk betas at formation.

**Portfolios**
- **Volatility quintiles** at t0 by rank among that day's universe: quintile = floor(5 × rank ÷ n). **Thirds** within a quintile by correlation rank r (0 = lowest): long r < n/3, short r ≥ 2n/3.
- **Returns in excess of the risk-free rate,** as AFGP and FP: quintile long-short = (1/β_L)(r_L − r_f) − (1/β_H)(r_H − r_f); BAC is the mean of the five. r_f is FRED's 3-month Treasury yield (`fred_dgs3mo` in the project's outside data), % a year ÷ 100 ÷ 252 per trading day, the latest value at or before the day.
- **Between rebalances,** buy and hold: each leg's stocks drift with their returns, and the leg's leverage 1/β stays fixed. Closes are forward-filled, so a missing day earns 0 and the gap's return is credited when the stock trades again.
- **Costs:** at each rebalance, turnover = Σ |new weight − drifted old weight| over every stock in the book (leverage included; BAC's weights are the quintile books' ÷ 5); the first formation counts its full entry. Cost = 5 bps × turnover, taken from the first day of the new holding period.
- **500 largest (test 2):** the 500 universe stocks with the highest median dollar volume over t0−251..t0; quintiles and thirds recomputed within them.
- **Controls:** BAB = thirds of the whole universe by shrunk beta; low volatility = thirds by volatility. Equal weight, legs scaled to beta 1, the same calendar, r_f and costs.

**Statistics**
- **Regressions** on daily returns, net of costs unless marked gross. Factors: the export's `market` (a mean of daily log returns, turned into a simple return with exp − 1) minus r_f, and SPY's daily return from the store's adjusted closes minus r_f. Alpha annualized × 252; **t from Newey-West with 5 lags.** Sharpe = mean ÷ sd × √252 of daily net BAC (already an excess return).
- **Test 1 passes only if both alphas have t ≥ 2** (the equal-weight market and SPY), and the Sharpe ratio is ≥ 0.3, all net.
- **Test 2 passes** if both alphas of the 500-largest BAC, net, over test 1's days, have test 1's sign and t ≥ 1.5. **Test 3 passes** if both alphas, net, over 2020-01-02..2025-06-27 have test 1's sign.
- **Periods:** test 1 is daily returns dated 2008-03-06..2019-12-31 (above); test 3 is 2020-01-02..2025-06-27; the unseen months are 2025-06-30..2026-10-19. By-period rows: 2008–09, 2010–14, 2015–19, 2020–25.
- **Test 4:** BAC on the equal-weight market, BAB and low volatility together (all net), Newey-West 5 lags.
- **Reported sensitivity, not pass/fail:** BAC without beta scaling ($1 long, $1 short per quintile).

**Money test**
- **Scores every day d:** the universe from the latest calendar day at or before d; volatility, correlation and quintiles recomputed at d among those stocks with valid inputs at d. Score = 1 − r ÷ (n_q − 1), with r the 0-based correlation rank in the stock's volatility quintile, so the lowest-correlation stock of each quintile scores 1 and the highest 0 (never picked). The top 20 are therefore about the 4 lowest-correlation stocks of each quintile, not the 20 lowest correlations overall.
- The judge picks from its own yearly universe; stocks outside this file's universe have NaN scores, so the account picks from the overlap.

**Unseen months**
- Everything is rebuilt on the new export, with `c2eval.twins(E_new, cache=False)` saved in this experiment's folder. The export's days are identical to the old one through 2025-06-27, so the calendar continues unchanged.

**Made after seeing results**
- **Four more names on the hand list:** FSCO (a closed-end credit fund), SYSB (a bond ETF), COLO (the renamed Colombia ETF) and GARP (an equity ETF). They were added after the old export's results, before any of the new export's returns were computed, by the scan of the new export's universe that was fixed in advance. None was ever in the old universe; the old universe was checked to be identical after the change.
- **Sharpe ratios use every BAC day.** The first report took the Sharpe ratio over the days where SPY also had a return, which drops 2 days in 2011 when the store has no SPY close. Each regression now drops only its own missing days. Test 1's Sharpe ratio moved from 0.30 to 0.31 and the equal-weight alpha from +2.94% (t = 1.29) to +2.99% (t = 1.31); the SPY alpha is unchanged. Neither changes a pass or fail: test 1 fails on its alphas.
- **A workaround for `c2eval.change_over_random`** (not edited, as the rules require): it reads the walk-forward JSON's `costs`, but a single holdout keeps them under `report.strategy`, so it raised `KeyError: 'costs'` on the unseen months. `money.py` passes `report.strategy` in as `costs`, as `v1_pair_finder/grade.py` and `unequal_move_lengths/money.py` do. For a single holdout the result is that period's difference (16 months), not a yearly figure.
- Nothing else was changed after results were seen.
