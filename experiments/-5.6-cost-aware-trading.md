# Cost-aware trading: does trading only when the expected gain beats the cost rescue the weekly picks?

**TL;DR:** This applies published cost-mitigation rules; nothing in the methods is new. Novy-Marx & Velikov (2016, US stocks, a sample ending by 2014: the working paper is dated December 2014) found buy/hold spreads (buy at a strict rank cutoff, sell only at a looser one) the most effective way to cut anomaly trading costs, and Gârleanu & Pedersen (2013) and DeMiguel et al. (2020) trade only as far as the expected gain pays for. What's new is applying them to this project's strongest gross signal, the weekly LightGBM top 10 (`+7.4-weekly-top10-picks.md`), with each rule's setting chosen on stockai's test years 2010–2016 and tested untouched on test years 2017–2023 (2017-06-27..2024-06-25), entirely after Novy-Marx & Velikov's sample. The wiring check reproduces the weekly file exactly: $214,869 at 0 bps and $19,052 at 20 bps, every year to the cent, from the seed-4 tie-break scores that produced them. The diagnostic shows a one-week signal: the rank IC against the market-relative return is +0.028 in week 1 (t 3.9) and +0.004 to +0.008 in weeks 2–4 (t 0.6–1.1), so holding longer saves cost only by giving up the edge. Of the 19 settings, the selection years picked the expected-gain switch with a one-week horizon and no margin: swap a holding only when the calibrated one-week gain of the best candidate beats the 40-bp round trip. It cut trading from 8.7 to 3.0 names a week and turned $10,000 into $30,326 at 20 bps, against SPY's $25,825. On the test years it made $10,520 at 20 bps (+0.7% a year) against SPY's $25,375 (+14.3%). Its weekly return minus the average stock's has t −0.06, and it lands on the median turnover-matched random account (p 0.54). The file-name number, its yearly return minus the median random account's at 0 bps, is −0.4 points (also −0.4 at 20 bps). After costs at 5 bps a side it trails SPY by 5.6 points a year on average. Among the 500 largest stocks it made $21,061 (p 0.21 against random at 20 bps), still short of SPY. The plan's accounting (no re-equalizing) makes each of the 10 slots a compounding lottery. With these scores the top-10 baseline ends the test years at $74,507 at 20 bps, but at $12,125–$23,278 on the other five tie-breaks of the same model. Settings that ranked near the bottom on the selection years ranked near the top on the test years. The secondary signals fail too: the 5-day reversal's selected rule ended at $3,065, and the daily LightGBM's beat its random accounts (p 0.008) but not SPY ($16,355 against $25,447). The locked year and the 16 unseen months were not used (the scores end in 2024-06), so a pass would only have been "within the development period, unconfirmed". Verdict: fails test 1 (money). Cost-aware trading does not rescue the weekly picks.

**What's new:** cost-aware trading applied to this project's strongest gross signal. Published methods.

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Signals:**
- **Primary:** the weekly LightGBM.
- **Secondary, reported, not graded:** the daily LightGBM (next-day returns) and the 5-day reversal rule. The daily model's edge was gone a day later, so expect little.

**Calendar and accounting:**
- **Primary book:** stockai's graded-week calendar: one decision every 5 trading days, the 700 graded weeks.
- **Account:** a $10,000 top-10 account with stockai's own accounting, closes from the judge's export.
- **Wiring check, before any rule is applied:** the baseline (the top 10 each week, at 20 bps a trade) must reproduce the weekly file's 14-year results: $19,052 at 20 bps, and the 0-bps figure in that file.

**Periods:**
- **Selection:** stockai's test years 2010–2016, the first seven.
- **Test:** test years 2017–2023 (to 2024-06-27).
- **Not used:** the locked year (2024-06-28..2025-06-27) and the 16 unseen months. See "Final confirmation".

**Costs:** 20 bps per trade (primary), 5 bps reported; SPY trades cost 1 bp.

**Dividends:** `pubdata.div_fix('old')` on stock returns from 2022-07; SPY is Yahoo's adjusted close (`pubdata.etf_adjclose`).

**Diagnostic, before any rule (reported, not a gate):**
- The rank IC of today's score against the market-relative return in week 1, 2, 3 and 4 ahead, on the selection years.
- If weeks 2+ are about 0, holding longer trades a real cost saving for a real loss of edge.

**Calibration** (fitted on the selection years only, then frozen):
- For each k = 1..4, an isotonic (monotone) map g_k from a stock's score percentile that day to its expected market-relative return in week k ahead.
- Expected gain over h weeks: G_h(s) = Σ_{k≤h} g_k(s).

**The three rule families.** All hold at most 10 stocks.

1. **Rank buffer:**
   - Keep a holding while its rank stays within the top K; fill empty slots with the best-ranked stocks not held.
   - A sold position's money buys its replacement; there is no re-equalizing.
   - K ∈ {10, 20, 30, 50, 100}. K = 10 is the baseline.
2. **Expected-gain switch:**
   - Replace holding i with the best candidate j only if G_h(j) − G_h(i) > (1 + m) × the round-trip cost (2 × 20 bps).
   - Go down the candidates in order; no re-equalizing.
   - h ∈ {1, 2, 4}, m ∈ {0, 0.5, 1}.
3. **Hold only when confident:**
   - Buy a stock only if G_h > c × the round-trip cost; sell it when G_h < 0.
   - Unused slots hold SPY.
   - h ∈ {1, 2, 4}, c ∈ {1, 2}.

**Selection, done once:**
- Over all 19 settings above (K = 10 excluded), the one with the highest compound net return at 20 bps over the selection years is the primary.
- Each family's best is reported as secondary.
- The choice is written into this file before any test-year number is computed.

**The null: turnover-matched random accounts.**
- 1,000 random accounts, each replacing **as many names each week as the strategy did that week**, with uniformly random sales among its holdings and uniformly random purchases from that day's scored stocks.
- For family 3, also the same split between stocks and SPY.
- Same accounting and costs.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

On the test years 2017–2023, net of 20 bps, for the selected rule:

1. **Money:** its compound return beats SPY's (adjusted, same span).
2. **Significance:** its weekly return minus the average scored stock's has t ≥ 2.
3. **Skill:** it beats the median turnover-matched random account, with p < 0.10.

**Reported:**
- every setting's selection- and test-year results;
- turnover;
- the 500 largest;
- by year;
- the secondary signals with the same procedure;
- the baseline.

## The number for the file name

Points of yearly return of the selected rule's account over the median turnover-matched random account, at 0 bps, over test years 2017–2023. The 20-bps figure is in the file.

## Final confirmation (not part of this run)

A pass here would be "passes within the development period, unconfirmed". Confirming it needs the locked year and the 16 unseen months. That means retraining stockai's weekly model on the new export, which also spends stockai's locked year. That's the user's decision, so this run doesn't touch it.

## Ways it can fool us

- **Selection:** 19 settings were tried on the selection years. The untouched test years are the guard.
- **Survivor-only data:** the weekly signal held among the 500 largest and without collapsed stocks, so this is less of a worry than for the monthly picks. The 500-largest row is reported.
- **Stale forecasts:** a buffer holds stocks on expired forecasts. The diagnostic says how much edge that costs.

### Step 1: the wiring check (before any rule)

The grader's account, rebuilt in numpy from `stockai/judge/src/holdout.rs`, reproduces stockai's judge JSON for every test year and every cost to the cent:

| Scores | 0 bps | 5 bps | 20 bps | 50 bps | stockai's judge |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Seed-4 tie-break (`jitter/lgbm_s4.npy`), 14 yearly accounts compounded | $214,869 | $117,344 | $19,052 | $495 | identical |
| Ties by ticker order (`lgbm/scores.npy`) | $152,839 | $84,079 | $13,953 | $379 | identical |

What the reproduction pins down about stockai's conventions:
- **Test years:** year Y runs from the first trading day on or after Y-06-28 to the last before (Y+1)-06-28. The account's first decision is the close before that, and the starts are 2010-06-25, 2011-06-27, 2012-06-27, 2013-06-27, 2014-06-27, 2015-06-26, 2016-06-27, 2017-06-27, 2018-06-27, 2019-06-27, 2020-06-26, 2021-06-25, 2022-06-27 and 2023-06-27. The last year ends 2024-06-27.
- **Graded weeks:** rebalance days every 5 trading days from each year's first decision. A day is graded when its week ends by the next year's first decision and inside the development columns: 50 per year, 700 in all, from 713 rebalance days.
- **Universe:** the grader's liquid set, fixed for each year at its first day. That means census values on 90% of the prior 756 days, plus a median close of at least $5 and a median dollar volume of at least $5M over the prior 252 days. It runs from 917 stocks (2010) to 1,968 (2023). Scored stocks are those with a finite score.
- **The account:** one $10,000 account per test year; the 14 are compounded. Each rebalance day it buys the top 10 scores above 0 (ties by column order).
- **Weights:** they are reset to 1/10 every day (the day's return is the mean of the 10), so the account re-equalizes daily at no charge.
- **Costs:** each name bought or sold costs the cost rate on a tenth of the account. The first day's buys are charged; nothing is sold at the year's end.
- **Missing closes:** a missing close is a 0 return.

**From stockai's accounting to the plan's** (the top-10 baseline, `bridge.py`; test years without div_fix):

| Account for the top 10 each week | Selection years: 0 bps; 20 bps | Test years: 0 bps; 20 bps |
|---|---|---|
| (i) stockai's grader: yearly accounts, every rebalance day, weights reset daily | $40,867; $11,945 | $52,577; $15,949 |
| (ii) the same daily 1/10 weights, graded weeks only, one continuous account | $43,922; $12,970 | $51,115; $15,649 |
| (iii) weights reset to 1/10 at each decision, held within the week | $41,843; $12,356 | $46,841; $14,340 |
| (iv) the plan's: no re-equalizing | $27,699; $8,224 | $239,347; $73,346 |

The calendar and the weekly reset change little. Dropping re-equalizing changes everything: each slot then compounds its own chain of weekly picks, and the account follows its luckiest slot. In the test years one slot went from $1,000 to $216,315 through ordinary picks; the largest single-week moves were PCG +91% and IVR +86%, both real. Under the seed-3 tie-break the same slot reached $40,018. This is the plan's rule and the random accounts share it, so the null comparison is fair, but every single account number below is very noisy.

## Decisions made while building it

Order of work: (1) the wiring check, (2) the diagnostic, (3) the calibration, (4) all 19 settings on the selection years, (5) the selection written into this file (section "Selection" above), (6) only then any test-year number (`test.py`, `bridge.py test`). `selection.py` reads no return after the close of 2017-06-27; `test.py` reads the frozen calibration and `out/selected.json`.

**Before any result:**
- **Scores:** the primary is the seed-4 random tie-break of the weekly LightGBM (`stockai/runs/1w/jitter/lgbm_s4.npy`), the file behind $19,052 (the median of stockai's five tie-breaks). The plan names `lgbm/scores.npy`, whose ties go by ticker order and which gives $13,953. The selected rule and the baseline are also reported on the other five files.
- **Calendar:** decisions only on the 700 graded days. At each year's end the last graded week's stocks are held through the 1–4 trading days before the next year's first decision, so 13 holding periods are 6–9 days. The 13 ungraded rebalance days are not decision days.
- **Accounts:** the selection and test years are separate accounts, each starting from $10,000 cash at its first decision. The selection account ends at the close of 2017-06-27 (the first test decision). The test account ends at the end of the last graded week, 2024-06-25.
- **The plan's accounting:**
  - 10 slots of $1,000, each held as is between decisions;
  - a sold slot's money buys the replacement in the same slot;
  - parked money (cash in families 1–2, which never happened in practice; SPY in family 3) is pooled across parked slots;
  - costs are charged per side on the slot's value: stocks 20 / 5 / 0 bps, SPY 1 bp (0 at the 0-bps level);
  - nothing is sold at the end.
- **Returns:** the grader's: close[d+1]/close[d] − 1, with a missing close a 0 return. `div_fix` is added to the daily log return from 2022-07-01 on (it is 0 before). SPY is Yahoo's adjusted close on export days.
- **Scored set and buys:** the scored set is the year's universe with a finite score that day. A holding that leaves it (no score, or a new year's universe without it) is sold. Buys in families 1–2 need a score above 0, stockai's "top 10 positive scores"; this never bound.
- **Family 1 (buffer):** ranks over the whole scored set; empty slots are filled best-first.
- **Family 2 (switch):**
  - empty slots and those of unscored holdings are filled with the best candidates, with no threshold, since there is no sale to pay for;
  - then holdings are sorted by G_h ascending (ties: lower score first) and paired in turn with the best unheld candidates;
  - a pair swaps while G_h(j) − G_h(i) > (1 + m) × 40 bps, and the swapping stops at the first pair that fails.
- **Family 3 (confident):**
  - a holding is sold when G_h < 0 or it loses its score;
  - qualifying candidates (G_h > c × 40 bps, best first) go first into the freed slots, then into SPY slots;
  - a freed slot with no candidate goes to SPY;
  - at the start, slots without a qualifying stock buy SPY.
- **Thresholds:** fixed at 20 bps (a 40-bp round trip) at every reporting cost, so the 0-, 5- and 20-bps accounts make the same trades.
- **Calibration:**
  - percentile = average rank in the day's scored set;
  - market-relative return = the stock's simple return over days (d + 5(k−1), d + 5k] minus the scored set's equal-weight mean over the same days;
  - fitted on every selection day (1,763, not only the 350 decision days) whose window ends by 2017-06-27;
  - 1,000 percentile bins, weighted pool-adjacent-violators, non-decreasing;
  - G_h = g_1 + … + g_h. The daily signal uses day-k windows, k = 1..5.
- **Diagnostic:** Spearman IC on the 350 graded decision days (the daily signal: every day). Its t comes from the daily IC series.
- **The null:**
  - follows the plan's text: uniformly random sales among the account's stocks (holdings that lost their score go first) and uniformly random purchases from the day's scored stocks (any score, not held, not just sold);
  - matches each week's counts of stock-for-stock, stock-to-park and park-to-stock moves, which also matches family 3's stock/SPY split;
  - 1,000 accounts, seed 20261021; p = the share of random finals at or above the strategy's;
  - a slot-shadowing null was added as a robustness row on the advisor's suggestion, before any result.
- **Test 2:** each week's account return at 20 bps (decision to next decision, longer stub weeks included as they are) minus the scored set's equal-weight mean return over the same days, with t over the 350 weeks.
- **The number:** compound yearly return (252-day years) of the selected account minus that of the median random final, at 0 bps. Mean-of-yearly-returns figures are reported alongside.
- **500 largest:**
  - each decision day, the 500 scored stocks with the largest median dollar volume over the 63 days through d;
  - G uses each stock's percentile in the full scored set (the frozen calibration's scale);
  - family 1 ranks within the 500, holdings that leave the set are sold, and random accounts draw from it;
  - the primary signal only.
- **Secondary signals:** the daily LightGBM decides every grader decision day (h0−1..t−2 of each year, so the test span ends 2024-06-27), with h ∈ {1, 2, 5} days; the reversal uses the weekly calendar.
- **Ties in the selection** went to the first setting listed:
  - weekly confident h = 1 vs h = 2 at c = 1 (identical trades);
  - the daily confident settings that only hold SPY.
- **The round's sanity check** (5-day reversal through the c2eval judge path) does not apply: nothing here goes through `c2eval.judge`, and the wiring check reproduces stockai's own grader exactly instead.

**After seeing results:**
- **The accounting bridge** (`bridge.py`) was added after the selection-year baseline came out at $8,224 against the grader's $11,945, to see why. It is a diagnostic only; no rule changed.
- **The coordinator's "after costs" number** was requested mid-run (before any test-year result) and added without changing anything else. It is the mean over the 7 June–June years of the account's yearly return at 5 bps minus SPY's total return over the same span. Its column on the tie-break robustness rows was added after the test results.
- **The seed-4 baseline:** after seeing its test-year $74,507, I traced it to one slot's compounding (no data error) and report the tie-break spread next to it.
- **Engine checks:** the engine was checked independently on the selection years. The 0- and 20-bps baseline match a per-slot recomputation to the cent, and the K = 10 holdings equal the grader's top 10 in all 350 weeks. No engine change was made after any result.
