# Amihud illiquidity: do stocks whose price moves more per dollar traded earn more?

**TL;DR:** Do stocks whose price moves more per dollar traded earn more over the next month, as Amihud (2002) found? Not here once dollar volume and volatility are held fixed: those two make up 98–99% of the measure, and what is left had t 0.42 among the judge's liquid stocks and t 1.34 with no trading floor over 2008–2019 (t 2 or more was needed). Buying the 20 least liquid of the liquid stocks beat SPY by 3.7 points a year after costs, but only through 2008-09 and 2020-21, on survivor-only data; the median year lost 2.2 points. Verdict: no edge.

## Why this is hard to make meaningful here

Five things work against a clean test. They are stated before any data is seen, so that a null result isn't explained away afterwards, and a positive one isn't oversold.

1. **The premium lives where this data is thinnest.**
   - The judge's candidates are not the 500 most-traded stocks. They are its liquid set: 90% of closes over 756 days, median close ≥ $5 and median dollar volume ≥ $5M over the last year. That was 301 stocks in 2007-08, 865 in 2009-10 and 2,036 in 2024-25 (from `-3.6`'s saved judge output).
   - That is roughly the more-traded half of the stocks above $5. Every published replication finds the premium concentrated in the smallest, least-traded stocks (Ben-Rephael, Kadan & Wohl 2015). The $5M floor cuts most of them out.
   - The judge's top 20 by ILLIQ are therefore the 20 thinnest names just above the floor. They are the slice most flattered by survivor-only data and the most expensive to trade.
2. **There is no market capitalization.** Stooq has no shares outstanding. ILLIQ is roughly volatility ÷ dollar volume, and dollar volume is also the only size measure here. So "illiquidity beyond size and volatility" leaves little of ILLIQ to test. Amihud's own size control was market cap (correlation −0.61 with ILLIQ), so his ILLIQ kept a turnover part that this data can't separate from size. The controlled test here is harsher than his.
3. **Survivor-only data flatters exactly this effect.** The export holds only companies still listed in 2025. Illiquid stocks delist more often, and those that failed are missing with their losses. That pushes the illiquid leg up, in the direction of the claim. It is worst in the wide universe.
4. **5 bps a side understates what illiquid stocks cost.** Amihud and Mendelson's theory is that the premium *is* pay for trading costs. So a premium measured at 5 bps a side may be exactly the cost the project doesn't charge. The cost audit's cost rule is reported beside it.
5. **The published record since 2002 is flat.** Chen & Zimmermann's survivor-free replication of Amihud's sort earned +0.39% a month in 1964–1997 (t 2.82) and +0.04% a month after publication (t 0.34). See the background check in Decisions. The prior for 2008–2019 is about zero.

## The claim

As listed on the page: "Stocks whose price moves more per dollar traded earn more over the next month, as pay for bearing that risk."

Amihud (2002), read in the paper (author's PDF, 10 pages):
- **The measure:** ILLIQ, the average over a year's days of |daily return| ÷ dollar volume: how far a dollar of trading moves the price.
- **Cross-section:** NYSE stocks, 1964–1997, monthly Fama–MacBeth regressions on characteristics from the previous year. Sample rules: more than 200 days of return and volume data in the year, price above $5 at year end, market cap on CRSP, and the top and bottom 1% of ILLIQ dropped (1,061 to 2,291 stocks).
  - ILLIQMA (ILLIQ ÷ its cross-sectional mean) with beta and past returns: coefficient 0.162, t 6.55.
  - Adding log size, the daily return's standard deviation (SDRET) and dividend yield: 0.112, t 5.39. Both halves of the sample, and outside January, are significant.
  - SDRET's coefficient is negative (−0.179, t −1.90), so volatility pulls the other way from ILLIQ's numerator.
- **Time series:** the log of market illiquidity (the mean of ILLIQ across stocks) follows an AR(1).
  - Expected illiquidity raises the next year's excess return on the equal-weighted NYSE market (annual: 10.2, t 2.68; monthly: 0.712, t 2.50).
  - Unexpected illiquidity lowers the same period's return (annual −23.6, t −4.52).
  - The effect fades with size: for the largest size decile the expected-illiquidity coefficient is 0.447 (t 0.13) annually and 0.319 (t 1.35) monthly. SPY is a large-stock fund, so the paper itself predicts little for it.

## What the project already measured

- **The measure itself.** The project has used Amihud's ratio before, as a cost of trading: the mean of |r| ÷ dollar volume over the 252 trading days ending at the decision day, at least 63 such days (its `amihud_panel`). Its cost rule is max(5, 5 × √(the stock's Amihud ÷ the median of the 500 most-traded)) bps a side.
- **Size and volatility in the liquid set, 2006–2019** (`-3.6-max-lottery.md`, Fama–MacBeth with percentile ranks):
  - the size proxy, log 63-day median dollar volume: −0.25 (t −1.25), so less-traded stocks earned a little more, not significantly;
  - idiosyncratic volatility alone: +0.29 (t 0.57), so no low-volatility premium there to offset.
- **Low-risk effects come from small stocks.** Betting against beta tracks AQR's series but earns nothing on the liquid stocks here (`-4.4-betting-against-beta.md`), as Novy-Marx & Velikov argue. Betting against correlation is mostly beta and low volatility (`-5.5`).
- **Survivorship.** The monthly top-10 picks (`+18.6`) rest on survivor-only data. `research/survivorship_stress` fitted a hazard model of which liquid stocks vanished between the two stores. Only low price (z −3.6) and low dollar volume (z −2.2) predicted it, which are the marks of the illiquid leg.

## Rules (proposed; fixed before any result is seen)

### Timeline and leakage

- **Calendar:** formations on the fixed 21-day grid (export day 252 + 21k, `c2eval.twins`' calendar), as in `-3.6`.
- **Decision and trade:** every signal and control at grid day t uses data through the close of t. Trades go at the close of t + 1 (the judge's `delay=1`). The holding period runs from the close of t + 1 to the close of t + 22.
- **Periods, by a formation's first return day (t + 2):**
  - development 2008-01-01..2019-12-31, the pass test;
  - check 2020-01-01..2025-06-27, complete periods only, a consistency check;
  - the unseen months, first return day ≥ 2025-06-30 on the new export, run once at the end;
  - 2006–2007 is computed and reported, not tested. The liquid set had only about 300 stocks then.

### The measure (ILLIQ)

The same formula as the earlier test, with three changes, each for a reason given.

- **Daily ratio:** |r_d| ÷ DV_d, with r_d = C_d ÷ C_{d−1} − 1 from the export's closes and DV_d the export's dollar volume on day d.
- **A day counts** if both closes exist and DV_d > 0 (as in the earlier test), and:
  - **bad print:** |log(C_d ÷ C_{d−1})| ≤ 0.5, the project's rule. *New:* one bad print can dominate a mean of ratios.
  - **broken volume:** DV_d ≥ 1% of the stock's median positive dollar volume over d − 63..d − 1, when that median has at least 50 days; otherwise no screen. *New:* the new store has a few dozen shares on 2026-03-03 for about 700 stocks. Unscreened, that one day would inflate their 252-day mean for a year, covering most of the unseen months.
- **ILLIQ at t:** the mean ratio over the counted days in t − 251..t, with **at least 200 counted days**. *Changed from the unscreened variant's 63:* 200 is Amihud's rule (more than 200 days in the year), and a sort needs a full-year estimate. The unscreened variant needed coverage for a cost estimate, not a signal.
- **Its two parts, over the same counted days:**
  - DV252: the median dollar volume;
  - σ252: the standard deviation (ddof 1) of the daily log returns, Amihud's SDRET.
- **Checks:**
  - with both screens off and the minimum at 63 days, the panel must equal `picks.amihud_panel` exactly;
  - The unscreened 63-day definition is reported as a variant.

### Universes

At each grid day, before any ILLIQ value is looked at:
- **U_J, the judge's liquid set:** 90% of closes over the 756 days ending at t; median close ≥ $5 and median dollar volume ≥ $5M over the last 252 days; minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)`. This is `-3.6`'s universe (copied from `research/night_and_day`), the judge's own rule recomputed on the grid.
- **U_W, Amihud's filters:** the same rules without the dollar-volume floor. Amihud had no volume floor; his liquidity floor was the 200-day rule, which ILLIQ's 200 counted days (each with positive volume) reproduce.
  - So the only difference between U_J and U_W is the band below $5M a day.
  - Row counts at six dates, before fund-like stocks are removed: U_W would hold about 1,730 stocks on 2008-01-02, 3,085 on 2019-12-31 and 4,286 on 2024-06-28, against 987, 1,687 and 2,187 for U_J. On 2006-06-30 both hold about 300.
- **Both:** a stock needs ILLIQ, DV252, σ252 and every Fama–MacBeth control. Then **Amihud's 1% rule:** stocks whose ILLIQ is in the top or bottom 1% of the universe that day are dropped, for every test and the judge scores.
- **The 500 largest** (reported): the top 500 of U_J by 63-day median dollar volume, the shared definition.

### P1 and P2 (primary). Is illiquidity priced beyond size and volatility?

One statistic per universe: P1 on U_J, P2 on U_W.

- **Fama–MacBeth, at each development formation:**
  - **Dependent variable:** the stock's return from the close of t + 1 to the close of t + 22 (forward-filled closes), minus the universe's equal-weight mean.
  - **Regressors:** percentile ranks in [0, 1] within the formation's sample (`-3.6`'s convention; it also makes Amihud's mean adjustment unnecessary):
    - ILLIQ;
    - log DV252, the size proxy. It uses the same window and days as ILLIQ, so it removes the level of ILLIQ's denominator exactly; the 63-day version only defines the 500 largest;
    - σ252, the volatility control;
    - beta: the OLS slope of daily log returns on SPY's (Yahoo adjusted close) over t − 251..t, at least 200 days;
    - the 21-day return, P_t ÷ P_{t−21} − 1, for short-term reversal;
    - 12-1 momentum, P_{t−21} ÷ P_{t−252} − 1.
  - Amihud's dividend yield is left out: there are no dividends in this data before 2022.
  - **The statistic:** the mean of ILLIQ's coefficient over the development formations, with a Newey–West t at 4 lags (`-3.6`'s, about 4(n/100)^(2/9) for 143 formations).
  - A coefficient is the 21-day return gap between the most and the least illiquid stock, the other ranks held fixed.
- **Bootstrap:** the coefficient series is resampled in moving blocks of 3 formations (63 trading days), 10,000 draws, centred on zero. The one-sided p is the share of means at or above the observed one.
  - The brief's 21-day block would be one observation here, an iid bootstrap. Three periods keep month-to-month dependence: the 252-day signal changes slowly, and volatility clusters.
  - The 1-period (iid) bootstrap is reported beside it.
- **The gate: the controlled book.** The portfolio version of the same control, net of costs:
  - e = the residual of log ILLIQ from an OLS across the formation's sample on 1, x, y, x², y² and xy, where x = log DV252 and y = log σ252. A quadratic surface, so that a curved size or volatility effect can't leak into e.
  - Long the top fifth by e, short the bottom fifth, equal weight, bought at the close of t + 1 and held to t + 22 with prices drifting.
  - Costs: 5 bps × turnover on both legs, turnover against the previous legs as they drifted.
  - Reported with its Newey–West t (4 lags), FF5 + UMD alpha, and the legs' mean log DV252 and log σ252 (they should match).
- **Pass, for each primary:**
  - Holm across P1 and P2 at a one-sided family level of 0.0228 (t = 2). The smaller p must be below 0.0114 (t ≥ 2.28), the other below 0.0228 (t ≥ 2.00). The p's come from the Newey–West t's;
  - the bootstrap p below the same level;
  - its controlled book's mean net return over development above zero.
- **Reading a pass:**
  - **P1 passes:** a premium for illiquidity beyond size and volatility in the liquid set. It is still survivor-flattered. If the survivorship haircut below takes P1's t under 2, the verdict says "survivor-bound" instead.
  - **P2 passes and P1 fails:** the effect shows only where survivorship flatters it most. That is a reason for a rerun on data with delisted stocks (Sharadar, `stockai/docs/sharadar.md`), not an edge.
  - **Both fail:** no illiquidity premium beyond size and volatility in these stocks over 2008–2019. It doesn't test Amihud's turnover component, which needs market cap, nor microcaps or the years before 2002.

### The bar

- **t ≥ 2 rather than the brief's 3.** The brief's t ≥ 3 is for new ideas. This is a published effect, and the project's rule for those is t ≥ 2 ("Published strategies (the fourth round)" in `experiments/README.md`), because a published result is the prior. The whole test window falls after the paper's sample.
- Amihud's own Table 2 controls for size and volatility (ILLIQMA t 5.39 with ln SIZE and SDRET), so the published prior covers the controlled test.
- **Flagged for review:** survivor-only data favours this effect, which is an argument for the stricter bar. Brandon's session may set t ≥ 3 before the run. The Newey–West t's are reported, so either reading can be checked.
- **No grid of candidates,** so no Romano–Wolf. Every variant below is reported, not a candidate. A variant that passes when its primary doesn't is not a pass. It would become the primary of a new test, judged on forward data only.

### Reported. Taking the published sort apart

Same universes, periods and mechanics as the controlled book (fifths, equal weight, net of 5 bps, Newey–West t, FF5 + UMD alpha):
- **R0, the published sort:** top fifth of raw ILLIQ minus bottom fifth (Chen & Zimmermann's form, close to Amihud's).
- **R1, dollar volume:** the lowest DV252 fifth minus the highest (Brennan, Chordia & Subrahmanyam's dollar-volume effect, the size proxy here).
- **R2, volatility:** the highest σ252 fifth minus the lowest, the low-volatility anomaly's mirror.
- **R3, ILLIQ beyond dollar volume only:** the residual on 1, x, x².
- **R4, ILLIQ beyond volatility only:** the residual on 1, y, y².
- **R5, 25 cells:** dollar-volume fifths, then volatility fifths within each, then ILLIQ thirds within each of the 25 cells. Long every cell's top third, short every bottom third.
- **Spanning:** the controlled book's period returns regressed on R1 and R2. Loadings near zero say the control worked, and the intercept is the controlled premium.
- **Fama–MacBeth variants:**
  - ILLIQ alone; ILLIQ with DV252 and σ252 only; all controls but ILLIQ;
  - Amihud's own form: ILLIQMA (ILLIQ ÷ the cross-sectional mean) and raw controls (log DV252, σ252, beta, the two returns), each winsorized at the 1st and 99th percentiles, to compare with his 0.112.
- **How much of ILLIQ is left to test:** the mean cross-sectional R² of ILLIQ's rank on DV252's and σ252's ranks, and of log ILLIQ on the quadratic surface.
- **Robustness of the primary and the controlled book:** the 500 largest; tenths instead of fifths; legs weighted by DV252 (no market cap, so this stands in for value weight); January periods (at least 11 January days) against the rest; the unscreened 63-day ILLIQ; 2006–2007; without 2008–2009.

### Survivorship haircut (reported)

- **The model:** `research/survivorship_stress`'s fitted hazard model (`out/part1.json`, `model`), applied read-only through that folder's `features_col`, `predict_p` and `monthly_hazard`. Its features at the formation day: 12-month return, collapse flag, 63-day volatility, log close and log 63-day median dollar volume.
- **The haircut:** each stock's period return becomes (1 − h) r + h L, with the monthly hazard h, k = 1 and L = −55% (Shumway & Warther's Nasdaq performance-delisting return).
- **Reported:**
  - P1's and P2's Fama–MacBeth t's, with the haircut on every stock's forward return (a coefficient has no legs);
  - the controlled book and R0, once with the haircut on every stock and once on the long leg only (the pessimistic bound).
- **Its limits:**
  - the model was fitted on the $5M set in the calm 2025–26 window, so it is an extrapolation for U_W's band and for 2008;
  - L is an assumption, not an estimate: failures leave no price in Stooq.

### Costs of trading (reported)

- The square-root rule per stock, max(5, 5 × √(ILLIQ ÷ the median ILLIQ of the 500 most-traded that day)) bps a side.
- Applied to every book's trades and to the judge account's trades, beside the 5-bps numbers.

### S1 (reported only). Does expected market illiquidity raise future market returns?

- **Market illiquidity,** as Amihud's monthly version: for grid period m (the 21 days ending at t_m), each stock's mean daily ratio over the period (at least 15 counted days), for the 500 most-traded stocks at the previous grid day. Then the cross-sectional mean after dropping the top 1% (his AILLIQ rule). L_m is its log.
  - The 500 most-traded keep a fixed count. U_J, which grows from 300 to 2,250 stocks, is a reported variant.
- **Returns:** SPY's (Yahoo adjusted close) and the equal-weight mean of U_J's stocks over the next period, minus the risk-free rate.
- **Amihud's form:** next-period excess return on L_m, the contemporaneous AR(1) residual U_{m+1} and a January dummy, with the AR(1) fitted in-sample over the period described, as he did. He predicts a positive coefficient on L_m and a negative one on U.
- **The trading form:** next-period excess return on D_m = L_m minus its mean over the previous 12 periods, with the log of SPY's realized variance over the last 21 days as a control (`-3.2`'s finding that volatility timing works).
- Newey–West t's at 4 lags, for development, check and unseen periods.
- **Why it is reported only:**
  - dollar volume grew many times over 2006–2025, so L_m trends down, and a regression on a trending level over about 143 periods is a spurious-regression trap. D_m removes the trend, but it is this plan's transform, not Amihud's;
  - a timing rule would have to add to volatility-timed SPY (`-3.2`) to count;
  - the paper's own result is weak for large stocks.
- **Reading:** "supports Amihud's H-1" if D_m's coefficient on the equal-weight market is positive with t ≥ 2 and survives the volatility control. Even then, it would need its own timing test on forward data.

### The money test (the judge)

- **Account A, the file-name number:** daily scores = ILLIQ's percentile within U_J of the latest grid day (NaN outside U_J and for the 1% tails). The judge buys the 20 most illiquid after the 1% trim, so it starts at roughly the 11th–20th most illiquid stock.
  - Graded with `rebalance=21`, `delay=1`, walk-forward on the old export, costs 0, 5 and 20 bps, uniform and volatility-matched random accounts.
  - Illiquid stocks are volatile, so the volatility-matched line is the one that means something.
  - This is the published signal's long-only version, as `-3.6` graded MAX itself. The controlled test is P1–P2.
- **Account B (reported):** the controlled residual e's percentile, graded the same way.
- **No judge number for U_W:** the judge's own candidate rule (`liquid_universe`) has the $5M floor built in, so P2's universe can't be graded by it. P2's money result is its books, net of 5 bps and of the square-root rule's costs.
- **Numbers:**
  - **improvement over random:** account A's `c2eval.change_over_random` at 0 bps, uniform;
  - **after costs:** `ac.judge_after_cost` on account A's JSON at 5 bps;
  - account A's square-root cost of trading, the median Amihud ratio of its trades mapped by the square-root rule, and its after-cost number at that cost, by regrading at that cost.
- **Delay:** every judge file used as a template ran at `delay=0`. This plan uses `delay=1`, as the brief says; a 252-day signal barely moves in a day. Account A at `delay=0` is reported for comparison with `-3.6` and `-7.4`.
- **Plumbing check:** 5-day reversal through this folder's scores and judge path at `delay=0` must give +11.8 points a year, as in `+5.4-reversal-control.md`.

### Data rules

- **Forward returns:** from 2022-07, `pubdata.div_fix` is added (primary), with the uncorrected numbers beside them. `div_fix` covers the 3,066 tickers of the judge-like set from 2022. U_W's extra stocks may lack it; the share covered is reported.
- **The split check, before any result:** if Stooq adjusts closes for splits but not volume, pre-split dollar volume would be understated by the split ratio, and ILLIQ inflated. For 20 known splits (from an earlier test's list, AAPL 2014-06-09 .. ORLY 2025-06-10), the 20-day median dollar volume before and after the split day must not jump by the split ratio. If it fails, the run stops and the fix goes in Decisions before any ILLIQ is used.
- **Dividends in dollar volume:**
  - the export's closes are back-adjusted for dividends through about 2022-11, and volume isn't, so a dividend payer's dollar volume is understated in early years. A stock yielding 3% a year reads about a third low in 2008;
  - that inflates the raw ILLIQ of high payers, which are mostly calm stocks;
  - in P1–P2 the same factor sits in the DV252 control, so it largely cancels; in R0 and account A it doesn't;
  - after 2022-12 closes are raw, so dollar volume is right.
- **Inherited look-aheads** (`experiments/README.md`):
  - the $5 rule reads split-adjusted closes, so a stock that later reverse-split looks above $5 earlier. Those are mostly struggling small stocks that survived, so this adds illiquid winners;
  - correlation2's bad-print repair looks one day ahead. Both are small and shared with every experiment.

### Check period, unseen months and forward confirmation

- **Check, 2020-01..2025-06:** each primary's coefficient and controlled book, with 95% intervals and by year. "Consistent" means the same sign as development, and also p < 0.05 one-sided to be called supportive. It is not an independent confirmation: other experiments read these years.
- **The 16 unseen months (2025-06-30..2026-10-19):** run once at the end with frozen code on `EXPORT_NEW` (and `STORE_NEW` for the split check). Everything is rebuilt, not spliced.
  - First, check that the new export's first 5,911 days of closes and dollar volume equal the old export's.
  - Then report the number of broken-volume days the screen removed.
  - About 15 formations: reported, never confirming.
- **Freeze:** sha256 hashes of every script before the unseen run, in this file and in `research/amihud_illiquidity/frozen_sha256.txt`. No commit or tag; none was asked for.
- **Forward:** `forward.py` runs each later download through the frozen code. It keeps first-seen values and logs missed downloads as gaps, never filled later. Report at 12 months; judge P1 and P2 at 36 formations.

## What the results can and can't show

- **A pass in P1** says illiquidity beyond dollar volume and volatility earned a premium among liquid survivors in 2008–2019. With survivorship running in its favour, it still needs delisted data before it counts as an edge.
- **R0 positive, P1–P2 not:** the published sort here is the dollar-volume effect or volatility, not price impact. That is Lou & Shu's (2017) finding: Amihud's measure is priced through its volume part.
- **A fail** rejects the controlled premium on liquid survivors since 2008. It doesn't reject Amihud's 1964–1997 result, his turnover component (this data has no market cap), or a premium among microcaps and stocks below $5.
- **The judge account** is a long-only bet on the 20 thinnest stocks above $5M. It says what that bet earned at 5 bps on survivor data, not whether illiquidity is priced.
- **S1** can at most describe. It can't pass or fail here.

## Ways it can fool us

- **Size and volatility in disguise.** ILLIQ is about σ ÷ DV. A raw sort mixes the dollar-volume effect (pushing illiquid up) with the low-volatility anomaly (pushing it down). Hence the controls, the quadratic residual, the spanning regression and R0–R5.
- **Survivors only.** Delisted illiquid losers are missing, which flatters the illiquid leg, most of all in U_W and in account A. Hence the haircut, the reading rule for P2, and the reported legs.
  - In the controlled test the legs have matched dollar volume and volatility, which the hazard model says drive disappearance, so the bias there should be smaller. But it isn't zero if failing firms also trade erratically.
- **Costs.** The premium may be pay for costs this project doesn't charge. Hence the square-root rule beside every 5-bps number.
- **Broken data.** A mean of ratios is dominated by its largest terms: one bad print, a near-zero volume day, or the 2026-03-03 volumes. Hence the two screens, the 1% trim, and counts of every screened day.
- **Bid-ask bounce.** Illiquid closes bounce between bid and ask. Equal-weight, rebalanced returns are biased up by bounce (Blume & Stambaugh 1983). The legs here are bought and held for 21 days, so the bias falls only on the formation day.
- **Too little left to test.** If ILLIQ's rank is nearly a function of DV252's and σ252's, its controlled coefficient is noisy whatever the truth. The reported R² shows how much variation is left.
- **A few years.** 2008–2009 and 2020 hold the big swings in small-stock returns. Results are reported by year and without 2008–2009.
- **January.** Small-stock premiums concentrate in January. Amihud's held outside it; here January is reported separately.
- **Looking twice.** Every rule, variant and threshold is fixed here. Anything decided during the run goes in Decisions, dated, and the Rules aren't edited after the snapshot.

## Decisions

**Background and novelty check (2026-10-25, web searches and published summaries read before any data; it changed no rules):** not new. This is a replication on new data with a stricter control.
- **The paper,** read in full (the author's PDF): the numbers in "The claim" come from its Tables 1–4 and its text.
- **Chen & Zimmermann, Open Source Asset Pricing**, signal `Illiquidity` (NYSE, price > $5, equal-weighted fifths, CRSP with delisted stocks), from their `PredictorSummary2024.xlsx` (github.com/OpenSourceAP/CrossSection):
  - in sample, 1964–1997: +0.39% a month, t 2.82 (408 months);
  - 1998–2002: −0.04%, t −0.08;
  - after publication (252 months): +0.04%, t 0.34.
  - Their `DolVol` (Brennan, Chordia & Subrahmanyam 1998, dollar volume, the size proxy here): +0.75% in sample (t 2.73), +0.41% after publication (t 1.93).
  - After publication their most liquid fifth earned 0.88% a month and their least liquid 0.93%, with no steady rise between (1.04%, 0.98%, 0.90%).
- **Harris & Amato (2019, *Critical Finance Review* 8, 173–202):** reproduce Amihud's results on current CRSP data, and find a much weaker relation on newer data. Amihud's measure does no better than simpler ones (abstract, via RePEc).
- **Drienko, Smith & von Reibnitz (2019, *CFR* 8, 127–171):** the time-series results replicate in sample but largely lose significance out of sample. They find a change point after publication, and the results depend on scaling the numerator by dollar volume (abstract).
- **Ben-Rephael, Kadan & Wohl (2015, *JFQA* 50, 197–229):** the characteristic liquidity premium fell over four decades and is now priced only among the smallest stocks (abstract). Secondary summaries add "not among NYSE and AMEX stocks"; **unverified** in the article.
- **Lou & Shu (2017, *RFS* 30, 4481):** the Amihud premium comes from its trading-volume component, not the return-to-volume construction meant to capture price impact (abstract). This is why P1–P2 control for dollar volume.
- **Amihud & Noh (2021, *RFS* 34, 2101–2123):** argue Lou & Shu's split misses an illiquidity component that is priced, and that the premium stays positive after controls for mispricing, sentiment and seasonality (abstract). Their numbers are **unverified**.
- **Hou, Xue & Zhang (2020, *RFS*):** with NYSE breakpoints and value weights, 96% of trading-friction anomalies fail t 1.96. Their working paper lists Amihud's measure among them (abstract; the table is **unverified**).
- **Amihud (2019, *CFR*, a revisit):** a search summary says his illiquidity factor stays positive and significant after 2002, though smaller. **Unverified**; the paper wasn't opened.
- **Not found:** a test of the Amihud premium that controls for dollar volume and volatility together, on data without market cap.

U_{k+1} is that model's residual. The excess return over period k + 1 is regressed on L_k, U_{k+1} and the January dummy of period k + 1;
  - **the trading form:** D_k = L_k minus the mean of L over the previous 12 periods, alone and with log RV_k (the log of the sum of SPY's squared daily log returns over t − 20..t);
  - Newey–West t's at 4 lags.

## Results (run 2026-10-25)

**What this tests.** Whether Amihud's illiquidity, the average |daily return| ÷ dollar volume over a year, predicts next month's return once dollar volume and volatility are held fixed. Here those two make up almost all of it. P1 tests the judge's liquid set ($5M a day and up) and P2 Amihud's own filters with no dollar-volume floor. Both use survivor-only Stooq data, 2008–2019 for the pass test.

**The tests, in order:**
1. **The data checks pass.**
   - The split check: dollar volume is continuous across 20 of 20 known splits.
   - Both panel equality checks hold.
   - Of ILLIQ's cross-sectional variation, 98.4% (P1) and 99.3% (P2) is explained by the ranks of dollar volume and volatility. On the log quadratic surface, 99.3% and 99.5%.
2. **P1 and P2 (primary): both fail.**
   - Over 2008–2019, ILLIQ's controlled coefficient was +0.92% per 21 days in P1, t 0.42 (needed 2.0 under Holm), and +4.43% in P2, t 1.34 (needed 2.28 as the smaller p, then 2.0). Holm passes neither.
   - Both gates hold (the controlled books' net means are positive), which doesn't matter once Holm fails. A t ≥ 3 reading fails too.
3. **The decomposition (reported).** The published sort earned +0.32% a period net in the liquid set (t 1.39). That is mostly the dollar-volume side of ILLIQ, as Lou & Shu found, with a little left in the controlled residual (+0.08%, t 0.28). With no dollar-volume floor, the most illiquid fifth *trailed* the most liquid: −0.19% (t −0.77) over 2008–2019 and −1.43% (t −1.97) over 2020–2025-06, despite survivor-only data.
4. **S1 (reported only).** Expected market illiquidity doesn't raise the next month's return over 2008–2019 (SPY −0.49, t −0.50; equal-weight +0.46, t 0.33). Unexpected illiquidity lowers the same month's return, as Amihud found (t −3.8 and −2.9).
5. **The money test.** The judge account that buys the 20 most illiquid liquid stocks beat SPY by **+3.7 points a year after costs** and random accounts by +5.4 before costs. The median year was −2.2 points, and without 2020-21 the average is −2.1. At its own cost of trading under the square-root rule, 34 bps a side, the margin is +1.8.

6. **The unseen months (reported).** P1 +4.96 (t 0.58) and P2 +1.43 (t 0.14) over 15 formations. Account A made +60.2% against SPY's +26.3%, in a speculative small-cap rally like 2020-21's.

The verdict stops at test 2. Every number was computed anyway.

### A data caveat found by other runs after this one: no dividends before about 2011

Other runs found that the store's dividend adjustment covers only about February 2011 to December 2022 (checked against Yahoo on 24 dividend payers and 23 funds). Before about 2011-02, as after 2022, the closes are price-only for stocks and funds alike. The plan's "Data rules" assumed the adjustment ran from the start to late 2022. Nothing was rerun. What it does here, estimated without new computation:
- **Forward returns in 2008-01..2011-01** (about 36 of the 144 development formations) miss dividends. High-yield stocks lose about their yield ÷ 12 a period there.
- **P1 and P2 (the primaries):**
  - a rank coefficient can pick this up only through ILLIQ's link to dividend yield beyond the controls. Even a perfect link, with a 0–5% yield spread, moves a 36-period stretch by at most about 0.4% a period, and so the 144-period mean by at most about 0.1%;
  - P1's coefficient has a standard error of about 2.2% and P2's about 3.3% (the mean ÷ its t), so the bias can't change either verdict. Its sign isn't known without yields.
- **R0 and R1, the raw sorts:** their short legs hold the larger, calmer and higher-yielding stocks, so missing dividends flatter them in 2008–2010. With a yield gap of 1–2% a year, that is roughly +0.03% a period on 2008–2019's mean (+0.32% for P1's R0). Small, but the bias runs toward the published sort.
- **The dollar-volume control:** before 2011 the export's dollar volume is understated by the 2011–2022 adjustment factor (not 2008–2022's), so the dividend bias in raw ILLIQ is somewhat smaller than "Data rules" said. In P1–P2 it sits in the DV252 control as well.
- **Account A against SPY:**
  - in judge years 2007-08..2010-11, the picks are price-only while the Yahoo SPY benchmark includes dividends;
  - with the picks yielding perhaps 1–2% a year, the after-cost number (+3.7) is understated by roughly 0.2–0.4 points a year on the 18-year mean;
  - the random accounts are price-only too, so the change over random moves only by the yield gap between the picks and the random accounts: a little in the picks' favour, since thin, volatile stocks yield less.
- **S1:** SPY's returns are Yahoo's, with dividends. The equal-weight market's returns are price-only in 2008–2010, lower by about its yield ÷ 12 a period. That is close to a level shift, which goes into the intercept rather than the slopes.

### Files

Code in `research/amihud_illiquidity/` (numpy only, system `python3 -I -B`, each under `systemd-run --user --scope -p MemoryMax=5G` with 4 threads). Run in this order:
- `check_splits.py old|new` → `out/check_splits_<tag>.txt`: dollar volume across 20 known splits.
- `panels.py old|new`: the ILLIQ panels and their checks, the screens, and the grid formations → `out/panels_<tag>.txt` (about 1 minute).
- `tests.py old|new`: P1, P2, the books, Fama–MacBeth, spanning, robustness and the haircut → `out/results_<tag>.txt`, `.json` (seconds).
- `timeseries.py old|new`: S1 → `out/timeseries_<tag>.txt`, `.json`.
- `money.py scores|judge|costs old|new`: accounts A and B, the judge runs one at a time, the year table, the rebuilt trades and the cost of trading → `out/money_<tag>.txt`, `.json`, `out/money_years_old.txt`, `out/money_costs_<tag>.json`, `out/picks_A_<tag>.txt`, `out/picks_B_<tag>.txt`.
- `sanity_reversal.py` → `out/sanity_reversal.txt`.
- `unseen.py`: the hashes, the overlap check and every script on the new export, once → `out/unseen.txt` and every `*_new` output.
- `forward.py EXPORT_DIR STORE_DIR`: later downloads → `out/forward_ledger.json`, `out/forward_report.txt`.
- `common.py`: the rules as constants and the shared helpers.
