# Gravity: do correlated stocks pull harder as they get closer?

**TL;DR:** Brandon asks whether correlations work better as gravitational attractors than as fixed offsets: a related stock pulls harder the closer the other stock gets, in price, in time, or in both. On the 500 most-traded US stocks in 2008–2019, with each stock's 10 most-correlated stocks as attractors, none of 30 force laws beat the old versions. The best price law added nothing to pairs trading's spring and plain reversal (t 0.82), the best time kernel nothing to the sharp offset (t 1.85), and the two together did worse than price alone (t −0.93). Measured directly, the pull grows with distance, as a spring's does, and random peers show it as strongly as real ones; the time kernel has no peak at the offset. The best long-short book broke even after 5 bps a side, and the 20-stock account made 16.7 points a year less than SPY. Verdict: no edge; the test stops at the first stage, price.

## The claim

Brandon's words: "How about if we considered the correlations as gravitational attractors instead of just offsets, so they start pulling on future data gradually as it approaches. Use all available data (not just closing data). Try multiple gravitational scaling factors." Asked what "approaches" means, he chose all three, in this order: price, then time, then both.

- **Offsets, the project's view so far:** stock A moves first, and stock B follows exactly N days later (the census, unequal move lengths), or B's price is pulled back toward A's in proportion to how far apart they are (pairs trading, twin reversal). That second rule is a spring: the pull is strongest when the gap is widest.
- **Gravity, the claim:** a correlated stock is a mass that pulls on B, and the pull grows as B gets closer to it. In price, the pull grows as B's price nears where A says it should be. In time, A's move pulls on B more and more as the day N days after it approaches, instead of acting on that day alone.
- **Scaling factors:** how fast the pull grows with closeness (the exponent p in price, a in time) and how close counts as "at" the attractor (the softening length ε).

## The claim as made, and the version tested

| Part of the claim | What is tested here | Difference |
|---|---|---|
| Correlations as attractors | Each stock's 10 most-correlated stocks are its attractors; each one's mass is its correlation | Masses use correlation alone, not size or volume |
| "Start pulling … as it approaches", in price | G1: pull = mass ÷ (gap + ε)^p, where the gap is how far B's price is from where its peer puts it | The gap is measured in units of its own recent spread |
| … in time | G2: A's move pulls on B with a weight that grows as the offset day N approaches, (\|τ − N\| + 1)^(−a) | A symmetric kernel: the pull also fades after day N |
| … in both | G3: B is pulled toward where A was τ days ago, harder the closer in price and the nearer τ is to N | A reduced grid, fixed now |
| All available data, not just closes | High and low give B's closest approach to the attractor during the day; the open is the execution price; open, high, low and close give σ (Garman–Klass); dollar volume picks the universe | Outside data isn't used: the idea is about correlations between stocks. The 5-minute bars give one reported check |
| Multiple gravitational scaling factors | p ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3}, ε ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 1}, N ∈ {5, 10, 21}, a ∈ {0.5, 1, 2, 3}, a grid fixed before any result | 30 candidates in all, corrected for |

## Nearest prior work

| Work | What they did | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| Gatev, Goetzmann & Rouwenhorst 2006, *RFS* | Pairs with the closest normalized prices; bet each gap closes once it is wide | Profits through 2002, fading. This project's replication: `+0.8-pairs-trading.md`, no edge after 2006 |
| Avellaneda & Lee 2010, *Quantitative Finance* | Each stock's residual against its sector as a mean-reverting process; trade on its s-score | The spring in its standard form: the pull is proportional to the distance |
| Michael, Nobay & Peel 1997, *JPE* | Real exchange rates with nonlinear adjustment | Reversion is faster the further the rate is from equilibrium, the opposite of gravity |
| Lo & MacKinlay 1990, *RFS*; Hou 2007, *RFS* | Lead-lag between stocks: large lead small, industry leaders lead followers | Lead-lag at fixed lags |
| This project | `+1.1-twin-reversal.md`, `+2.0-correlation-census.md`, `-1.9-unequal-move-lengths.md`, `-5.2-peer-momentum.md`, `-16.2-twin-gap-fade.md` | Same-day co-movement is real; lagged pairs are at chance; spreads to a twin or a pair don't revert enough to pay |

The run adds a novelty check (a web search before any test) for force laws on spreads and power-law lag kernels.

**Novelty check (2026-10-24, web search before any test; it changed no rules):**
- **Force laws:** no test was found of a force on peer spreads that strengthens near equilibrium. The nonlinear spread models found revert faster far from equilibrium, the opposite of gravity: ESTAR and band-of-inaction models of exchange rates, and Zhang 2020's nonlinear state-space pairs model (arXiv 2005.09794). The "gravity" indicators in trading software are moving-average reversion. A patent and a 2010 blog post speculate about inverse-square price forces, with no test.
- **Power-law kernels** appear in high-frequency Hawkes cross-excitation, decaying like 1/t (for example arXiv 2303.02038). None was found as a daily lead-lag kernel centred on an offset.

## Rules (proposed; fixed before any result is seen)

### Data

- **Prices:** the store's open, high, low and close, split- and dividend-adjusted by Stooq. Closes before 2008 (the export's) are used only for correlations, β and the spreads' history.
- **Dividends:** back-adjusted through about late 2022 and raw after. Raw ex-dates move a spread by the dividend. **2023-01-01..2025-06-27 is reported separately** as the check.
- **Bad prints:** a day is missing when (H − L)/C > 0.5, H < max(O, C), L > min(O, C), or H = L, as in an earlier test on the same OHLC data. A missing day gives no force and no return.
- **Universe:** at each refit on the fixed 21-day calendar of that earlier test, the 500 stocks with the highest 63-day median dollar volume (on at least 57 of the 63 days), minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)`.
- **σ:** each stock's Garman–Klass daily volatility over the 63 trading days through t − 1. Where fewer than 57 of those days have a valid open, high and low (all days before 2008), σ falls back to the standard deviation of daily log close-to-close returns over the same 63 days. So G2's masses can use 2007's moves.
- **Development:** 2008–2019, from the first day the 63-day Garman–Klass σ exists for most of the universe (about 2008-04). Every stage's forces start on that same day.

### Attractors (peers, masses, β)

- **At each refit,** for each stock B in the universe: its K = 10 attractors are the universe stocks A ≠ B with the highest correlation ρ of daily log close-to-close returns over the 252 trading days through the refit day (at least 227 common valid days), with ρ > 0.
- **Mass** m = ρ. **β** = the OLS slope of B's daily log returns on A's over the same window.
- **They stay fixed** until the next refit. A stock with fewer than 5 valid attractors on a day has no force that day.
- **Random attractors (the null, reported for every stage):** 10 universe stocks drawn at random for each B at each refit (seeded), through the same formulas. In G1 and G3 they carry their own ρ (clipped at 0) and β; in G2, their own m, signed.

### G1. Gravity in price

- **The attractor's position:** where A puts B. The spread is s(t) = ln C_B(t) − β ln C_A(t). Its reference mean μ and standard deviation sd come from the 63 trading days t − 63..t − 1 (at least 57 valid).
- **The gap at the close:** g_C = (s(t) − μ) / sd. The same with B's high and low in place of its close gives g_H and g_L.
- **Closest approach during the day:** r = 0 if g_L ≤ 0 ≤ g_H (B's range reached the attractor), otherwise min(|g_L|, |g_H|).
- **The force on B:** F = −Σ over B's attractors of m · sign(g_C) / (r + ε)^p. Negative means B is pulled down.
- **The grid (10 candidates):**
  - p = 0: a constant pull, where only the direction counts (ε does nothing);
  - p ∈ {1, 2, 3}, each with ε ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 1}: the pull rises ever more steeply near the attractor. p = 2 is Newton's law. ε is how close counts as "at" the attractor, in gap standard deviations, and caps the pull at m / ε^p.
- **Benchmark (the spring):** F = −Σ m · g_C. Pull ∝ distance: pairs trading's s-score.
- **Closes only (reported):** r = |g_C|. This shows whether high and low add anything.
- **The measured force law (reported):** every (B, A) pair-day falls in a bin of r: 0, (0, 0.25], (0.25, 0.5], (0.5, 1], (1, 1.5], (1.5, 2], (2, 3], above 3. For each bin: the mean of B's next open-to-open return in the attractor's direction, in units of B's σ, for real minus random attractors, with bootstrap intervals. Gravity predicts the largest pull in the nearest bins; the spring, in the farthest.

### G2. Gravity in time

- **Moves:** z(t) = (r(t) − β_M r_M(t)) / σ(t − 1), clipped at ±5. r is the stock's daily log return, r_M the export's market, and β_M is from the 252 days through t − 1. This is the census variable, with σ from open, high, low and close.
- **The kernel:** for offset N and exponent a, weights w(τ) = (|τ − N| + 1)^(−a) for τ = 1..2N − 1, normalized to sum to 1.
  - a = ∞ puts all the weight on τ = N: the sharp offset, the project's view so far.
  - a = 0 weights every day equally: a plain (2N − 1)-day move.
- **A's pull on B's next day:** f_A(t) = Σ_τ w(τ) · z_A(t + 1 − τ).
- **Masses for time:** m = the correlation of f_A(t) with z_B(t + 1) over the last 252 pairs (t, t + 1) with t + 1 at or before the refit day, so z_B on the refit day is the last target. B's 10 attractors are the universe stocks with the largest |m|, each candidate choosing its own.
- **The force:** F = Σ m · f_A(t).
- **The grid (12 candidates):** N ∈ {5, 10, 21} (a week, two weeks, a month) × a ∈ {0.5, 1, 2, 3} (from a gentle build-up to one close to the sharp offset).
- **Benchmark (the sharp offset):** a = ∞ at the same N, with its own attractors. Flat kernels (a = 0) are reported.
- **The measured kernel (reported):** for each N, using attractors chosen by the flat kernel, so the choice favors no τ: the cross-sectional slope of z_B(t + 1) on z_A(t + 1 − τ) for each τ = 1..2N − 1, real minus random attractors. Gravity in time predicts a peak at τ = N that falls off as a power.

### G3. Gravity in both

- **Attractors, masses and β from G1.** B is pulled toward where A was τ days ago.
- **The lagged spread:** s_τ(t) = ln C_B(t) − β ln C_A(t − τ). It has its own μ_τ and sd_τ over t − 63..t − 1, and its own gaps and closest approach r_τ, exactly as in G1.
- **The force:** F = −Σ over attractors of m · Σ_τ w(τ) · sign(g_C,τ) / (r_τ + ε)^p, with w from G2.
- **The grid (8 candidates):** p ∈ {1, 2} × ε = 0.3 × N ∈ {5, 21} × a ∈ {1, 2}. These are the middles of G1's and G2's grids, fixed now, not chosen from their results. Each candidate costs 2N − 1 spreads per pair.
- **Benchmark:** G1's force with the same p and ε (τ = 0 only). G3 asks whether time adds to price.

### Execution and returns

- **Every force is decided at the close of t.** Day t's high, low and close are known then. Means and standard deviations run through t − 1. Attractors come from the window through the refit day.
- **Primary execution:** enter at the open of t + 1 and hold to the open of t + 2 (an open-to-open return).
- **Reported:** the next close (t + 1 to t + 2, the judge's delay 1), and the same close (t to t + 1; optimistic, since it trades at the close the signal used).
- **Costs:** 5 bps a side (primary), 0 and 20 bps reported, with a break-even cost per side.

### Statistics

- **Forces are ranked** across the universe each day, centred to −0.5..+0.5. No single near-singular force can dominate.
- **S, the signal test:** each day, a cross-sectional OLS of the centred rank of the next open-to-open return on four centred ranks: the candidate's force, the stage's benchmark force, B's 1-day return and B's 5-day return. The statistic is the force's coefficient, averaged over the days of 2008–2019 (Fama–MacBeth). It is positive if gravity adds something to the old version and to plain reversal.
- **M, the money test:** each day, long the top fifth of the universe by force and short the bottom fifth, equal weight, $1 a side, entered at the next open and held one day. Costs are on the traded weight. The statistic is the net daily return at 5 bps, 2008–2019.
- **Standard errors:** a circular block bootstrap of whole dates (blocks of 21 trading days, 10,000 draws), using the same draws for every candidate and statistic. Newey-West (10 lags) is reported beside it.
- **Within a stage:** Romano–Wolf max-t across the stage's candidates gives one p for S and one for M (one-sided, positive).
- **Across stages:** Holm's correction across the six p's (G1-S, G1-M, G2-S, G2-M, G3-S, G3-M) at a family level of 0.0027. The smallest must be below 0.00045, the next below 0.00054, and so on; each must also have t ≥ 3.
- **The verdict** takes the stages in Brandon's order. A stage passes only if both S and M pass. Every number is computed and reported either way.
- **Every comparison reports** the actual difference with a 90% interval, not only whether it passes.

### Reported for every candidate

- S and M with t; the force's coefficient without the benchmark (raw); the plain rank IC.
- M at 0, 5 and 20 bps; the break-even cost; turnover; volatility and maximum drawdown.
- The book's alpha over 5-day reversal's long-short (the top and bottom fifths of the same universe) and over the benchmark's own book, Newey-West t.
- The random-attractor null; closes only (G1 and G3); the two other executions.
- Each of 2008–2019, 2020–2025-06, 2023-01..2025-06 and 2008–2025-06.

### The judge (improvement over random and the file-name number)

- **Each of the 30 gravity candidates and the benchmarks** goes to `c2eval.judge` as scores: delay 1, daily rebalance, costs 0, 5 and 20. Graded over the judge's July–June years 2008-09..2024-25 (17 years). If the judge can't start its walk-forward in 2008, each year runs as a single holdout and the years are stitched, as in an earlier test.
- **Improvement over random:** `c2eval.change_over_random` at 0 bps, averaged over the 30 candidates; each stage's mean is reported.
- **The file-name number:** the account's yearly return at 5 bps a side minus SPY's Yahoo total return, averaged over the 17 years, then over the 30 candidates. Each stage's mean and each candidate's are reported.

### Holdouts: what each period has already been exposed to

- **2008–2019, development:** read by every earlier experiment.
- **2020–2025-06:** read by nearly every experiment. A chronological evaluation period, not a holdout.
- **2023-01..2025-06** is also reported alone, as the dividend check above.
- **The 16 unseen months (2025-06-30 to 2026-10-19):** read once by each of about 72 earlier experiments. Run once at the end with the frozen code, and reported as used.
- **5-minute bars (reported, once, at the end):** G1's measured force law at 5-minute steps on the cached bars of `-100.0-intraday-lead-lag.md`. They cover about 60 days, all inside the unseen months, with attractors from the daily data before the bars begin. They can't carry a primary.
- **Untouched: closes after 2026-10-19.** The code is frozen by sha256 hashes, and under a git tag once one is made, before that data arrives. Forces are logged from each new download before their outcomes exist. Confirmation after about 250 trading days: each primary that passed must be positive at one-sided p < 0.05, under Holm. If none passed, the forward test can only confirm the failure.

### Pilot gate

- **First,** `pilot.py` times one year and 50 stocks of G3's heaviest candidates (N = 21) and of G2's peer selection, and extrapolates to the full run at the machine's load.
- **The pilot decides only how to run it:** as written, with numba, or in chunks. If G3 still can't finish, it is reported as "not run: <reason>". No grid is ever shrunk after a result is seen.

### Checks (before any outcome)

- **Look-ahead, by truncation:** for 12 stocks, each cut at a random day, rebuild the panel and every candidate's force through the cut. The forces must be identical.
- **Kernels against slow versions:** each force recomputed in plain Python for a sample of stock-days.
- **Hand prints:** a few stock-days with the attractors, gaps, closest approach and force printed in full.

## What the results can and can't show

- **A stage's S passes:** the pull's shape carries information that the spring or the sharp offset, and plain reversal, don't.
- **S passes and M fails:** a real gravity signal that costs eat. A daily long-short trades much of its book every day.
- **Both pass:** an edge, to be confirmed on the untouched data. The long-only number is flattered by the survivor-only data.
- **Everything fails:** with these definitions and this grid, gravity adds nothing to what the project already found. It can't rule out other definitions of distance or mass.
- **The measured force law and kernel** show the actual shape of the pull, whatever the tests say.

## Ways it can fool us

- **Reversal in disguise.** B's own reversal pulls it back toward any reference. Hence B's 1- and 5-day returns as controls, the random-attractor null, and the alpha over reversal.
- **The old idea in disguise.** Summed over 10 attractors and ranked across stocks, a gravity force can order stocks much as the spring or the sharp offset does. Hence the benchmark inside S: only what gravity adds beyond it counts.
- **Bid-ask bounce.** A close above the spread's mean tends to come back. Hence execution at the next open, not the same close, and the same close only reported.
- **Near-singular forces.** Softening ε, and ranks rather than raw forces.
- **Many candidates.** Romano–Wolf within each stage and Holm across the six, so 30 candidates cost the bar what they should.
- **Dividends and survivors.** Raw ex-dates move spreads after late 2022; the survivor-only data flatters the long-only number. Hence the 2023 check, and the long-short book as the money test.

## Decisions

Every deviation from the plan, and every choice the plan left open. Each is marked by when it was made. The timestamped log is `research/gravity/out/decisions_log.txt`.

**Deviations from the plan, in short:**
- The measured kernel uses sign(m) × z_A rather than z_A alone. The unsigned slope is reported beside it.
- The judge's own walk-forward grades the plan's 17 years, so its years were used directly. The plan's per-year stitching fallback wasn't needed.
- The plan leaves open what "most of the universe" means for the start day, how the random null is set up, and the block length for the 5-minute bars. They were fixed as below.
- Nothing else departs from the Rules, and no grid, universe or period was changed.

**Before any result was seen:**
- **Novelty check:** done first, before any code ran; see "Nearest prior work". It changed no rules.
- **Environment:** numpy 2.2.6 and numba 0.61.2 (llvmlite 0.44.0) on Python 3.12.12, in `research/gravity/.venv` (git-ignored; the same pins as an earlier test's environment). Every script ran through `run.sh`, under `systemd-run --user --scope -p MemoryMax=5G` with 4 threads (`RAYON_NUM_THREADS=4` for the judge).
- **The pilot** (`out/pilot.txt`) ran before any outcome, at a load of 11 (lower than the usual 40–46). It timed one year (the 12 refits of 2012) and 50 stocks:
  - attractor selection (G1, the random draws and all 18 G2 kernels, all 500 stocks) took 15.5 s, about 4.5 minutes for the full run;
  - G3's N = 21 pass (N = 5 shares it) took 0.2 s with numba, about 1.1 minutes for both worlds, against 122 minutes in plain numpy;
  - numpy and numba agreed on 1,680 stock-day values to 6 × 10⁻⁸ (the float32 output).
  - By its fixed rule (plain numpy only if under 30 minutes), G3 ran with numba, in chunks of one refit, and no lagged spread was ever materialized.
- **Closes:**
  - the export's before 2008, for history only;
  - the store's on days that pass the bad-print rule from 2008 (the export's close has a repair that looks one day ahead).
  - The two agree on all 4,481,821 valid stock-days from 2008.
- **σ:**
  - σ[t] covers t − 62..t, so "through t − 1" is σ[t − 1]. z(t) uses σ[t − 1], and the force law divides the next return by σ[t], known at the close of t.
  - The close-to-close fallback also needs 57 of 63 returns. From the first force day, σ is Garman–Klass on 99.99% of universe stock-days.
- **The first force day** ("σ exists for most of the universe"): the first day on which Garman–Klass σ[t − 1] exists for at least 90% of that day's universe, 2008-03-26.
- **Universe:** the rule of an earlier test on the same OHLC data, not yet published here (the plan's text, no twin required), including a finite export close on the refit day, which that test inherited from another. It gives 211 refits, 1,219 distinct stocks and 161 bad prints (0.007%), identical to that earlier test.
- **Attractors:**
  - correlation and slope are pairwise-complete, on common valid days;
  - a stock keeps fewer than 10 attractors when fewer than 10 have ρ > 0 and 227 common days. 0.9% of stock-refits have fewer than 5, so no force.
- **Random attractors:**
  - drawn without replacement from the refit's universe (sorted by store id, B left out), seeded by (seed, refit date, B's store id), so a truncated build or the new export draws the same ones;
  - one random set per stock-refit, shared by G1, G2 and G3;
  - one with ρ < 0 has mass 0 and still counts as a valid term (zero pull); one with fewer than 227 common days is no attractor.
- **The random null is a parallel world:** the random-attractor candidate, controlled for the random-attractor benchmark, with its own book. The plan says "through the same formulas" and doesn't say which benchmark the null's S controls for.
- **β to the market for z:** the 252 days t − 252..t − 1, at least 227 valid.
- **Missing data in kernel sums** (G2's f_A, and G3's sum over τ for each attractor):
  - missing terms count 0;
  - the sum is valid only when at least 90% of the kernel's weight has data, so the sharp offset needs its one day.
- **G3's gaps at lag τ** use B's close, high and low on t and A's close on t − τ.
- **A force is missing** unless at least 5 attractor terms are valid, B is in the universe that day and t is on or after the first force day.
- **Returns:**
  - simple returns from the store's prices, with both days valid: open t + 1 → open t + 2 (primary), close t + 1 → close t + 2, close t → close t + 1;
  - B's 1- and 5-day returns are log close-to-close through t.
- **Days:** daily statistics belong to the decision day t, and periods are cut by it. They run from the first force day to the export's third-last day.
- **S:**
  - the sample is universe stocks with the force, the benchmark, both reversal controls and the return;
  - all five are re-ranked within that sample each day: average ranks, centred as (rank − 1) ÷ (n − 1) − 0.5;
  - at least 50 stocks a day.
  - Raw is the same regression without the benchmark. IC is the rank correlation of force and return.
- **M:**
  - n ÷ 5 (rounded down) names a side among universe stocks with a force, ties broken by row order;
  - a picked stock without a return that day is left out of its leg's mean;
  - turnover is the sum of |w_t − w_{t−1}| over all stocks, with no drift adjustment; the cost is c bps × turnover.
- **Benchmarks:**
  - G1 → the spring;
  - G2 (N, a) → the sharp offset at the same N, with its own attractors;
  - G3 (N, a, p) → G1's force at (p, 0.3);
  - closes-only candidates → the closes-only benchmark.
- **Statistics:**
  - t = estimate ÷ bootstrap sd. One set of 10,000 circular 21-day block draws per period (seed 20261024) is shared by every series.
  - Romano–Wolf is step-down max-t, each candidate studentized by its bootstrap sd; a stage's p is its best candidate's adjusted p.
  - Holm runs on the six stage p's at 0.0027, and each pass also needs the best candidate's t ≥ 3.
- **Alphas:** the net 5 bps series regressed on the gross daily series of 5-day reversal's long-short (bottom fifth of the 5-day return long, the same universe and execution), and separately on its benchmark's gross book. Newey-West, 10 lags.
- **Force law:**
  - unweighted means over attractor-days, not weighted by mass;
  - the outcome is −sign(g_C) × B's next open-to-open return ÷ σ[t];
  - the date bootstrap runs on per-date sums and counts.
  - Each law's predicted shape is its mean pull in the bin, 1 ÷ (r + ε)^p; the spring's is the mean |g_C|.
- **Kernel:**
  - attractors are chosen by |m|, so about half the masses are negative, and an unsigned slope would mix the two directions;
  - the slope is therefore on sign(m) × z_A(t + 1 − τ), the direction the mass predicts, with the plan's literal unsigned slope reported beside it;
  - slopes come per day over all (B, A) pairs with an intercept, need at least 100 pairs, and are averaged over days, real minus random.
- **Judge:**
  - **scores:** the force's centred rank among the day's universe stocks with a force (the judge buys the top 20 with a positive score);
  - **years:** the judge's walk-forward (start 2024-06-28) runs its years 2007-08..2024-25 and skips 2006-07. Its 2008-09 year exists, so no per-year stitching was needed. The 17 years from 2008-06-28 are graded; 2007-08, with forces only from 2008-03-26, is dropped;
  - **SPY:** Yahoo total return over each year's first to last day (`research/after_costs/group_a/spy.py`);
  - **random accounts:** 1,000, uniform.
- **Unseen months:**
  - the new export's universe starts at the refit in force on 2025-01-01, so attractors, masses, kernels and spread windows are full by 2025-06-30;
  - statistics and books run from 2025-06-30;
  - the judge's single holdout starts 2025-06-30, and its returns are over the 16 months, not annualized.
- **The 5-minute check:**
  - the cached bars with `research/intraday_lead_lag/build.py`'s filters, high and low read from the same CSVs;
  - full days (SPY has all 78 bars) inside the new export;
  - the attractors of the last refit before the first bar day;
  - each symbol-day scaled to the store by f = store close (d − 1) ÷ Yahoo daily close (d − 1), and kept only if its last bar × f is within 2% of the store's close on d and Yahoo shows no split on d − 1 or d;
  - the outcome is the next 5-minute bar's log return toward the attractor over σ[d − 1] ÷ √78;
  - days are bootstrapped in 5-day blocks, since 58 days are too few for 21-day blocks.

### Verdict

- **No edge: all six primaries fail under Holm, and the verdict stops at G1, price.** With correlations as attractors and these force laws, the pull's shape carries nothing beyond pairs trading's spring and plain reversal (G1-S t 0.82), and nothing that pays (G1-M +0.13 bps a day).
- **In time**, kernels around an offset add nothing beyond the sharp offset (G2-S t 1.85). **In both**, they make G1 worse (G3-S t −0.93).
- **The measured pull grows with distance, as a spring's does,** and random peers show it as strongly as real ones. It is each stock's own reversal toward any reference, not a pull from its peers. Real peers add only a sliver on days a stock's range touches its peer's price (+0.005σ, under 1 bp, gone in the unseen months).
- **The measured time kernel is flat,** with no peak at the offset.
- **Against SPY,** the 20-stock accounts trailed it by 16.7 points a year after costs. They beat random accounts by 1.8, below the old spring's 11.6.
- **What this can't show,** as the plan says: other definitions of distance (residual spreads, prices in dollars, intraday paths), other masses (size, volume, the census's lagged correlations) or other grids. The force laws tested here are the ones fixed before any result.
