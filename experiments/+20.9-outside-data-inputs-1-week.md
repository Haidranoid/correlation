# Outside data as extra model inputs, one week

**TL;DR:** Do outside data series, such as economic statistics and daylight hours, help a model pick next week's 10 best stocks? Not really: the picks beat SPY by +20.9 points a year after costs of 5 basis points a trade, but the gain rested on officially revised statistics whose stored values contain hindsight. Without them the gain vanished: t 1.6 over comparable stocks, versus 2.0 for stock data alone. Verdict: no real improvement.

## Results (700 weeks; grader's accounts, median of 5 tie-breaks)

| Model | Extra over comparable stocks (t) | Extra over the average stock (t) | Rank correlation (t) | $10,000: before costs | at 20 bps |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stock only | +0.21 (2.0) | +0.32 (2.4) | +0.025 (4.9) | $214,869 | $19,052 |
| With outside data | +0.25 (2.5) | +0.46 (3.3) | +0.018 (3.7) | $620,847 | $52,079 |
| Outside data shifted 4 years (control) | +0.15 (1.3) | +0.08 (0.5) | +0.010 (2.0) | $40,474 | $3,640 |
| With outside data, revised statistics left out | +0.18 (1.6) | +0.35 (2.4) | +0.020 (4.1) | $247,970 | $21,504 |
| SPY | | | | | $65,036 |

- **Why this test:** the outside-data scan (`+4.0-outside-data-leaders.md`) found that no outside series leads any stock directly. This test asked whether outside conditions change which kinds of stocks do well. The LightGBM from the weekly top-10 test (`+7.4-weekly-top10-picks.md`) got 69 extra inputs built from the 1,753 outside series; a control got the same series shifted four years out of line.
- **The pass rule, fixed before the runs:**
  - The outside-data model must beat both the stock-only model and the shifted control, on comparable-stock t and on the account at 20 bps.
  - A pass must then survive dropping the revised categories.
- **How it came out:** with everything, the outside-data model passed the first part of the rule. The revised categories (macro, prosperity, migration, population) are officially revised statistics whose stored values contain hindsight; without them the gain was gone. Even the version with everything ended below SPY after costs at 20 bps ($52,079 against $65,036).
- **How the inputs were built:** each series as correlation2's scan builds it, counting from its publication lag and standardized over a trailing year. The 23 categories are averaged and summed over 1, 5 and 21 days, giving 69 inputs, the same for every stock on a day.
- **What the model leaned on:** 62–70% of its splitting gain went to outside inputs, mostly slow calendar-like series such as daylight hours. The shifted and sign-flip controls used them as heavily: the pattern of telling periods apart.
- **The sign-flip control** with outside data showed nothing (comparable-stock t 0.2).
