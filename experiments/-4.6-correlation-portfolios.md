# Correlation-built portfolios: does knowing correlations build a better portfolio?

**TL;DR:** Does knowing how stocks move together build a better portfolio than weighting by volatility alone? Minimum-risk portfolios of the 500 most-traded stocks built from correlations had a quarter to a third less risk than volatility-only weights in every period, but no better return for that risk (p 0.21 over 2006–2019). After costs of 5 basis points a trade they made −4.6 points a year against SPY. Verdict: works for risk, not returns.

## Nearest prior work

| Work | What it found |
|---|---|
| Clarke, de Silva & Thorley 2006, *JPM* | Long-only minimum variance: market-like return at about three-quarters of the risk |
| Jagannathan & Ma 2003, *JF* | Long-only constraints act like shrinkage and help minimum variance |
| Ledoit & Wolf 2004 | Shrinking the sample covariance toward constant correlation |
| DeMiguel, Garlappi & Uppal 2009, *RFS* | 1/N is hard to beat out of sample |
| López de Prado 2016, *JPM* | Hierarchical risk parity: allocation by correlation clusters, no matrix inversion |

**What's new:** for this project, the first test of correlations' value in building a portfolio, with inverse volatility as the yardstick that isolates them. Published methods.

**Novelty check (2026-10-21, a separate search; it changed no rules):** done, whether correlations add anything over volatility-only weights is a published question; the twin-fund covariance is a version of standard industry-factor risk models.
- **Kirby & Ostdiek 2012** (JFQA, "It's all in the timing"): Volatility timing, which weights by variances alone and ignores covariances, beats 1/N even at high trading costs, while mean-variance optimization often beats 1/N before costs but loses the edge to turnover.
- **Scherer 2011** (Journal of Empirical Finance, returns from minimum variance investing): Fama-French factors plus low-beta and low-residual-risk portfolios explain about 83% of minimum variance's excess-return variation; holding those tilts directly did better. Leote de Carvalho, Lu & Moulin (2012, MSCI World 1997–2010) likewise find minimum variance is mostly low beta and low volatility.
- **Ledoit & Wolf 2017** (RFS, "Markowitz meets Goldilocks"): Nonlinear shrinkage dominates the linear shrinkage the plan uses; for 100 stocks the global minimum-variance portfolio has 10.99% annual volatility against 13.11% with the sample covariance, and for an S&P 500-sized universe almost 50% lower volatility than 1/N (authors' own backtests).
- **Jain & Jain 2019** (Risks, machine-learning versus risk-based portfolios): With misspecified covariances, inverse volatility is the most robust, hierarchical risk parity next, and minimum variance and maximum diversification the most sensitive; Ardia, Bolliger, Boudt & Gagnon-Fleury (2017, Monte Carlo) find the same ordering.
- **Chan, Karceski & Lakonishok 1999** (RFS, forecasting covariances and choosing the risk model): A three-factor model is enough to pick the minimum-variance portfolio, and different covariance models give similar results, so a twin-fund factor covariance is unlikely to differ much from a shrunk one. Live funds since 2011: SPLV (inverse volatility, no correlations) and USMV (optimized with a covariance model); press comparisons say USMV did better over the last decade, mostly through sector mix, with SPLV ahead in 2022; no audited figures were found.
- **Not found:** a net-of-cost comparison of minimum variance against inverse volatility on the 500 most-traded US stocks after 2019, or one using a covariance built from each stock's tracking fund.

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Universe:** the 500 most-traded stocks of the judge-like liquid set, minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)`, every 21 trading days on the fixed calendar.

**Estimation window:** daily log returns over t−251..t (export closes); a stock needs at least 90% of days, and missing returns are set to 0 after demeaning.

**Portfolios** (long only, fully invested):
- **EW:** equal weight.
- **IV:** weights ∝ 1/σ_i.
- **MV-LW (primary):**
  - minimum variance on the Ledoit-Wolf (2004) covariance, shrunk toward constant correlation;
  - each weight between 0 and 2% (fixed now).
  - The sample covariance of 500 stocks over 252 days is singular, so the shrinkage is part of the rule, not a tuning choice.
- **MV-TW:**
  - minimum variance on a twin-structure covariance: the market (SPY) plus one factor per twin fund (the fund's return net of its market beta);
  - each stock loads on the market and its own twin's factor (OLS betas over the window), plus a diagonal residual variance;
  - the same 2% cap.
- **HRP:** hierarchical risk parity on the Ledoit-Wolf correlation, single linkage, as in López de Prado (2016).

**Solver:** an exact method for the capped long-only minimum variance, such as projected gradient to a 1e-10 tolerance or an active set. Document it, and check it against brute force on a small case.

**Holding:** 21 days, with weights drifting with prices. Costs are 5 bps per side on turnover at each rebalance.

**Returns:** daily portfolio returns from export closes (actual returns), in excess of the 3-month T-bill.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **2006–2019, net of costs:** MV-LW's realized volatility is at least 10% below IV's, **and** MV-LW's Sharpe ratio is above IV's with a block-bootstrap p < 0.10 (21-day blocks).
2. **2020–2025-06:** same direction on both.
3. **The 16 unseen months** (new export, once, at the end): MV-LW's realized volatility is below IV's. Fifteen months can't test a Sharpe ratio; it is reported.

**Reported:**
- all five portfolios' return, volatility, Sharpe, maximum drawdown and turnover, by period;
- the 2008 and 2020 drawdowns;
- SPY.

## The number for the file name

(Sharpe of MV-LW − Sharpe of IV) × 15, before costs, 2006–2025-06. That is points of yearly return at 15% volatility: a positive number means the correlations bought return at equal risk. It is not a comparison with random accounts; the README's key says so.

## Ways it can fool us

- **The low-volatility anomaly:** minimum variance can win on Sharpe purely by tilting to low-beta stocks. Inverse volatility tilts that way too, which is why it's the yardstick.
- **Survivor-only data:** missing delistings flatter equal weight and volatile stocks the most, which works against minimum variance.
- **Concentration:** the 2% cap prevents a portfolio of a dozen utilities.

## Results (run 2026-10-21)

**Plumbing.** 5-day reversal through this folder's `save_scores` + `judge` path gives +11.8 points a year, with 8 of 18 years beating random at p < 0.1 (`sanity_reversal.txt`), as it should. This plan has no judge test; the check only confirms the export wiring.

**Checks of the estimators and the solver** (`tests_out.txt`):
- Ledoit-Wolf matches a loop-by-loop transcription of the paper's formulas to 7e-16 on five random cases, with intensities from 0.67 to 1.
- The solver matches brute force over every {0, cap, free} pattern on 300 random cases of up to 8 stocks (worst objective gap 1.5e-15). At 500 stocks it matches a pure active-set run from equal weights exactly.
- At all 246 + 29 real rebalances it finished on the face that projected gradient found, with KKT errors ≤ 2.5e-15. MV-LW held about 40 names at the 2% cap on average (32 in the unseen months).
- HRP's single linkage matches a naive agglomerative loop on 20 random cases. HRP returns inverse-variance weights on a diagonal covariance, and keeps the blocks of a two-block toy together.

### What it means

- **Correlations reliably lower risk.** Every portfolio that uses them was calmer than IV in every period, including the unseen months, each at p ≤ 0.003:
  - the two minimum-variance portfolios by 19–37%;
  - HRP by 10–13%.

  This agrees with the project's one positive lesson, that same-day co-movement predicts risk (`+5.1-co-movement-tools.md`).
- **They don't buy return at equal risk.** Over 2006–2025-06 no pair's Sharpe difference comes near significance (the smallest two-sided p is 0.34).
  - MV-LW's lead over IV in 2006–2019 (+0.11) points the way the published low-risk effect does, but it isn't significant here.
  - That lead reversed in 2020–2025, when low-beta stocks lagged a rising market: MV-LW made +3.7% a year against SPY's +14.2%.
- **The yardstick doesn't share the tilt.** The plan expected inverse volatility to tilt to low beta too, but here IV's beta is 1.0, against 0.6 for MV-LW and MV-TW. So part of what minimum variance adds over IV is a beta tilt, which its correlations find, and that tilt still didn't produce a significantly better Sharpe ratio.
- **What the portfolios hold:**
  - MV-LW: about 60 names above 0.1%, about 40 of them at the 2% cap. They are utilities, staples, insurers, waste haulers, mortgage REITs and defense (in 2017-07, for example, AFL, AGNC, ALL, TRV, SO, RSG, MMM, NOC). IV's largest weights are the same kind of names, at 0.4–0.5%.
  - The Ledoit-Wolf intensity averaged 0.22, and the mean pairwise correlation 0.33 (0.19 in the unseen months).
- **Twins against Ledoit-Wolf.** The twin-fund covariance (SPY plus one factor per twin fund, about 90 funds) delivered the same risk as Ledoit-Wolf over 2006–2025-06 (ratio 0.99–1.04, p ≥ 0.24). Their Sharpe ratios differ in opposite directions in the two halves: MV-LW was ahead by 0.07 in 2008–2019 (p 0.05) and behind by 0.18 in 2020–2025 (p 0.03). In the unseen months MV-LW was 10% calmer (p < 0.001).
- **Survivor-only data** flatters equal weight and volatile stocks, which works against minimum variance, as the plan says. It can't explain the absent Sharpe gain in 2020–2025, when EW and IV beat MV-LW by about 0.3.

## Decisions made while building it

All made before any portfolio return was computed, unless marked otherwise.

- **Universe:** the 500 largest are picked first, with `research/night_and_day/common.py`'s rule (copied). A stock among them with fewer than 227 of the window's 252 returns is dropped at that rebalance, so a portfolio can hold slightly fewer than 500 names. All five portfolios use the same names.
- **Window returns:** log returns of consecutive export closes, missing when either close is missing (c2eval's convention), with no clipping. Each stock's returns are demeaned over its own finite days, then missing days are set to 0.
- **Ledoit-Wolf:** the 2004 paper's estimator, with the paper's 1/T sample covariance, r̄ the mean off-diagonal sample correlation, and the intensity clipped to [0, 1]. Implemented in numpy and checked against a loop-by-loop transcription of the paper's Appendix B formulas on five small random cases; they agree to 7e-16.
- **IV:** σ is the square root of the same sample covariance's diagonal, which is also the Ledoit-Wolf diagonal.
- **Solver** for both minimum-variance portfolios:
  - accelerated projected gradient (FISTA with gradient restart, projection onto the capped simplex) to a projected-gradient residual of 1e-10;
  - then an active-set finish: the KKT system is solved on the face it identified, and the multipliers are verified (primal error ≤ 1e-12, dual ≤ 1e-9 relative);
  - if that fails, primal active-set iterations (Nocedal & Wright, Algorithm 16.3) take over.
  - Checked against brute force (every assignment of each weight to 0, the cap, or free) on 300 random cases of up to 8 stocks, 203 of them with the cap binding: worst objective gap 1.5e-15. At 500 stocks it matches a pure active-set run from equal weights exactly.
- **HRP:**
  - no weight cap, as published;
  - the correlation and variances come from the Ledoit-Wolf covariance;
  - distance d = √((1 − ρ)/2), then the Euclidean distance between columns of d (the paper's second step, and what its code's `linkage` call computes);
  - single linkage in scipy's labelling convention, reimplemented in numpy because scipy isn't installed, and checked against a naive agglomerative loop;
  - quasi-diagonalization and recursive bisection transcribed from the paper's code, halves by integer division.
- **MV-TW:**
  - the twin is `c2eval.twins`' choice at the rebalance itself, since the two calendars coincide;
  - fund returns come from store closes, with |log return| > 0.5 treated as missing (c2eval's rule); SPY from store closes. Both are demeaned and zero-filled like the stocks;
  - fund beta: OLS on SPY over the window; factor = fund − beta × SPY. A stock's OLS on SPY and its twin's factor (orthogonal in sample, so its SPY loading is its plain beta), with residual variance over 1/T;
  - Ω is the sample covariance of SPY and the factors of the twins in use;
  - stocks without a twin, or whose twin is SPY itself (factor identically zero), load on the market only.
- **Holding and costs:**
  - weights drift with forward-filled export closes, so a stock without a close is carried flat;
  - turnover is Σ|w_new − w_drifted|, buys plus sells, charged at 5 bps and subtracted from the first holding day's return;
  - the first formation (from cash) is charged, but it falls in 2005, outside every graded period.
- **Returns:** daily simple returns, in excess of DGS3MO / 100 / 252 (lazy prices' `rf_daily`). The CSV ends 2026-10-15, so its last value carries forward to 10-19.
- **Periods:** by return date: 2006-01-01..2019-12-31, 2020-01-01..2025-06-27, and the whole 2006-01-01..2025-06-27. The unseen months are 2025-06-30..2026-10-19.
- **Measures:**
  - Sharpe = mean daily excess return / its standard deviation × √252;
  - volatility = standard deviation of daily returns × √252;
  - return = compound annual growth of total return;
  - maximum drawdown on compounded total (not excess) return. The 2008 and 2020 drawdowns are the largest peak-to-trough within each calendar year;
  - beta = OLS slope of daily excess return on SPY's daily excess return, per period. The formation-day weighted beta is shown alongside;
  - names above 0.1% and turnover (one-way, ½Σ|Δw|) are averaged over the rebalances whose first holding day falls in the period.
- **Tests:**
  - net of costs everywhere except the file-name number, which is gross;
  - test 1's 10% is on the point estimate of the volatility ratio. Its Sharpe p is one-sided (MV-LW above IV);
  - test 2's "same direction" means point estimates only: MV-LW's volatility below IV's and its Sharpe above IV's, with no threshold.
- **Bootstrap:** paired circular block bootstrap with 21-day blocks, 10,000 draws, seed 0:
  - one-sided p = share of draws with (Δ* − Δ̂) ≥ Δ̂;
  - all-pairs tables give two-sided p (share with |Δ* − Δ̂| ≥ |Δ̂|) for the Sharpe difference and for the log volatility ratio, with 95% intervals.
- **Unseen months:** built on the new export with its own twins, universe and store SPY. Rebalances start 2024-06-01, so turnover has a base, and only returns from 2025-06-30 count.
- **What the data allowed** (decided after the first build counted the universe, before any return was looked at):
  - **About 300 names until 2007-11.** The judge-like set needs closes on 90% of the past 756 days, and the export is thin before 2005. So it holds only about 300 stocks until the 2007-11-01 rebalance, and 500 from 2007-12-03 on. Until then all five portfolios hold the whole set.
  - **Test 1 keeps the plan's 2006–2019.** 2008–2019, when the portfolios hold 500 names, is reported beside it.
  - **SPY in the store starts 2005-02-25.** The 2005-12-30 rebalance, which holds January 2006, has 214 of 252 SPY returns. The plan's missing-return rule (demean, then 0) applies to SPY too. Formations with no SPY return at all (2004-12-31, 2005-02-01; never graded) get no market factor.
  - **Few funds early.** Before 2006 the store's eligible funds give every stock the same twin (QQQ), so MV-TW starts out as the market plus a single factor. It has 42 distinct twin funds by 2007 and 60–130 after that.
- **After the results:** no rule, period or measure was changed. The unseen months were run once, after the walk-forward tables above were final.
