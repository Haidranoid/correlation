# Forecasting a stock's price from its relationships

**TL;DR:** Can stock B's next month be forecast from every stored relationship where some stock A moves first and B follows? Barely: the relationships cancel out, so the forecast is nearly flat. Over 61 large caps it got the direction right 50.9% of the time against a 50.1% base rate (p 1.7–8%), and 51.7% against 50.0% in the 16 unseen months. Verdict: no practical skill.

## Method

- **Relationships used:** every stored relationship where a leader A moves first and the chosen stock B follows `offset` days later, from the most recent census correlation period that had ended by the as-of date.
- **Each relationship's estimate:** A's actual moves × r × (B's volatility ÷ A's volatility), relative to the market. A's moves are capped at 5 typical moves, as the census clips.
- **Combining:** the forecast for each future day is the **mean** of the relationships covering it. The first draft summed them and produced paths like −82% in a month for AAPL.
- **Track record:** 21-day forecasts remade every 21 trading days, compared with the actual 21-day move relative to the market.

## Results

| | Direction right | Predicted vs actual correlation |
|---|---|---|
| AAPL | 58.0% | +0.05 |
| BOH | 54.4% | −0.12 |
| NAD | 48.4% | −0.24 (reliably wrong) |
| 61 large caps pooled (13,694 forecasts) | 50.9% | +0.015 (39–40 of 61 positive) |
| Same, periods ending in the last 3 years | 50.9% | +0.013 |
| **16 unseen months** (915 forecasts, 15 dates) | **51.7%** (95% range 47.9–55.5%; 50.0% expected) | +0.014 pooled; −0.021 mean per stock (26 of 61 positive) |

- **The forecast comes out nearly flat,** because hundreds of relationships per stock disagree and cancel: predicted moves average 0.10% against actual moves of 4.70%.
- **The pooled 50.9% is borderline** against the 50.1% from up/down base rates alone: p = 1.7–8%.

**Before the fixes:**
- The first version used raw moves rather than market-relative ones and got 50.2% / +0.005.
- The market leak it had turned BOH as of 2008-10-10 from −1.36% into +0.39%.
- Bad prints made NUE's "typical daily move" 20.76% instead of 1.99%.

## What browsing the relationships showed

- **The strongest-relationships ranking** is led by very volatile stocks (SAVA, RHP, TXT). It is driven by long-move periods with 33–50 observations, where chance alone reaches |r| 0.5–0.8.
- **Plain-terms counts** were added: how often B rose after A rose by its typical move, inside the period and in the rest of history. The out-of-period count is the honest one. For example, NZF → NAD daily: NAD rose 60% of the time after NZF rose 1%+, against 51% normally, a real stale-price effect.
- **SPAC histories:** Stooq files them under the later ticker. OKLO's flat pre-merger price once produced a "+6 billion %" row; the cap fixed it.
- **The browsing tools** built for it (by stock, timeline, explainer) work and remain in the GUI.
