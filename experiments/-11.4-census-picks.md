# Trading the census: the $10,000 holdout and every year since 2006

**TL;DR:** The census's stored lead-lag pairs were used to pick the 20 stocks most likely to rise each day, and the picks don't beat random guessing. In the holdout year 2024–25 they made +23.3% before costs against SPY's +13.6%. But 10% of random accounts with the same turnover did as well (p = 0.10), the same rule on a slightly different census grid gave +10.8% (p = 0.39), and at 20 bps per trade the picks lost 27%. In every year from 2006 the picks beat random guessers in 2 of 19 years, which is what chance gives. Over those years they averaged 3 points a year more than random, well within noise. Weekly and monthly targets also found nothing. What does exist is a faint ranking tilt (rank IC z = 4.15) that never becomes money: the long-short earns 0.45 bps/day gross (z = 0.92). Verdict: no edge.

## The test (the harness every later family uses)

- **Holdout:** the last year (2024-06-28..2025-06-27) is the future. Only census pairs from correlation periods that *ended before* it may be used.
- **Timing:** at the close of day d, a pair (A → B, offset k ≥ 1) adds r × A's move ending at d+1−k to B's score. Nothing after day d is used.
- **Account:** $10,000 in the top 20 positive scores, equal weight, rebalanced every close, with costs charged on turnover.
- **Random guessers:** 1,000 accounts holding as many stocks and replacing exactly as many each day as the strategy does.
  - This matters. In the first version the random accounts turned over 100% a day, and the picks seemed to "beat random" at 20 bps (p = 0.000) only because they traded less. With turnover matched, p was 0.39.
- **Quality measures:**
  - hit rate;
  - daily rank IC between scores and next-day residual returns;
  - a market-neutral long-short (top 10% minus bottom 10%) in actual returns.
- **Walk-forward:** each year from 2006-06-28 on is the future in turn.

## Results

**Holdout 2024-06-28..2025-06-27,** full census: 81,900 pairs from the 4 latest pre-holdout periods; 2,217 stocks can be picked.

| | Final | Return | Random guessers |
|---|---|---|---|
| SPY | $11,361 | 13.6% | |
| QQQ | $11,432 | 14.3% | |
| All 2,217 candidates | $10,826 | 8.3% | |
| Census picks, 0 bps | $12,328 | 23.3% | median $10,771; 10.1% did as well (p = 0.101) |
| Census picks, 5 bps | $10,817 | 8.2% | p = 0.103 |
| Census picks, 20 bps | $7,303 | −27.0% | p = 0.106 |

- **Prediction quality:** hit rate 49.9%; daily rank IC +0.0009 (t = 0.57).
- **The same rule on the smoke census** (fewer move lengths) gave +10.8%, p = 0.39. A result that moves that much with an arbitrary grid choice is noise.

**Walk-forward, 19 one-year holdouts:**

| Target horizon | Years beating random at p < 0.1 | Rank IC (residual) | Long-short in actual returns |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 day | 2 of 19 (chance ≈ 1.9) | z = 4.15 | 0.45 bps/day, z = 0.92 |
| 5 days | 2 of 19 | z = 3.29 | −0.44 bps/day, z = −1.11 |
| 21 days | 1 of 17 | z = 4.55 | 0.21 bps/day, z = 0.68 |

- The picks swing from +92.5% (2020–21) to −35.1% (2008–09): a concentrated, volatile 20-stock book.
- **About 40% of the book changes daily,** so even 1 bp of cost erases the long-short.

**What the rank IC is.** It is a faint, consistent tilt of unexplained origin:
- not next-day (offset-1 pairs alone give z = +0.19; offsets of 2+ days give +4.17);
- sign-dependent, and mostly from negative-r pairs;
- not the stock's own reversal;
- not only thin stocks.

It never becomes money.

## Caveats

- **The random baseline is generous.** Picks lean toward volatile stocks, which beat a uniform draw in strong years on their own, so p = 0.10 overstates the result. stockai later added volatility-matched random accounts.
- **Survivorship:** the data holds only survivors. That inflates the picks and the random guessers equally.
- **The 16 months after the census** were tested later, on fresh data (`-19.9-out-of-sample-16-months.md`): +21.0% against +20.3% for the median random account.
