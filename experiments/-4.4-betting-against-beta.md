# Betting against beta: do low-beta stocks, levered up, beat high-beta stocks?

**TL;DR:** Does Frazzini & Pedersen's betting against beta, buying stocks that swing less than the market (levered up) and shorting those that swing more, pay in this project's liquid stocks? No. Built their exact way, it made 0.25% a month after costs over 2008–2019 (t = 0.91; 2 needed) and lost money from 2020 on. An account of the 20 lowest-beta stocks trailed SPY by 4.4 points a year after costs. Verdict: no edge.

## Published claim

| Paper | Sample | What it reports |
|---|---|---|
| Frazzini & Pedersen 2014, *JFE* | US 1926–2012; 20 international markets | BAB earns significant positive alpha, in the US and in most international markets |
| Novy-Marx & Velikov 2022, *JFE*, "Betting against betting against beta" | US | BAB's performance relies on non-standard construction choices that overweight small, illiquid stocks; conventional versions earn much less |

**Our test period, 2006–2026, overlaps Frazzini & Pedersen's only in 2006–2012.** (As run, it starts in 2008-03; see Decisions.)

**A replication:** nothing in the method is new. Only the data is new: this project's liquid, survivor-only stocks.

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Universe:** the judge-like liquid set, minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)`.

**Beta, Frazzini & Pedersen's construction:**
- β_TS = ρ × σ_i ÷ σ_m.
- σ from one year of daily log returns (at least 120 days).
- ρ from five years of overlapping 3-day log returns (at least 750 days).
- The market is SPY.
- Shrunk: β = 0.6 × β_TS + 0.4.

**Book** (monthly, at each calendar month-end):
- Rank betas; z = rank, z̄ = the mean rank, k = 2 ÷ Σ|z − z̄|.
- High-beta weights k(z − z̄)⁺, low-beta weights k(z − z̄)⁻.
- r_BAB = (r_L − r_f) ÷ β_L − (r_H − r_f) ÷ β_H, with β_L and β_H the legs' weighted betas.
- r_f is the DGS3MO CSV in `research/lazy_prices/`.
- Costs: 5 bps per side on each leg's turnover, scaled by its leverage.

**Fidelity check, done before any test is read:**
- The monthly correlation with AQR's US BAB series over the overlapping months must be at least 0.5.
- If it isn't, fix only bugs against the published construction, and record them.

**Novy-Marx & Velikov's check, reported:**
- an equal-weight low-beta-third minus high-beta-third portfolio, unlevered;
- the same weighted by dollar volume, since there is no market cap.

**Alphas:** FF5 + UMD (French).

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **2006–2019:** BAB's mean monthly net excess return. **Pass:** t ≥ 2, **and** a positive FF5 + UMD alpha.
2. **The 500 largest:** same sign.
3. **2020–2025-06:** same sign. **The 16 unseen months** (new export, once, at the end): same sign.

**Money test (the judge):**
- **Score:** 1 − the beta percentile, so the low-beta stocks are bought. That is the long-only half of BAB. Graded with `rebalance=21`.
- **The judge can't lever, so this is a report, not the verdict.** The volatility-matched random comparison is the relevant line, because low-beta stocks are calm.

## The number for the file name

The judge's walk-forward change over random, with uniform random accounts, 0 bps (`c2eval.change_over_random`).

## Ways it can fool us

- **Construction:** Novy-Marx & Velikov's point. That's why the plain versions are reported.
- **Leverage costs:** the low-beta leg is levered about 1.5×, and borrowing above the T-bill rate eats into it. Report the break-even borrowing spread.

## Results (run 2026-10-21)

**What the data allowed.**
- **SPY in the old store starts on 2005-02-25.** The correlation needs 750 three-day returns against it, so the first beta exists at the close of 2008-02-22. The first book is formed at the close of 2008-02-29, and the first return month is 2008-03.
  - Test 1 therefore covers return months 2008-03..2019-12 (142 months), not 2006–2019.
  - Before mid-2005 the export has only about 320 stocks anyway; most begin in 2005.
- **The universe:** 980 stocks with a beta in 2008–09, rising to about 2,240 by 2025.
- **The book:** about 719 stocks in each leg, since rank weights give every stock except the median one some weight.
  - The ex-ante leg betas average 0.84 (low) and 1.42 (high), so the long leg is levered 1.19× and the short leg 0.72×.
  - Frazzini & Pedersen's US book was $1.40 long and $0.70 short. The liquid universe has no microcaps, whose estimated betas are the lowest.
- **Turnover:** 0.28 of a book per month, so 5 bps per side costs 0.17% a year.
- **The low-beta stocks,** checked by hand (`picks_old.txt`):
  - packaged food, staples and grocers (General Mills, Campbell's, Hormel, Clorox, Smucker, Kroger);
  - utilities (Southern, Con Ed, Duke);
  - gold miners and royalty companies (Franco-Nevada, Barrick, Agnico Eagle);
  - mortgage REITs (Annaly, AGNC);
  - in 2021–22, a few COVID-diagnostics and vaccine names.

  No funds.
- **Plumbing check:** 5-day reversal through this folder's scores and judge path gives +11.8 points a year, 8 of 18 years beating random at p < 0.1, as required.

### Test 2: the 500 largest — same sign (would pass)

The 500 universe stocks with the highest 63-day median dollar volume, ranks and weights rebuilt within them, 2008-03..2019-12, net: **+0.295% a month (t = 0.99)**, Sharpe 0.29, FF5 + UMD alpha +0.227% (t = 1.05). Gross: +0.312% (t = 1.05). Same sign as test 1, alpha too, and just as insignificant.

### Test 3: 2020–2025-06 and the unseen months — fails

- **2020-01..2025-06 (old export), net:** −0.570% a month (t = −1.28), −6.8% a year, Sharpe −0.54. FF5 + UMD alpha **−0.96% a month (t = −2.75)**.

- **Unlevered, low beta minus high beta loses money:** −2.2% a year equal-weighted over 2008–2019. Its market loading is about −0.5, and the market rose. FP's leverage step removes that loading, and that alone turns the raw mean from −0.21% into +0.25%. Every version's alpha is about the same, +0.2% a month (t ≈ 1). So on this data the leverage changes the raw return but not the risk-adjusted one.
- **Weighting toward large stocks doesn't hurt here before 2020.** The dollar-volume-weighted alpha (+0.36%, t = 1.61) is higher than the equal-weighted one. That is the opposite of Novy-Marx & Velikov's US finding, but this universe has no micro- or nano-caps, where their BAB earned its returns. Since 2020 the large-stock version did worst (alpha −1.72%, t = −2.79): the high-beta third of the largest stocks holds the technology names that led the market.

### Leverage cost

The break-even borrowing spread over the T-bill is the spread on the borrowed (1/β_L − 1) dollars per dollar of capital that brings BAB's mean net return to zero: 12 × mean ÷ mean(1/β_L − 1).

| Period | Long-leg leverage | Borrowed per $1 | Break-even spread, % a year | If the whole long leg were borrowed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2008-03..2019 | 1.20 | 0.20 | +15.4 | +2.5 |
| 2020–2025-06 | 1.19 | 0.19 | −35.5 (loses at the T-bill rate) | −5.7 |
| 2020–2025-06, dividends restored | 1.19 | 0.19 | −29.2 | −4.7 |
| All 2008-03..2025-06 | 1.19 | 0.19 | −0.6 (+1.3 with dividends) | −0.1 (+0.2) |
| Unseen months | 1.31 | 0.31 | −13.7 (−4.0 with dividends) | |

- **Borrowing costs aren't what decides it here.** In the liquid universe the long leg is levered only about 1.2×, not the 1.5× the plan expected, so even a large spread costs little. But BAB isn't significant at a zero spread over 2008–2019, and from 2020 on it loses money before any spread.

## Decisions made while building it

All of these were made on 2026-10-21 **before any BAB return was computed or looked at**, unless a line says otherwise.

**Period**
- **Test 1 starts in 2008-03, not 2006** (decided from data availability, before any returns).
  - SPY in the old store starts on 2005-02-25. ρ needs 750 overlapping 3-day returns against it, so the first beta exists at the close of 2008-02-22.
  - Test 1 is therefore return months 2008-03..2019-12 (142 months). Most of the export's stocks also begin only in 2005.
  - The prompt fixes SPY from the store, so Yahoo's longer SPY history wasn't substituted. The betting-against-correlation experiment hit the same limit.
- **Months:** a book is formed at the close of each month's last trading day and held to the close of the next month's.
  - The old export's June 2025 is a partial month, through 2025-06-27.
  - **Unseen months:** books formed 2025-06-30..2026-09-30, return months 2025-07..2026-10, with October partial through 10-19. The single day 2025-06-30 belongs to June's month and is dropped.
  - French's factors and AQR's series end in 2026-08, so the unseen-month alphas use 14 months.

**Beta**
- **σ_i:** the sd of daily log returns from the export's closes (both closes present) over d−251..d, with ≥ 120.
- **σ_m:** the sd of SPY's daily log returns (store closes) over the same 252 days, with ≥ 120.
- **ρ:** the Pearson correlation of log(c_d ÷ c_{d−3}) for the stock and for SPY, over the 3-day returns ending d−1259..d, with ≥ 750 pairs where both exist. The code is copied from `research/betting_against_correlation/panels.py`, with SPY in place of the export's equal-weight market.
- **No extra bad-print filter:** the export's closes are already repaired.

**Universe and book**
- **Universe at a month-end:** the shared judge-like set (`night_and_day/common.py`'s `universe()`, copied, which applies `c2eval.excluded`) at the latest twins-calendar day on or before the month-end, plus a finite beta at the month-end. A book needs ≥ 100 stocks; it always had about 980 or more.
- **The 500 largest:** the shared definition, the top 500 of the universe by 63-day median dollar volume at that calendar day, with a beta. Ranks and weights are recomputed within them.
- **Rank weights:** average ranks (ties share one); z̄ = the mean rank; k = 2 ÷ Σ|z − z̄|, so each leg's weights sum to 1 and the median stock gets none. β_L and β_H are the weighted means of the shrunk betas at formation.
- **Returns:** buy and hold within the month on forward-filled export closes: R_leg = Σ w_i (P_end ÷ P_start − 1).
- **r_f:** for the month, Π(1 + DGS3MO ÷ 100 ÷ 252) − 1 over its trading days. The plan names the DGS3MO CSV "in `research/lazy_prices/`"; that code reads it from `~/.local/share/correlation2/external/series/fred_dgs3mo.csv`, and that file is the one used here.
- **Costs:** turnover is measured on positions, with w_L ÷ β_L long and w_H ÷ β_H short. Last month's positions drift with returns, each leg renormalized to its leverage. Turnover = Σ |new − drifted old|, cost = 5 bps × turnover, taken from the month's return. The first book pays full entry.
- **Novy-Marx & Velikov's checks:**
  - thirds of the universe by beta (rank < n/3 and rank ≥ 2n/3), $1 long and $1 short;
  - equal weight, or weights proportional to the 63-day median dollar volume at the calendar day (the same measure that picks the 500 largest).
  - Also reported: FP's rank weights without the leverage. This was added before results, to separate the weighting from the leverage.

**Statistics**
- **Monthly returns throughout:** BAB is defined monthly, and AQR's series is monthly.
- **The t:** test 1's t is the plain mean ÷ standard error, Frazzini & Pedersen's convention; Newey-West with 3 lags is reported alongside.
- **The alpha:** the intercept of monthly net returns on French's monthly Mkt-RF, SMB, HML, RMW, CMA and UMD, plain OLS. BAB is self-financing, so it isn't reduced by RF first.
- **"Same sign" in tests 2, 3 and the unseen months** means the mean monthly net excess return has test 1's sign, which is test 1's own statistic. The alphas are reported.
- **The fidelity check** compares gross BAB with AQR's US BAB on the old export's months only (2008-03..2025-06), keyed on the return month. The unseen months were kept out.
- **Dividends:** the plan doesn't ask for the correction, so the primary numbers use the export's closes as they are, as the judge does. The dividend-corrected version (`pubdata.div_fix('old'|'new')` added to daily log returns, which it leaves at 0 before 2022-07) is reported for every period touching 2022-07 on. Without it, the bias runs against BAB: its levered long leg holds the heavy payers.
- **Break-even spread:** 12 × mean monthly net BAB ÷ mean(1/β_L − 1). This is the spread over the T-bill, on the dollars of the long leg that the capital doesn't cover, that brings the mean to zero.

**Money test**
- **Scores every day d:** the universe from the latest calendar day on or before d, with a finite beta at d. Score = 1 − r ÷ (n − 1), where r is the 0-based beta rank, so the lowest beta scores 1. Scores begin on 2008-02-22.

**Made after seeing results**
- **A workaround for `c2eval.change_over_random`** (not edited), as in betting against correlation: a single holdout keeps its costs under `report.strategy`, so `money.py` passes them in as `costs` for the unseen months. The result is that period's difference over the 16 months, not a yearly figure.
- **Nothing else was changed after results were seen.** The fidelity check passed on the first run, so no construction fix was needed.
