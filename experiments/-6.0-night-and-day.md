# Night and day: where do the market's slow followers catch up?

**TL;DR:** Slow-following stocks catch up to yesterday's market move; can buying at the next open and selling at the close collect it? Over 2008–2019 that earned +11.6 basis points a day before costs, but +3.6 (t 2.4, 3 needed) after costs of 2 basis points a trade. Its daily top 20 made −6.0 points a year against SPY after costs of 5 basis points a trade. Verdict: real, but not tradable after costs.

**Novelty check (2026-10-21, a separate search; it changed no rules):** partly done.
- **Yang & Yin 2024** (*IREF* 96, "Stock price delay and the cross-section of expected returns: a story of night and day"): sorting on the gap between day and night beta earns about 0.8% a month, monthly and cross-sectional.
- **Wang 2025/26** (*JFQA*): peers' overnight moves carry overnight and reverse intraday; peers' intraday moves carry intraday.
- **Lou, Polk & Skouras 2019; Hendershott, Livdan & Rösch 2020:** anomalies, and beta, split by night and day.
- **Chordia, Sarkar & Subrahmanyam 2005:** the big-to-small lead fell from 0.21 to 0.085 by 1988–2002.
- **Not found:** splitting the catch-up to yesterday's market into the next gap and the next day, or trading it daily.

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**Universe:**
- the judge-like liquid set, minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)`;
- from 2008 (opens before 2008 are unreliable);
- also a pre-registered subset: the 500 most-traded stocks (median dollar volume over 63 days). The near-miss lived in all-exchange small caps, where spreads are real.

**Signal (the slow-follower forecast), at the close of day t:**
- For each stock, over the previous 252 days, regress its next-day return on the market's return that day and the day before (Dimson). The lagged coefficient b_lag is how much of yesterday's market move it still has to catch up.
- The market is SPY's close-to-close log return.
- s = b_lag × SPY's return on day t.

**The two legs of the next day:**
- overnight: log(open_{t+1} / close_t);
- intraday: log(close_{t+1} / open_{t+1}).
- SPY's own legs come from its open and close in the store.

**Portfolios:**
- **Intraday (tradeable):** at the open of t+1, long the top 10% of s and short the bottom 10%, equal weight; exit at the close of t+1. Costs: 2 bps per side on each leg as the headline (auction prints sit at the bid or ask, so even this is optimistic); 0 and 5 bps reported too.
- **Overnight (not tradeable for a daily trader):** the same book from the close of t to the open of t+1. Gross only, to show where the catch-up lives.

**Optional Part B:** the cluster lead-lag strategy's own forecasts (`crates/c2-census/src/cluster.rs`, all-exchanges settings), split the same way. Only through a new example file or Python; never by editing existing source.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **Where the catch-up lives, 2008–2019:** the cross-sectional rank correlation of s with each leg (mean daily IC with t, clustered by date). Reported, not pass/fail.
2. **The intraday long-short, 2008–2019,** all liquid stocks. **Pass:** net of 2 bps per side, mean daily return with t ≥ 3.
3. **The 500 largest:** same sign, with t ≥ 2.
4. **2020–2025-06:** same sign. Then the 16 unseen months (new export and store, once, at the end): same sign.

**Money test (the judge):**
- Scores = s at the close of each day, graded with `rebalance=1`.
- The judge trades close to close, so it includes the overnight leg the intraday book can't reach.
- Uniform and volatility-matched random accounts are both reported.

## The number for the file name

The judge's walk-forward change over random, with uniform random accounts, 0 bps (`c2eval.change_over_random`).

## Ways it can fool us

- **Opening prints:** Stooq's open is the first trade or the auction price. On thin stocks it can be stale, which shows up as overnight-intraday reversal. Report the 500 largest separately.
- **Auction costs:** prices print at the bid or ask, and the impact of a real order is unknown. Report the break-even cost per side.

## Results (run 2026-10-21)

**The setup in numbers:**
- **Universe:** a median of 1,125 stocks a day over 2008–2019 (976 at the end of 2007, 2,072 at the end of 2023), 2,023 over 2020–2025-06 and 2,297 in the unseen months.
- **Each book:** holds the top and bottom 10%, about 110 names a side in 2008–2019 and 50 a side among the 500 largest.
- **Days:** 3,007 trading days in 2008–2019, 1,377 in 2020–2025-06 and 329 unseen.
- **Costs:** both books open and close every position every day, which is four trades a day. Net = gross − 4c at c bps per side, and the break-even cost per side is gross / 4.
- **Units:** books are in bps a day, long leg minus short leg, simple returns. t is from the daily series, one value per day, so it is clustered by date.

**The tests, in order:**
- **Test 1** (reported): both legs carry the catch-up in 2008–2019, the day more than the gap.
- **Test 2 fails:** +3.57 bps a day net of 2 bps a side, t 2.41, against t ≥ 3.
- **Test 3 fails:** the 500 largest net +1.70 bps a day, t 1.04, against t ≥ 2.
- **Test 4 fails:** 2020–2025-06 nets −6.99 bps a day (gross +1.01), and the unseen months net −10.43 (gross −2.43).

**What else the books show:**
- **Not a beta mismatch.** The book is long one end of b_lag and short the other, so its exposure to the next day's market flips with SPY's sign. Hedging each stock's leg with its own contemporaneous beta times SPY's same leg barely moves anything: intraday +11.58 (t 7.95), overnight +3.39 (t 5.38).
- **Direction matters.**
  - After SPY falls, the catch-up waits for the session: intraday +17.19 (t 7.43), overnight −3.14 (t −3.05).
  - After SPY rises, it is split: overnight +8.32 (t 9.59), intraday +6.98 (t 3.64).
- **Long and short sides** (2008–2019, against the universe's equal-weight mean):
  - The overnight gain is on the long side: +3.73 (t 8.66) against +0.56 for the short side.
  - The intraday gain is mostly on the short side: −7.41 (t −7.09) against +4.16 (t 4.02) for the long side. A real book would also pay to borrow.
- **The ways it can fool us:**
  - **Opening prints:** stale opens don't explain the 2008–2019 split. The 500 largest show the same pattern (intraday +9.70, overnight +2.86). Stale prints (open = close = high = low) are 0.007% of liquid stock-days and were dropped.
  - **Auction costs:** the best case breaks even at 2.9 bps a side, the same as the near-miss's 2.8. The auctions' bid-ask prints alone would use most of it.

- **The near-miss is a weaker copy of s.** Its intraday share is similar to s's in 2008–2019, but its decile book earns a quarter as much (2.7 against 11.6 bps a day).
- **Its break-even falls once it trades only in the day.** Closed every evening, it can't keep the names that stay in the book, so break-even drops from 2.8 bps a side close to close to 0.7 bps.
- **The unseen months' +10.0 bps intraday** is one noisy period (t 1.4), after 2020–2025's −1.9.

**What it means:**
- The slow followers exist, and through 2019 most of their catch-up was reachable at the next open.
- But the gain is too small to pay for four auction trades a day, except in 2008–09.
- Since 2020 the catch-up has moved into the overnight gap, which only a trade at the signal's own close can capture. The judge's close-to-close account captures it before costs, and lost to random in the unseen months.
- A trader could estimate s a few minutes before the close and send market-on-close orders. That might reach the overnight leg, but it is a different, untested strategy. The overnight book's gross since 2020 (+6.0 bps a day) would still have to cover four trades a day.

## Decisions made while building it

**Before any outcome was computed:**
- **The regression.** "Regress its next-day return on the market's return that day and the day before" is read as the standard Dimson regression with one lag. Each daily return r(τ) for τ = t−251..t is regressed on [1, m(τ), m(τ−1)], and b_lag is the coefficient on m(τ−1). The forecast for t+1 is b_lag × m(t).
- **Stock returns:** close-to-close log returns from the export's closes. Moves beyond ±50% (log) count as missing, and at least 227 of the 252 days (90%) are needed.
- **SPY:**
  - returns from the store's closes;
  - the market-wide gap day 2011-02-17 has none, so that day and the next get no score;
  - days with SPY's return exactly 0 get no score.
- **Legs:** from the store's opens and closes. The store's close equals the export's close on every stock-day (19.0 million old, 19.4 million new), so the legs add up to the judge's return. A stock-day is dropped from both legs when:
  - a price is missing or ≤ 0;
  - open = close = high = low (a stale print, 0.007% of liquid stock-days);
  - either leg moves more than ±50% (log), under 0.1% of stock-days.
- **Universe:**
  - **The liquid set:** the judge's rule made point in time on `c2eval.twins`' calendar (every 21 trading days). That is closes on ≥ 90% of the trailing 756 days, and median close ≥ $5 and median dollar volume ≥ $5M over the trailing 252 days. Minus `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)` at that rebalance, and the stock needs a finite s.
  - **The 500 largest:** the universe's top 500 by median dollar volume over the 63 days through the rebalance close. The deciles are re-ranked inside it.
- **Books:**
  - the deciles are round(10% × names) on s at the close of t;
  - members without valid legs on t+1 are dropped from that day's means, the same names in both books, since their t+1 data can't enter the ranking;
  - returns are simple (exp(leg) − 1) and equal weight, long mean minus short mean.
- **Periods** are by the day held: 2008-01-01..2019-12-31, 2020-01-01..2025-06-27, and 2025-06-30..2026-10-19.
- **Tests 3 and 4 use Test 2's measure:** the intraday book net of 2 bps a side. "Same sign" means positive.
- **Statistics:**
  - pass/fail uses the plain t of the daily series, one value per day;
  - Newey-West t (5 lags) is reported alongside;
  - IC is Spearman with tied ranks averaged.
- **Money test:** s on the universe on every day it exists, from 2006-01-23, not only from 2008. The judge trades close to close and needs no opens, and its first graded year is 2007–08.
- **Extra outputs:** the beta-hedged legs (stock leg − b0 × SPY's same leg, b0 from the same regression at t), the split by SPY's direction and the long and short sides were all in the first run's code, as diagnostics, not tests.
- **The plumbing check** uses weekly rebalancing, as the +11.8 reference does. It reproduced +11.8 and 8 of 18 years exactly.

**After Part A's results, before Part B's:**
- **Part B replays `c2 census clusters --all-exchanges`:** a fit every 42 rows from row 282, on the strategy's own 500 stocks. That is not the judge's per-year refit calendar, and not this file's universe.
  - **Check:** the replay is exact on the new export, to 8e-9, through 2025-06-27. On the old export it differs by a mean of 0.18% a day, because the published series was made on the newer store.
  - **Books:** both the sign book and the decile long-short of the harness's exact scores (forecast × (1 + 1e-9 × follow rank)) are reported.

**After the results:**
- The sub-period splits (2008–09, 2010–19, 2013–19) were chosen after seeing the by-year table. They are descriptive only.
