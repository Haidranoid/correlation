# Excess growth: does rebalancing to fixed weights beat buy-and-hold after costs?

**TL;DR:** Does rebalancing back to equal weight beat holding the same 500 most-traded stocks, as the excess growth rate γ\* suggests? Over 2008–2019 daily rebalancing came out 0.58 points a year ahead after costs (t 1.1; the bar was t 2), all of it from the 2008–2009 rebound of survivors, and 0.34 a year behind over 2020–2025. γ\* did predict the rebalanced portfolio's extra growth, but buy-and-hold collected 80% of it too, and survivor-free sector funds and a century of industry portfolios show no premium. Verdict: no edge.

## The claim

As listed on the page: "A portfolio kept at fixed weights grows faster than the weighted average of its stocks' growth rates by half of (their weighted average variance − the portfolio's variance). The test asks whether that extra growth survives trading costs against buy-and-hold."

The source is Fernholz's stochastic portfolio theory. By Itô's lemma a stock's log growth rate is μ − σ²/2. A portfolio held at fixed weights w, rebalanced continuously, has log growth γₚ = Σ wᵢγᵢ + γ\*, where the **excess growth rate** γ\* = ½(Σ wᵢσᵢ² − σₚ²) is never negative. Booth & Fama (1992) call the same quantity the diversification return.

The tested reading: an equal-weight portfolio rebalanced back to equal weights grows faster than buy-and-hold of the same stocks from the same starting weights, by about the accumulated γ\*, and enough to pay 5 bps a side on every rebalancing trade.

## Stage 1. What the math says before any data

Three kinds of statement, kept apart, as in an earlier volatility-timing test (not yet published here).

**Proved**, for any prices. Notation: N stocks at weights w (here 1/N), a period of n trading days, ℓᵢd = stock i's log return on day d, gᵢ = Σ_d ℓᵢd its log return over the period.
1. **Rebalanced every close (no delay, no costs):** its log growth over the period is Σ wᵢgᵢ + EG, where the realized excess growth EG = Σ_d [log Σᵢ wᵢe^{ℓᵢd} − Σᵢ wᵢℓᵢd] ≥ 0 by Jensen's inequality. Each day's term is about half the day's weighted cross-sectional variance of log returns. This is γ\*'s identity in discrete time; γ\* is its continuous-time limit.
2. **Buy-and-hold from w:** its log growth is Σ wᵢgᵢ + J, where J = log Σᵢ wᵢe^{gᵢ} − Σᵢ wᵢgᵢ ≥ 0, again by Jensen. **Buy-and-hold also beats the weighted average of its stocks' log growth.**
3. **So the gap, rebalanced minus buy-and-hold, is EG − J,** and it can have either sign.
4. **Expected wealth.** If daily returns are independent from one day to the next (with any correlation across stocks on the same day) and stock i's expected daily return is μᵢ, the rebalanced portfolio's expected end wealth is (1 + Σ wᵢμᵢ)ⁿ and buy-and-hold's is Σ wᵢ(1 + μᵢ)ⁿ. The second is never smaller, and they are equal when all μᵢ are equal. **Rebalancing can beat buy-and-hold in expected wealth only through serial dependence** in returns.
5. **To second order,** gap ≈ ½(Σ_d V_d − V_Y), where V_d is day d's weighted cross-sectional variance of log returns and V_Y that of the period's log returns. Writing xᵢd for stock i's log return minus the day's weighted mean, this equals −Σ_{d<d′} Σᵢ wᵢ xᵢd xᵢd′: **minus the sum of the cross-sectional autocovariances of relative returns at every lag inside the period.** γ\*·n is the first term alone; buy-and-hold's own J ≈ ½V_Y is the second. The gap is a contrarian strategy's profit summed over all lags, the kind of profit Lo & MacKinlay (1990) split into own autocovariances, cross-autocovariances and the spread of mean returns.

**Expected, not proved; measured in E1:**
- With 500 stocks and one-year periods, buy-and-hold's weights move little, so J should come close to EG and the expected gap should be near zero under a random walk, far below the accumulated γ\*. γ\* itself is roughly half the average stock's idiosyncratic variance, a few points a year for large US stocks.
- Over longer holds buy-and-hold concentrates in the winners and the gap grows toward γ\*. That is the horizon behind Maeso & Martellini's 86 bps a year at five years. The yearly reconstitution fixed below keeps this term small; H5 reports five-year holds.
- The sign of the gap is set by reversal (which favors rebalancing) against momentum (which favors buy-and-hold) inside the year. Among the 500 most-traded stocks this project found both weak: plain 1-month reversal +0.12% a month (t 0.2, `+1.1-twin-reversal.md`), and 12-1 momentum earning nothing (`+2.1-momentum-12-1.md`).
- Daily rebalancing trades about the mean absolute relative daily move of the stocks each day, a few hundred percent of the portfolio a year, so 5 bps a side should cost a fraction of a point a year.

**What this means for the claim.** γ\* is the rebalanced portfolio's gain over the weighted average of its stocks' growth rates, and that part is an identity. Over a year, buy-and-hold collects nearly the same gain. Cuthbertson, Hayley, Motson & Nitzsche (2016) make this distinction: diversification returns can be earned by both rebalanced and unrebalanced strategies. Hallerbach (2014) splits the rebalancing return into a "volatility return" and a "dispersion discount", which correspond to EG and J here. So E1 writes down two predictions of the gap before any data: the claim's (the accumulated γ\*) and the random walk's (about zero). The money test then asks whether what is left, the reversal inside the year, pays for its trades.

## Rules (proposed; fixed before any result is seen)

### Data

- **Prices:** the export's closes (Stooq's split- and dividend-adjusted closes after correlation2's bad-print repair).
- **Dividends:** the store is back-adjusted for dividends through about Oct–Nov 2022 and raw after. Every daily stock return in every portfolio adds `pubdata.div_fix` (zero before 2022-07), so both portfolios earn total returns and the after-costs number isn't biased against them in 2023–2025. The gap itself barely depends on it, since both portfolios hold the same stocks. The 254 old-export tickers without Yahoo data get no correction; the run counts how many fall in the universe.
- **A stock-day is untradeable** if its close is missing or its dollar volume is zero or missing (a stale quote). Its price is carried forward (return 0), and the move shows up on its next valid close.
- **The 2011-02-17 gap:** the export has almost no closes that day, so nearly every stock carries its price forward and 2011-02-18 holds two days' moves. Both portfolios see the same.

### Universe and periods

- **Decision dates:** the close of the last trading day of each calendar year (t0). Calendar years match the after-costs number. The fixed 21-day calendar is used only to look up `c2eval.excluded(E, tw)[c2eval.rebal_index(tw, t0)]`.
- **The 500:** at t0, stocks not excluded, with a close on t0, at least 227 valid daily log returns over t0−251..t0 (the γ\* window, as in `-4.6-correlation-portfolios.md`) and a positive median dollar volume over t0−62..t0. Take the 500 with the highest 63-day median dollar volume.
- **Purchase:** at the close of t0 + 1 (the first trading day of the year), the judge's `delay=1`. The target is equal weights at the close of t0, filled at the close of t0 + 1, so the starting weights are proportional to (1 + rᵢ,t0+1), the same convention as "Delay 1, exactly" below. The delay-0 versions (E1's identities) buy exactly 1/N at the close of t0 + 1. A selected stock untradeable that day is dropped for the period from both portfolios. Both portfolios make the identical first purchase, so they start the same.
- **A period** is year Y's portfolio, from the close of Y's first trading day to the close of Y+1's first trading day, when it is sold and the next one bought.
- **Development:** the 12 periods 2008..2019. **Check:** 2020..2024 and 2025 to the close of 2025-06-27. **The unseen months:** a fresh 500 chosen at the close of 2025-06-27 on the new export and bought at the close of 2025-06-30, then the year-end rule (decision 2025-12-31, held to 2026-10-19).
- **A stock whose export data ends inside a period** (its last close comes before the period's end and no later close exists) is sold at its last close in both portfolios. In both, the proceeds are spread over the other holdings in proportion to their value at that close, as an index would; the rebalanced portfolio's next rebalance resets the weights. Counted and listed.

### The two portfolios

- **Buy-and-hold (B):** the first purchase, then no trades until the period ends, except a forced sale above.
- **Rebalanced (R):** trades back to equal weight on its rebalancing days.
  - **P1, daily:** every trading day of the period.
  - **P2, monthly:** every 21 trading days from the purchase day.
- **Delay 1, exactly:** on a rebalancing decision day t, the target is equal weights at the close of t. The order is fixed then and filled at the close of t + 1, so after the trade the weights at the close of t + 1 are proportional to (1 + rᵢ,t+1): the weights the close-t equal-weight portfolio has drifted to by t + 1. The account stays fully invested. An untradeable stock keeps its holding; the others are set to their targets scaled to the remaining value.
- **Delay 0 (reported):** the target is filled at the same close, so weights are exactly 1/N after each rebalance. E1's identities use this version.
- **Costs:** 5 bps of the value traded, each side, on every trade of both portfolios: rebalancing trades, purchases, sales at the period's end and forced sales. Charged on the trade day.

### E1 (reported, with fixed readings). γ\* against the realized gap

For each period, on the 500 stocks:
- **Predicted:** γ̂\* = ½(Σ wᵢσ̂ᵢ² − w′Σ̂w) per day, from the sample covariance (1/T) of the daily log returns over t0−251..t0, each stock demeaned over its own valid days and missing days set to 0. Times the period's trading days.
- **Realized, gross, delay 0:** EG (daily and monthly rebalancing), buy-and-hold's J, and the gap EG − J, all in log points.
- **A random-walk prediction:** 200 draws per period, each resampling n whole days with replacement from the window's 252 daily return vectors, with each stock's log returns demeaned (equal expected log growth, Fernholz's setting). This keeps the same-day covariance and removes serial dependence. Each draw computes EG, J and the gaps, daily and monthly. The prediction is the mean over draws; summing draw j across periods gives 200 draws of the development total.
- **Readings, fixed now:**
  - **γ̂\* predicts excess growth** if the development ratio ΣEG ÷ Σγ̂\*·n lies in 0.80..1.25 and the slope of realized EG on predicted, across the 12 periods, has a 95% interval that includes 1.
  - **The claim's reading of the gap is rejected** if the development gross gap (daily, delay 0) falls short of half the accumulated γ̂\*: the Newey-West t (21 lags) of the daily series eₜ − ½γ̂\* at or below −3, where eₜ is the gross, delay-0 daily log difference, rebalanced minus buy-and-hold, and γ̂\* is its period's daily value.
  - **The random walk's reading is consistent** if the realized development gap lies between the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of its 200 simulated totals.
- **Reported:** every quantity by year; the ratio realized ÷ predicted; the 20-stock subsets' versions.

### P1 and P2 (primary). Does rebalancing beat buy-and-hold after costs?

- **Series:** dₜ = log(1 + rᴿₜ) − log(1 + rᴮₜ), each portfolio's daily return net of its 5 bps costs, delay 1, over the 12 development periods. Its sum is the cumulative log gap, and its mean × 252 is the net gap in log points a year.
- **Why the daily series:** with no serial dependence, dₜ is close to a martingale difference, since buy-and-hold's tilt away from w uses only past returns. So a t-statistic on it is valid under the null.
- **Statistics:** Newey-West t with 21 lags (63 reported), and a circular block bootstrap of whole dates in 21-day blocks, 10,000 draws, on dₜ centred at its mean. One-sided p (rebalancing ahead) = (1 + draws with centred mean ≥ the observed mean) ÷ 10,001. Each primary's p is the larger of the bootstrap p and the Newey-West t's normal p.
- **Pass:** Holm across P1 and P2 at a family level of 0.0228 (t ≥ 2, one-sided), the bar `experiments/README.md` sets for published strategies, since a published result is the prior: the smaller p must be below 0.0114 and the other below 0.0228. Whether each primary also clears t ≥ 3 (p < 0.00135) is reported.
- **Check period, 2020..2025-06:** a primary that passed must have the same sign there with one-sided p < 0.05. It is a consistency check, not a confirmation: other experiments have read these years.
- **No grid.** Two primaries under Holm. Every variant below is reported and can't pass in place of a primary.
- **The nearest known effect.** Buy-and-hold of the same stocks is the baseline: the two portfolios differ only by the rebalancing trades. By item 5 of Stage 1, any gap is a contrarian profit, so L reports which lags it comes from and whether that is short-term reversal.

### Reported variants (not pass/fail)

- **Delay 0,** daily and monthly; **quarterly** (every 63 days, delay 1).
- **Gross, and 20 bps a side;** the break-even cost per side (the gross gap ÷ the rebalancing turnover beyond buy-and-hold's).
- **Raw store closes:** P1 and P2 rerun on the old store's unrepaired closes (by `E['ids']`). The export's repair blanks about 100 prints in 24 years (`+0.8-pairs-trading.md`) and uses the next close to do so. A bad print that undoes itself would hand a daily rebalancer a fake profit, so the two runs bracket it.
- **L, which lags the gap comes from:** the second-order gap of item 5 for daily delay-0 rebalancing, split into lag bands 1, 2–5, 6–21, 22–63 and 64 or more days, with the remainder (exact gap minus second order).
- **20 stocks, like the judge's account:** 1,000 random 20-stock subsets of each period's 500 (seed fixed; the same subsets for every frequency), each with its own equal-weight rebalanced and buy-and-hold portfolios, gross and net. Reported: the mean gap per period, the spread across subsets, and the share of subset-years in which rebalancing won. Fractional shares are assumed: a $10,000 account in 20 stocks would trade fractions of a share most days.
- **H5, five-year holds:** the 500 chosen at the ends of 2007, 2012 and 2017, held five years each (2008–2012, 2013–2017, 2018–2022), against Maeso & Martellini's five-year horizon. Three periods; descriptive only.
- **By calendar year,** and development without 2008–2009.

### Survivorship (decides whether a pass means anything)

**The bias, plainly.** The price data holds only companies still listed in 2025 (2026 for the new store). Rebalancing buys losers. In a survivor-only store every loser survived, and many recovered, so the data holds the recoveries rebalancing profits from and leaves out the failures it loses most on. A stock sliding to zero is the worst case for rebalancing: buy-and-hold's stake shrinks with the price, while the rebalanced portfolio keeps topping it back up to 1/N on the way down. This flatters the gap, and it can't be measured directly without delisted stocks (Sharadar; the code is ready in `stockai/docs/sharadar.md`). Five checks bound it.

- **S1, deep losers (diagnostic, uses hindsight):**
  - a deep loser is a stock whose close falls at least 50% below its purchase price at any close in the period;
  - the count per period, and its share of the gap through item 5's per-stock terms Gᵢ = ½[Σ_d xᵢd² − (Σ_d xᵢd)²], grouped by whether the stock ended the period still down more than 50% or recovered above that, and by quintile of period return;
  - P1 and P2 recomputed exactly, gross and net, with deep losers removed from both portfolios at the purchase.
- **S2, synthetic failures (a stress test, as `+18.6-monthly-top10-picks.md`'s):**
  - k failing stocks a year are added to each period's 500 at equal weight (1/(500 + k));
  - each one's log return each day is the day's equal-weight mean log return plus a constant, so that it ends the period at 20% of its purchase price relative to the index, and then it delists at return L on the period's last day;
  - L ∈ {−30%, −55%, −100%}: Shumway's average performance-delisting returns (NYSE/AMEX; Nasdaq, with Warther), as `+18.6` used them, and a total loss;
  - the path has no noise of its own, which is harsh on rebalancing (a volatile path adds back some of item 5's first term);
  - reported: P1's and P2's development net gap and t for k = 0..10, and the break-even k per L at which the net gap reaches zero and at which its t falls below 3.
- **S3, the nine SPDR sector funds** (XLB, XLE, XLF, XLI, XLK, XLP, XLU, XLV, XLY): funds, not survivor-selected. The same R and B, daily and monthly, delay 1, gross and net, and E1, by calendar year 2007..2025-06 (the first full γ̂\* window ends 2006-12-29). Store closes; their dividends after about 2022-12 are missing for both portfolios alike. The README notes the two stores disagree on about a dozen ex-dividend days of XLB, XLE, XLK and XLY since late 2019; those are level shifts, not reversals.
- **S4, French's 30 industry portfolios, daily** (value-weighted within each industry; CRSP, so delistings included): R and B across the 30, daily and monthly, gross and at 5 bps, reset each calendar year, 1927..2026-08. Reported by era: 1927–1962, 1963–2007, 2008–2019, 2020–2026-08. An industry with a missing value in a year is left out of that year. The zip goes to its own folder with its sha256 recorded. French's data is a separate dataset, outside the export's unseen-month rule; its months from 2025-07 on are still reported separately.
- **S5, a real equal-weight fund:** RSP (equal-weight S&P 500, rebalanced quarterly, after its fees) minus SPY, both from the store (both lack dividends after about 2022-12), by calendar year 2006..2025-06, beside P1's portfolio minus the store's SPY over the same days. The difference is a rough size for how much survivors flatter this project's equal weight.

**Verdict tiers, fixed now.** The two thresholds are judgment calls, labeled as such. The data in hand can't measure how often the 500 most-traded stocks fail. Two failures a year is a few times what calm years should bring and less than a crisis year can.
- **No edge:** neither primary passes Holm.
- **Passes on survivor data; survivorship can explain it:** a primary passes and is consistent in 2020..2025-06, but that primary's development net gap without deep losers (S1) is at or below zero, or its S2 break-even k at L = −55% (the k at which its development net gap reaches zero) is below 2 a year.
- **Worth a survivor-free rerun:** a primary passes, is consistent, and S1 and S2 don't explain it. Even then, the real test is the Sharadar rerun.

### The two README numbers

- **"After costs"** (the file name): P1's rebalanced portfolio (daily, delay 1, the 500, 5 bps a side). Each calendar year's compounded daily return minus SPY's Yahoo adjusted-close total return over the same days (2008 from the close of 2008-01-02; 2025 to the close of 2025-06-27), averaged over the 18 years, as `-4.6` and the earlier volatility-timing test compute theirs.
  - Reported beside it: the same for buy-and-hold and for P2's portfolio, and S5.
  - Against SPY this number mostly measures equal weighting of survivors, a size tilt plus survivorship, not rebalancing. The rebalancing part is the difference from buy-and-hold's number.
- **"Before costs, vs random":** P1's rebalanced portfolio minus buy-and-hold, both gross, each calendar year's compounded return, averaged over the same 18 years.
  - **Why this analogue:** neither usual kind fits. The strategy picks no stocks, so the judge's random accounts aren't the alternative. And it chooses no exposure: random reorderings of a daily rule's identical exposures are the rule itself. What it does choose is to trade back to fixed weights, and the zero-skill alternative to that is not trading: buy-and-hold of the same stocks from the same weights.

### Holdouts: what each period has been exposed to

- **2008–2019, development:** read by every earlier experiment, though never for this question.
- **2020..2025-06:** read by nearly every experiment. A chronological check, not a holdout.
- **The 16 unseen months (2025-06-30..2026-10-19):** read once by each of about 73 earlier experiments. Run once at the end with the frozen code, reported, never confirming. Before using the new export, check that its first 5,911 days equal the old export's.
- **Untouched: closes after 2026-10-19.** The scripts are frozen by sha256 hashes in this file and in `research/excess_growth/frozen_sha256.txt` before the unseen run. No git commit or tag; Brandon hasn't asked for one. `forward.py` runs each later download through the frozen code: a 500 chosen at the close of 2026-10-19, bought at the next close, then the year-end rule. A missed download is logged as a gap, never filled later; revisions to past prices are ignored (first-seen values kept). Report at 12 months, about 250 trading days: a primary that passed must be positive there at one-sided p < 0.05.

## What the results can and can't show

- **E1 stands on its own.** It says whether γ̂\* predicts realized excess growth (it should, nearly by construction), and which written-down prediction of the gap the data matches: the claim's or the random walk's.
- **If a primary passes and survives S1 and S2:** the 500 most-traded survivors' relative returns reverted inside the year by enough to pay 5 bps a side, 2008–2019. The survivor-free rerun decides.
- **If both fail:** rebalancing to equal weight doesn't beat holding among large US stocks over one-year periods after costs, 2008–2019. It says nothing about small stocks, where reversal lives (`experiments/README.md`'s lessons), or about holds longer than H5's three descriptive periods.
- **Either way,** γ\* is real; the question is only whether buy-and-hold collects it too.

## Ways it can fool us

- **Survivor-only stocks.** The largest risk here. See "Survivorship".
- **The bid-ask bounce.** Closes alternate between bid and ask, and a portfolio rebalanced at every close earns part of the bounce on paper (Blume & Stambaugh 1983). Delay 1 removes the same-close part; delay 0 is reported beside it; 5 bps covers the spread of the most liquid stocks.
- **The bad-print repair looks a day ahead.** It removes self-undoing spikes, which would otherwise be fake rebalancing profits. The raw-closes rerun brackets it.
- **Stale prices.** A zero-volume close can't be traded, so it isn't. Its catch-up move would otherwise look like reversal.
- **The size effect.** Equal weight against SPY is mostly size plus survivorship. The test compares with buy-and-hold of the same stocks, which removes it.
- **Dividends after 2022.** Corrected with `div_fix`; S3 and S5 use store prices for both sides alike.
- **Crisis years.** The 2009 rebound of survivors that had fallen in 2008 is exactly what rebalancing profits from. Results by year, and without 2008–2009.
- **Statistically clear but small.** Daily data can make a gap of a few basis points a year significant. The bar is after costs, and points a year and the break-even cost are reported beside every t.
- **Delay 1 against the theory.** One day's delay turns the lag-1 part of the gap into nothing; that is the cost of not trading at a close you haven't seen yet. Delay 0 shows its size.
- **Choosing after looking.** Frequencies, delay, costs, universe, periods, the survivorship thresholds and E1's readings are all fixed here.

**Before any result was seen (2026-10-25):**
- **Machine:** each script ran as `systemd-run --user --scope -p MemoryMax=5G env OMP_NUM_THREADS=4 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=4 MKL_NUM_THREADS=4 python3 -I -B`, on the system Python 3.14.4 with numpy 2.3.5. No numba.
- **Untradeable:** a stock-day whose export close is missing, or whose dollar volume is zero or missing.
- **Reconstitution trades are netted.** At each year's first close, each portfolio pays 5 bps on Σ|w_new − w_old| over the union of the old and new stocks, its own drifted weights against the new purchase. That is the "sale at the period's end" and the "purchase", combined for stocks held in both. The first purchase from cash (turnover 1) is charged on the first holding day, 2008-01-03 (2025-07-01 for the unseen months). There is no final sale at 2025-06-27 or 2026-10-19.
- **Monthly and quarterly days:** decisions every 21 (63) trading days from the purchase, at offsets 21k (63k). Under delay 1 the fill is at offset 21k + 1; under delay 0, at 21k. Daily rebalancing fills at every offset from 1 to n − 1. Nothing fills on the period's last close, which is the reconstitution.
- **Delay 1, fully invested:** at a fill, the tradeable stocks get weights proportional to (1 + their return that day), scaled to the account's value at that close less what sits in untradeable stocks. A real account sizing its order from the previous close would hold a small cash residual instead; it is ignored.
- **Forced sales:** a stock whose last export close falls inside a period is sold at that close. 5 bps is charged on the sale and on spreading the proceeds over the other holdings (turnover 2 × its value). A stock whose last close is the purchase day itself would be sold at offset 1; none was.
- **Returns:** forward-filled export closes plus `div_fix` on valid closes (zero before 2022-07), as simple total returns.
- **γ̂\*:** total log returns over t0−251..t0, each stock demeaned over its valid days, missing set to 0, the covariance with 1/252, w = 1/N.
- **Random walk:** 200 draws per period with seed 20261025 + the purchase day's export index; each draw resamples n window days with replacement and runs the delay-0 daily, monthly and buy-and-hold portfolios, gross, everything tradeable.
- **E1's slope interval** uses t with 10 degrees of freedom (2.228) for the 12 development periods.
- **Statistics:** Newey-West with a Bartlett kernel. The bootstrap is circular, 21-day blocks of whole dates, 10,000 draws, seed 20261025. Each primary's p is the larger of the bootstrap p and the normal p of the NW-21 t, as the Rules say.
- **20-stock subsets and H5** have no chain across periods (each period draws new subsets, and H5 has three separate holds). So each period charges both portfolios the purchase from cash and a full sale at the end. The two portfolios pay nearly the same for both.
- **Raw-closes rerun:** the same 500 as the export run. Returns and tradeable flags come from the old store's closes and volumes, by `E['ids']`.
- **S1:** "close" is the export's forward-filled close (price, not total return). "Ended down" means the stock was still more than 50% below its purchase price at its last close in the period. The per-stock terms Gᵢ use the holding-period total log returns.

## Results (run 2026-10-25)

**What this tests.** Each calendar year, the 500 most-traded stocks are bought at equal weights. One copy is rebalanced back to equal weights; the other is held. Both pay 5 bps a side on everything they trade. P1 rebalances daily and P2 monthly, both filling the day after the decision. Stage 1 says what to expect: γ\* predicts the rebalanced portfolio's gain over the weighted average of its stocks' growth, and buy-and-hold collects most of that gain too, so the gap between the two is a contrarian profit.

**The tests, in order:**
1. **P1 and P2 (primary): both fail.** Over 2008–2019, rebalancing daily beat holding by +0.58 log points a year after costs (NW t 1.13, p 0.13), and monthly by +0.47 (t 0.92, p 0.18). Holm needed the smaller p below 0.0114. Neither reaches t 2, let alone t 3.
2. **The check years agree there's nothing:** 2020–2025-06, −0.34 (P1) and −0.28 (P2) a year.
3. **All of the development gain is 2008–2009.** Without those two years, P1 is −0.05 a year (t −0.16) and P2 −0.11.
4. **E1 (reported): γ\* is right about excess growth and wrong about the gap.** The rebalanced portfolio's realized excess growth matched γ̂\* (ratio 0.98). But buy-and-hold collected 0.80 of γ̂\* itself, so the gap was 0.18 of the claim's prediction, and the claim's reading is rejected (t −3.01).
5. **Survivorship (reported):** the survivor-free series show no premium: sector funds +0.07 a year after costs (t 0.54), French's industries at or below zero after costs in every era. Each synthetic failure a year would take 0.21–0.32 points a year from P1.

The verdict stops at test 1. Every number below is computed anyway.
