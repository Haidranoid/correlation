# Analog days: has the market been here before?

**TL;DR:** Can the weather forecasters' analogue method, finding past days whose sector moves looked like today's, forecast next week's best and worst sector funds? No: going long the 3 best-forecast sector funds and short the 3 worst made +0.2 basis points a week after costs over 2007–2019 (t 0.02; 2 needed), and lost money after 2019. A one-sector stock version trailed SPY by 11.5 points a year after costs. Verdict: no edge.

## The idea

- **What the census ruled out:** single-stock lead-lag is chance. But the census looked for *linear*, *pairwise* relationships at fixed offsets.
- **What analogues can catch:** a market-wide state, such as "energy up, tech down, high volatility", might recur and be followed by similar rotations. An analog search finds that without assuming any shape.
- **Cost:** sector funds cost 1–2 bps per side to trade, so a weak signal has a chance after costs.

## Nearest prior work

From the earlier survey and well-known literature. A separate novelty check runs alongside, and its findings will be added here.

| Work | What it found |
|---|---|
| Lorenz 1969 | The method of analogues for weather prediction; limited by how rarely the atmosphere repeats |
| Mizrach 1992; Fernández-Rodríguez, Sosvilla-Rivero & Andrada-Félix 1999 | Nearest-neighbour forecasts of exchange rates: small, unstable gains |
| Lo, Mamaysky & Wang 2000, *JF* | Technical chart patterns carry some information |
| Moskowitz & Grinblatt 1999, *JF* | Industry momentum (the baseline here) |
| Rapach, Strauss, Tu & Zhou 2019 | Industry returns predicted from lagged industry returns with machine learning, monthly |

**What's new:** a weekly nearest-analog forecast of sector rotation from the cross-sector state, tested against momentum and reversal.

**Novelty check (2026-10-21, a separate search; it changed no rules):** partly done.
- **Nearest precedent:** Zakamulin, "Industry Rotation Using Market-State Similarity" (SSRN 6792618, working paper, May 2026; read through a CXO Advisory summary).
  - It finds past *months* whose market excess return is most similar to today's (a one-number state). It goes long the industries that rose afterwards and short those that fell, on 30 French industry portfolios, 1926–2025.
  - It reports a higher Sharpe ratio and smaller drawdowns than equal weight. The signal sits mostly in the downside tail.
- **Similar-period forecasting:** Czasonis, Kritzman & Turkington 2020 (*JPM*, "Addition by Subtraction") forecast factor returns from the most similar past periods.
- **Industry rotation from lagged returns:** Rapach et al. 2019 found it works monthly but shrinks in replication.
- **Nearest-neighbour forecasting of exchange rates** (Mizrach 1992; Fernández-Rodríguez et al. 1999) gave small, unstable gains.
- **Not found:** a multi-dimensional cross-sector state, weekly forecasts, or sector funds.

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**The funds:** SPY and the 9 original SPDR sector funds (XLB, XLE, XLF, XLI, XLK, XLP, XLU, XLV, XLY). Daily closes come from the store (`c2eval.load_store`, `store_panel`), aligned to the export's trading days.

**State at day t (21 features):**
- for each sector: its 5-day and 21-day log return minus SPY's;
- SPY's 5-day and 21-day log return;
- SPY's 21-day realized volatility.

Each feature is z-scored with its own mean and sd over the 252 days up to and including t. That uses data through t only, and every past day gets the same treatment from its own trailing year.

**Analogs:**
- Candidates are past days a with a complete state and a + 5 ≤ t, so their next-5-day outcome is known at t.
- Distance is Euclidean over the 21 z-scored features.
- Take the nearest 50, accepting a candidate only if it is at least 5 trading days from every analog already accepted. Otherwise the 50 nearest would mostly be one episode.

**Forecast:** for each sector, the mean over the 50 analogs of its log return over a+1..a+5 minus SPY's.

**Portfolio:**
- Every 5 trading days on a fixed calendar, long the 3 sectors with the highest forecasts and short the 3 lowest.
- Equal weight, held 5 days.
- Costs: 2 bps per side per fund on actual turnover.

**Baselines** (same structure, calendar and costs):
- **(a) sector momentum:** rank by the 21-day return;
- **(b) sector reversal:** rank by minus the 5-day return;
- **(c) random:** 3 random sectors long and 3 short, 10,000 draws.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **2005–2019:** the analog long-short's mean weekly return, net. **Pass:** t ≥ 2.
2. **Beats the nearest known effects:** the paired weekly difference against (a) and (b). **Pass:** t ≥ 1.5 against both.
3. **2020–2025-06:** same sign.
4. **The 16 unseen months** (new export and store, once, at the end): same sign.

**Story check, reported but not pass/fail:** are the 50 analogs' next-week sector returns more alike than those of 50 random past days? If analogs carry information, their outcomes should cluster.

**Money test (the judge), a proxy for the file name**
- Each liquid stock that isn't fund-like is mapped to the sector fund it correlates with most over the trailing 252 days, recomputed every 21 days.
- Score = the rank of its sector's forecast (9 = best) + its correlation with that sector fund. The correlation is in [0, 1], so within a sector the most correlated stocks rank first and there are no ties.
- `rebalance=5`. Uniform and volatility-matched random accounts are both reported.

## The number for the file name

- **What it is:** the judge's walk-forward change over random (uniform, 0 bps) for the stock proxy above.
- **The real test is the fund long-short (tests 1–3).** `experiments/README.md`'s key notes this.

## Ways it can fool us

- **Few independent episodes:** 26 years of weekly data hold only a few hundred truly different states, and some regimes (2008, 2020) dominate the nearest neighbours. Report results with 2008–09 and 2020 left out.
- **Choices like K = 50, the 21 features and Euclidean distance** are fixed here. No other settings are tried; trying several and keeping the best is the overfitting the project avoids.

## Results (run 2026-10-21)

All returns are actual simple returns from closes. A long-short holds 1/3 in each of 3 sector funds long and 1/3 in each of 3 short, from one close to the close 5 trading days later; "bps a week" is per hold. Net is after 2 bps per side on actual turnover. The book starts 2007-07-11, not 2005 (see the first two decisions below).

### Test 1: the analog long-short, 2007-07..2019 (629 weeks)

| | Gross, bps a week (t) | Net, bps a week (t) | Turnover a week | Net, % a year | Sharpe (net) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Analog** | +4.43 (+0.61) | **+0.17 (+0.02)** | 2.13 | +0.1 | +0.01 |
| (a) Sector momentum (21-day) | −9.10 (−1.16) | −11.77 (−1.50) | 1.33 | −6.1 | −0.43 |
| (b) Sector reversal (5-day) | +8.97 (+1.23) | +3.54 (+0.49) | 2.71 | +1.8 | +0.14 |
| (c) Random, median of 10,000 | −0.01 | −5.38 | | | |

- **Pass mark:** t ≥ 2 net. **Fails:** t +0.02.
- **Against random sector books:** 22% of the 10,000 did at least as well gross, 17% net. The random books' net means run from −14.9 to +4.3 bps a week (5th–95th percentile); they pay more turnover than the analog book.
- **Turnover:** 2.13 of a possible 4 a week, so costs take 4.3 bps a week (2.2% a year). The weekly sd is 1.8%.

### The story check: do the analogs' next weeks cluster?

Each week's 50 analogs against 20 sets of 50 random past days per week, drawn from the same pool with the same 5-day spacing:

| | Weeks | Dispersion: analogs vs random (ratio) | Analogs tighter | Agreement (mean pairwise cosine): analogs vs random | Analogs agree more | Analogs from the last 63 days (random) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2007-07..2019 | 629 | 1.563% vs 1.742% (0.90) | 84% of weeks | +0.0053 vs +0.0037 | 44% of weeks | 6.3% (4.5%) |
| 2020–2025-06 | 275 | 1.579% vs 1.673% (0.94) | 75% | +0.0044 vs +0.0017 | 52% | 3.3% (1.4%) |
| Unseen months | 65 | 1.643% vs 1.736% (0.95) | 72% | +0.0024 vs +0.0019 | 46% | 3.0% (1.1%) |

- **Dispersion** is the across-day sd of each sector's next-week return against SPY, averaged over the 9 sectors. The analogs' is 5–10% lower. But analogs are days of similar volatility, and calm days are followed by calm weeks, so this is what volatility clustering gives without any directional information.
- **Agreement** is scale-free: the mean cosine similarity between pairs of the analogs' next-week sector patterns, each demeaned across sectors. A cosine of +0.005 means the 50 next weeks point in almost unrelated directions. The analogs agree slightly more than random days on average (t +2.5 to +3.7 on the weekly differences), but they agree more in only 44–52% of weeks, and consecutive weeks share many analogs, so those t-values overstate it.
- **The recent past doesn't dominate.** Only 3–6% of the 50 analogs fall in the 63 days before the forecast, so the forecast isn't momentum or reversal under another name.
- **Forecast skill:** the weekly rank correlation between the forecast and the next week's sector returns against SPY is +0.021 (t +1.25) in 2007-07..2019 and +0.000 (t +0.01) in 2020–2025-06.

### The money test (the judge, a proxy for the file name)

The proxy maps each liquid, not fund-like stock to its most correlated sector fund. The score is the rank of that sector's forecast plus the correlation, so the judge's 20 picks are the 20 stocks most correlated with the top-forecast sector: a one-sector, long-only account, rebalanced every 5 days. About 300 stocks were scored in 2007 and about 2,200 by 2024.

**Walk-forward, 18 years 2007-08..2024-25** (the judge skipped 2006-07, too little history):

| | Change over random, 0 bps | 5 bps | 20 bps | Years ahead | se | Years beating random at p < 0.1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Uniform random accounts** | **−6.56** | −6.41 | −5.98 | 7 of 18 | 4.05 | 3 of 18 |
| Volatility-matched | −4.67 | −4.51 | −4.06 | 8 of 18 | 2.89 | 3 of 18 |

- **The picks** averaged +3.9% a year, against +10.5% for the median uniform random account, +8.6% for the volatility-matched one and +11.0% for SPY.
- **By year (picks against the median uniform random account, %):** 2008–09 −58.1 vs −17.3; 2010–11 +4.4 vs +22.4; 2020–21 +28.3 vs +72.4; 2022–23 +27.6 vs +7.5; 2024–25 +22.2 vs +7.7.
- **Judge quality measures:** rank IC in actual returns z +1.17; the top-minus-bottom-decile long-short −1.06 bps a day (z +0.24); turnover 70% per rebalance.
- **A fairer null (a diagnostic added after seeing the results).** The judge's random accounts spread over all sectors, so they understate how far a one-sector account swings. Accounts that rank the 9 sectors at random each rebalance day, and otherwise follow the same rule, were replayed on the judge's own candidates; the replay reproduces the judge's yearly returns exactly. Against them the proxy was −5.6 points a year (se 3.5), ahead in 7 of 18 years, and better than 90% of them in none.

**The new store doesn't reproduce the old one exactly** (planned check; the breakdown was added after seeing it):
- Before 2019-12-20 the two stores' fund returns differ only by float rounding (at most 4 × 10⁻⁵). The analog sets are the same on 97% of days and the weekly book in 99.4% of weeks.
- From 2019-12-20 they disagree on about a dozen ex-dividend days in XLB, XLE, XLK and XLY (and one in XLU), by up to 1.0% in a day: one store adjusts for the dividend and the other doesn't.
- That alone makes the analog sets differ on 49% of days after 2019-12-20 and the weekly book in 27% of weeks. On the new store's closes, Test 3 would read −16.5 bps a week net (t −1.26) instead of −9.6 (t −0.73); Test 1 barely moves (+0.09 against +0.17).
- A nearest-neighbour set is sensitive to small changes in the state. That fragility is a reason to distrust any single good period, such as the proxy's unseen months.

### Caveats

- **Fewer years than planned.** The stores' funds start in 2005-02, so Test 1 covers 2007-07..2019 (12.5 years), not 2005–2019. The analog pool is also small at first: about 15 months of candidate days in mid-2007, 7 years by 2013.
- **Fund-like stocks in the proxy:** `c2eval.twins`' fund-like flag misses a few funds filed as stocks, and some appear among the proxy's picks (JEPQ and QQQE in 2025-04; ARTY and GARP in 2026-10). The plan named that flag, so they were kept. They affect only the proxy, not the verdict.
- **Survivorship** doesn't touch the fund long-short (the 10 funds all trade throughout). It flatters the proxy's stocks and its random accounts equally.

## Decisions made while building it

All made **before seeing any results** unless marked otherwise. Written into this file at 2026-10-21 before any weekly return or score was computed; the only data looked at by then were the 10 funds' coverage and daily moves. The one item added after the forecasts were built ("Where the book starts") is marked as such.

- **The funds start in 2005, not 1998.** Both stores (`stooq-2025-06-27` and `stooq-2026-10-19`) hold SPY and the 9 sector funds only from 2005-02-25 (5,116 rows each in the old store), so the data line above is wrong. A day's state needs 21 days of returns and then 252 days of z-score history, and the first forecast needs 50 spaced analogs, so the first forecast comes about two years after 2005-02-25. **Test 1 runs from the first formation with a forecast through 2019-12-31**, not from 2005. The 252-day window is not shortened to recover 2005–06.
- **Bad prints:** none. No daily move of any of the 10 funds is beyond ±25% in either store (the largest is XLE's 20.1%). Nothing is treated as missing for that reason.
- **One missing day:** all 10 funds lack a close on 2011-02-17 (a market-wide gap in the stores; the export has it too). That close is forward-filled from 2011-02-16, which uses only past data, as twin reversal did. The state is then computed normally on every day.
- **Features:** 5- and 21-day log returns from closes d−5 and d−21 to d; a sector's minus SPY's. SPY's realized volatility is the sample sd (ddof 1) of its 21 daily log returns d−20..d, not annualized.
- **Z-scores:** each feature's mean and sample sd over the 252 days d−251..d, all 252 present. Each candidate's vector is the one computed at that candidate's own day and is never re-standardized with today's statistics. A day's state is complete when all 21 z-scores are finite.
- **Analogs:** candidates are days a ≤ t−5 with a complete state. Euclidean distance over the 21 z-scores. Candidates are taken in order of distance (ties: the earlier day first) and accepted when at least 5 trading days from every analog already accepted (|a − a′| ≥ 5). A forecast exists only when exactly 50 are accepted.
- **Where the book starts (found while checking the analog search, before any returns):** the first complete state is 2006-03-27 and the first forecast 2007-06-12. Until 2007-07-09 the pool is so small that the spacing rule sometimes can't reach 50 analogs (10 days lack a forecast). The weekly book starts at the first calendar formation after the last such day, **2007-07-11**, so Test 1 is in practice 2007-07..2019-12. The proxy's scores are NaN on days without a forecast.
- **Forecast:** each sector's mean over the 50 analogs of log(c[a+5]/c[a]) minus SPY's. Forecasts are computed for every day, not only portfolio days, because the judge's rebalance days differ from this calendar.
- **Calendar:** portfolio formations are the export day indices divisible by 5 (index 0 = 2001-12-31). Positions are taken at the formation close (the project's convention, as in the judge with delay 0) and held to the close 5 trading days later. The old export's last index is 5910 (2025-06-27): its last formation with a complete hold is 5905, holding to 5910; the first unseen formation is 5910, holding from the 2025-06-27 close. No overlap and no gap.
- **Returns:** simple buy-and-hold returns from close to close. Each leg is equal weight (±1/3 per fund); the weekly long-short is the long leg's mean return minus the short leg's.
- **Costs:** 2 bps × Σ|w_new − w_drifted| at each formation, where w_drifted is last week's weight times that fund's growth over the hold. The book runs continuously from the first forecast, so the first week pays for building it (turnover 2) and every later week, the unseen ones included, pays real turnover.
- **Baselines:** momentum ranks by the 21-day log return d−21..d, reversal by minus the 5-day log return d−5..d; same calendar, weeks, legs and costs. Random: 10,000 independent strategies, each drawing every week 3 long and 3 different short sectors from the 9, with the same costs on their own turnover, over the same weeks. The analog's p is the share of random strategies whose mean weekly net return is at least the analog's.
- **Periods** are assigned by formation date. Test 1: first forecast..2019-12-31. Test 3: 2020-01-01..the formation at index 5905. "Without 2008–09 and 2020" drops weeks formed in 2008-01-01..2009-12-31 and 2020-01-01..2020-12-31. By-period blocks: each calendar year, and 2007–2009, 2010–2014, 2015–2019, 2020–2025-06.
- **t-statistics:** plain t of the non-overlapping weekly returns (mean / (sd / √n)).
- **Test 2** uses the paired **net** weekly differences over Test 1's weeks, since costs are part of the "same structure"; gross differences are reported too.
- **"Same sign"** in Tests 3 and 4 means positive, the direction Test 1 requires.
- **Story check**, two measures on each formation of 2007–2025-06, each against 20 sets of 50 random candidates per formation drawn from the same pool under the same 5-day spacing rule:
  - **dispersion:** the mean over the 9 sectors of the across-day sd of the next-week sector-minus-SPY log return. Analogs are similar-volatility days, so this can come out "more alike" from volatility clustering alone;
  - **agreement:** the mean pairwise cosine similarity of the days' next-week outcome vectors, each demeaned across the 9 sectors. It is scale-free, so volatility alone doesn't move it.
  - Reported: the mean of each for analogs and random, the ratio, and the share of formations where the analogs are tighter. A diagnostic too: the share of the 50 analogs within the 63 trading days before t, since overlapping windows make the recent past look alike, which matters when comparing with momentum and reversal.
- **Proxy universe:** the judge-like liquid set, recomputed on `c2eval.twins`' calendar (export days 252 + 21k) so the fund-like flag lines up: closes on ≥ 90% of the trailing 756 days, median close ≥ $5 and median dollar volume ≥ $5M over the trailing 252 days, and not fund-like at that rebalance.
- **Proxy mapping:** at each such rebalance, each stock's Pearson correlation with each of the 9 sector funds over the daily log returns of the trailing 252 days (days where both have a return; the stock needs returns on ≥ 90% of them). The highest wins. Stocks whose best correlation is ≤ 0 get no score; the correlation is clipped to at most 0.999 so the sector rank always dominates.
- **Proxy score** on day d: the rank of the stock's sector's forecast at d (1 = worst, 9 = best; never 0, because the judge drops scores ≤ 0) + that correlation, using the mapping of the latest rebalance at or before d. NaN where there is no forecast.
- **Unseen months:** the whole pipeline is recomputed on the new store and export (same day indices; the new export also starts 2001-12-31). Weeks formed from index 5910 (2025-06-27) whose hold ends by 2026-10-19 are the unseen ones. The new export's twins are read from c2eval's existing cache (`twins3_5e2aaa6254c435e6_…`), checked to exist first so nothing is written outside this experiment's folders. The weekly returns before 2025-06 from the new store are compared with the old store's, and any drift (from re-adjusted dividends) is reported, not tuned.
