# The correlation census: every pair, every offset, every move length

**TL;DR:** Across every pair of liquid US stocks, does A move first and B follow more often than chance? Over 2005–2025, at move lengths of 1–63 days and offsets up to two years, strong lagged pairs turn up at 1.0–1.2× the chance rate, and the strongest keep their sign in the next period only 50–53% of the time. Same-day co-movement is 43× chance. Verdict: lead-lag at chance; same-day co-movement real.

## Method

- **The census variable:** a stock's daily log return relative to the market (minus beta × the market's), divided by its trailing 252-day volatility, clipped at ±5. Beta, volatility and liquidity use data up to the day before only.
- **Move lengths** (A over h₁ days, B over h₂):
  - first run: {1, 5, 10, 21, 63};
  - wider run: {1, 3, 5, 10, 21, 63} with every combination, 36 pairs.
- **Offsets:** start at h₂, so A's and B's moves never share days. This was v1's leak. They run from a day to two years.
- **Correlation periods:** rolling 252/504/756/1,260 trading days, one starting every quarter from 2005, plus the whole history.
- **Universe per period:**
  - at least 90% of days with a value;
  - median price ≥ $5 and median dollar volume ≥ $5M over the period's last year;
  - 283–2,345 stocks per period.
- **Chance comparison:** 3 sign-flip nulls per matrix. B's observations are multiplied by a random ±1 per date, the same for every stock, which keeps volatility alignment and destroys only the relationship.
- **Storage:**
  - the top 100 real entries of each matrix;
  - everything above a false-discovery threshold (q = 0.05);
  - the top 50 of one null, for the replication test.
- **Engine:** cuBLASLt INT8 on the RTX 5080 (342 TOPS) for screening, exact recomputation for stored pairs. An AVX-VNNI CPU kernel is bit-identical. Validated against a Python oracle: max |r| difference 3.6e-7.
- **Scale:** the Rust rebuild ran v1's idea at full scale on the GPU: 83,879 real correlation matrices in under 6 minutes, later 358.7 billion correlations in 11 minutes.

## The null that mattered

The first chance comparison shifted B's timeline by months. It made lagged correlations look **17× chance**. That was volatility clustering: a turbulent week is turbulent for both stocks, and a time shift puts them in different regimes. The sign-flip null fixed it, and the excess fell to 1.0–1.2×. Every later result uses the sign-flip null.

**Wider census (36 move-length pairs):**
- Across all lagged matrices: 1.02× chance at |z| ≥ 4, 1.05× at |z| ≥ 5.
- The significance test fired in 94 real matrices, and in 163 on pure chance (the placebo).

**Replication:**
- Same-day pairs keep their sign in the next period 100% of the time, with r ≈ 0.78.
- Every lagged group of real pairs keeps its sign 49.7–52.7% of the time; chance-picked pairs 48.5–51%.
- The best lagged group is daily next-day pairs: 52.7%, mean next-period r +0.004.

**What the significant relationships are.** 895 stored pairs passed (444 stocks); noise passed the same test at a similar rate. They fall into three groups:
- **Municipal-bond closed-end funds** (Nuveen NAD, NAC, NZF, NEA, NVG, filed by Stooq as stocks) leading each other by a day: thinly traded funds catching up. Real, persistent, hard to trade.
- **Foreign companies' US shares** (MUFG, HMC, HSBC) following by a day, because of time zones.
- **The 2020 crash,** at a 13-day offset (regional banks, utilities): the crash came in two waves about 13 trading days apart. A one-off event.

## Also found

- **The chance comparison is too lenient at 63-day moves.** Pure-noise series show 2.4× the null's rate there. So the census's own 63→63 ratio of 2.41 is an artifact.
- **The $5 price rule looks ahead.** It uses split-adjusted prices: AAPL's 2009 close is $2.73 in the data, so AAPL was left out of the 2009 periods. This changes which stocks are compared in old periods; recent periods, which later splits barely touch, show no edge either.
- **The bad-print repair looks one day ahead** (72 cells; found by stockai's verification).
