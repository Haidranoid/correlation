# Free timing: use costly signals only on trades you make anyway

**TL;DR:** A monthly rebalancer makes trades anyway, so choosing which of the next five days to make them costs nothing extra. This test timed two investors' monthly trades with three short-term signals: buy when the stock looks cheap, sell when it looks dear, at the next day's open. It compared that with making the same trades on a random day of the window. P1 holds the 100 most-traded stocks at equal weight; P2 holds 12-1 momentum's top 20. The headline signal was the twin residual, a stock's 5-day move against the fund that tracks it best. It failed the first test. Over 2008–2019 a timed trade beat random-day execution by +4.9 bps against SPY, with a standard error of 6.2 (t = 0.8). The pass needed +5 bps with t ≥ 3. In 2020–2025 it was +4.4 bps (t = 0.3). For P2 it was worth +0.7 points a year in raw returns (standard error 0.7). That was ahead in 12 of 18 years, which meets the two-thirds rule only because 2025's half year counts (11 of 17 without it). Over the same years, plain 5-day reversal gave +6.6 bps (t = 0.8) and stockai's LightGBM +9.9 bps (t = 1.4, from mid-2010), so neither comparison signal passes either. The same-close bound, which allows bid-ask bounce, was no stronger. The one cut near significance was P1 on its own, +11.7 bps (t = 2.3). That is a side cut, not a pre-registered test, and P1 trades so little that it is worth only +0.2 points a year. In the 16 unseen months the sign held (+55.6 bps, t = 1.2), but trading everything at once on day 0 did as well (+54.8 bps). Verdict: fails test 1; timing these trades with these signals adds nothing measurable over a random day.

## The idea

- **The signals this project found real before costs:**
  - 5-day reversal: +11.8 points a year over random, but fading and costly (`+5.4-reversal-control.md`);
  - LightGBM on actual returns: the strongest daily signal (IC z = 9.15), but gone a day later (`+5.8-lightgbm-next-day-actual-returns.md`);
  - the twin residual, new here: a stock's move relative to the fund that tracks it best.
- **Why they fail as strategies:** each needs its own trades, and the costs exceed the gain.
- **The idea:** a monthly rebalancer makes trades anyway. Within a 5-day window the choice of day is free, so even a small real signal should beat picking the day at random.

## Nearest prior work

From the earlier survey and well-known literature; no new search this round.

| Work | What it says |
|---|---|
| Gârleanu & Pedersen 2013, *JF*, "Dynamic trading with predictable returns and transaction costs" | When returns are predictable and trading is costly, trade partially toward an aim portfolio, using the signal to decide how fast |
| Frazzini, Israel & Moskowitz (AQR), "Trading costs" | Patient execution keeps real costs far below academic estimates; short-term reversal is the most cost-constrained anomaly |
| Novy-Marx & Velikov 2016, *RFS* | Cost-mitigation rules (buy/hold spreads) let some anomalies survive costs |
| Institutional "alpha-aware" execution | Short-horizon signals are routinely used to time parts of large orders; rarely published |
| Medhat & Schmeling 2022, *RFS* | Low-turnover moves reverse, high-turnover moves continue |

**What's new:** not the idea. The test is whether *these* signals have value for a monthly rebalancer when they cost nothing extra, measured against random-day execution and executed at the next open.

## Rules (fixed before looking at results)

**The two investors**
- **P1, index-like:**
  - equal weight in the 100 most-traded stocks (median dollar volume over the prior 63 days, price ≥ $5, not fund-like per `c2eval.twins`);
  - rebalanced every 21 trading days to equal weight, with members refreshed at each rebalance;
  - its trades restore equal weight. Trades under 0.1% of the portfolio are ignored.
- **P2, active:**
  - 12-1 momentum, top 20, equal weight, rebalanced every 21 trading days, from stockai's saved scores (`stockai/runs/poc/baselines/momentum_12_1.npy`, aligned to the old export). This is the strategy in `+2.1-momentum-12-1.md`.
  - Names leaving are sold, names entering are bought.
  - For the unseen months, recompute 12-1 momentum on the new export with the same rule: past 252-day return skipping the last 21, judge universe.
- **Calendar:** rebalances fall every 21 trading days on a fixed calendar.

**The window**
- Each planned trade may execute on window day k = 0..4. Day 0 is the rebalance decision day.
- **Execution, the headline:** the decision is made at the close of day k and executed at the open of day k+1. This holds from 2008, since opens before 2008 are unreliable.
- **Optimistic bound, reported alongside:** executed at the close of day k, the same close the signal used. That version can include bid-ask bounce.

**Random baseline:** the expected outcome of executing on a uniformly random window day. It is the exact average over k = 0..4, with no random draws.

**Timing rule**
- At each window day's close, a buy triggers if the stock is buy-favourable that day; otherwise it waits.
- On day 4 it triggers regardless.
- Sells mirror this with sell-favourable.

**Signals** (data through the close of day k only; "thirds" are of the day's values across all liquid, not fund-like stocks):
- **S1, the twin residual (headline):** `c2eval.twin_residual(E, tw, 5, scaled=True)`, the stock's 5-day log return minus β × its twin fund's, in units of a typical 5-day residual move. Bottom third = buy-favourable; top third = sell-favourable.
- **S2, 5-day reversal:** the stock's 5-day log return minus the export's `market` over the same days. Thirds as S1.
- **S3, LightGBM on actual returns:** stockai's saved daily scores (`stockai/runs/poc/baselines/lgbm_ret/scores.npy`; available 2010-06..2024-06, higher = expected to rise). Top third = buy-favourable; bottom third = sell-favourable.

**Measured per trade**
- END = the close of day 5, after which the timed and random portfolios are identical. e = the execution point.
- **Buy:** captured = log(P_END / P_e), and Δ = captured − the average of the same over the 5 window days' execution points.
- **Sell:** the negative of that.
- **Market-relative version,** for the statistical tests: subtract SPY's log return over the same points (SPY has opens in the store).
- **Raw version,** for money.
- Each trade is weighted by its dollar size.
- **Portfolio effect:** the sum of Δ × trade size ÷ portfolio value over a year. That is the extra yearly return from timing, against random-day execution, at no extra cost, since it's the same trades.

## Tests, in order (the verdict stops at the first failure; every number is still computed)

1. **Per trade.** S1, next-open, P1 and P2 pooled, 2008–2019: mean market-relative Δ in bps, with standard errors clustered by rebalance date. **Pass:** ≥ +5 bps with t ≥ 3.
2. **Later years.** 2020–2025-06 (old export): same sign, with t ≥ 2.
3. **Portfolio.** P2's extra yearly return against random-day execution (S1, next-open, raw), walk-forward 2008–2025. **Pass:** ahead in at least two-thirds of years.
4. **The 16 unseen months** (new export, once, at the end): same sign.

**Also reported:**
- S2 and S3;
- the same-close bound;
- P1 alone;
- buys and sells separately;
- executing everything on day 0, as a second reference.

## The number for the file name

- **What it is:** P2's average extra yearly return against random-day execution, in points a year: S1, next-open, raw, 2008–2025.
- **How it differs:** unlike the other files, "random" here is a random execution day for the same trades, not a random account. `experiments/README.md`'s file-name key notes this.

## Ways it can fool us

- **Bid-ask bounce:** the same-close bound includes it. The next-open headline avoids most of it.
- **Waiting costs drift:** the timed rule waits longer on average than a random day. Δ in raw returns includes that cost.
- **Survivorship:** the same trades are timed in both arms, so it affects them alike.
- **Overlap with known effects:** S2 is plain reversal. S1 and S3 are partly reversal too, so their comparison with S2 is the interesting part.

### What the rule actually does

From `diagnostics_old.txt` (added after the results):

| Trades (S1) | Day 0 | Day 1 | Day 2 | Day 3 | Day 4 | Favourable on day 0 | Opposite third on day 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 buys | 48% | 9% | 6% | 5% | 33% | 48% | 22% |
| P1 sells | 47% | 9% | 6% | 5% | 34% | 47% | 21% |
| P2 buys | 32% | 9% | 7% | 7% | 45% | 32% | 34% |
| P2 sells | 33% | 8% | 6% | 6% | 47% | 33% | 32% |

- **For P1 the signal mostly says "now".** Its buys top up last month's laggards and its sells trim last month's winners. So about half its trades are already in the favourable third on day 0, against a third by chance. S2 does the same (51–53%).
- **For P2 the signal is close to neutral on day 0,** and nearly half its trades wait for the forced day 4.
- **No bad prints drive the result.** The largest single contributions to P2 are real moves: CRIS in June 2021 (−1.8 points), RIOT after the bitcoin funds launched in January 2024 (−1.5), VERU in August 2022 (+1.5). The ten largest sum to −2.9 points, against a total of +11.9 over the 210 rebalances.

## Decisions made while building it

All of these were made before any outcome (a Δ, a captured return, a per-year figure) was computed, unless marked otherwise.

- **Calendar (before results):** rebalances use the twins' fixed calendar, export day indices 252 + 21k. That way the twin, β and the fund-like flags are fresh on day 0, and the window never straddles a twin refresh. The first rebalance is the first twin refresh on or after 2008-01-01 (2008-01-03). The last is the last whose day 5 is on or before 2025-06-27 (day 0 2025-06-13). The new export has the same days up to 2025-06-27, so the calendar continues unbroken into the unseen months; their first day 0 is 2025-07-16, not 2025-06-30.
- **"Liquid" (before results):** the judge's liquid rule applied point in time on day 0: closes on at least 90% of the trailing 252 days, median close ≥ $5 and median dollar volume ≥ $5M over those days, and not fund-like at that twin refresh. The same set is used on all five window days. It is the pool for the signals' thirds and P2's universe.
- **P1 (before results):** among stocks with a close ≥ $5 on day 0, at least 57 of the 63 dollar volumes finite and not fund-like, the 100 with the highest median dollar volume over days −62..0. Weights drift with closes from the previous rebalance's day 0 to this one's. Trades restore 1% each; members leaving are sold in full; trades under 0.1% of the portfolio are dropped, and untraded names keep their drifted weight.
- **P2 (before results):** the saved momentum file is NaN from 2024-06 on (stockai locked its last year), but test 3 runs to 2025-06. The scores are recomputed with stockai's own `momentum_scores` (log close[d−21] / close[d−252]), checked equal to the saved file wherever it has a value, and used throughout. As in the judge, the top 20 need a positive score, so in bad markets P2 can hold fewer than 20 and keep the rest in cash. Universe: the liquid set above, so fund-like stocks are dropped (the brief says to drop them from every universe; the judge did not). Buys are 1/20 of the portfolio. A sale is the name's drifted weight since the last rebalance, assuming the book was equal weight then.
- **Signals with no value (before results):** the plan's literal rule ("otherwise it waits") sends every trade a signal can't score to day 4, which measures drift, not the signal. A trade whose stock has no signal value on day 0 is untimable. It is left out of that signal's per-trade statistics, and adds Δ = 0 to the portfolio effect (it is executed as random). A missing value on a later window day just means "not favourable", so it waits. Coverage is reported.
- **Thirds (before results):** the 1/3 and 2/3 quantiles of that day's signal values across the liquid set. S1 and S2: buy-favourable at or below the lower cut, sell-favourable at or above the upper. S3 the other way round. The traded stock need not be in the liquid set; it is compared with the set's cuts.
- **Prices (before results):** the export's closes and the store's closes agreed exactly on a 300-stock sample from 2008 on, so the export's close is used for closes and END (close of day 5), and the store's open for next-open execution. SPY is found by ticker (SPY, kind etf) in each store; it is id 9673 in the old one. Next-open: window day k executes at the open of day k+1. Same-close: at the close of day k.
- **Bad opens (before results):** store prices are unrepaired. An open is treated as missing if it is more than 0.25 log points away from both the previous close and the same day's close. Opens equal to the close are kept. A trade missing any of its prices, stock or SPY, is dropped from both arms. Counts are reported.
- **Weights (before results):** each trade is weighted by its size as a fraction of its portfolio, and P1 and P2 are taken as equal-value portfolios. So P2's 5% trades weigh more than P1's in the pooled test; P1 alone is reported too.
- **Standard errors (before results):** clustered by rebalance date. For a weighted mean m, SE² = G/(G−1) × Σ_g (Σ_{i∈g} wᵢ(Δᵢ − m))² / (Σ w)².
- **The portfolio number (before results):** each rebalance date's effect is x = Σ Δ_raw × size. The yearly figure is the mean x × 12 (252 / 21 rebalances a year), in points; its standard error is 12 × sd(x) / √G. Per-year rows are the sums of x over the rebalances whose day 0 is in that calendar year.
- **Years (before results):** calendar years by day 0. Test 1 covers 2008-01-01..2019-12-31; test 2 covers 2020-01-01 to the last window ending by 2025-06-27. Test 3 counts 18 years, 2008..2025, with 2025's half year counted as a year, so it needs 12 ahead.
- **Test 4 (before results):** "same sign" means the test-1 measure (S1, next-open, market-relative, P1 and P2 pooled, per trade) has the sign it had in 2008–2019. P2's raw portfolio number is reported there too.
- **S3's span (before results):** S3 exists only 2010-07..2024-06, so it is compared on rebalances inside that span, and S1 and S2 are shown on the same span too. A rebalance is inside if S3 scores most of its trades on day 0: 168 rebalances, 2010-07-06..2024-06-11. In the all-years tables, S3's trades outside the span are untimable.
- **Twins on the new export (before results):** built with `c2eval.twins(E_new)` as the brief asks. The library writes its cache to the shared experiments folder (`twins3_5e2aaa6254c435e6_…npz`), outside this experiment's own folder.
- **Diagnostics (after results; analysis only, no rule changed):** after the first results, three checks were added. They show which window day the rule picks, by portfolio and side; how sensitive test 3 is to the 2025 half year and to the execution price; and the largest single contributions, checked against the prices for bad prints. They are in `diagnostics_old.txt` and the "What the rule actually does" part of the results.
