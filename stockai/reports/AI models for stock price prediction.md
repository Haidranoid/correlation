# Trading costs erase next-token stock models' edge

People have built exactly this kind of model, and it works until trades cost money. StockGPT (2024) trained a 0.93M-parameter GPT on 402 daily-return tokens; Kronos (2025, published at AAAI 2026) released MIT-licensed models of 4–102M parameters, pretrained on 12 billion tokenized price bars with data ending in June 2024; and a 2025 study pretrained more than 600 Chronos and TimesFM models from scratch on daily returns from 94 countries. The best fit for the goal is neither a text LLM nor a diffusion model but a small (1–10M-parameter) autoregressive transformer over volatility-scaled return tokens. An independent NeurIPS 2024 ablation found that deleting the language model from LLM forecasters cost no accuracy in most comparisons, text LLMs memorize historical prices, and diffusion forecasters sample tens to thousands of times more slowly and help with scenarios and volatility rather than direction. Out of sample on US stocks (2001–2023), return-token models reach **51–52% daily directional accuracy and gross equal-weighted long-short Sharpe ratios of roughly 4–8**, which CatBoost matches and plain regression nearly matches, because the edge is short-term reversal in small, illiquid stocks; **every daily strategy in the largest study turns negative at 20 bps per trade**, and zero-shot foundation models forecast worse than a constant zero. The lack of published net-of-cost successes is real evidence, since the incentives favor publishing gross wins and this exact model class has been tested with costs at scale, but nobody has published the long-only, low-turnover, large-cap version that correlation2's $10,000 harness measures, so that test is a genuine gap. On one RTX 5080, compute is not the constraint: a StockGPT-sized model needs about a minute per pass over the 26.7M US bars. The real constraints are survivorship-free data, per-stock costs and above all statistical power, because **one held-out year gives a Sharpe-1 strategy a t-statistic near 1**, so the locked 2024–25 year has to sit inside a multi-year walk-forward with a ledger of every configuration tried.

## StockGPT and Kronos already predict the next return token

A large language model reads text as tokens from a fixed vocabulary and outputs a probability for every possible next token. Training uses a cross-entropy loss, which penalizes the model whenever the token that actually came next got a low probability. To generate, the model samples a token, appends it and repeats; that loop is what "autoregressive" means.

Prices can be forced into that loop in three ways. LLMTime writes prices as digit strings and lets an off-the-shelf LLM continue them, so the model never knows it is reading prices ([LLMTime](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.07820)). Chronos rescales each series and cuts the value range into 4,094 buckets, so each day becomes a bucket ID that behaves like a word ID ([Chronos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07815)). StockGPT does the same with daily returns, in 50-basis-point buckets (a basis point, or bp, is 0.01%). Kronos trains a small network to compress each day's open-high-low-close-volume (OHLCV) bar into a learned discrete code, then trains a GPT-like model to predict the next bar's code ([Kronos GitHub](https://github.com/shiyu-coder/Kronos)).

Most other time-series "foundation models" (large models pretrained on many series so they can forecast new ones) skip discrete tokens. They embed patches of 16–64 values as if each patch were a word. Some, like TimesFM, then output numbers directly; others, like Sundial, sample the next patch from noise with a flow-matching head ([Sundial](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00816)). Diffusion models are not next-token predictors at all. They start from noise shaped like the whole future window and denoise it 50–100 times ([ProbTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.07446v5)).

The first practical lesson is to predict the next return, not the next price. Tomorrow's price is almost exactly today's, so a model that outputs "same as today" already has tiny price error. On daily S&P 500 index levels, the best LSTM in one study had a mean absolute error of 21.43, against **10.54 for simply repeating the last value** ([Elliot & Hsu](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.05751)). The trap is common. Of 612 deep-learning asset-management papers from 2018–2023, 73.4% forecast prices, and **only 4.0% of those compared against a random-walk forecast** ([Reis, Serra & Gama](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.01591v1)). Every model worth discussing here predicts returns, or how stocks rank against each other on the same day.

StockGPT is the purest version of the idea and the easiest to rebuild ([StockGPT v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.05101v3)). Each daily return becomes one of 402 tokens: 50-bp bins from −100% to +100%. A decoder with 4 layers, 4 attention heads, 128-dimensional embeddings and a 256-day context, **about 0.93M parameters** in total, learns to predict the next token. The forecast is the probability-weighted average of the bin midpoints. StockGPT was trained once, on a MacBook, on CRSP NYSE returns from 1926–2000, and tested on 2001–2023. No code or weights were released, and the research found no independent replication through October 2026.

Kronos is the closest thing to "an LLM for candlesticks". It is also the only open foundation model pretrained on full OHLCV bars, the user's own data type ([Kronos paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02739); [AAAI-26](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/39730)). Its tokenizer turns each bar into one 20-bit code, split into a coarse and a fine 10-bit subtoken. Three predictors are public under the MIT license: mini (4.1M parameters, 2,048-bar context), small (24.7M) and base (102.3M). Small and base see 512 bars; the 499M large model is not public. The corpus is 12.1 billion bars from more than 40 exchanges, including 8,725 Nasdaq and 7,073 NYSE stocks and ETFs from 2000. The paper says the data "extends up to June 2024", and every test starts in July 2024, so **at most one bar, 2024-06-28, overlaps the user's holdout**. The authors report an average return RankIC of 0.062–0.063 for the released models, against 0.042 for the best model without pretraining. (RankIC is the correlation between predicted and realized rankings of assets on a day, averaged over days.) The gain is large in relative terms but small in absolute ones.

Related work fills in around these two. General foundation models such as Chronos, TimesFM, Moirai and Toto were pretrained on corpora in which finance is a sliver: 0.10% of Moirai's 27.6 billion observations, and 1,715 series out of Time-MoE's 309 billion points ([Moirai](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.02592); [Time-MoE](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.16040)). Rahimikia, Ni and Wang pretrained Chronos and TimesFM architectures from scratch on daily excess returns from 94 countries, one model per year, and released 613 of them as FinText-TSFM ([Rahimikia et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18578v1); [FinText](https://huggingface.co/FinText)). Preferred Networks continued TimesFM's pretraining on stock, FX and crypto prices ending before 2023 ([Fu, Hirano & Imajo](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.09880v1)). VAIOM (July 2026) is a 0.9M-parameter decoder over volatility-normalized return buckets for hourly FX ([VAIOM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13929)). Order-book GPTs such as MarketGPT and J.P. Morgan's TradeFM need message-level exchange feeds, so they cannot use daily bars. Their composite per-event tokens and ticker-ID tokens are still worth borrowing ([MarketGPT](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.16585); [TradeFM](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.23784v1)).

## The best fit is a 1–10M-parameter return-token transformer

A text LLM is the worst-value choice for forecasting from prices alone. In the strongest independent test, Tan and colleagues at NeurIPS 2024 took three published LLM forecasters: Time-LLM on LLaMA, and CALF and OneFitsAll on GPT-2. They replaced the language model with nothing, with a single attention layer or with a single transformer block. **The stripped-down versions won 26 of 26 comparisons against Time-LLM**, 22 of 26 against CALF and 19 of 26 against OneFitsAll ([Tan et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16964)). On the ETTh1 benchmark, Time-LLM used 6,652M parameters and 181 minutes of training; its LLM-free version used 0.198M parameters and under a minute. A randomly initialized CALF beat the pretrained one 8 to 3. Time-LLM's default configuration also needs 32,136–39,004 MiB of GPU memory, about twice the RTX 5080's 16 GB ([Time-LLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.01728)). A 2026 preprint does report gains from LLMs when generalizing across domains ([Qiu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14744)). But a controlled 2026 study found that LLM backbones "do not consistently surpass" models pretrained on time series ([Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08818)), and the research found no paper showing an LLM extracting tradeable signal from daily returns.

Memorization makes text-LLM backtests on this data impossible to interpret. GPT-4o's training data ends in October 2023. Before that cutoff it forecast the daily S&P 500 level with 0.61% error and 80.58% directional accuracy; after it, with 16.87% error and 45.70% accuracy. Telling the model to ignore later knowledge did not stop the recall ([Lopez-Lira, Tang & Zhu](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14765v2)). Any LLM trained after 2025-06-27 has read the entire holdout. LLM trading agents tested after their cutoffs did no better than buy-and-hold. From 2025-03-03 to 2025-06-30, 13 agents returned −2.8% to +2.5%, against +0.4% for buying and holding ([StockBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02209v1)). Over two decades and more than 100 symbols, earlier reported advantages "deteriorate significantly" ([FINSABER](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07078)).

The one documented job for LLMs is reading text. GPT-4 scores of overnight headlines predicted next-day returns with a Sharpe ratio of 2.97 before costs, from October 2021 to May 2024. (The Sharpe ratio is annualized mean return divided by annualized volatility.) That Sharpe decayed from 6.54 in late 2021 to 1.22 in early 2024, and the strategy **turned unprofitable at 20 bps round-trip costs** ([Lopez-Lira & Tang](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619)). That route needs two things Stooq does not supply: timestamped news, and a language model whose training predates the test, such as the point-in-time ChronoGPT ([He, Lv, Manela & Wu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.21206)).

Diffusion and flow-matching models answer a different question. They output a distribution of plausible futures, which helps with fat tails, volatility, position sizing and stress tests. But a well-calibrated distribution centered near zero scores well on probabilistic metrics while carrying no directional signal. In the independent ProbTS benchmark, these models were also slow and weak at long horizons ([ProbTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.07446v5)). Published times per forecast run from 870–6,724 ms for TimeGrad and 90–513 ms for CSDI, against about 3 ms for a one-step generator; the source does not confirm the hardware ([TARFVAE](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22853)). The field has since moved generative heads onto autoregressive transformers. Sundial, an ICML 2025 oral paper, samples each next patch with a small flow-matching head, and **in its own ablation the diffusion objective did worst**: zero-shot MSE of 0.314, against 0.296 for plain regression and 0.290 for flow matching ([Sundial](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00816)). Synthetic data does not rescue a directional model either. A controlled 2026 study compared augmentation against a same-size null augmentation. It found that augmentation helps variance-dominated tasks such as volatility forecasting but "degrades performance" in "near-efficient directional prediction" ([Sohm et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14498)).

That leaves the small purpose-built autoregressive transformer, which is what StockGPT and Kronos are. The finance-specific evidence favors discrete return tokens over regression. In Kronos's ablation at similar size, cross-entropy over discrete tokens reached a price-forecast IC of 0.0431, against 0.0212 for a squared-error head and 0.0179 for a Gaussian-likelihood head ([Kronos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02739)). IC, the information coefficient, is the same-day correlation across assets between predicted and realized values. Among models pretrained from scratch on returns, binned Chronos-small earned a Sharpe of 5.42 before costs, against 3.66 for patch-based TimesFM-20M at the same 512-day window ([Rahimikia et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18578v1)). VAIOM's 0.9M-parameter decoder beat LightGBM on likelihood for hourly FX in both 2025 test halves, by 0.029 and 0.043 bits per event, and its smallest architecture did best on validation. It reported no IC, Sharpe or cost-adjusted result ([VAIOM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13929)).

For stockai, the natural design is StockGPT's loop on a variable correlation2 already computes: each stock's market-residual daily log return, minus its trailing 252-day mean, divided by its trailing 252-day residual volatility and clipped at ±5 ([correlation2 test-results.md](/home/bhoult/programming/correlation/test-results.md)). Binned at 0.1 standard deviations, it yields 101 tokens that mean the same thing for a utility and a biotech. StockGPT's fixed 50-bp bins, by contrast, give a large cap with 1–1.5% daily volatility only 4–6 bins across ±1 standard deviation. The 0.1σ width is a reasoned default, not a tested one. Patching saves compute at long contexts without adding signal, and a Student-t or quantile head is a sensible second experiment.

Architecture is still second-order. In the largest US study, CatBoost was the strongest benchmark. It beat every zero-shot and fine-tuned foundation model and was matched only by from-scratch Chronos given global data, factor features and synthetic augmentation (Sharpe 6.78 against 6.46). Plain Huber-loss regression reached a gross Sharpe near 4.9 ([FoFI 2026 paper](http://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2026/files/2026/03/FoFI-2026-020-Eghbal-Rahimikia.pdf)). On Microsoft Qlib's China benchmark, gradient-boosted trees win on hand-built features, and sequence models win modestly on raw price windows (HIST RankIC 0.0667 against LightGBM's 0.0499). Deep models' information ratios swing by ±0.25–0.47 across 20 random seeds, against ±0.00 for the trees ([Qlib benchmarks](https://github.com/microsoft/qlib/blob/main/examples/benchmarks/README.md)). All top three finishers in G-Research's crypto contest used LightGBM and credited feature engineering ([G-Research](https://www.gresearch.com/news/wrapping-up-the-g-research-crypto-forecasting-competition/)). One documented top solution in Jane Street's 2024 contest used a GRU with daily online updates, after "time-series transformers, cross-symbol attention and embeddings didn't work" ([solution write-up](https://github.com/evgeniavolkova/kagglejanestreet/blob/master/solution.md)). A LightGBM or CatBoost model on the same inputs is a required baseline.

## Gross Sharpe of 4 to 8 turns negative at 20 bps

Used off the shelf ("zero-shot", with no training on the new data), general foundation models fail on stock returns. The largest independent test used US daily excess returns, out of sample over 2001–2023, with yearly refits. Zero-shot Chronos-large had an out-of-sample R² of −1.37%, and TimesFM-500M −2.80%. An R² below zero means forecasting worse than always predicting a zero return. A dozen more model families scored negative R² at every window, with 48.2–51.4% directional accuracy ([Rahimikia et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.18578)). Toto, trained mostly on observability telemetry, still produced the best zero-shot long-short Sharpe before costs: 6.22, with an R² of −114%. So portfolio Sharpe here measures how stocks are ranked against each other, mostly through reversal, not forecast accuracy.

Other tests point the same way. On five US mega-caps from August 2024, foundation models beat a random walk by "small and sparse" margins, with skill "of order 10^-3" ([Noguer i Alonso & Franklin](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27100)). Fine-tuning, which continues a pretrained model's training on new data, "mostly" made models worse in the largest study. Fine-tuned Chronos also trailed zero-shot Chronos on Nasdaq-100 and S&P 500 data ([Łaniewski & Ślepaczuk](https://aisel.aisnet.org/isd2014/proceedings2025/datascience/18)). Preferred Networks' finance-pretrained TimesFM reached a market-neutral S&P 500 Sharpe of 1.68 from 2023, with no trading costs. That is **barely above the 1.58 of a one-lag autoregression** ([Fu, Hirano & Imajo](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.09880v1)). Kronos did best in the one independent test run after its cutoff, which measured forecast loss rather than trading. On 12 large caps over 2025-01-17 to 2026-07-23, it tied a per-stock neural network on quantile loss (6.55 against 6.56), while Chronos-2, TimesFM-2.5 and Moirai trailed ([Yu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26792v1)). Unreviewed community tests on crypto found its direction calls "close to random" ([Kronos issue #325](https://github.com/shiyu-coder/Kronos/issues/325)).

Training from scratch on returns, which is stockai's plan, works much better before costs. Rahimikia's yearly from-scratch Chronos-small earned 36.84% a year at a gross Sharpe of 5.42. With global data, factor features and synthetic augmentation, it reached 6.78 at 51.74% accuracy, against CatBoost's 6.46 at 51.16% ([FoFI 2026 paper](http://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2026/files/2026/03/FoFI-2026-020-Eghbal-Rahimikia.pdf)). These are equal-weighted decile long-short portfolios rebalanced daily: buy the tenth of stocks with the highest forecasts, short the lowest tenth, and weight every stock equally. The predictability sits where trading is expensive. CatBoost's R² was +0.51% with 52.29% accuracy in small caps, but −0.37% and 50.76% in large caps. Plain regression's Sharpe near 4.9 says most of the edge is generic short-horizon reversal.

Tiny correlations make big Sharpe ratios because breadth multiplies them. Under the fundamental law of active management, the information ratio is about IC × √breadth. A daily IC of 0.005 across 500 stocks then implies a gross information ratio near 1.8, and an IC of 0.02 one near 7 (derived; [CFI](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/capital-markets/fundamental-law-of-active-management/)). Recent evidence on liquid stocks sits at the bottom of that range. S&P 500 models using OHLCV inputs alone reached daily ICs of 0.001–0.003 over 2021–2025, with mostly negative excess returns ([AlphaAgent](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16789)).

Costs then decide. **Charging 20 bps drove every daily long-short strategy in Rahimikia's study to a negative Sharpe** ([Rahimikia et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.18578)). CatBoost's average fell from 6.44 to −3.62, and to −13.59 at 40 bps. The most cost-resilient model, factor-augmented TimesFM-20M, fell from 7.68 to −2.03 at 20 bps. Under size-based costs of about 11 bps for large caps and 21 bps for small ones, it reached −1.87. The extracted text does not say whether the 20 bps is charged per one-way trade or per unit of turnover.

StockGPT's headline result comes apart the same way ([StockGPT v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.05101v3)). Equal-weighted, it earned 119% a year at a Sharpe of 6.5. Value-weighted (weights proportional to company size), that becomes 27% at a Sharpe of 1.0, with a −76% drawdown. When trades wait a day, it earns 26% equal-weighted, and **5.8% at a Sharpe of 0.2 value-weighted**. Its portfolio loads heavily on short-term reversal, and its only cost test assumes 5 bps per trade. At its own worst-case turnover of 400% a day, a 21-bp small-cap cost would consume about 215% a year, against 119% gross (derived). Zero-shot Chronos-tiny, run on the residual returns of about 550 large US stocks, turned negative at 3 bps of slippage ([Valeyre & Aboura](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.09394v1)).

The pattern has a mechanical cause. Short-horizon reversal profits are largely bid-ask bounce: measuring with bid prices "eliminates all profits from price reversals for NASDAQ-NMS stocks" ([Conrad, Gultekin & Kaul](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bes/jnlbes/v15y1997i3p379-86.html)). The strategy is effectively paid for supplying liquidity ([Nagel](https://nber.org/papers/w17653)). It has also decayed. A daily contrarian strategy earned 1.38% a day in 1995 and 0.13% in 2007 ([Khandani & Lo](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/conference/2007/liquidity/Khandani_Lo.pdf)). Reversal among the 500 largest stocks is worth about 2 bps a month, against about 2% a month in microcaps ([Medhat & Schmeling](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/31278/1/MS_short_term_mom_v27.pdf)). Daily machine-learning strategies on the S&P 500 netted over 200% a year in the 1990s but lost 14–25% a year after costs in 2010–2015 ([Krauss, Do & Huck](https://www.econstor.eu/bitstream/10419/130166/1/856307327.pdf)). In Ken French's data, the Sharpe of the daily-rebalanced 20-day reversal factor fell from 2.42 over 1963–2014 to 0.38 over 2015–2025 ([French Data Library](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/data_library.html), computed by the research team). A model trained on 2005–2024 will spend much of its capacity on an effect that is mostly gone.

Most published numbers also measure a different game from correlation2's. Its harness puts $10,000 into the top 20 predicted gainers each day, then compares the result with SPY, QQQ, and random accounts that trade the same candidates under the same rules and costs ([correlation2 ANALYSIS.md](/home/bhoult/programming/correlation/ANALYSIS.md)). Some evidence carries over. The long leg consistently beat the short leg across model classes in Rahimikia's study, and deep-learning profits "remain in long positions" ([Avramov, Cheng & Metzker](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/machine-learning-vs-economic-restrictions-evidence-from-stock-ret/)), so a long-only test keeps the signal. StockGPT's value-weighted and delayed results are the relevant ones for a liquid universe. The closest published analogue is Kronos's own test on Chinese A-shares. A long-only portfolio swapped at most 5 of its 50 names a day on the CSI 300 (10 of 200 on the CSI 800), held each name at least 5 days and paid 0.15% per trade, and the released models earned **18–19% a year above those indices** ([Kronos paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02739)). The test covers one market from July 2024 and has not been replicated, and a maintainer calls its signals reversal-like ([Kronos issue #33](https://github.com/shiyu-coder/Kronos/issues/33)). Still, its turnover-limited construction is the template to copy.

Simple baselines set a high bar. In French's data, US market excess returns had a Sharpe of 0.76 over 2015–2025. That beats every classic price-only long-short rule even before costs: 12-2 momentum scored 0.16, and one-month reversal 0.02 ([French Data Library](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/data_library.html), computed by the research team). Beating SPY and QQQ at similar risk after costs is the real test. correlation2's own lead-lag run shows the arithmetic. It made 15.6% before costs, against 13.6% for SPY and 14.3% for QQQ, then **−8.0% at 5 bps and −53.7% at 20 bps**. Replacing 20 names daily costs about 2 × 5 bps × 250 days, or roughly 25% a year, at 5 bps ([correlation2 ANALYSIS.md](/home/bhoult/programming/correlation/ANALYSIS.md)). Those cost levels are realistic for a small account. Measured retail round trips on small orders cost 0.07–0.46% against the quote midpoint, depending on the broker, and 0.62% at the full quoted spread ([Schwarz et al.](https://cqa.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Huang_Actual-Retail-Price_CQA.pdf)).

## Missing published wins are evidence, with one untested gap

The skeptic has a real case that working models go unpublished. Across 97 published predictors, average returns were 26% lower out of sample and 58% lower after publication. The authors attribute the 32-point difference to investors trading on the published result ([McLean & Pontiff](https://www.gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf)). Whoever finds a working edge therefore has a reason to trade it quietly. Professional versions of the problem also run on data a hobbyist lacks, such as the anonymized production features in Jane Street's Kaggle contest ([Numerai forum write-up](https://forum.numer.ai/t/autoencoder-and-multitask-mlp-on-new-dataset-from-kaggle-jane-street/4338)). Even there, achievable scores are tiny: Numerai's tutorial model averages a per-period rank correlation of 0.025–0.029 ([Numerai forum](https://forum.numer.ai/t/v5-atlas-data-release/7576)). Some edges are also too small for institutions to bother with. Novy-Marx and Velikov put a high-frequency combination strategy's capacity at $0.38 billion, against $5.16 billion for momentum, and a $10,000 account needs no capacity at all ([Novy-Marx & Velikov](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/ToAatTC.pdf)). Capacity is not what binds a small account, though. The same study charges effective bid-ask spreads, which small accounts pay too, and short-term reversal still lost 1.28% a month after them.

Four facts make the silence informative anyway.

First, the literature leans toward positive results, and those shrink on contact. 65% of 452 published anomalies fail once microcaps are controlled for ([Hou, Xue & Zhang](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v33y2020i5p2019-2133..html)). By multiple-testing standards, "most claimed research findings in financial economics are likely false" ([Harvey, Liu & Zhu](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P118_and_the_cross.PDF)). Across 888 Quantopian algorithms, backtest Sharpe explained under 2.5% of the variance in live Sharpe ([Wiecki et al.](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/208/All%20that%20Glitters%20Is%20Not%20Gold%20-%20Comparing%20Backtest%20and%20Out-of-Sample%20Performance%20on%20a%20Large%20Cohort%20o.pdf)). A third-party re-run of the MASTER stock transformer measured an IC of 0.0215, near the bottom of its table, and MASTER's authors had already conceded that their published test data were mis-processed ([R&D-Agent(Q)](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15155v2); [MASTER README](https://github.com/SJTU-DMTai/MASTER)).

Second, this model class has been tested with costs at scale, and it failed. The evidence includes Rahimikia's 600-plus from-scratch models and the daily S&P 500 machine-learning literature after 2010. It also includes a Markowitz-ML portfolio whose gross Sharpe of about 2.0 "immediately dives into negative expected return territory" after costs, because it leans on "fleeting small-scale characteristics" such as small-stock reversal ([Jensen, Kelly, Malamud & Pedersen](http://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2024/program/paper/SYTF3aaz)). That is evidence of absence, not absence of evidence.

Third, the mechanism is understood. The gross alpha in daily price prediction is largely the bid-ask bounce and the liquidity premium described above. A better forecaster cannot keep that alpha, because it pays the same spread the moment it trades.

Fourth, researchers do publish net-of-cost successes, and those successes share a profile. A news-momentum signal "survives transaction cost" while "a leading hedge fund still actively trades on it" ([Wharton discussion slides](https://jacobslevycenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/ExpectedReturnAndLLM_Discussion_SophiaZhengziLi_NoPause.pdf)). LSTM strategies built on 320 published anomalies netted up to 1.42% a month with monthly value-weighted rebalancing ([Azevedo, Hoegner & Velikov](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=75544)). Robeco's researchers report, per the publisher's summary, net alpha above 6% a year from a composite of short-term signals traded with a "buy 10 / hold 50" band, although each signal alone lost money at 25 bps ([CFA Institute summary](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2023/beyond-fama-french-factors-alpha-from-short-term-signals)). Deep statistical arbitrage on the 550 largest US stocks kept a net Sharpe near 1 ([Guijarro-Ordonez, Pelger & Zanotti](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.04028v2)). **The published winners use text, fundamentals or composite signals, monthly horizons, turnover bands or liquid universes. None is a daily, price-only next-token model.**

The gap is narrow and specific. The research found no study that runs any of these models through a test like correlation2's: a $10,000 long-only account over 2024-06-28 to 2025-06-27, against SPY, QQQ and turnover-matched random accounts. It also found none that reports after-cost results for a weekly or monthly next-token model. StockGPT's monthly version earned 13.5% a year at a Sharpe of 0.9 equal-weighted, against 8.8% for short-term reversal, but that figure is gross, with no turnover analysis ([StockGPT v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.05101v3)). stockai can therefore produce a genuinely new result. The priors say it will most likely be a well-measured negative. A positive result is more likely a bug than an edge until it survives leakage canaries and the random accounts.

## One RTX 5080 suffices; data, costs and statistics bind

### Stooq's 26.7M bars need four repairs before training

The data in hand is larger than the models need and dirtier than it looks. correlation2's store holds 26,656,424 daily rows through 2025-06-27, for about 11,500 US symbols ([correlation2 test-results.md](/home/bhoult/programming/correlation/test-results.md)). The prices are adjusted for both splits and dividends; AAPL's close on 2020-01-02 reads 72.72, against an actual $300.35. The series therefore behave like total-return series. That suits return targets but distorts price-level filters such as "price ≥ $5" ([correlation2 ANALYSIS.md](/home/bhoult/programming/correlation/ANALYSIS.md)).

Four repairs come first. Remove the NASDAQ test ticker ZVZZT, which moves 3,000× in a day, and repair the 69 one-day bad prints since mid-2021, while keeping the real crashes. Start in 2005: before decimalization, prices moved in fractional ticks, and decades of back-adjustment turn those ticks into fake zero-return days and stair-stepped returns. Splice newer snapshots by returns rather than price levels, because dividend back-adjustment rewrites old prices. And fix survivorship. Stooq ships only the symbols alive when the snapshot was taken: 11,183 of 11,431 symbols were still trading in June 2025 ([correlation2 ANALYSIS.md](/home/bhoult/programming/correlation/ANALYSIS.md)), and SIVB, BBBY, FRC and TWTR are missing ([local zip inspection](file:///home/bhoult/programming/correlation/data/stooq/d_us_txt.zip)).

Survivorship matters most for exactly what a next-return model learns. About 3.3–3.5% of US listed firms delist for cause each year ([Doidge, Karolyi & Stulz](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w21181/w21181.pdf)). Their final returns are usually missing, and researchers conventionally fill them in at −30% for NYSE/AMEX stocks and −55% for Nasdaq ([SAFE data guide](https://datacenter.safe-frankfurt.de/documents/Data_Description_CRSP_Shumway_20250317.pdf)). Delisting returns account for about 40% of momentum profits ([Eisdorfer](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v11y2008i2p160-179.html)). The research team's rough arithmetic puts the overstatement for an equal-weighted all-stock universe at 1–2 percentage points a year. It is far larger for portfolios tilted toward recent losers, which is the long leg of reversal. Random accounts drawn from the same survivor pool share the pool's bias, but not that tilt toward losers.

The cheapest documented fix is one month of Sharadar's full-history Prices tier. It costs $39 under a personal license and covers end-of-day data from 1998, which the vendor calls "survivorship bias free", plus corporate-action and S&P 500 membership tables. Check on the first day that SIVB, BBBY, FRC and TWTR are present ([Sharadar](https://sharadar.com/subscribe); [Sharadar home](https://sharadar.com/); [QuantRocket](https://www.quantrocket.com/sharadar/)). correlation2's analysis named MarketParquet as the first thing to try ($79 one-time, 8,500 delisted names, data through 2026-10-16). Its operator and upstream source are undisclosed, so treat it as a candidate only after its free samples check out ([MarketParquet](https://marketparquet.com/historical-market-data); [MarketParquet home](https://marketparquet.com/)). Norgate Platinum gives the most explicit guarantee, at $346.50 per six months, but its updater runs only on Windows ([Norgate](https://norgatedata.com/stockmarketpackages.php)).

The same purchase buys a second, cleaner test. correlation2 has already examined the 2024–25 holdout, and researchers who have lived through a holdout cannot treat it as truly unseen ([Arnott, Harvey & Markowitz](https://www.smallake.kr/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/SSRN-id3275654.pdf)). Sharadar's data, even on its $9 five-year tier, covers 2025-06-30 to the present, with delisted coverage claimed. Stooq itself now requires a CAPTCHA-issued API key with an unpublished daily limit, and it hides its bulk page behind a JavaScript check, so refresh the bulk zip by hand in a browser ([pandas-datareader #1012](https://github.com/pydata/pandas-datareader/issues/1012); [Stooq](https://stooq.com/db/h/)).

For costs, estimate each stock's effective bid-ask spread from its daily open, high, low and close with the EDGE estimator. Its estimates correlate 78.9% with spreads measured from TAQ trade-and-quote data. The `bidask` package implements it, alongside the Roll, Corwin-Schultz and Abdi-Ranaldo estimators. Floor large caps at a few bps, because EDGE computed from daily bars comes out negative in 41% of large-cap stock-months ([EDGE](https://irf.fhnw.ch/bitstreams/e8c469d2-9c64-4004-8e87-53ee091f0cb1/download); [bidask](https://ftp.fau.de/cran/web/packages/bidask/readme/README.html)).

Other markets add training data. The roughly 100M bars across all Stooq markets is the user's own figure and has not been verified. The largest finance study found performance "distinctly data limited", and adding global data raised accuracy ([Rahimikia et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18578v1)). The sensible design gives each market an ID embedding, trains on all of them and evaluates only on US stocks. Even 100M bars is about 120× smaller than Kronos's 12.1 billion.

### Python 3.12, PyTorch 2.14.1 and no flash-attn package

The software works on this card if a few traps are avoided. Pin Python 3.12 with uv, even though the core stack installs on 3.14. Qlib's wheels, Kronos's pinned requirements (pandas 2.2.2) and the Aim tracker all stop at 3.12. Python 3.14 also switched its default process start method to `forkserver`, which changes how DataLoader workers receive their data ([PyPI pyqlib](https://pypi.org/project/pyqlib/0.9.7/#files); [Kronos requirements](https://github.com/shiyu-coder/Kronos/blob/master/requirements.txt); [PyTorch dev-discuss](https://dev-discuss.pytorch.org/t/torch-compile-support-for-python-3-14-completed/3276)).

Install **PyTorch 2.14.1 from plain PyPI**. Its Linux wheel is the CUDA 13.0 build, with support for the Blackwell architecture (sm_120) compiled in. Never use the `cu126` builds, which stop at the older Hopper architecture. Ignore old "use cu128" guides too, since that index stops at torch 2.11 ([PyTorch RELEASE.md](https://github.com/pytorch/pytorch/blob/main/RELEASE.md); [PyPI torch](https://pypi.org/project/torch/2.14.1/); [cu128 index](https://download.pytorch.org/whl/cu128/torch/)). Confirm that `torch.cuda.get_arch_list()` lists sm_120.

Train in bf16; the 5080's dense bf16 rate is 112.6 TFLOPS. Use PyTorch's built-in `scaled_dot_product_attention(..., is_causal=True)`, which already uses flash-attention kernels on sm_120. Skip the separate `flash-attn` package: it ships only as source and crashes the CUDA compiler when built for sm_120 ([NVIDIA whitepaper](https://images.nvidia.com/aem-dam/Solutions/geforce/blackwell/nvidia-rtx-blackwell-gpu-architecture.pdf); [sdp_utils.cpp](https://github.com/pytorch/pytorch/blob/main/aten/src/ATen/native/transformers/cuda/sdp_utils.cpp); [flash-attention #2361](https://github.com/Dao-AILab/flash-attention/issues/2361)).

One open bug matters directly. On an RTX 5080, `torch.compile` silently produced errors of about 2.2, relative to uncompiled execution, for a bf16 linear layer added to fp32 embeddings and fed into an RMSNorm. That is exactly a GPT's input block, so compare compiled and uncompiled outputs on a fixed batch at the start of each run ([pytorch #191433](https://github.com/pytorch/pytorch/issues/191433)).

Keep the data on the GPU. 26.7M bars of a few float32 features fit in under 1 GB, so training windows can be sampled straight from GPU memory, with no DataLoader workers. Give Kronos its own uv environment with its git commit pinned, because its pins conflict with transformers 5.x and pandas 3.x. Set `use_comet=False` to keep its logging local ([Kronos config](https://github.com/shiyu-coder/Kronos/blob/master/finetune/config.py)).

Track runs locally with MLflow or Hugging Face's Trackio, which stores runs in SQLite behind a wandb-style API. Avoid Aim, which was last released on 2025-05-08 ([MLflow](https://mlflow.org/docs/latest/ml/tracking/); [Trackio](https://github.com/gradio-app/trackio); [PyPI aim](https://pypi.org/project/aim/3.29.1/#files)). Commit `uv.lock`, and log the git SHA, config, data-snapshot hash and seed with every run. PyTorch does not promise bit-identical results across releases or GPUs ([PyTorch reproducibility](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.14/notes/randomness.html)).

For code, nanoGPT's ~300-line `model.py` and `train.py` (MIT license, frozen since its 2025 deprecation) are the clearest base for a from-scratch return GPT. Its maintained successor, nanochat, carries tokenizer and chat machinery that a return model does not need ([nanoGPT](https://github.com/karpathy/nanoGPT); [nanochat](https://github.com/karpathy/nanochat)).

### A StockGPT-sized model trains in about a minute per pass

Data, not memory or compute, limits model size. With one token per stock-day, the US file is about 26.7M tokens, and all markets together about 100M. Two heuristics from language models apply. One is about 20 training tokens per parameter ([Chinchilla](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556)). The other is that up to about four passes over repeated data are nearly as good as fresh data ([Muennighoff et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16264)). Together they point to **roughly 1–5M parameters for US data alone and 5–20M with all markets**. Same-day moves are correlated across stocks and daily returns are mostly noise, so the true optimum is lower still. These heuristics come from text and are untested on returns. StockGPT's 0.93M sits at the US optimum, while a fresh 100M model would need about 64 passes over the US data to reach the Chinchilla ratio.

Compute is trivial at these sizes. The research team used the standard rule of about six floating-point operations per parameter per token ([Kaplan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361)). On the 5080, it estimates one pass over the US data at about 0.1–1.1 minutes for a 1M-parameter model, 1–5 minutes for 10M and 5–21 minutes for 100M. 16 GB holds the model state and activations for up to roughly 100M parameters at a batch of 64 windows of 512 days, which is enough to fine-tune Kronos-base.

A beginner's recipe follows from those numbers and published settings ([nanoGPT config](https://github.com/karpathy/nanoGPT/blob/master/config/train_shakespeare_char.py); [StockGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05101)). From correlation2's store, export one array per stock for each of four series: z (the de-meaned, volatility-scaled, clipped market-residual return), raw log return, 252-day volatility and dollar volume. Bin z at 0.1σ into 101 tokens, plus a padding token. Split strictly by date, with a gap at least as long as the label horizon at each boundary. Train on 2005 to 2021-06-27 and validate on 2021-06-28 to 2023-06-27, a period that includes the 2022 drawdown. Optionally keep 2023-06-28 to 2024-06-27 as a development test, and lock 2024-06-28 to 2025-06-27 for one final run. Then repeat the whole pipeline as a walk-forward that refits each year from 2015 to 2024 on all earlier data, as Rahimikia's study did. Model v0 is a nanoGPT decoder with 4–6 layers, width 128–256, 4 attention heads, a 256-day context and dropout of 0.1–0.2. Train it with AdamW (learning rate 1e-3 decaying to 1e-4, 100–500 warmup steps, weight decay 0.1) on cross-entropy at every position, for at most about four passes.

The forecast is the expected z under the predicted bin probabilities, and forecasts are ranked across stocks each evening. Validate on two numbers rather than one: the cross-entropy gain over the unconditional bin histogram, in bits per token, and the daily rank IC. Stop training on rank IC, because cross-entropy can keep falling as the model learns volatility while it says nothing about direction. Expect the cross-entropy gain to be tiny; a large one usually means leakage, such as a volatility or beta estimate that used the forecast day's data.

For multi-day holds, train a separate head directly on the 5- or 20-day return, as StockGPT's monthly model did, instead of rolling the daily model forward. StockGPT's daily signal lost most of its strength within one day. Its Fama-MacBeth slope, a measure of how strongly forecasts line up with realized returns, fell from 0.50 to 0.09. And rollouts that feed a model its own predictions accumulate error with every step ([LOB-Bench](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/nagy25a.html)).

### Kronos-small is the one clean pretrained shortcut

Pretrained weights save training but bring contamination risk. A model whose pretraining data includes the test period has, in effect, seen the answers. Documented leakage in forecasting benchmarks has inflated test scores by more than 50% ([Meyer et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13654v3)). For the holdout starting 2024-06-28, two groups are provably clean. The first is models whose weights were public before the holdout: Chronos-T5, TimesFM 1.0, Lag-Llama, Moirai 1.x and MOMENT. The second is models whose disclosed data cutoff precedes it: Kronos ("up to June 2024"), Preferred Networks' TimesFM-fin (before 2023) and the FinText-TSFM 2023 versions ([Kronos](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02739); [PFN](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.09880v1); [FinText card](https://huggingface.co/FinText/TimesFM_20M_2023_Augmented)). Chronos-2 and TimesFM 2.5 list only real-data sources from before 2024; for TimesFM 2.5, those are Wikipedia pageviews to November 2023 and Google Trends to end-2022. They cannot be certified, so report them as secondary controls ([TimesFM 2.5 card](https://huggingface.co/google/timesfm-2.5-200m-pytorch)). Moirai 2.0's corpus overlaps the holdout, and Sundial, TimeGPT and FinCast include finance data with undisclosed dates. The danger is smaller for numeric series than for text. A 2026 study found that financial foundation models trained past the forecast date generally *reduced* accuracy ([Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.20554)). Of the clean models, only Kronos was pretrained on OHLCV bars rather than on close prices or returns.

That makes Kronos the one pretrained shortcut worth real effort. Run Kronos-small zero-shot first, re-anchored on real data each day. Mini's 2,048-bar context covers about eight years of daily bars. Small and base cover only about two, and that limit includes the 102M-parameter base model that fits on the 5080 for fine-tuning, a real constraint on a 20-year dataset. Kronos's CSV fine-tuning pipeline (`finetune_csv/`, which trains the tokenizer first, then the predictor) accepts timestamps plus OHLCV. Stooq's missing "amount" column can be zero-filled, and a single GPU should work with `torchrun --standalone --nproc_per_node=1`, though that is untested here ([finetune_csv README](https://github.com/shiyu-coder/Kronos/tree/master/finetune_csv)).

Patch one bug first. The pipeline computes each window's normalization statistics over the forecast horizon as well as the history, which leaks future information. The leak was reported in March 2026 and fixed in the Qlib pipeline in April, but as of 2026-10-18 it is still present in `finetune_csv/finetune_base_model.py` on master. Normalize on the lookback only ([Issue #227](https://github.com/shiyu-coder/Kronos/issues/227); [source](https://github.com/shiyu-coder/Kronos/blob/master/finetune_csv/finetune_base_model.py)). Trust the code over its comments; the README warns that many were written by an AI assistant and "may contain inaccuracies" ([Kronos README](https://github.com/shiyu-coder/Kronos)).

Budget for slow inference. The released loop lacks a key-value cache, the standard trick for not recomputing earlier tokens, so it re-runs the full window for every sample. The research team estimates 4–5 minutes per trading day for 5,000 symbols at 10 samples with Kronos-base. That is about 15–20 GPU-hours for the holdout year, or 4–5 hours with Kronos-small ([kronos.py](https://github.com/shiyu-coder/Kronos/blob/master/model/kronos.py)).

The general models are cheap controls. Chronos-2 (Apache-2.0) fine-tunes through one `fit(finetune_mode="lora")` call ([Chronos-2 pipeline](https://github.com/amazon-science/chronos-forecasting/blob/main/src/chronos/chronos2/pipeline.py)). TimesFM 2.5 has an official LoRA example, while TimesFM 3.0's weights are non-commercial ([TimesFM](https://github.com/google-research/timesfm)). Expect little from fine-tuning them, since fine-tuning mostly hurt in the largest study. What closed the gap to CatBoost was pretraining on finance data with yearly cutoffs, not fine-tuning.

### One held-out year cannot confirm a Sharpe-1 edge

The evaluation deserves more care than the model, and statistical power is why. A signal's t-statistic is roughly its ICIR (mean daily IC divided by its standard deviation) times the square root of the number of days. Over 250 days, an ICIR of 0.4, about the best Qlib reports in China, gives t ≈ 6.3, while a more realistic 0.1 gives t ≈ 1.6. A strategy's t-statistic is roughly its Sharpe ratio times the square root of the years tested, so **a Sharpe-1 strategy tested for one year has t ≈ 1** and cannot be told apart from luck (derived). Selection compounds the problem. After only seven independent configurations on a two-year backtest, the expected best in-sample Sharpe is 1, while the expected out-of-sample Sharpe is 0 ([Bailey et al.](https://www.ams.org/notices/201405/rnoti-p458.pdf)). The plan therefore needs a ledger of every configuration tried and a deflated Sharpe ratio that corrects for the number of tries. It also needs a t > 3 bar for new signals ([Harvey, Liu & Zhu](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P118_and_the_cross.PDF)), model selection on validation and walk-forward years only, and a single look at the locked year.

correlation2's current harness already supplies most of this. It runs the $10,000 top-20 account against turnover-matched random guessers across 19 one-year walk-forward holdouts. Its SPEC also calls for a ledger with deflated Sharpe ratios, which is not built yet, and its forward log records picks before their outcomes exist. Its lead-lag picks beat random in 2 of 19 years, where chance alone gives about 1.9 ([correlation2 test-results.md](/home/bhoult/programming/correlation/test-results.md)).

A next-token model needs three additions. First, match the random accounts on volatility as well as turnover. correlation2's own notes warn that picks tilted toward volatile stocks beat uniform random draws in strong years on the tilt alone, and a model that has learned reversal will tilt that way. Second, judge signals in actual returns as well as residual z. correlation2's residual rank IC was positive in 18 of 19 years (combined z = 4.15), while its long-short in actual returns earned 0.45 bps a day with z = 0.92. Its authors trace the gap partly to market exposure left over after the residual adjustment. Third, make the 5-day reversal rule the baseline to beat. In the same harness it beat random guessers in 8 of 18 years, with a rank-IC z of 8.96, then faded: **its long-only picks have lost money every year since 2021**, a span that includes the holdout ([correlation2 test-results.md](/home/bhoult/programming/correlation/test-results.md)). A next-token model that cannot beat its own most likely rediscovery has learned nothing new.

Portfolio construction decides turnover. A top-k/drop-n rule like Qlib's (hold 50 names, replace at most 5 a day), or Kronos's 5-day minimum hold, turns over about 10% of the book a day. That costs roughly 2.5–10% a year at 5–20 bps, against 25–101% a year for replacing the whole book daily (derived from [Qlib's benchmark configuration](https://github.com/microsoft/qlib/blob/main/examples/benchmarks/LightGBM/workflow_config_lightgbm_Alpha158.yaml)). Compute signals at the close, trade at the next session, and also report a variant that waits one more day; StockGPT lost 78% of its equal-weighted return to a one-day delay. Charge each stock its estimated spread, and report value-weighted and size-bucketed results beside equal-weighted ones.

Set expectations before the first run. Plausible holdout values on liquid names are a daily RankIC of 0.005–0.02 and directional accuracy of 50.5–52%. S&P 500 models on OHLCV alone reached daily ICs of 0.001–0.003 over 2021–2025 ([AlphaAgent](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16789)), and correlation2's lead-lag signal scored +0.0034. Treat a daily RankIC above about 0.05 on large caps as a leak until the canaries (shifting targets by a day, permuting dates) collapse it to chance.

| Pitfall | How common or how large | Safeguard in stockai |
|---|---|---|

## Conclusion

The research moves stockai's central question from architecture to trading design. A next-token return model is easy to build and will learn something real: about 51–52% directional accuracy, enough for spectacular gross Sharpe ratios across thousands of stocks. But it learns the bid-ask bounce and volatility first. The break-even arithmetic is unforgiving. A strategy that replaces its holdings daily needs names whose next-day edge exceeds twice the one-way cost, which is 10–40 bps at 5–20 bps per trade. The daily price-only literature on liquid stocks since 2010 shows a gross edge of a few bps to about 10 bps per trade. Holding for H days divides the required daily edge by roughly H. So the first serious experiment should be a model trained directly on 5- to 20-day returns, traded long-only in liquid names with banded turnover. The rule it must beat is correlation2's own 5-day reversal control, which was strong from 2008 to 2021 and has lost money long-only every year since. Until rank IC in actual returns and net P&L say otherwise, read any fall in cross-entropy as volatility forecasting: useful for sizing and risk, but not for direction.

The project's cost structure is the reverse of what the "AI model" framing suggests. The transformer is the cheap part: a few hundred lines of nanoGPT and minutes of GPU time per run. The expensive parts are the ones that decide the result: survivorship-free data, per-stock spread estimates, random accounts matched on turnover and volatility, and picks logged before their outcomes exist, which correlation2's forward log already does ([correlation2 test-results.md](/home/bhoult/programming/correlation/test-results.md)). Effort spent there turns a likely negative into a credible one. A clean negative on correlation2's terms would settle the question for a hobbyist with daily bars, at the cost of a few weeks and about $40 of data. A positive result that survives those tests would be new.
