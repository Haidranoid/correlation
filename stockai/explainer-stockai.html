How stockGPT turns prices into a pick

A step-by-step animation using the real numbers from stockai's saved test run.

In short. stockai tests whether a small AI model can learn to pick stocks from price history alone. Each stock's daily price change is first cleaned up: the part caused by the whole market rising or falling is removed, and what is left is compared with that stock's normal day. Each day then becomes one “word”, and the last year of days becomes a “sentence”. The model, stockGPT, reads that sentence the way a chatbot reads text, but instead of guessing the next word it guesses whether tomorrow will be a better or worse day than usual for that stock.

Each evening every stock gets a score, and a test account puts $10,000 into the 20 highest-scoring stocks, holds them for a day, and repeats. Over 14 years, each predicted by a version of the model that had never seen it, the model did find a small, real pattern. But trading every day cost more than the pattern earned, and its picks did no better than random picks. Simply holding the S&P 500 did far better.

    What is real here

    Every number on the canvas comes from stockai's saved proof-of-concept run or from the data it reads:

    • the example stock's prices, returns and census values (recomputed with correlation2's formulas; they match the export to within float rounding);
    • its 256-token window and three extra inputs, exactly as the model receives them;
    • the probabilities from the three saved 2017 models (checkpoints in runs/poc/gpt/checkpoints/) and their average, which equals the stored score;
    • the attention weights: seed 0's last block, averaged over its 4 heads. They are one internal step of the model, not an explanation of its score;
    • that evening's ranking and picks, and the next day's returns;
    • the yearly results from results/poc/gpt_ensemble__r1_d0_uniform.json and the summary numbers from test-results.md.

    Two parts are schematic: the icons in the first scene, and the shaded embedding cells in "Inside stockGPT" (labeled there).

    Terms in the last scenes

    • bps (basis points): hundredths of a percent. 20 bps per trade is 0.20% of each buy or sell.
    • Weekly: the picks are held for 5 trading days instead of 1.
    • 5-day reversal rule: buy the stocks that fell most over the last 5 days.
    • 12-1 momentum: buy the stocks that rose most over the past year, skipping the latest month.
    • LightGBM: a model made of many small decision trees. stockai's version reads 21 features built from prices, v, dollar volume and the market's returns, and was trained to predict the next day's actual return.
    • Rank correlation: how well the order of the scores matches the order of what happened next, from −1 to +1. Values like +0.01 are small but can be real.
    • z-score: how sure we can be that a signal isn't luck, not how big it is. Around 2 or more is unlikely to be chance.

    Where it comes from in the code

    • Tokens: stockai/gpt/tokens.py.
    • Extra inputs: stockai/gpt/features.py and windows.py.
    • The model (4 blocks, 4 heads, width 128, 256-day window): stockai/gpt/model.py. The score is expected_value.
    • Training and walk-forward splits: stockai/gpt/train.py, walkforward.py and stockai/splits.py.
    • The grader (top 20 positive scores at an equal 5% each, turnover-matched random accounts): judge/, a fork of correlation2's holdout pinned at commit 9335126.
    • The census variable v: correlation2's crates/c2-census/src/variable.rs.

    Caveats

    • v subtracts each stock's average residual over the past year. So a small part of tomorrow's v is already known today. That is why stockai judges signals by their correlation with actual next-day returns, not with v.
    • The LightGBM model trained on actual returns was added after the first results were seen, so its numbers are a lead to test, not a finding.
    • The data is Stooq's survivor-only history: companies that later disappeared are missing. That flatters the stock-picking accounts, the strategy's and the random ones, though not necessarily by the same amount, and it doesn't affect SPY.
    • correlation2's data has two small look-aheads that stockai inherits. Its one-day bad-print repair looks at the next day's close. Its $5 price test uses prices adjusted for later splits, so some stocks that later split heavily are missing from early years. Both are described in test-results.md.
    • The top 20 on the example evening include two closed-end funds (NAD, DSL) and a preferred share (BAC_L), which the data files as stocks. Tickers are today's: in 2018 KDP was DPS and RTX was UTX.
    • Prices are adjusted for later splits and dividends, so older prices look lower than they were quoted at the time. Day-to-day returns are unaffected (they include dividends).
    • Nothing from June 28, 2024 onward is used or shown. That year is locked for one final test.
    • Compounded returns run over the 14 test years back to back (June 2010 to June 2024).
