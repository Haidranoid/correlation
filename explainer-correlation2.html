How correlation2 looks for stocks that move first

A step-by-step animation using real numbers from correlation2's census run.

In short. correlation2 tests a simple trading idea: when one stock moves, do certain other stocks tend to follow a day or more later? It takes 20 years of daily prices for thousands of US stocks and removes the part of each day's move caused by the whole market. Then it compares every pair of stocks, at delays from one day to three years, across rolling one- to five-year stretches of history. That is over 350 billion comparisons, done on a graphics card in about 12 minutes.

With that many comparisons, some pairs look linked by pure luck. So correlation2 also counts how many links it finds in scrambled data where none can exist. It then uses the links found in the past to pick 20 stocks each day, and checks those picks against random picks and against the S&P 500 in years it hadn't seen. The answer: stocks clearly move together on the same day, but one stock's move doesn't reliably predict another's, so the picks did no better than random.

    • The example relationship (CSCO → DE, daily moves at offset 1, 2022-04-08 to 2023-04-11) is a record stored by the census run main-2025-06-27 (read-only): its r and z, and its matrix's summary; its rank (15th) comes from the matrix's stored top 100. Its 251 observations are the census variable itself (from stockai's export), checked against the run's stored sums. The example's correlation period ended before June 28, 2024.
    • The histogram of every pair in that matrix, the counts of strong pairs, and the three chance copies were recomputed exactly from the census values with the census's own random seeds; they reproduce the run's stored chance records. The run's own summary, from a coarser GPU count, gives slightly lower numbers (23,756 / 1,209 / 49 real against 24,075 / 1,370 / 61 chance at |z| ≥ 3 / 4 / 5).
    • The census variable steps use CSCO's real prices, recomputed with correlation2's own formulas; they match the census variable in stockai's export to within float rounding (correlation2 itself doesn't store daily values).
    • The forecast for Deere on August 17, 2023 agrees with c2 census forecast. The score on that day was recomputed with the rules in holdout.rs (no c2 command prints it), from the four correlation periods that ended April 11, 2023.
    • The counts of strong correlations by offset, the replication shares and the walk-forward and holdout results are quoted from correlation2's research/census_main_summary.txt, census_main_replicate.txt, census_main_walkforward.txt, census_main_holdout.txt and walkforward_reversal_5.txt. They come from the first full census (main, 15 move-length pairs). The wider current census (36 pairs) gave the same answer (test-results.md).

    Where it comes from in the code

    • The census variable: crates/c2-census/src/variable.rs.
    • Move lengths, offsets and correlation periods: grid.rs.
    • The all-pairs product, skipping each stock against itself, the shared sign flip and the false-discovery cut-off: crates/c2-engine/src/gpu.rs; what is stored and the exact recomputation: run.rs.
    • Scores, picks, costs and random accounts: holdout.rs. The forecast: forecast.rs.
    • The step-by-step recomputation of one relationship (the GUI's Explainer tab and c2 census explain): explain.rs.

    Caveats

    • Stooq's data is survivor-only: stocks that later delisted are missing. That flatters both the picks and the random accounts. correlation2's write-up treats the two as affected alike, which is only roughly true, and SPY is unaffected.
    • The chance copies are a fair yardstick for short moves. For 21- and 63-day moves they are too lenient: pure-noise test series show 1.2 and 2.4 times as many strong correlations as the copies predict (test-results.md).
    • The random accounts are a generous yardstick: the picks lean toward volatile stocks, which in a strong year beat a uniform random draw on their own. The 2024–25 result also moved with the census's settings: a smaller grid of move lengths and periods gave +10.8% (p 0.39) for the same year.
    • Walk-forward accounts are graded before costs; costs only lower them. The June 2024 to June 2025 numbers are correlation2's own published result for the year its test was designed around.
    • stockai's checks found three issues in correlation2's data handling at commit 9335126, described in stockai's test-results.md ("Notes for correlation2"). The $5 price test uses split-adjusted prices, so stocks that later split heavily drop out of early universes. The one-day bad-print repair looks at the next day's close. And rank correlations in census units, such as the z = 4.15 tilt, include a part already known at decision time.
    • The census run main-2025-06-27 was made by an earlier build (commit 2c5f342 plus uncommitted changes); correlation2's test-results.md reports that a recount of its counts after a later engine fix found it unaffected.
