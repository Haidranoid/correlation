13:55  Before any result: the tests (tests.py), written before any outcome was computed
- Returns: simple returns from the store's prices, both days valid: open t+1 -> open t+2 (primary), close t+1 ->
  close t+2, close t -> close t+1. B's 1- and 5-day returns: log close-to-close through t.
- Daily statistics are assigned to the decision day t; periods cut by decision day. Days run from D0 to the
  third-last day of the export.
- S: universe stocks with the force, the benchmark, both reversal controls and the return; all five re-ranked
  within that sample each day (average ranks, centred (rank - 1) / (n - 1) - 0.5); >= 50 stocks a day. Raw: the
  same without the benchmark. IC: the rank correlation of force and return over stocks with both.
- M: n // 5 names a side among universe stocks with a force (ties broken by row order); a picked stock without a
  return that day is left out of its leg's mean (drop and renormalize); turnover = sum |w_t - w_{t-1}| over all
  stocks, with no drift adjustment; cost = c bps x turnover.
- Benchmarks: G1 -> the spring; G2 (N, a) -> the sharp offset at the same N with its own attractors; G3 (N, a, p)
  -> G1's force at (p, 0.3). Closes-only candidates are controlled for the closes-only benchmark (the spring is
  the same; G3 closes only -> G1 closes only).
- The random null is a parallel world: the random-attractor candidate force, controlled for the random-attractor
  benchmark, and its own book. Reported, not a primary.
- t = estimate / bootstrap sd; the p used for Romano-Wolf is the bootstrap max-t; Holm on the six stage p's at
  0.0027, each also needing the best candidate's t >= 3. The bootstrap: one set of 10,000 circular 21-day block
  draws per period (seed 20261024), shared by every series.
- Romano-Wolf: step-down max-t (studentized by each candidate's bootstrap sd); a stage's p is its best candidate's
  adjusted p.
- Alphas: the candidate's net 5 bps daily series regressed on the gross daily series of 5-day reversal's long-short
  (bottom fifth of the 5-day return long, same universe, same open-to-open execution) and, separately, on its
  benchmark's gross book, Newey-West (10 lags).

- Force law (shape.py): unweighted means over pair-days (not weighted by mass); the outcome is B's next
  open-to-open simple return divided by sigma[t] (known at the close of t), signed by -sign(g_C); pair-days need the
  gap and the return; date-block bootstrap from per-date sums and counts. The closes-only distance (r = |g_C|) is
  reported beside it. Predicted shapes: each law's mean 1/(r + eps)^p in the bin, and the spring's mean |g_C|.
- Kernel (shape.py): the plan says "the cross-sectional slope of z_B(t+1) on z_A(t+1-tau)". With attractors chosen by
  |m|, half the masses are negative, so an unsigned slope would mix the two directions. The primary reading is the
  slope on sign(m) z_A(t+1-tau) (the direction the mass predicts); the unsigned slope is reported beside it. Slopes
  per day over all (B, A) pairs with an intercept (>= 100 pairs), averaged over days, real minus random.
- Judge (judge.py): scores are the force's centred rank among the day's universe stocks with a force (the judge buys
  the top 20 with a positive score). The judge's walk-forward (start 2024-06-28) runs its years 2007-08 .. 2024-25 and
  skips 2006-07; its year 2008-09 exists, so no per-year stitching is needed. The 17 years with start >= 2008-06-28 are
  graded; 2007-08 (forces only from 2008-03-26) is dropped. SPY: Yahoo total return over each year's first..last day
