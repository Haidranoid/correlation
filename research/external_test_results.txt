run ext-2026-10-18 (2026-10-19), census run main-2025-06-27, data /home/bhoult/.local/share/correlation2/external
command: cargo run --release -p c2-census --example external_test -- --run ext-2026-10-18 --noise 220 --catalogs data-markets,data-other,data-weather

How to read this: A moves first over its move length, B follows over its move length, and the offset is the
number of trading days from the end of A's move to the end of B's. Offset 0 = the same day (no prediction);
offset >= 1 = predictive. 'chance' is the mean of the 3 sign-flip null copies (the census's null); a real/chance
ratio near 1 means no more strong correlations than noise produces.

== Method (rules chosen) ==
Stocks: the production census variable and the census run's own stock universe for each correlation period.
Timing: an observation dated d with known_after_days k counts from the first trading day on or after d + k
  (calendar days); the weekly Trends-style series dated by week start with k = 7 land on the first trading day
  after the week ends (self-checked).
logdiff/diff: the move on trading day t is the change in the latest known level since t-1, so release days
  carry the whole move and other days are 0. A change across a gap longer than the series' normal spacing
  (the 90th percentile of its previous 250 gaps plus 2 days; exactly 1 day for series observed every day, so a
  prediction market's roll gap or an outage is never bridged) is not counted as a move.
anomaly/anomaly_log1p: value (or ln(1 + value)) minus the mean over the 15 days centred on the same date in each
  of the 10 previous years (at least 3 years); anomalies landing on the same trading day are averaged.
level: value (averaged over the days landing on a trading day) minus its mean over the previous 252 trading days.
Standardization, exactly as the census does for stocks: minus the trailing 252-day mean, divided by the trailing
  252-day sd (both through t-1, at least 126 values), clipped at +-5. No value when the trailing year holds no
  non-zero move at all, so a sparse 0/1 indicator can never divide by a zero or round-off sd.
Live: a series has values while its latest observation is recent for its frequency (10 trading days for daily,
  15 weekly, 50 monthly, 140 quarterly, 300 annual); elsewhere it is missing, which counts as 0 in sums.
Joining a period: >= 90% of the period's trading days have a value AND >= 10 of them carry a non-zero move (so a
  single event can't dominate a correlation through a tiny trailing sd; the +-5 clip bounds each day too).
Single-contract series (flagged finite-life in a catalog's notes) are kept in category single_contract, outside
  every headline number; so are the noise calibration series and the synthetic controls.
At offset 0 with equal move lengths, ext->stock and stock->ext hold the same correlations transposed.
Grid, sums / sqrt(move length), >= 30 observations, the 3 sign-flip null copies (census seeds), the false-
  discovery rule and its placebo: copies of the census's code; checked against stored census records below.

weather, climate indices, commodities, energy, rates, credit, FX, world equity indices and US-listed ETFs, crypto,
macro, population, space weather, geophysical, astronomical, attention, health, other) were tested against the
census's own stocks in its four non-overlapping 1260-day correlation periods and full history, for all 36 move-length
pairs and every census offset: 5.5 billion real correlations against 16.6 billion chance ones.

1. Counts. ext->stock at predictive offsets: 433,254 correlations at |z| >= 4 against 411,937 by chance (1.05). The 220
   pure-noise externals give 1.04 through the same pipeline, and the census's own stock->stock gives 1.03 for the same
   correlation periods and move lengths. The ratio rises with move length (63/63 days: 1.81 real, 2.44 for noise,
   2.41 in the census's stock->stock), which is the census's null and not the data: the trailing-mean step leaves
   long-move observations slightly anti-correlated in every series, and the sign flip destroys that.
2. False-discovery rule on the ext->stock predictive matrices: fired in 25 of 4,284 (0.6%), its placebo in 26.
3. Out-of-sample, the decisive test. ext->stock relationships picked at |z| >= 4 in one 1260-day period keep their sign
   in the next one 51.0% of the time (104,176 relationships), against 50.3% for relationships picked from the null
   copies and 50.0% for the noise externals. The mean signed r in the next period is +0.0020 (null-picked +0.0006).
   Leaving out the US-listed ETFs: 50.9% against 50.4%, mean r +0.0021. The top 1,000 per period are a little better
   (55.1% against 51.1%), and the survivors below show where that comes from.
4. What survives (|z| >= 4 in one period, then the same sign at |z| >= 3 in the next): 350 ext->stock relationships,
   against 242 on average when picked from the null copies (0.36 against 0.25 per million pairs tested). The excess
   of about 110 is:
   a. US-listed ETFs (IWM, SPY, QQQ, HYG, LQD, XLE, ...) moving first and thinly traded or foreign-linked stocks
      following a day later (CorVel, the ASML ADR, municipal and bond closed-end funds such as NVG, BDCs such as FSK).
      This is the known stale-price lead-lag inside the stock market, |r| about 0.12 to 0.14. ETFs are 8% of the
      series but 88 of the 350 survivors. In raw price terms it barely shows: inside 2015-2020, after LQD's
      top-quarter days NVG rose 52.4% of the time and after its bottom-quarter days 49.8%, against 53.3% on all days.
   b. Calendar alignments: monthly releases (payrolls, population) and the lunar cycle against stocks whose
      market-relative moves carry fixed dividend calendars (PDI with monthly distributions, utilities, municipal bond
      funds). These are deterministic schedules, not information. The lunar placebo's 1.28 ratio traces to the same
      utilities and municipal bond funds.
   c. The rest look like what chance produces at this scale: the strongest non-ETF survivors are absent or reversed in
      the other correlation periods, and their plain-terms counts don't hold outside the period. For example, Atlanta
      heating degree days -> US Bancorp (A over 21 days, B over 1 day, offset 36) has z +4.4 and +5.0 in 2015-2020 and
      2020-2025 but -1.2 and +1.0 in 2005-2015. After a top-quarter 21-day HDD move, USB rose in 60% of cases inside
      2015-2020 against 52% normally, and in 36% against 44% in the rest of history.
   d. Climate indices look best by category (ratio 1.27, 54.9% same sign over 2,501 picks), but it is clustering. The
      excess sits in the two near-duplicate weekly Nino series (1,624 of 5,261 strong entries, against 847 in null
      copy 1) and in 2015-2020 (1,620 against 1,039), mostly with B over 1 day. These are smooth, persistent series for
      which the sign-flip null is too lenient, and only 7 climate-index relationships survive into the next period at
      |z| >= 3, scattered over series and move lengths.
5. Same day (offset 0), real effects as expected: oil vs energy stocks, gold vs miners, rates vs banks and utilities,
   FX and world indices vs exporters. 349,900 correlations at |z| >= 4 against 43,244 by chance, and 91% keep their
   sign in the next period, which shows that both the pipeline and the out-of-sample check work. None of it predicts.
6. stock->ext: within the external's reporting delay (offset shorter than B's move length plus its known_after_days)
   the excess (1.89) is timing, because B's value describes the days A moved. Beyond it the ratio is 1.06, the same as
   noise. What persists there is US stocks moving first and markets that trade after the US close following (bank
   stocks -> Australian and Korean 10-year yields at the next session), the same non-synchronous effect as
   SPY -> Nikkei.
7. ext->ext: large and persistent (weather systems moving east, degree days vs temperatures, hurricane energy vs
   active storms, the MJO's phases, astronomical calendars). Real physics and arithmetic, but nothing about stocks.

Is any of it tradable? No. The one persistent predictive effect, ETFs leading illiquid stocks and funds by a day, is a
known stale-price effect of r about 0.13, concentrated in thin names. The census holdout already shows that 5 bps per
trade erases the census's own picks, so this isn't a strategy either. Nothing from weather, climate, crypto, macro,
population, space weather, attention or the calendar leads US stocks out of sample.

Controls (all passed):
- Oil (FRED WTI and Brent spot, USO) vs 12 energy stocks' market-relative moves, same day: median z +7.6 to +32.7 in
  every correlation period.
- Gold (GLD, XAU/USD) vs 11 miners: +24.9 to +52.2. XAU/USD is known a day later and shows up as stock->ext at offset 1,
  exactly as its timing implies; the same holds for Henry Hub gas.
- Natural gas (UNG, Henry Hub) vs 7 gas producers: +3.7 to +14.9.
- SPY moving first and 8 Asian indices following the next day: r +0.15 (Shanghai) to +0.54 (ASX); Nikkei +0.43 to +0.50.
- Heating degree days vs natural gas: the physical same-day link is there (Henry Hub: median z +3.1 for 1-day moves
  and +3.2 for 5-day moves over 154 HDD series and periods, about 80 of them at z >= 3), and 'HDD known, gas follows'
  is not (largest |median z| 1.7). Markets trade the forecasts.
- Synthetic leads and follows (a stock's own moves shifted 1, 3 or 5 days, plus noise): each found at exactly the
  injected offset and move lengths (z +9 to +23), and every grid point within tolerance of the expected r.
- Placebos look like chance against stocks: sunspots 1.05, F10.7 solar flux 0.93, Wikipedia 'Cat'/'Philosophy' 0.97,
  synthetic noise 1.02. Lunar phase 1.28 is the dividend-calendar alignment in 4b.
- Census replication: 350 stored census correlations (175 of them null copies) reproduced to 3e-8, so the grid, the
  sums and the null seeds are the census's own. GPU FP32 r matches exact f64 recomputation to 2.3e-7.

Known weaknesses:
- Timing. known_after_days are the data agents' bounds. Settlement times (WTI 2:30 pm New York), FX closes after 4 pm
  (k = 1), local-time weather days, UTC hurricane days; daily resolution only; monthly data dated the 1st with k counted
  from the 1st. A k that is too conservative only weakens leads. One that is too aggressive would create false leads
  at offset 1, and none were found.
- Revisions. Population, payrolls and most FRED macro, USGS, flu, EIA-930, GPR and SF Fed are latest revisions, not
  what was known at the time. Anomaly base periods (ONI, teleconnections) were chosen after the fact. HURDAT2 and ERA5
  are reanalyses. A real-time (ALFRED vintage) test would be stricter. With nothing found, revisions can't be hiding a
  lead; they could only have created a false one.
- Seasonality and calendars. Anomalies remove the day-of-year cycle using earlier years only. Weekday cycles
  (Wikipedia, TSA, electricity) and monthly release calendars remain, and stocks with dividend calendars (funds,
  utilities) pick up spurious calendar alignments that can persist.
- The census's null is not calibrated at long move lengths, in the census and here alike. The noise externals give the
  right baseline, but for strongly persistent series (climate indices) even that may understate chance.
- Survivorship. The stock universes are the census's (>= 90% coverage, liquid, in the store). The out-of-sample check
  needs both series in the next period's universe, which drops 7.5%.
- Monthly series. A release-day move is about 4.6 trailing sd, so many are clipped at +-5 (nearly binary).
- Near-duplicates (nearby stations, TMAX/TMIN/HDD, ETF families) mean the counts overstate the number of independent
  tests. Crypto, Wikipedia and COVID data start in 2015 or 2020, so they get one or two correlation periods and at most
  one out-of-sample step. Annual population joins only full history (about 20 changes), so it has no out-of-sample
  step at all.
- Plain-terms counts use raw stock price moves, as the census's Words do, so effects of r about 0.1 barely show in them.

Integrating this into the census and the GUI later:
- Pseudo-stocks. Give each external series an id above the store's (this test uses 1,000,000 + its row in
  externals.csv) and keep a run-level externals.tsv (id, catalog id, name, category, transform, known_after_days,
  frequency, units). Record.leader and Record.target are u32 and hold these ids unchanged; universes.bin can list them.
- Variable. Add an external-column builder next to variable::production: this example's build_ext (availability day,
  release-day moves, causal anomalies, the census's standardization). It would produce columns on the same days,
  appended to the Variable with the pseudo ids. Plan::new and Plan::for_run would add each period's qualifying
  externals (>= 90% values, >= 10 non-zero moves) to its universe.
- Census run. The INT8 path works unchanged, because externals are standardized and clipped like stocks. To avoid
  recomputing stock->stock, a run could compute only the external rows and columns (X_ext^T Y and X^T Y_ext). That
  needs a non-square product, a small addition to gpu.rs. At 2,000 externals this example's full run takes about
  3 minutes and 7 GB.
- Null. Run noise pseudo-externals as a calibration set in every census, or replace the sign flip at long move lengths
  with a block sign flip or a circular shift within the period, so that ratios at 21 and 63 days are calibrated. That
  would fix the census's stock->stock 63/63 ratio of 2.41 too.
- Timing classes. Mark stock->ext and ext->ext offsets within the follower's reporting delay as 'timing' wherever the
  GUI ranks relationships (Strongest, Stock timeline).
- GUI. Show external names and categories for pseudo ids (Strongest ranking, Stock timeline, Explainer, Data tab). Give
  plain-terms Words in the series' own units (this example's describe_raw). The Explainer's day steps for an external
  would show the raw value, the day it became known and the standardized value. Add a category filter, and list
  'too sparse to test' series in the Data tab.
- Forecast. External leaders could feed the forecast like stock leaders, respecting known_after_days. Given these
  results they shouldn't, except as a demonstration.
- Refresh. 'c2 update' would rerun the download scripts (external/scripts/) and keep vintages of revised series.
