External series vs US stocks: census-faithful lead-lag test
run ext-all-1 (2026-10-19), census run main-2025-06-27, data /home/bhoult/.local/share/correlation2/external
command: cargo run --release -p c2-census --example external_test -- --run ext-all-1 --noise 220

How to read this: A moves first over its move length, B follows over its move length, and the offset is the
number of trading days from the end of A's move to the end of B's. Offset 0 = the same day (no prediction);
offset >= 1 = predictive. 'chance' is the mean of the 3 sign-flip null copies (the census's null); a real/chance
ratio near 1 means no more strong correlations than noise produces.

== Method (rules chosen) ==
Stocks: the production census variable and the census run's own stock universe for each correlation period.
Timing: an observation dated d with known_after_days k counts from the first trading day on or after d + k
  (calendar days); the weekly Trends-style series dated by week start with k = 7 land on the first trading day
  after the week ends (self-checked).
logdiff/diff: the move on trading day t is the change in the latest known level since t-1, so release days
  carry the whole move and other days are 0. A change across a gap longer than the series' normal spacing
  (the 90th percentile of its previous 250 gaps plus 2 days; exactly 1 day for series observed every day, so a
  prediction market's roll gap or an outage is never bridged) is not counted as a move.
anomaly/anomaly_log1p: value (or ln(1 + value)) minus the mean over the 15 days centred on the same date in each
  of the 10 previous years (at least 3 years); anomalies landing on the same trading day are averaged.
level: value (averaged over the days landing on a trading day) minus its mean over the previous 252 trading days.
Standardization, exactly as the census does for stocks: minus the trailing 252-day mean, divided by the trailing
  252-day sd (both through t-1, at least 126 values), clipped at +-5. No value when the trailing year holds no
  non-zero move at all, so a sparse 0/1 indicator can never divide by a zero or round-off sd.
Live: a series has values while its latest observation is recent for its frequency (10 trading days for daily,
  15 weekly, 50 monthly, 140 quarterly, 300 annual); elsewhere it is missing, which counts as 0 in sums.
Joining a period: >= 90% of the period's trading days have a value AND >= 10 of them carry a non-zero move (so a
  single event can't dominate a correlation through a tiny trailing sd; the +-5 clip bounds each day too).
Kept outside every headline number and reported as their own categories: single_contract (notes marked
  FINITE-LIFE: one prediction-market contract with a finite life, not a rolling series), annual (one release a
  year, often all on one date: a calendar pulse that standardization pushes to +-5; --include-annual counts them),
  the noise calibration series and the synthetic controls.
At offset 0 with equal move lengths, ext->stock and stock->ext hold the same correlations transposed.
Grid, sums / sqrt(move length), >= 30 observations, the 3 sign-flip null copies (census seeds), the false-
  discovery rule and its placebo: copies of the census's code; checked against stored census records below.

== Interpretation (written by hand after reading the numbers above) ==

Verdict: unchanged from the first run. Nothing in 1,726 external series leads US stocks in a way that survives into
the next correlation period, apart from known market-plumbing and calendar effects that carry no outside information.
The decisive numbers for ext->stock at predictive offsets:
- Real vs chance at |z| >= 4: 881,342 against 847,281 (1.04). Pure-noise externals give 1.04 too, and the census's own
  stock->stock gives 1.03.
- Out of sample: relationships picked at |z| >= 4 in one 1260-day period keep their sign in the next 50.7% of the time
  (292,142), against 50.1% for picks from the null copies and 50.0% for noise externals. The mean signed r in the next
  period is +0.0017 (null-picked +0.0002). Without the US-listed ETFs: 50.6% against 50.1%.
- New categories, ext->stock predictive real/chance and out-of-sample same sign against null picks:
    news 0.92 and 50.6% vs 50.1%
    politics 1.02 and 49.6% vs 50.0%
    prosperity 1.05 and 51.0% vs 50.0%
    migration 1.01 and 50.5% vs 49.9%
    Google Trends (in attention) 1.03 and 50.4% vs 49.9%
  No continuous prediction-market series covers 90% of 2020-2025, and the 74 finite-life contracts are too short by
  construction, so prediction markets could not be tested.

What does go beyond chance, and why it isn't a lead:
1. The false-discovery rule fires in 52 of the 4,284 ext->stock predictive matrices against 15 for its placebo (first
   run: 25 against 26). 43 of the 52 are in full history, where 20 years of data let small persistent effects pass.
   The passing entries are two known mechanisms:
   a. US-listed ETFs (IWM, XLF, SPY, ...) leading thinly traded small banks and closed-end funds by one day (A over
      1 day, B over 1 day, offset 1): stale prices. 76% of the strongest world_equity entries.
   b. Monthly or quarterly releases on fixed schedules (the Philadelphia Fed's 50 state coincident indexes, BEA state
      personal income, QCEW county wages) against stocks with fixed calendars of their own: closed-end funds with
      monthly distributions (Cornerstone CLM and CRF, Nuveen municipal funds, PDI) and earnings dates. The offsets are
      roughly multiples of a month (20, 40, 125 days). Two schedules line up; neither carries information.
2. Survivors (|z| >= 4, then the same sign at |z| >= 3 in the next period): 1,580 ext->stock relationships, against
   919 when picked from the null copies (0.64 against 0.37 per million pairs; first run 0.36 against 0.25). The new
   excess is the release pulses of 1b. Their 1-day moves are zero except on release days, and their plain-terms
   counts are flat. For example, BEA state per-capita income (quarterly) -> Garmin 2 days later: inside 2015-2020,
   GRMN rose 52.5% of the time after the series' top quarter and 55.0% after its bottom quarter, against 53.0% on all
   days. The ETF-led survivors are the same 88 as before.
3. Same day (offset 0): real effects as expected (commodities, rates, FX, world indices against their sectors), with
   ratios 6 to 22 and 89% same sign in the next period. None of it predicts.
4. stock->ext beyond the reporting delay is 1.05 (noise 1.06). ext->ext is large and persistent: weather systems,
   related macro series, the state indexes among themselves. Real, but nothing about stocks.

Is any of it tradable? No, for the reasons given in the first run: the ETF stale-price effect is weak (r about 0.13)
and sits in thin names, and the calendar alignments are fixed schedules known in advance, not signals.

What changed from the first run (ext-2026-10-18: three catalogs, 549 series):
- Data: 9 catalogs and 1,726 series. New: migration 292, news 135, politics 212, predictions 84, prosperity 342 and
  Google Trends 88; weather grew from 215 to 239. Real correlations rose from 5.5 billion to 15.5 billion.
- Annual series (346: population, migration, county income) are now reported as their own category, outside the
  headline numbers (--include-annual counts them). They release once a year, often on the same date, so they act as
  one calendar pulse that standardization pushes to +-5; 345 join only full history. As a group they show
  ext->stock 1.00.
- Finite-life prediction-market contracts (74, marked FINITE-LIFE) are excluded. ROLLING series built by stitching
  contracts are kept; the matcher is self-checked on both wordings.
- Correlation periods are now read through RunMeta and chosen by length and date (the chain of non-overlapping
  consecutive 1260-day periods from the census's first day, plus full history), not by id. They are validated: exact
  length, consecutive, dates matching the rows. They came out the same 222, 242, 262, 282 and 284.
- Headline unchanged: ext->stock predictive 1.05 -> 1.04 (noise 1.04 both); out of sample 51.0% -> 50.7% against null
  50.3% -> 50.1%.
- Changed: the FDR firings (25 vs 26 -> 52 vs 15) and the survivor excess (about 110 -> about 660), both from the
  calendar alignments of the new monthly and quarterly series and in full history. All controls give the same results.
- Run: 2 min 34 s and 8.4 GB for 1,136 to 1,461 external series per period (including the 236 calibration and
  control series). entries.bin grew to 3.2 GB.

Known weaknesses (as in the first run, plus):
- Calendar pulses. Monthly and quarterly releases on fixed dates, and stocks with fixed dividend and earnings
  calendars, produce persistent but meaningless alignments, which the sign-flip null does not model. A null that
  keeps each series' release calendar (a circular shift within the period, or flipping whole months) would separate
  them. So would grouping 'same release schedule' series into one effective test.
- Plain-terms quarters are degenerate for pulse series (most h-day moves are zero), so their counts say little.
- Revisions, near-duplicates, timing bounds, survivorship and the census null at long move lengths: as before.

Integration into the census and the GUI: as in the first run. In addition, a census-wide null that preserves release
calendars and dividend schedules would remove the calendar alignments for stock->stock pairs too (closed-end funds
and utilities are in the census's universes).
