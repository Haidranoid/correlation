External series vs US stocks: census-faithful lead-lag test
run ext-all-2 (2026-10-19), census run main-2025-06-27, data /home/bhoult/.local/share/correlation2/external
command: cargo run --release -p c2-census --example external_test -- --run ext-all-2 --noise 220

How to read this: A moves first over its move length, B follows over its move length, and the offset is the
number of trading days from the end of A's move to the end of B's. Offset 0 = the same day (no prediction);
offset >= 1 = predictive. 'chance' is the mean of the 3 sign-flip null copies (the census's null); a real/chance
ratio near 1 means no more strong correlations than noise produces.

== Method (rules chosen) ==
Stocks: the production census variable and the census run's own stock universe for each correlation period.
Timing: an observation dated d with known_after_days k counts from the first trading day on or after d + k
  (calendar days); the weekly Trends-style series dated by week start with k = 7 land on the first trading day
  after the week ends (self-checked).
logdiff/diff: the move on trading day t is the change in the latest known level since t-1, so release days
  carry the whole move and other days are 0. A change across a gap longer than the series' normal spacing
  (the 90th percentile of its previous 250 gaps plus 2 days; exactly 1 day for series observed every day, so a
  prediction market's roll gap or an outage is never bridged) is not counted as a move.
anomaly/anomaly_log1p: value (or ln(1 + value)) minus the mean over the 15 days centred on the same date in each
  of the 10 previous years (at least 3 years); anomalies landing on the same trading day are averaged.
level: value (averaged over the days landing on a trading day) minus its mean over the previous 252 trading days.
Standardization, exactly as the census does for stocks: minus the trailing 252-day mean, divided by the trailing
  252-day sd (both through t-1, at least 126 values), clipped at +-5. No value when the trailing year holds no
  non-zero move at all, so a sparse 0/1 indicator can never divide by a zero or round-off sd.
Live: a series has values while its latest observation is recent for its frequency (10 trading days for daily,
  15 weekly, 50 monthly, 140 quarterly, 300 annual); elsewhere it is missing, which counts as 0 in sums.
Joining a period: >= 90% of the period's trading days have a value AND >= 10 of them carry a non-zero move (so a
  single event can't dominate a correlation through a tiny trailing sd; the +-5 clip bounds each day too).
Kept outside every headline number and reported as their own categories: single_contract (notes marked
  FINITE-LIFE: one prediction-market contract with a finite life, not a rolling series), annual (one release a
  year, often all on one date: a calendar pulse that standardization pushes to +-5; --include-annual counts them),
  the noise calibration series and the synthetic controls.
At offset 0 with equal move lengths, ext->stock and stock->ext hold the same correlations transposed.
Grid, sums / sqrt(move length), >= 30 observations, the 3 sign-flip null copies (census seeds), the false-
  discovery rule and its placebo: copies of the census's code; checked against stored census records below.

== Interpretation (written by hand after reading the numbers above) ==

Verdict, final data: nothing in 1,753 external series leads US stocks in a way that survives into the next correlation
period, apart from known market-plumbing and calendar effects that carry no outside information. Prediction markets,
tested separately on short periods, behave like every baseline.

The decisive numbers for ext->stock at predictive offsets:
- Real vs chance at |z| >= 4: 882,605 against 848,396 (1.04). Pure-noise externals 1.04; the census's stock->stock 1.03.
- Out of sample: picks at |z| >= 4 keep their sign in the next 1260-day period 50.7% of the time (292,506), against
  50.1% for picks from the null copies and 50.0% for noise externals. Mean signed r next period +0.0017 (null-picked
  +0.0002). Without US-listed ETFs: 50.6% against 50.1%.
- The false-discovery rule fires in 52 of 4,284 matrices against 15 for its placebo. 43 of the 52 are in full history,
  and the passing entries are two known mechanisms:
  a. ETFs leading thin small banks and closed-end funds by a day (stale prices);
  b. monthly or quarterly releases on fixed schedules (the 50 state coincident indexes, BEA state personal income,
     QCEW county wages) lining up with fixed calendars of their own: closed-end funds' monthly distributions and
     earnings dates.
- Survivors (|z| >= 4, then the same sign at |z| >= 3 next period): 1,576 against 918 when picked from the null copies
  (0.63 against 0.37 per million pairs). 88 are the ETF stale-price pairs; the rest are the release pulses of (b),
  whose plain-terms counts are flat.

What changed from ext-all-1: only the final predictions catalog (84 -> 111 series: 20 rolling, 91 finite-life single
contracts) and a final Google Trends file.
- Prediction markets: none qualify for a 1260-day period. The rolling series start in 2021-2024; 89 single contracts
  are too short and 2 too sparse. They are tested on short periods below.
- Google Trends (in attention): ext->stock 1.03 as before; out of sample 50.6% against 50.0% (was 50.4% against 49.9%).
- Everything else agrees to rounding: ratio 1.04, FDR 52 vs 15, out of sample 50.7% vs 50.1%, all controls passed
  (oil, gold and gas vs their producers; SPY -> Asian indices; heating degree days vs gas; the synthetic leads;
  sunspots, F10.7, Wikipedia and noise as chance).
- Run: 2 min 03 s and 8.4 GB.

Prediction markets on short periods (--short-periods: the census's own non-overlapping 252-day periods, chosen by
length and date; 1-5 day moves only, since longer moves don't reach 30 observations in 252 days). The headline is
the 20 rolling series; the 91 finite-life contracts are reported apart. Every baseline runs through the same periods
and null: Google Trends daily (8), crypto (71), a seeded random sample of 100 other series, and 220 noise series.
- ext-short-1, as specified (periods 66, 70 and 74: 2021-07-12 to 2024-07-12). The census's 126-day warm-up for the
  trailing sd keeps young series out: 0, 3 and 11 rolling series join, so there is one out-of-sample step with
  3 series. Predictive ratio 1.01 (|z| >= 3) and 1.02 (|z| >= 4); same sign next period 47.6% (578) against 49.5% for
  null picks; FDR 0 against 0.
- ext-short-2, the latest chain that fits (69, 73 and 77: 2022-04-08 to 2025-04-14). 2, 8 and 12 join, giving two
  out-of-sample steps. This is the fairer test:

      group                predictive real/chance     same sign next period     FDR fired

      prediction markets   1.02        1.03           50.4% (2,885) vs 49.2%    0 vs 3
      crypto               1.04        1.05           49.5% vs 49.7%            8 vs 9
      Google Trends daily  1.00        1.00           49.3% vs 50.1%            0 vs 0

  Survivors: 15 ext->stock relationships against 16.0 from the null copies (2.10 against 2.24 per million pairs).
  The top ones (Fed-change expectations -> USPH or CPRI some days later) rest on a handful of non-zero moves: inside
  the period the top quarter has 21 cases, and in the rest of history the effect regresses (71% -> 55%).
- Same day the prediction markets do co-move with stocks (ratio 1.77). This is what the predictions agent warned
  about:
  - Roll days coincide with FOMC, CPI and jobs releases, when stocks move on the same news.
  - The S&P year-end, gas and BTC contracts largely copy prices already in the panel.
  - The recession and Fed-hike contracts move with rate-sensitive stocks.
  None of it leads.
- Other caveats from the predictions agent. Long flat stretches pinned near 0 or 1 give few non-zero moves; the >= 10
  moves rule and the +-5 clip bound their influence, but their quarters are thin. The single contracts were chosen
  with hindsight (markets that turned out to matter); only 1 to 3 of them cover any 252-day period, so they are not a
  usable test.
- Short periods are weak in any case: 252 observations per period at best, two out-of-sample steps, and 1-5 day moves
  only. A prediction-market lead of r below about 0.15 would not be seen. What the test does say is that the strongest
  relationships found in one year don't come back the next any more often than for crypto, Google Trends, random
  series or noise.

Is any of it tradable? No. The ETF stale-price effect is weak (r about 0.13) and sits in thin names. The calendar
alignments are fixed schedules known in advance, not signals. Prediction markets show nothing beyond chance.

Known weaknesses:
- Timing. known_after_days are the data agents' bounds; a too-aggressive k would show up as false leads at offset 1,
  and none were found.
- Revisions. Population, macro, flu and others are stored as latest revisions; anomaly base periods were chosen
  after the fact. That could only create false leads.
- Calendar pulses. Releases on fixed dates and stocks with fixed dividend or earnings calendars produce persistent but
  meaningless alignments, which the sign-flip null doesn't model. A calendar-preserving null (a circular shift within
  the period) would separate them.
- The census's null is too lenient at long move lengths (63/63: 2.41 in the census itself); compare with the noise
  externals, not with 1.
- Survivorship. The census's universes; 7.5% of out-of-sample picks dropped because a series left the next period's
  universe.
- Monthly series. Release-day moves are often clipped at +-5.
- Near-duplicates (stations, ETF families, the 50 state indexes, Fed-expectation contracts) mean the counts overstate
  the number of independent tests.
- Plain-terms quarters are degenerate for pulse and pinned series.

Integration into the census and the GUI: as written for the first run.
- External series become pseudo-stocks with ids above the store's, an external-column builder sits next to
  variable::production, and only the external rows and columns of each product are computed.
- A calendar-preserving null and noise calibration series would fix the census's long-move ratios and calendar
  alignments for stock->stock pairs too.
- The GUI shows external names, units and the day each value became known, labels 'within delay' offsets as timing,
  and lists 'too sparse to test' series.
- The short-period mode is the right home for young series such as prediction markets.
