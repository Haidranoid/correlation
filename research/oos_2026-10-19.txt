Out of sample: the census's picks and forecasts on the 16 months after it (2026-10-19)

Data
- A new Stooq bulk file, d_us_txt.zip, downloaded 2026-10-19: 13,383 files (34 empty), 28,067,896 rows, prices through 2026-10-19.
  The old file had 11,550 files; the new store has about 1,800 more symbols.
- Built into a separate data folder, ~/.local/share/correlation2-2026-10-19: its own store plus an unchanged copy of
  census/main-2025-06-27. The real data folder ~/.local/share/correlation2 was not touched (same sizes, times and paths
  before and after).
- Since 2026-10-20 this store is stores/stooq-2026-10-19 in the main data folder, and the census is read from there
  too, so the commands below become `c2 --store stooq-2026-10-19 census holdout ...` (C2_STORE=stooq-2026-10-19 for
  oos_track). Run that way, sections 1 and 2 come out identical, with no copy of the census.
- Census main-2025-06-27 ends 2025-06-27, so everything from 2025-06-30 on is data it never saw: 329 trading days,
  2025-06-30 .. 2026-10-19.
- Matching check: the parts recomputed from prices agree. AAPL's relationships give the same plain-terms counts on
  both stores (e.g. "after AAPL rose 3.6%+, OUT rose 7/23 (30%) vs 48% normally"), and sizes differ only in the
  second decimal because the market average changed. The in-sample forecast track record uses the identical 13,694
  directional forecasts on both stores (50.9% old, 50.7% new). 312 of the run's 8,503 stocks are not in the new
  file (delisted or renamed since); pairs with them are left out.

Reading
- The census picks did what random picks do: +21.0% before costs against a random median of +20.3%; 47.5% of random
  accounts did as well. Buying SPY made +24.6%. With costs of 5 bps per trade the picks made +0.4%; at 20 bps they
  lost 42.7%.
- The rank correlation of predictions with outcomes was +0.003, at the edge of significance (t = +2.01), but far
  too small to use: the long-short version of the same ranking lost 0.43 bps a day (t = -0.26), and the picks ended
  where buying all 2,147 candidates did (+21.0% against +20.8%). It is the same faint tilt the in-sample track
  record showed.
- The forecasts were right 51.7% of the time (473 of 915), with 50.0% expected from the up/down rates alone. Counting
  each forecast day once, the 95% range is 47.9% to 55.5%. Mean correlation per stock -0.021, 26 of 61 positive.
  Predicted moves averaged 0.06% against actual moves of 6.36%. AAPL, the in-sample best at 58.0%, was right 8 of 15
  times, correlation -0.20.
- The reversal control was positive but not significant here (+23.0% against a random median of +19.3%, p = 0.41).
  One 16-month window can only rule out large effects: it can tell 50% from about 55%, not from 51%.

Caveats
- Survivor-only data: stocks that left between 2025-06-27 and 2026-10-19 are missing from this period. The $10,000
  test left out 18,078 stored pairs for that reason against 89,022 used, about a sixth of the total.
- About 1,800 more symbols change the market average slightly, so "relative to the market" means this store's market.
- Prices are still split- and dividend-adjusted, so the $5 look-ahead applies as before.
- No out-of-sample replication: the replication test needs the next correlation period to be one of the run's own,
  which takes a census rerun on the new data.
